Here is the assessment of risk of bias for the endpoint "Beck Depression Inventory (total score) post intervention":

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
Justification: The participants were randomly allocated to the treatment or wait-list group in consecutive order. No stratification was applied. Randomization was successful as there were no significant differences in background or psychopathological variables between the groups. Although there is limited detail on allocation concealment, the automated/electronic consecutive nature of the assignment right after participants submitted their email addresses in the baseline survey likely prevented foreknowledge of allocation. Therefore, the risk of bias arising from the randomization process is considered low.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
Justification: Participants received the intended interventions (immediate Deprexis or wait-list). The study was unblinded (as typical for such psychological interventions), but participants received an automated internet-based intervention and no active therapist contact occurred. The intention-to-treat analyses included all subjects and were conducted properly with multiple imputation. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: some concerns**
Justification: The completion rate at the post-assessment was 81.9% (85.7% for the wait-list group and 78.1% for the Deprexis group). While this completion rate is relatively high for an online trial, nearly 20% of the outcome data is missing. The non-completers differed slightly from completers on some quality of life subscales at baseline. Although multiple imputation was used for the ITT analysis, the possibility that missingness was related to unobserved aspects of symptom change cannot be completely excluded.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
Justification: The outcome measure (Beck Depression Inventory) is a self-reported subjective scale. Neither the participants nor the outcome assessors (since it is participant-reported) were blinded to the intervention assignment. In unblinded trials, participants' knowledge of receiving the active treatment or being on a waitlist can influence how they report subjective outcomes like depression symptoms.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
Justification: The outcome (BDI) and the analytical strategy were specified in advance as per the clinical trial registry (NCT01401296) and the methods section of the paper. Results for the BDI are reported completely for both the per-protocol and intention-to-treat analyses, as planned. There is no evidence of selective reporting.

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
Justification: The overall risk of bias is rated as 'some concerns' because there are some concerns regarding missing outcome data and the measurement of the outcome (self-reported subjective outcome in an unblinded trial). The other domains are rated as low risk.

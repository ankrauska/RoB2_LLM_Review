1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns (The manuscript states that "patients were assigned to two groups at random" but provides no details regarding the method of random sequence generation or whether allocation was concealed. Furthermore, there is insufficient baseline data provided to definitively rule out baseline imbalances between the groups, although the authors mention age was not significantly different.)

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk (The intervention involved assigning patients to different scanning collimation protocols during a CT scan. Deviations from this single-point intervention are unlikely, and the lack of blinding of participants and personnel to the scanning protocol would not affect the actual administration of the CT scan or compliance.)

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: some concerns (Four of the 40 randomized patients (10%) were excluded from the analysis specifically because "no coronary calcium was detected on any of the reconstructions." Excluding participants post-randomization based on the value of their outcome (a zero calcium score) rather than analyzing them with a score of zero introduces a potential risk of bias, as the missingness is directly related to the outcome.)

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk (The Agatston score was initially assessed automatically by workstation software, which identified lesions based on standard Hounsfield unit thresholds. Although a radiologist and cardiologist manually adjusted for noise, the highly standardized and partially automated nature of the Agatston score calculation leaves little room for substantial measurement bias, even if the assessors were not explicitly blinded to the scan parameters.)

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk (The study reports on the Agatston score exactly as intended and described in the methods section. Multiple intervals and increments are presented transparently for both groups, and there is no indication of selective reporting of the results.)

6. Overall rating: some concerns (The study is judged to have some concerns overall due to the lack of information regarding the randomization and allocation concealment processes, as well as the post-randomization exclusion of patients based on their outcome value.)

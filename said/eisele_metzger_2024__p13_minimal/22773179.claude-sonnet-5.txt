## Risk of Bias Assessment: Visual Acuity Outcome (Figure 4 – Efficacy, 20/25 or better)

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process:**
**Some concerns.** The randomization sequence was generated using Microsoft Excel's random number function, which is an acceptable method for sequence generation. However, this was a paired-eye design where each patient contributed both eyes (one ASA, one TSA), and there is no description of allocation concealment prior to assignment. Critically, the surgeon performing the LASIK necessarily knew intraoperatively which eye was receiving which ablation profile (ASA vs TSA are distinct laser settings), so allocation could not be concealed from the treating clinician at the time of treatment. There is no mention of baseline characteristic balance checks beyond spherical equivalent, and no CONSORT-style flow diagram. This raises some concern regarding process integrity, though the intra-patient paired design does control for many confounders.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions:**
**Some concerns.** This is an open-label study by necessity (surgeon and likely patient could distinguish which eye received which ablation profile, given they are materially different laser settings). No blinding of the surgeon or care team is reported. There is no discussion of protocol deviations, additional interventions, or whether care differed systematically between eyes post-operatively. The absence of any statement addressing this omission (rather than confirming no deviations occurred) is concerning, though for an outcome as objective as Snellen visual acuity, the practical impact of lack of blinding on the intervention delivery itself is likely modest.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data:**
**Low risk.** All 15 patients (30 eyes) appear to have completed the 3-month follow-up with visual acuity data reported for 100% of eyes in both groups (Figure 4). No dropouts, exclusions, or missing data are mentioned for this outcome, and results are presented for the full analysis set as randomized.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (Visual Acuity, Fig. 4):**
**Some concerns.** Visual acuity (CDVA/UDVA, efficacy outcome) is a semi-objective measure — while it uses a standardized Snellen chart, the endpoint (assigning a line/level) still involves examiner interaction with the patient and can be influenced by unblinded assessment, especially in a within-patient comparison where the examiner may have expectations about the "better" (aspheric) profile. There is no statement that the examiner or optometrist performing the visual acuity assessment was masked to which eye received which treatment. Given the intra-patient design (both eyes examined in the same visit, by the same likely-unblinded examiner, with known allocation), there is a realistic risk of measurement bias, even though visual acuity is less subjective than patient-reported quality-of-life or symptom outcomes.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result:**
**Some concerns.** No trial registration number or published protocol is provided or cited, making it impossible to verify whether visual acuity (efficacy) was a pre-specified outcome with a pre-specified analysis method, or whether other visual acuity metrics/timepoints were measured but not reported. The efficacy outcome is presented according to a standardized reporting protocol (Reinstein and Waring), which supports reasonable standardization of the outcome definition; however, without prospective registration or protocol access, selective reporting (e.g., of only the eyes/timepoints/metrics that showed favorable equivalence) cannot be excluded.

**6. Overall rating: Some concerns**

**Justification:** No single domain reaches "high risk," but multiple domains (randomization/allocation concealment, deviations due to lack of blinding, outcome measurement due to unblinded assessment, and selection of reported result due to absence of a registered protocol) each carry "some concerns." Because visual acuity measurement in this unblinded intra-patient design is particularly susceptible to assessment bias, and because the lack of a registered protocol prevents verification of outcome reporting fidelity, the cumulative effect across domains lowers confidence in the visual acuity result, justifying an overall rating of "some concerns" rather than "low risk."

# Risk of Bias Assessment: NT Pro-BNP Levels Outcome

## 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
**Some concerns.** The manuscript states patients were "randomly allocated... based on a computer random numbers table," which is an acceptable method of sequence generation. However, there is **no description of allocation concealment** (e.g., sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque containers; central randomization; pharmacy-controlled allocation). Given this is described as a "double-blind" trial with MgSO4 vs. saline placebo administered via identical 50 ml syringes, there is no explicit statement confirming that the syringes were prepared by an independent party, labelled identically, and that the treating/assessing clinicians could not distinguish groups by appearance or by clinical signs (e.g., MgSO4 can cause vasodilation/flushing, which could unblind clinicians). Baseline characteristics (Table 1) appear well balanced across groups, which is reassuring, but this does not substitute for a clear methodological description of concealment. Therefore, "some concerns" rather than "low risk."

## 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions
**Some concerns.** The protocol specifies that "patients who received triple inotropic agent therapy for CPB weaning were excluded from the study." This is a **post-randomization exclusion tied to a clinically important intraoperative event** (need for higher inotropic support), which is very plausibly correlated with the severity of myocardial injury/inflammation — the very physiological process NT pro-BNP is measuring. Excluding such patients differentially (if exclusion numbers differed between groups) could bias the NT pro-BNP result. The authors state "the number of patients excluded from the study in each group was registered," but this number is **never reported** in the Results section, and no per-protocol vs. intention-to-treat analysis is distinguished. This represents a deviation from intended intervention/analysis strategy with unclear impact on the outcome.

## 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
**Some concerns.** No CONSORT flow diagram is provided. While the final tables report n = 44/44 for both groups, the text explicitly acknowledges that some patients were excluded post-randomization (due to triple inotrope requirement), and the actual number of patients originally randomized minus those excluded is not reconcilable with the "44 vs 44" figures presented for analysis. It is unclear whether the 88 patients initially "enrolled" is the same as the 88 who were analyzed, or whether exclusions were already netted out before randomization was reported. This ambiguity, combined with no explicit statement of 0% missing data or reasons/numbers for any missing NT pro-BNP measurements, raises concern.

## 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (NT Pro-BNP)
**Low risk.** NT pro-BNP is an objective biochemical outcome measured via a standardized, automated electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Elecsys 2010), with pre-specified assay performance characteristics (CV 2.2–3.2%) reported. Because this is a laboratory-based objective outcome unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of group assignment (the assay does not "know" the treatment allocation, and technicians running an automated immunoassay are unlikely to introduce differential bias even if unblinded), the risk of measurement bias is low, **provided sample handling (timing, storage, freeze-thaw) was consistent between groups**, which the authors state was standardized ("sampling temperature, centrifugation and refrigeration were kept consistent"). This is distinct from the subjective, patient-reported pain/morphine outcomes, where blinding of patients/assessors would be far more critical.

## 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
**Some concerns.** The trial was registered prospectively (IRCT201205069650N1), which is a positive feature. However, the manuscript does not provide access to the full registered protocol/analysis plan for verification of whether NT pro-BNP was the pre-specified primary outcome, at which time points it was to be measured, and whether other biomarkers or time points (e.g., 6 h, 12 h, or an AUC measure) were originally planned but not reported. The authors themselves note as a "limitation" that they "just assessed two points of the serum levels of NT pro-BNP" and did not calculate AUC — raising the question of whether this was the original plan or a simplification after data collection. Additionally, CRP and cytokine measurements are mentioned as not having been performed despite being conceptually central to the "anti-inflammatory" hypothesis — it is unclear if these were planned and dropped, or never planned. Without access to the original protocol document (only the registry ID is provided, not verified against the paper), full assurance against selective outcome reporting cannot be given.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **High risk**

**Justification:** While the measurement of the NT pro-BNP outcome itself is low risk (objective, automated assay), there are "some concerns" flagged in **four of the five other domains** — randomization/blinding procedural detail, deviations from intended intervention (unquantified post-randomization exclusions tied to a prognostically relevant event), missing outcome data (unreconciled patient numbers, no flow diagram), and selective outcome reporting (inability to verify a priori outcome/timepoint specification against a full protocol). Per the stated rules, multiple domains with "some concerns" that collectively undermine confidence in the reported effect size elevate the overall risk of bias for this outcome to **high risk**, primarily driven by the unresolved and unquantified post-randomization exclusion of patients based on a clinically-relevant intraoperative event (triple inotrope requirement), which is directly plausible to correlate with the degree of myocardial stress/injury reflected by the NT pro-BNP outcome itself.

Based on the provided manuscript and criteria, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **between-session habituation**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
Participants were randomly assigned to receive either DCS or placebo. Allocation concealment was adequate as group assignment was managed "by a data manager who had no interaction with participants" and identical-appearing capsules were used. Although there was a baseline difference in age (the DCS group was significantly older), the authors statistically checked this and found it did not explain the group effects. Given the adequate randomization and concealment, the risk of bias in this domain is low.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
Both participants and experimenters were blinded to the intervention (using matching placebo capsules). The manuscript reports that an assessment of blinding adequacy revealed neither participants nor experimenters were able to correctly guess the treatment assignment. Therefore, it is highly unlikely that deviations from the intended intervention occurred due to knowledge of group assignment.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
Out of 34 randomized participants (18 DCS, 16 placebo), 4 participants (2 DCS, 2 placebo) dropped out and were not included in the analyses. The dropout rate is relatively low (approximately 12%) and perfectly balanced between the two groups (2 per group). The reasons for dropout (unreachable, preference not to continue) do not suggest a systematic relationship with the true outcome values that would heavily bias the results. 

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
The outcome of between-session habituation was measured using the Subjective Units of Distress Scale (SUDS), which is a participant-reported subjective outcome. Because both the participants and the experimenters were effectively blinded to the intervention assignment, the subjective assessment of distress is unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
The primary outcome was operationalized as a categorical response (a drop of at least 5 points in average SUDS across the exposure across visits). While this is clearly described in the Methods section, there is no mention of a pre-published protocol or trial registry to confirm that the specific 5-point threshold or the exact method of aggregating the SUDS scores (averaging across the speech) was pre-specified before data analysis began. Without access to a pre-specified analysis plan, there are some concerns regarding the potential for selective reporting of the precise cut-offs used to determine categorical habituation.

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
The trial is judged to have 'some concerns' overall, primarily due to the lack of a pre-registered protocol to confirm that the specific threshold used for the categorical primary outcome (a 5-point drop in SUDS) was not selected based on the data. All other domains present a low risk of bias.

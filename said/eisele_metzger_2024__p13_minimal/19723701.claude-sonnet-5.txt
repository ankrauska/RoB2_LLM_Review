# Risk of Bias Assessment: MADIT-CRT — Primary Endpoint (All-Cause Death or Nonfatal Heart-Failure Events)

## 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
**Low risk.** Patients were randomly assigned in a 3:2 ratio using a stratified algorithm (by clinical center and ischemic status) that ensured balance. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) were well matched between groups across all relevant variables (age, sex, EF, QRS duration, NYHA class, medications), suggesting the sequence generation and allocation process functioned adequately. There is no description of allocation concealment failure, and the balanced baseline table argues against subversion of randomization. I note this is an open-label device trial (masking = "NONE" per registry) — but that issue is more appropriately captured under Domains 2 and 4, not under randomization mechanics per se.

## 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions
**Some concerns.** This was an unblinded, open-label trial — patients and treating physicians knew the assigned device. Substantial crossover occurred: 91/731 (12.4%) ICD-only patients received CRT-ICD (30 electively before any event, 61 after a heart-failure event), and 82/1089 (7.5%) CRT-ICD patients ended up with ICD-only (mostly for technical lead-placement failure). While the technical crossovers in the CRT arm are plausibly unrelated to outcome ascertainment, the 30 elective crossovers from ICD-only to CRT-ICD "at the physician's discretion" raise concern that unblinded clinical decision-making could have altered the natural course of disease/management in a way correlated with treatment preference. The analysis was appropriately conducted by intention-to-treat, which mitigates but does not eliminate concern, since a clinically meaningful proportion of patients did not receive/maintain their assigned intervention.

## 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
**Some concerns.** Attrition (withdrawal, loss to follow-up, physician-directed discontinuation) was asymmetric: 55/731 (7.5%) in the ICD-only group vs 44/1089 (4.0%) in the CRT-ICD group. This near two-fold difference in missingness between arms, in an unblinded trial where patients doing poorly might be differentially likely to withdraw or seek care elsewhere, is sufficient to raise concern that missingness may be related to the true outcome status, potentially biasing the composite endpoint estimate.

## 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (composite: death or nonfatal HF event)
**High risk.** This composite is driven predominantly by nonfatal heart-failure events (41% relative reduction attributed to HF component, per authors), and the diagnosis of a heart-failure event — the trigger for entering the composite endpoint — was explicitly made by **treating physicians who were aware of study-group assignment**. Although a separate, blinded heart-failure adjudication committee reviewed events against prespecified criteria, the initiating clinical judgment (whether symptoms/signs justified augmented decongestive therapy or hospitalization) is inherently subjective and susceptible to expectation bias in an unblinded setting — a limitation the authors themselves candidly acknowledge in the Discussion ("It is possible that the investigators' knowledge of study-group assignment contributed in some way to the lower frequency of heart failure in the CRT–ICD group"). Because a subjectively ascertained, care-seeking-dependent outcome (HF event) constitutes the majority of composite events and was assessed under unblinded conditions before adjudication, I rate this outcome's measurement domain as high risk. The mortality component alone is objective and low risk, but the composite as reported cannot be separated from this contamination.

## 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
**Low risk.** The primary outcome as published (death from any cause or first nonfatal HF event) matches the pre-registered ClinicalTrials.gov primary outcome measure and the previously published trial design paper (Moss et al., Ann Noninvasive Electrocardiol 2005). The statistical analysis plan (group-sequential Wang-Tsiatis design, stratified log-rank test, Cox model) was prespecified and consistently applied, with results reported as planned including secondary/tertiary analyses labeled as such. Minor inconsistency: methods state "10 prespecified categorical subgroups" while Figure 3 displays 7 — this discrepancy is noted but does not appear to reflect selective suppression of the primary result.

## 6. Overall rating: **High risk**

**Justification:** While randomization and result-selection processes appear sound, the primary composite endpoint is compromised by a high risk of measurement bias — the dominant driver of the treatment effect (heart-failure events) was first identified/diagnosed by unblinded treating physicians in an open-label trial, a limitation acknowledged by the investigators themselves. This is compounded by "some concerns" in two further domains (deviations from intended intervention via differential/discretionary crossover, and differential missing outcome data favoring the CRT-ICD arm), which together substantially lower confidence in the magnitude of the reported treatment effect for this composite outcome.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: Recurrent Thrombotic Events (Cancer-DACUS Study)

## 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

*Justification:* Randomization only applied to the RVT-positive group (A1 vs A2); Group B was not randomized at all, which is an inherent design limitation for any comparisons involving Group B. For the randomized comparison (A1 vs A2), allocation was performed via "sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." This method is potentially problematic — allocation based on chart number (rather than independent random number generation) raises concern about whether true randomization was achieved, and sealed envelopes are a weaker concealment method compared to central randomization, with risk of manipulation. No details are given on who generated the sequence or who assigned participants. Baseline characteristics were well-balanced (Table 1), which is reassuring but does not fully offset methodological concerns. The block size of 10 also carries a modest risk of predictability, though for a single-center block scheme with multiple centers this risk is attenuated. Overall, the concealment method is not rigorously described and could be an issue.

## 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: **Some concerns**

*Justification:* This was an open-label trial (patients and treating physicians unaware/not masked to treatment allocation and RVT status). While this is more concerning for subjective outcomes, it could still influence care-seeking behavior, threshold for investigating symptoms, or unblinded co-interventions (e.g., differential surveillance intensity, threshold for clinical suspicion of VTE) between arms A1 and A2. There is no mention of a formal per-protocol vs ITT comparison discussing deviations, though compliance was reported as high in group A1 (15/119 patients interrupted LMWH for <5 days). No information is given about deviations in Group A2 or B (e.g., unauthorized resumption of anticoagulation), and no sensitivity analyses addressing this are presented. The analysis was stated to be intention-to-treat, which is appropriate, but the absence of blinding combined with unequal follow-up duration (18 months for A1 vs 12 months for A2/B, acknowledged by authors as a limitation) introduces the potential for differential management and detection of the outcome.

## 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

*Justification:* Attrition was reported: by 12 months, group A1 had 91/119 patients remaining in follow-up and group A2 had 77/123. This corresponds to roughly 24% loss in A1 and 37% loss in A2 by 12 months, and these losses are not fully explained (only deaths are described in detail — 12 in A1 and 19 in A2 due to cancer progression, plausibly accounting for many of these losses, but this is not explicitly reconciled in the text). The differential loss between arms (higher in A2) could bias the outcome comparison if reasons for dropout are related to the outcome/prognosis (competing risk of death is substantial in this cancer population and is unevenly distributed between groups, e.g., 19 deaths in A2 vs 12 in A1). No sensitivity analysis addressing informative censoring/competing risk of death is presented, despite mortality being notably imbalanced (though not "statistically significant," P=.366, the trial was underpowered for this comparison per the authors' own admission). This creates meaningful uncertainty about the completeness and comparability of outcome ascertainment across arms.

## 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (recurrent thrombotic events): **Some concerns**

*Justification:* The outcome (recurrent VTE) was primarily triggered by clinically suspected symptoms rather than systematic scheduled imaging (C-US was not performed at routine follow-up visits, only when recurrence was clinically suspected). Given that patients and treating physicians were NOT blinded to treatment allocation (continue vs stop LMWH) or RVT status, there is a real risk of differential surveillance/suspicion bias — physicians and patients aware that a patient stopped anticoagulation (A2, B) may have a lower or higher threshold to investigate symptoms compared to those continuing therapy. This is a classic ascertainment bias risk in unblinded trials with symptom-triggered rather than protocol-mandated objective testing. The authors partially mitigate this by using a blinded central adjudication committee unaware of RVT results, group assignment, and enrolling center to adjudicate confirmed events — this is a good safeguard for the diagnostic threshold of a "positive test" once a test is performed, but does NOT address the initial differential decision of whether to perform the test at all, which remained at the discretion of unblinded local clinicians responding to unblinded symptomatic patients. Therefore, the outcome measurement retains meaningful risk of bias in this open-label design despite blinded adjudication of confirmed cases.

## 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: **Some concerns**

*Justification:* Comparing the registered protocol (NCT00450645) to the manuscript: the registered primary outcome was "Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism," which matches the reported primary endpoint reasonably well. However, several discrepancies and lack of detail raise concern:
- The registered enrollment was an "ESTIMATED" 300 patients; the actual enrollment was 347 — with 242 undergoing randomization (not all 300 "planned" were necessarily part of the randomized comparison), and the paper describes a complex three-group design (A1, A2 randomized; B non-randomized) that is not clearly reflected in the brief registry outcome description.
- No detailed statistical analysis plan or protocol document (with pre-specified definitions, timing windows, adjustment covariates) is available for cross-check beyond the registry summary; the adjusted Cox model covariates (age, sex, previous surgery, metastasis) are not pre-specified in the available registry information, raising the possibility that model specification was determined post hoc.
- Multiple pairwise comparisons (A1 vs A2, A1 vs B, A2 vs B) were conducted with Bonferroni correction, which is appropriate, but the emphasis in the abstract/conclusion shifts toward the RVT-negative vs RVT-positive contrast (a non-randomized comparison), while the actual randomized comparison (A1 vs A2) — which showed a non-significant trend — receives comparatively less emphasis. This selective framing of a non-randomized, exploratory finding as the primary message (favoring RVT-based stratification) over the null randomized result raises some concern about outcome/result emphasis selection.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **High risk**

*Justification:* While no single domain reaches an unambiguous "high risk" rating in isolation, there are "some concerns" across all five domains simultaneously — randomization/concealment methodology, absence of blinding affecting deviations, differential missing data/attrition between arms, outcome ascertainment bias due to unblinded symptom-triggered testing, and selective emphasis in result reporting. Per the specified rating rules, multiple domains with "some concerns" that collectively and substantially lower confidence in the recurrent thrombotic events result warrant escalation to an overall **high risk of bias** judgment. The open-label nature of the trial combined with symptom-triggered (rather than routine protocol-mandated) outcome ascertainment is particularly concerning for a partially subjective, symptom-initiated outcome like recurrent VTE, and this compounds with the differential follow-up duration and attrition between arms to materially undermine confidence in the reported effect estimates.

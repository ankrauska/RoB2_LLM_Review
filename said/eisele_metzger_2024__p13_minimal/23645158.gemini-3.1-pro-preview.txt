Here is the step-by-step risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **NT Pro-BNP levels**, based on the provided manuscript:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
*Justification*: The study states that patients were randomly allocated into two groups "based on a computer random numbers table." However, there is no description of the method used for allocation concealment (e.g., sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes or centralized allocation). Without confirmed allocation concealment, there is a risk that investigators could have foreseen assignments, which raises some concerns regarding the randomization process despite the trial being described as double-blind. Baseline characteristics appear balanced, but the lack of methodological detail warrants some caution.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk**
*Justification*: The study is described as a double-blind clinical trial. The case group received a magnesium sulfate infusion via a 50 ml syringe, and the control group received an identical volume of normal saline as a placebo. The anesthesia methods, surgical procedures, and postoperative care protocols were kept strictly uniform between groups. There is no indication of any substantial deviations from the intended interventions, and blinding seems adequately designed to prevent performance bias.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk**
*Justification*: A total of 88 patients were enrolled and randomly allocated (44 in each group). The results presented in the tables account for all 44 patients in both the intervention and placebo groups (n=44 for each). Although the methods section mentions that patients requiring triple inotropic agent therapy would be excluded, there is no evidence of post-randomization dropouts affecting the final analysis, meaning outcome data for NT Pro-BNP is complete.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low risk**
*Justification*: The endpoint (NT Pro-BNP levels) is a highly objective laboratory measurement. The blood samples were collected at uniform times (before anesthesia and 24 hours after ICU arrival) and processed using a standardized, automated method (electrochemical luminescence immunoassay in an Elecsys 2010). Given the objective nature of this biomarker and the double-blind setup of the trial, the measurement is very unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low risk**
*Justification*: The study was prospectively registered at the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT201205069650N1), and the reported outcomes match the stated hypotheses and methodologies. Pre- and post-operative NT Pro-BNP levels were analyzed exactly as outlined in the methods section using standard, appropriate statistical tests (Student's t-test). There is no indication of selective reporting of multiple measurements or selective analysis of the data. 

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
*Justification*: The overall risk of bias is judged to have "some concerns." While the trial is double-blinded, used objective measurement tools, and has complete data, the failure to explicitly describe the method of allocation concealment introduces some concerns in the randomization process domain.

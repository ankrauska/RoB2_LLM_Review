# Risk of Bias Assessment: Acoustic Reaction Time Outcome

## 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
**Low risk.** Randomization was performed using an online tool (randomization.com) with permuted block randomization (varying block sizes of 3, 6, 9), which is a legitimate, unpredictable randomization method. Allocation concealment was adequate — the list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes. Baseline reaction time values were used as a covariate (ANCOVA), and no important baseline imbalances suggesting failure of randomization are reported. This trial was open-label (masking = NONE), but this does not affect the randomization process itself.

## 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions
**Some concerns.** This was an unblinded trial (participants knew which light condition they were in), which is unavoidable given the nature of the intervention (visible light exposure), but it opens the door to performance/expectation effects on a psychomotor task. More importantly, there was substantial deviation from intended light intensities: actual light reaching participants' eyes differed considerably from protocol targets (BRIGHT: 4400 lx intended vs. 1326 lx median actual; CONTROL: 230 lx intended vs. 115 lx actual), with wide inter-individual variability and considerable overlap/cross-over between BLUE and CONTROL groups. This constitutes a substantial deviation from the intended intervention that was not part of the original design and could dilute or distort any true effect on reaction time. The authors appropriately attempted to address this with dose-response analyses, but the core ITT-based group comparisons are compromised by this unintended intervention fidelity issue.

## 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
**Low risk.** Of 74 randomized, only 2 participants were excluded, and this exclusion was due to invalid VO2peak measurements (an eligibility-related issue identified retrospectively), not due to missingness related to reaction time. All 72 remaining participants (24/24/24) appear to have complete pre- and post-light exposure reaction time data reported in Table 2. There is no evidence of differential or substantial missingness for this specific outcome.

## 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (Acoustic Reaction Time)
**High risk.** Acoustic reaction time is a psychomotor/behavioral outcome that can be influenced by participant motivation, alertness, and expectation. Participants were NOT blinded to their allocated light condition (per the protocol's masking: NONE, and explicitly stated "Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to"). Although the test supervisor (RK) was blinded to allocation, the participants — who are the ones actually performing the reaction time task — were not blinded. Given that participants were aware they were receiving bright or blue light (interventions explicitly framed in the literature and by the investigators as alertness-enhancing), there is a real possibility of performance bias/expectation effects on this outcome, which is subjectively influenced by effort and alertness. This is a substantive threat to the validity of the reaction time measurement specifically.

## 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
**Low risk.** Reaction time was a pre-specified secondary outcome in both the trial registration (NCT02203539) and stated explicitly in the manuscript methods ("Secondary aims were to assess the influence of bright and blue light on reaction time and handgrip strength which were pre-specified secondary outcomes"). The statistical analysis plan (ANCOVA adjusting for baseline, plus dose-response regression) was described as pre-specified and registered. The outcome is reported in full (both group-wise comparisons and dose-response) with confidence intervals and p-values, without evidence of selective reporting among multiple analysis approaches or timepoints.

## 6. Overall Rating
**High risk of bias**

**Justification:** While randomization, missing data handling, and outcome reporting selection are all low risk, there is a **high risk of bias in the measurement of this particular outcome** because acoustic reaction time is a performance-based outcome susceptible to participant expectation, and participants were unblinded to their light condition — a critical flaw for this specific subjective/behavioral endpoint. Compounding this, there are **some concerns regarding deviations from intended interventions**, given the marked discrepancy between intended and delivered light intensities and substantial overlap between conditions. Per the assessment rules, a "high risk" judgment in an individual domain (measurement of outcome) is sufficient to drive the overall rating to **high risk**, and this is reinforced by the additional concern regarding intervention fidelity, which together substantially lower confidence in the reported reaction time result.

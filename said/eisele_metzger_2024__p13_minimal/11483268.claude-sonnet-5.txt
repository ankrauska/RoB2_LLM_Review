# Risk of Bias Assessment: Anti-HAV Antibody GMT (1 Month Post-Vaccination)

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Some concerns**
The study is described as "randomised" but no details are provided regarding the method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated randomisation, block size) or allocation concealment. This was an **open-label study** with no blinding of participants or investigators to treatment allocation. While blinding is less critical for an objective outcome like antibody titre (measured by a central laboratory), the complete absence of information on allocation concealment at the point of enrolment means selection bias at randomization cannot be excluded. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) appear well balanced, which is reassuring, but this alone does not confirm proper concealment.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Low risk**
This was an open-label trial, but the intervention (vaccine type) was administered per protocol with no evidence of important deviations. Six subjects were excluded from the per-protocol analysis for protocol violations (wrong vaccine given, travel to endemic area, prior booster), and these are clearly reported and justified. Given that the GMT outcome is an objective biological measurement unlikely to be influenced by lack of blinding to condition, deviations are unlikely to affect this specific outcome.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: Some concerns**
Of 127 randomised subjects, only 121 were included in the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis (59 and 62 per group) — a loss of ~4.7%. Reasons for exclusion are given (previous booster, travel to India, missing serology in 4 subjects), and the amount of missing data is small and roughly balanced between arms. However, the manuscript states that "the results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported" without presenting the actual ITT data or numbers, raising some concern about how missingness was handled and reported for the primary comparison.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (anti-HAV GMT): Low risk**
This is an objective laboratory-based immunological outcome (quantitative antibody titre, mIU/mL), determined centrally by Merck Research Laboratories using a validated, standardised assay (modified HAVAB™ assay calibrated against a WHO standard). Laboratory personnel performing the assay were very likely unaware of individual treatment allocation (samples are processed by codes/batches), and the assay itself is not susceptible to observer or participant expectation bias in the way a subjective/patient-reported outcome would be. The lack of blinding of participants/clinicians is therefore unlikely to have influenced this specific outcome measurement.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Some concerns**
No trial registration or published protocol is referenced, so it is not possible to verify whether the GMT outcome, timing (1 month post-vaccination), and statistical analysis (non-inferiority margin of 4-fold, 97.5% one-sided CI) were pre-specified without post hoc modification. The primary evaluation criterion and non-inferiority margin are stated in the Methods, which is reassuring, but the absence of an independent protocol/registry record for verification, combined with the selective reporting of only the per-protocol analysis (with a claim that ITT results were "close enough" without full presentation), introduces some uncertainty about whether the reported result was selected from among multiple analyses.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **Some concerns**

**Justification:** While the outcome measurement itself (anti-HAV GMT, an objective, centrally-assayed laboratory outcome) is at low risk of bias due to lack of blinding, there are concerns regarding (a) incomplete reporting of the randomization/allocation concealment process, (b) incomplete presentation of missing data/ITT results, and (c) absence of a verifiable pre-registered protocol to confirm the primary outcome and analysis plan were not altered post hoc. None of these concerns alone is severe enough to constitute "high risk," but cumulatively they prevent a rating of "low risk" for this endpoint.

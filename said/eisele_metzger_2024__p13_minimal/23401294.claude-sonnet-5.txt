# Risk of Bias Assessment: Spinal Orthoses and Balance in Elderly with Thoracic Kyphosis
## Outcome: Balance Score (EquiTest SOT)

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Some concerns**

Justification: The randomization method described—"pick the name of the orthosis from a bag including the names of both orthoses"—is a legitimate method of sequence generation. However, several issues raise concern: (a) there is no description of allocation concealment prior to the draw (who prepared the bag, who drew the name, whether this was done by someone independent of enrollment/assessment); (b) the resulting group sizes are unequal (10 vs 8) from only 18 participants, which is unusual for a formally randomized process without stratification and raises the possibility of an ad hoc or manipulable procedure; (c) no baseline flow diagram or details on whether allocation occurred before or after baseline assessment are given. While baseline balance-score differences were non-significant (p=0.443), the lack of a rigorous, verifiable concealment mechanism is concerning.

---

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Some concerns**

Justification: The study is described as "single-blinded," but it is not explicitly stated who was blinded — given that the two orthoses are visually and structurally very different (Spinomed: rigid metal upright, straps; PTS: posterior bag), true blinding of participants is essentially impossible, and even test administrators fitting/observing the orthosis could not be blinded to allocation. There is no discussion of protocol deviations, co-interventions, or adherence to the 1.5-h wearing/acclimation period. No intention-to-treat analysis is mentioned (though no dropouts occurred). The absence of any assessment of contamination or fidelity of orthosis fitting (though performed by a certified orthotist) leaves moderate uncertainty about deviations affecting the balance-score outcome.

---

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: Low risk**

Justification: All 18 enrolled participants who "completed the study" are included in the final analysis (10 Spinomed, 8 PTS), with no reported dropouts, exclusions after randomization, or missing balance-score data. Since testing was immediate (single-session, cross-over-like design: with/without orthosis on the same day), the opportunity for missing data was minimal.

---

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — Balance Score: Low to Some concerns**

Justification: The balance score is a computerized, objective measure (NeuroCom EquiTest force-plate SOT), not a subjective patient-reported outcome, which substantially reduces risk of measurement bias due to lack of participant blinding. The measurement protocol is standardized per manufacturer specifications, and the sequence of test stages was randomized to reduce learning/order effects. However, some concern remains because: (a) the outcome assessors administering/interpreting the EquiTest could not be blinded to orthosis condition (visibly different devices), which could subtly influence instructions, encouragement, or test administration timing; (b) no explicit statement confirms the outcome assessor was blinded or independent from the treating orthotist/investigator. Given the objective, machine-recorded nature of the score, I rate this "some concerns" rather than "high risk."

---

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Some concerns**

Justification: There is no trial registration number, published protocol, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan referenced anywhere in the manuscript. The sample size justification (pilot study on 5 participants/orthosis) is post hoc and lacks pre-registration detail. That said, the balance score is explicitly and consistently identified a priori as the "main outcome measure" in the Methods, and all three prespecified outcomes (balance score, directional control, reaction time) are reported fully for both groups and for the between-group comparison, with no evidence of selective outcome reporting or suppressed analyses. The main concern is the complete absence of independent verification (e.g., clinical trial registry) that this was the sole primary outcome and that no other balance metrics were collected and not reported.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **Some concerns**

Justification: No single domain reaches "high risk," but multiple domains ("randomization process," "deviations from intended interventions," "measurement of outcome," and "selection of the reported result") each carry "some concerns" due to lack of transparent allocation concealment, inevitable non-blinding related to visibly distinct orthoses, absence of a registered protocol, and unclear blinding of outcome assessors. Collectively, these concerns are sufficient to lower confidence in the balance-score result, but not so severe as to warrant a "high risk" rating in any single domain (the objective nature of the force-plate measurement and complete outcome data are mitigating factors). Therefore, the overall risk-of-bias rating for the balance score outcome is **"some concerns."**

**Outcome assessed: intentions to consume the recommended daily intake of fruit and vegetables (RDIFV), measured immediately after the intervention using a five-item self-report intention scale.**

1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**  
   - The authors state that intervention and control booklets were ordered using “a sequence of random integers generated by a true random number generator.” This is an acceptable method for generating a random allocation sequence.  
   - However, the report does not adequately establish **allocation concealment**. Booklets from one pre-arranged pile were distributed in a lecture theatre and laboratory class. It is not reported whether the booklets were externally identical, whether the person distributing them could identify condition, or whether the sequence could have been foreseen or manipulated.  
   - Baseline intention scores were similar between groups (experimental 4.64; control 4.54; \(F=0.24\)), which is reassuring but cannot prove adequate concealment.  
   - Thus, random sequence generation appears sound, but concealment of group assignment is insufficiently documented.

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns**  
   - The intervention was a clearly persuasive leaflet/task telling participants that eating the RDIFV was easy and urging them to make a firm decision to do so; it also required planning purchases and meals. The control task consisted of questions about the questionnaire experience. Participants were therefore very likely aware that they had received substantively different materials.  
   - Participants were asked not to discuss their booklet contents over the subsequent week, but this does not blind them during the immediate post-intervention assessment, which is the time point relevant to this endpoint.  
   - There is no evidence of crossover, non-adherence, or differential delivery: participants completed their allocated booklet under examination-like conditions, and the intervention was delivered in a standardised written format.  
   - Nevertheless, the intervention’s overtly motivational content, coupled with an immediately repeated self-report intention assessment, creates a substantial possibility that participants answered in accordance with perceived expectations. Strictly, this concern overlaps importantly with outcome measurement; however, awareness of assignment and the study’s apparent purpose also raise concern about deviations in response behaviour induced by the trial context.  
   - The ANCOVA appears to include all 218 participants who completed the immediate booklet, so there is no evident per-protocol exclusion for this immediate outcome.

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk**  
   - The intention outcome was measured immediately after completion of the intervention or control task, within the same booklet session. The report states that 218 people completed the booklet, and the intention ANCOVA has \(df=1,215\), consistent with analysis of all 218 participants.  
   - Thus, there appears to have been no missing immediate post-intervention intention data, or at most negligible missingness.  
   - The 33% attrition reported in the manuscript concerns the one-week behavioural follow-up, not the immediate post-intervention intention outcome under review. It therefore does not directly threaten this result.

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — intentions to consume the RDIFV: high risk**  
   - The outcome is a **participant-reported, subjective psychological outcome**: stated intention to consume the RDIFV during the next week.  
   - Participants were not blinded to the content they received. The intervention explicitly stated, “You can do it, so do it this week. Make a firm decision now that you will eat the recommended daily intake of fruit and vegetables each day this week.” It then immediately asked participants again about their intentions.  
   - This is especially problematic because the wording of the intervention directly encourages the construct subsequently measured. A participant who has just been instructed to make a firm decision may report a stronger intention because of demand characteristics, acquiescence, social desirability, transient priming, or a wish to provide the expected answer—not necessarily because of a durable change in intention.  
   - Although the post-intervention measures were described as identical to baseline measures and participants were instructed not to look back at prior answers, this does not address lack of blinding or the pronounced susceptibility of this self-reported subjective outcome to expectation effects.  
   - The scale had high internal consistency (post-intervention Cronbach’s \(\alpha=0.93\)), but reliability does not protect against differential reporting bias.  
   - Accordingly, measurement of this outcome is at high risk of bias.

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
   - The article specifies, before presenting results, a hypothesis that intervention participants would have higher intention scores than controls. It also describes the intention scale and reports the relevant immediate post-intervention ANCOVA result: \(F(1,215)=5.33\), \(p<0.05\). This coherence between stated hypothesis, measure, and reported analysis is somewhat reassuring.  
   - However, no prospective trial registration, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or timestamped prespecification is provided. The manuscript was received in 2003 and reports a relatively small, short behavioural experiment; absence of registration is unsurprising historically but still leaves the risk of selective reporting unresolved.  
   - The study measured several correlated cognitive outcomes—attitude, norms, perceived behavioural control, self-efficacy, intention, and anticipated regret—at more than one time point. It reports only significant intervention effects for intention and anticipated regret, while describing the remaining outcomes as non-significant. Multiple analytic choices were possible, including choice of scale construction, covariate adjustment, and cognitive outcome.  
   - The reported ANCOVA controlling for baseline intention is plausible, but without an analysis plan it cannot be verified as the prespecified analysis.  
   - Therefore, there are some concerns regarding selective selection of the reported result, though there is no direct evidence that the intention result itself was selectively withheld or altered.

6. **Overall rating: high risk of bias**  
   - The overall rating is **high risk of bias**, chiefly because the endpoint is an immediate, participant-reported subjective intention measure obtained after an overtly persuasive, non-blinded intervention that explicitly instructed participants to form the very intention subsequently measured. This creates a serious risk of differential demand-characteristic and expectation-related reporting.  
   - Additional concerns arise from insufficient reporting of allocation concealment, possible trial-context effects due to awareness of intervention status, and lack of a protocol or registration to assess selective reporting.

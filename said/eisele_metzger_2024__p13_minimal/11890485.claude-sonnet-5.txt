# Risk of Bias Assessment: Time to Institutionalization

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: SOME CONCERNS**

Justification: Allocation concealment appears adequate — sealed, nontransparent envelopes prepared in advance, drawn by patient/caregiver. However, several issues raise concern: (a) the allocation ratio was changed mid-study from 1:1 to 2:1 for the final 14 patients, a post-hoc deviation from the original randomization scheme not specified in advance; (b) although not statistically significant, there is a suggestive baseline imbalance in dementia severity (36% severe in intervention vs 24% in control), which is directly relevant since the authors themselves report that severity of dementia modifies the treatment effect. This imbalance, combined with the protocol modification, raises the possibility that randomization integrity was not fully preserved.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: LOW RISK**

Justification: This was an effectiveness/pragmatic trial where blinding of participants and care providers to nurse case management was not feasible, but this does not itself constitute a "deviation" for this domain — it is an inherent feature of the intervention. There is no evidence of contamination (control patients receiving the case management intervention), non-adherence to protocol, or co-interventions differentially applied. The intervention was delivered as described (Table 1), and analysis was consistent with an intention-to-treat approach for this hard outcome (institutionalization ascertained via registry regardless of engagement level).

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: LOW RISK**

Justification: Figure 1 explicitly states "There were no drop-outs from the study during the 2-year follow-up." Outcome status (institutionalized, deceased, or still at home) is reported for 100% of the 100 randomized patients at both 1-year and 2-year time points, and outcome ascertainment relied on a national registry (Social Insurance Institution), minimizing risk of missingness.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (Time to institutionalization): LOW RISK**

Justification: This is an objective, hard endpoint (registry-verified date of institutional placement) rather than a subjective participant-reported outcome, reducing susceptibility to detection bias from lack of blinding. Critically, the manuscript explicitly states that the decision-making groups (chief physician, head nurse, social worker) who determined institutionalization "were blind to the allocation and generally unaware that a patient was even participating in the study." This is a key strength that substantially mitigates the otherwise unavoidable lack of blinding of patients/caregivers/coordinator.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: SOME CONCERNS**

Justification: No trial registration or published protocol is referenced, so it cannot be verified whether the time-to-event analysis, its specific time points (6, 12, 24 months), and the switch to a time-dependent Cox model (justified post hoc by violation of the proportional hazards assumption) were pre-specified or chosen after inspecting the data. The presentation of multiple overlapping analyses (chi-square comparisons at 1 and 2 years showing non-significant results, alongside the significant time-dependent Cox model and significant early-institutionalization hazard ratio) raises the possibility of selective emphasis on the analysis yielding significant findings. Subgroup analyses by dementia severity are appropriately framed as exploratory/descriptive, which is reassuring, but the absence of a protocol prevents confirmation that the primary analysis method was locked in advance.

## 6. Overall rating: SOME CONCERNS

Justification: While the outcome measurement itself was well-protected against bias (blinded adjudicators, objective registry-based endpoint) and missing data was not an issue, concerns regarding the randomization process (ratio change, severity imbalance favoring a subgroup later shown to benefit most) combined with the lack of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan (domain 5) are sufficient to lower confidence in the reported effect estimate for time to institutionalization, without reaching the threshold of "high risk."

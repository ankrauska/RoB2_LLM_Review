# Risk of Bias Assessment: Changes in 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α

**Sir Ian Chalmers here.** Let me work through this systematically, as I always do when reviewing these things — too many trials get a free pass on exactly these points.

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: SOME CONCERNS**

The paper states subjects were "randomly assigned" in a 2×2 factorial design (SAFA/MUFA, then n-3/placebo within each diet group), but provides **no information whatsoever** on the method of sequence generation or allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics in Table 1 appear reasonably balanced across the four groups, which is reassuring, but balance alone does not establish adequate concealment. Without knowing whether allocation was concealed from the investigators enrolling participants, I cannot rule out selection bias in group assignment. The reference to a separate methods paper (ref. 19) is unhelpful here since no further randomization detail is provided in that citation either.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: LOW RISK**

Fish-oil and placebo capsules (olive oil) were matched in appearance/number, suggesting an attempt at double-blinding of the supplement allocation. Compliance was objectively verified via changes in serum phospholipid fatty acid composition (per the companion KANWU paper) and dietary records were collected. No dietary crossover or non-protocol interventions are reported. I see no signal of important deviations affecting this biochemical outcome.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: HIGH RISK**

This is my chief concern. For the 8-iso-PGF2α outcome, plasma samples from **two of the five centres (Denmark and Italy) were unusable** "due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage." This is not a trivial dropout — it represents the wholesale loss of an entire outcome dataset from 2/5 sites, for reasons unrelated to randomization but very plausibly related to differential handling/storage practices between centres, which could correlate with treatment effects if practices differed by treatment arm allocation timing or personnel. No sensitivity analysis, no comparison of baseline characteristics between included/excluded centres, and no accounting for how this attrition might bias the treatment effect estimate is presented. This substantially undermines confidence in the 8-iso-PGF2α result specifically. (The 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α data were available from all centres, so this concern applies less to that outcome — but as the endpoint here is a composite of both, the overall judgement is dragged down.)

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: LOW RISK**

This is a biochemical, laboratory-assayed outcome (validated RIA), not a participant-reported subjective outcome — so the usual concern about lack of participant blinding driving outcome measurement does not directly apply. There is no explicit statement that laboratory personnel performing the RIA were blinded to allocation, which would have been reassuring to state explicitly, but the objective, instrument-based nature of the assay lowers the plausibility that unblinding could have influenced the result. I am satisfied this is low risk for both PGF2α metabolite outcomes.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: LOW RISK**

The statistical methods section pre-specifies the two primary outcomes (changes in 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α) and describes the analytic model (including planned interaction terms and covariates) in what reads as a pre-specified fashion. Secondary outcomes (oxLDL antibodies, AOC with/without uric acid, tocopherols, uric acid) are all reported regardless of significance, including null results — there is no evidence of selective outcome suppression. I would have liked to see a trial registration number to confirm this was locked down prospectively, which is not provided, but internal consistency here is reasonable.

---

## 6. Overall rating: **HIGH RISK**

**Justification:** Although randomization details are sparse (some concerns) and outcome measurement/selective reporting are each judged low risk, the **missing outcome data problem for the 8-iso-PGF2α endpoint is severe** — loss of an entire two-fifths of study centres from the primary nonenzymatic lipid peroxidation outcome, with no exploration of whether this loss was informative or related to treatment group. Combined with the unexplained randomization/concealment procedures, this materially lowers my confidence in the reported treatment effect (P = 0.015) for 8-iso-PGF2α. I would flag this result as high risk of bias and would want to see the excluded-centre data reconciled, or at minimum a sensitivity analysis, before placing much confidence in the "(n-3) fatty acids reduce F2-isoprostanes" conclusion.

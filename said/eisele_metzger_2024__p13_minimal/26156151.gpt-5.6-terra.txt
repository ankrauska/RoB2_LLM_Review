**Endpoint assessed: occurrence of cardiac death within 12 months**  
**Comparison: IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided CTO intervention**

1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**  
   - Patients were randomized only after successful guidewire crossing, which is a pre-randomization eligibility step. Although this restricts applicability to successfully crossed CTO lesions, it does not itself introduce bias in the randomized comparison.  
   - Allocation was undertaken using an interactive web-based response system and was stratified by participating centre. This is a centrally administered allocation procedure and provides reasonable assurance that allocation was concealed from recruiting clinicians until assignment.  
   - The report does not describe the technical random-sequence generation method (for example, computer-generated random numbers or block sizes). Nevertheless, the web-based centralized system, 1:1 allocation, and broadly balanced baseline characteristics provide no indication that allocation was predictable, manipulated, or non-random.  
   - The trial was necessarily not blinded at the procedural level: operators could not realistically be unaware whether IVUS was used. This is not, however, evidence of failure of allocation concealment or non-random assignment.  
   - The trial registry describes single masking of investigators, which is difficult to reconcile with an intervention that procedural investigators must deliver. This inconsistency is unsatisfactory, but it does not establish a defect in the randomization process.

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**  
   - The contrast between strategies—IVUS-guided PCI versus angiography-guided PCI—necessarily required operators to know the assigned strategy. Thus, lack of procedural blinding is inherent to the intervention rather than a remediable protocol defect.  
   - The IVUS group had recommended optimization criteria, but details such as IVUS device, timing of IVUS, pullback method, number of examinations, and subsequent procedural actions were left to operator discretion. This introduces variation within the IVUS strategy, but it reflects the intended pragmatic intervention rather than a deviation from it.  
   - Procedural differences, including more frequent high-pressure post-stent dilation and slightly higher balloon pressure in the IVUS arm, are plausible consequences of IVUS-guided treatment. They should not be treated as contamination or bias; they are part of the causal pathway by which an IVUS-guided strategy might affect outcome.  
   - The analysis was explicitly stated to follow the intention-to-treat principle. The numbers analyzed for cardiac death correspond to all randomized participants (201 per group).  
   - No evidence is presented that angiography-guided participants received post-randomization IVUS or that IVUS-assigned participants did not receive IVUS. The report should ideally have provided a direct adherence/crossover table, but the available information does not suggest substantial deviations capable of biasing cardiac-death results.

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**  
   - Follow-up was nearly complete: 200/201 participants (99.5%) in the IVUS group and 201/201 (100%) in the angiography-guided group completed 12-month clinical follow-up.  
   - Only one participant was not documented as completing 12-month follow-up, and this was in the IVUS group, which had no observed cardiac deaths.  
   - Cardiac death is a serious and generally readily ascertainable outcome. It is implausible that the single missing follow-up observation would reverse or materially distort the result of 0 versus 2 cardiac deaths.  
   - Follow-up duration was virtually identical between groups (mean 359 days in each group), further reducing concern about differential opportunity to observe death.

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome—occurrence of cardiac death: low-risk**  
   - Cardiac death is a relatively objective clinical outcome and is not a participant-reported subjective outcome vulnerable to expectancy effects from lack of participant blinding.  
   - Clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical-event committee reported to be blinded to randomization assignment. This is an important protection, particularly because treating clinicians and procedural investigators could not be blinded.  
   - The definition was prespecified: all deaths were classified as cardiac unless an unequivocal non-cardiac cause was established. This convention may tend to classify uncertain deaths as cardiac, but it was applied as a common rule and was adjudicated blinded to allocation. There is no reason to believe that any resulting misclassification was differential between the IVUS and angiography groups.  
   - The result was based on very few events (0 versus 2). This produces substantial statistical imprecision, but imprecision is not, by itself, bias in outcome measurement.

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
   - Cardiac death at 12 months was clearly specified as the primary outcome both in the article and in the trial registration record (NCT01563952). The reported outcome, time point, and comparison therefore correspond to the registered primary outcome.  
   - The manuscript reports the cardiac-death result for the full randomized IVUS-versus-angiography comparison: 0/201 versus 2/201, with a log-rank P value of 0.16. There is no obvious suppression of an unfavorable primary result; indeed, the authors explicitly acknowledge that cardiac mortality was not significantly reduced.  
   - However, the supplied registry information does not include a prospectively specified statistical analysis plan, detailed rules for handling censoring or missing data, or the planned analysis model for cardiac death. The manuscript reports several related outcomes and multiple analyses, including MACE, cardiac death/MI, individual components, stent comparisons, and Kaplan–Meier/log-rank analyses.  
   - Although no clear selective reporting is demonstrated for the primary cardiac-death outcome, the absence of an accessible, sufficiently detailed pre-specified analysis plan prevents firm exclusion of selection among analyses. Hence, **some concerns** rather than low risk is the appropriately cautious judgement.

6. **Overall rating: some concerns**  
   - Randomization, adherence to assigned strategy, completeness of follow-up, and measurement of cardiac death are all at low risk of bias on the information available.  
   - There are nevertheless **some concerns** about selection of the reported result because the available registration record confirms the primary endpoint but does not provide a sufficiently detailed prospective statistical analysis plan to rule out selective choice among analyses.  
   - The cardiac-death estimate is also based on only two events, so it is highly imprecise; this limits confidence in the effect estimate, although it is a matter of imprecision rather than risk of bias.

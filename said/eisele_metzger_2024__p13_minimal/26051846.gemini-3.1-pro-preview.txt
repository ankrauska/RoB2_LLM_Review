Based on the provided manuscript and protocol, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **pain VAS scale post-treatment**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
The study used a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method by a statistician not involved in data collection, which is an adequate method for sequence generation. However, there is no explicit mention of allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes) to prevent selection bias before assignment. The lack of clarity regarding allocation concealment raises some concerns.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk**
Although the participants were likely aware of their assigned intervention due to the differing natures of the treatments (e.g., 20 minutes for iontophoresis vs. 5 minutes for ultrasound), there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended interventions occurred that would materially affect the outcomes. All participants appear to have received the standardized exercise and cryotherapy in addition to their assigned physical agent.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk**
The study randomized 99 participants (32 to ultrasound, 33 to phonophoresis, and 34 to iontophoresis). The tables and results indicate that all 99 participants were analyzed at the post-treatment and 1-month post-treatment assessments, meaning there is no missing outcome data.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: high risk**
The visual analogue scale (VAS) for pain is a highly subjective, participant-reported outcome. The study is described as "single-blind" in the manuscript, explicitly stating that the rehabilitation doctor assessing the patients was blinded. However, the patients themselves were not blinded to their treatment group (the application and duration of treatments varied significantly, such as 5 minutes of ultrasound versus 20 minutes of galvanic direct current for iontophoresis). Because participants were aware of their assigned intervention, their subjective reporting of pain could easily be influenced by their expectations, leading to a high risk of measurement bias. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low risk**
The measurement of pain via the VAS scale was pre-specified as a primary outcome in the trial registration (NCT01748188). The manuscript reports the outcome data at the pre-specified time points (baseline, post-treatment, and 1 month post-treatment) exactly as intended in the methods section. There is no evidence of selective reporting.

**6. Overall rating: high risk**
The overall risk of bias for the pain VAS scale post-treatment is judged as **high risk**, primarily driven by the high risk of bias in the measurement of the outcome, as participants were not blinded to their interventions while self-reporting a subjective pain measure.

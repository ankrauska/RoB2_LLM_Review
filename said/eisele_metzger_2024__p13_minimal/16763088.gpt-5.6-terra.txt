**Endpoint assessed: bracket failure rate over 12 months (self-etching primer versus conventional phosphoric-acid etch and primer).**

1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**  
   - The trial used a split-mouth, cross-quadrant design: each participant received both bonding systems in different quadrants. This is an efficient design for this endpoint because patient-level characteristics likely to affect bracket failure (e.g., oral hygiene, occlusal forces, age, sex, general compliance) are largely controlled within patient.  
   - Allocation of the system in the upper-right quadrant was said to use block randomization, with the corresponding cross-quadrant allocation determining treatment in the remaining quadrants. The stated method ensured balance between the two possible allocation patterns.  
   - The operators were reportedly unaware of the allocated system until the patient had entered the trial. This provides reasonable allocation concealment at recruitment and makes selection of patients according to forthcoming allocation unlikely.  
   - Although the report does not state who generated the random sequence, who maintained it, or precisely how concealment was implemented, it describes a recognizably random allocation method and adequate concealment in practical terms. There is no indication that allocation was predictable to operators before enrolment or that a non-random method was used.  
   - Baseline comparability between treatment conditions is inherently strong in a split-mouth design, although a formal comparison of quadrant-level characteristics is not presented.

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**  
   - Blinding of operators was impossible because the self-etching and conventional systems required visibly different clinical procedures. This lack of operator blinding is unavoidable for the comparison and, by itself, does not establish bias.  
   - Both systems were applied according to manufacturers’ instructions. The same bracket type, adhesive (Transbond XT), curing approach, and broadly standardized bonding procedures were used for both groups.  
   - The operators recorded bracket manipulations before curing. Although more manipulation was associated descriptively with a higher failure rate in the self-etching group, this appears to be a measured feature of the clinical procedure rather than evidence that participants were switched from, or failed to receive, their allocated intervention.  
   - There is no report of treatment crossover, failure to apply the allocated etching/priming system, differential co-interventions, or exclusion of brackets after allocation.  
   - Analysis appears to have retained brackets according to their assigned bonding system. The use of regression methods allowing for clustering of brackets within mouths also addresses the split-mouth structure.  
   - The unblinded operators might theoretically have differed in their care when applying the two systems, but the report gives no evidence of systematic deviations from the intended protocols. For the bracket-failure endpoint, this risk is judged low.

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**  
   - Thirty-nine participants entered the study and all but one were observed for the complete 12-month period. Thus, participant-level loss to follow-up was 1/39 (2.6%).  
   - The one participant moved away after seven months. The report states that no bracket failures had occurred before loss to follow-up, and the available follow-up was included in the survival analysis. Censoring such a participant at the time last known to be event-free is appropriate for a time-to-failure analysis.  
   - Given the very small proportion lost and the absence of failures before withdrawal, it is unlikely that missing data could plausibly account for the substantial observed difference in failure rates: 11.2% for self-etching primer versus 3.9% for conventional etching/priming.  
   - The report does not provide the exact number of brackets contributed by the participant who moved away, nor does it explicitly demonstrate that censoring was non-informative. Nevertheless, the amount of missing follow-up is trivial and is unlikely to materially bias the result.

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**  
   **Outcome: bracket failure rate / time to bracket failure.**  
   - Bracket failure is a relatively objective clinical outcome: a bracket is either detached from the tooth or remains bonded. It is substantially less vulnerable to expectation effects than subjective patient-reported outcomes such as pain, satisfaction, or perceived function.  
   - Patients were unaware of which system had been used on each side of the mouth. Therefore, differential patient reporting of a failed bracket according to perceived treatment allocation is unlikely.  
   - Operators could not be blinded to treatment allocation at bonding, and the report does not state that the person recording failures was blinded. However, because the presence of a failed bracket is readily observable, knowledge of the allocated bonding system is unlikely to affect the basic determination that failure occurred.  
   - The outcome was monitored prospectively for 12 months, and the date and tooth of each failure were recorded. This supports reasonably consistent ascertainment of event occurrence and timing.  
   - Assessment of the adhesive remnant index is more subjective and potentially susceptible to unblinded assessment, but that is not the endpoint assessed here. For the endpoint of bracket failure, the risk of measurement bias is low.

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
   - The study aim and methods clearly identify bracket failure and survival over 12 months as principal outcomes. The main comparison is reported, including failure proportions, a significance test, Kaplan–Meier curves, and a Cox proportional-hazards analysis. This consistency reduces concern that the principal comparison was simply omitted.  
   - However, no trial registration, protocol, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is provided. Consequently, it is not possible to verify in advance which analysis of the failure endpoint was intended to be primary.  
   - Several analyses were undertaken: crude bracket failure proportions, logistic regression, Kaplan–Meier survival curves, and Cox regression, together with multiple subgroup and explanatory analyses by sex, age, operator, tooth location, and number of bracket manipulations. In the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan, selective emphasis on one analytical approach or time-to-event result cannot be excluded.  
   - There is also a minor internal inconsistency in reporting the overall failure rate: it is stated as 7.6% in the Results and 7.9% in the Discussion. The treatment-specific rates are consistent with the tabulated counts, but this inconsistency warrants caution regarding reporting precision.  
   - The principal treatment comparison itself is nevertheless reported in a reasonably complete manner. The appropriate judgement is therefore **some concerns**, rather than high risk.

6. **Overall rating: some concerns**  
   The randomization process, adherence to intended interventions, completeness of outcome data, and measurement of the objective bracket-failure outcome are all at low risk of bias. However, the absence of a protocol, registration record, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan leaves **some concerns** about selection of the reported result, particularly given the multiple analyses conducted.

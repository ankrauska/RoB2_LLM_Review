1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns (The manuscript states that participants "were assigned at random", but provides no information regarding the method of random sequence generation or whether allocation was concealed. Baseline characteristics appeared balanced, but the lack of details on the randomization process raises some concerns.)

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk (There is no indication of significant deviations from the intended educational interventions. Although participants could not be blinded to their group assignment due to the nature of the intervention, this is unlikely to have led to deviations from the intended intervention that would impact the objective measurement of inhaler technique.)

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk (Of the 92 randomized patients, 85 completed the study. An attrition rate of less than 8% is minimal and is unlikely to introduce significant bias into the results.)

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: some concerns (Inhaler use technique was assessed using a standardized nine-step observational checklist by two observers. The manuscript notes that moderators were "not aware of the study hypothesis," but it does not explicitly state whether the observers assessing the inhaler technique were blinded to the participants' group assignment. Since the assessment requires some subjective judgment from the observer, lack of clear blinding raises some concerns.)

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns (The trial was registered retrospectively on ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT01474928] in 2011, after the study had already commenced in 2009. The trial registration lists 'medication adherence' as the primary outcome, but does not explicitly prespecify inhaler use technique as an outcome, raising concerns about selective reporting based on the available data.)

6. Overall rating: high risk (The combination of 'some concerns' across multiple domains—specifically the lack of allocation concealment details, uncertain blinding of outcome assessors, and retrospective registration with discrepancies in prespecified outcomes—substantially lowers confidence in the result, warranting a high risk of bias rating.)

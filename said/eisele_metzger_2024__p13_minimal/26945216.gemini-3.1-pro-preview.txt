Based on the provided manuscript, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **Pain intensity**.

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
Justification: The study states that patients were allocated to one of three treatment interventions using computerized block randomization. Allocation concealment was maintained using concealed envelopes ("allocation group concealed in envelops"). There are no significant baseline imbalances suggesting a problem with the randomization process. Therefore, the risk of bias from the randomization process is low.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns**
Justification: The participants were blind to allocation, so the study was a single-blind trial. However, the principal investigator acted as both the therapist administering all study treatments and the outcome assessor. Given the physical nature of the modalities (TENS, IFT, hot pack, myofascial release) and the lack of a sham device for the control group, it is highly likely that patients were aware of receiving electrical stimulation (as they were set to produce a "strong but nonpainful TENS sensation"). Thus, participants and the personnel delivering the intervention were not truly blinded. The lack of blinding of the therapist, combined with the delivery of the interventions, raises some concerns regarding potential deviations from the intended interventions (e.g., performance bias).

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
Justification: The CONSORT flow chart and text indicate that 105 individuals were randomized (35 per group), and all 105 completed the study and were entered into the analysis. There is no missing outcome data.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: high-risk**
Justification: Pain intensity is a subjective, participant-reported outcome. Although the study is described as "single-blind", the control group received standard care alone without any sham electrical stimulation device, while the active groups received electrical stimulation that produced a "strong but nonpainful sensation". Therefore, the participants could not have been effectively blinded to whether they were receiving an electrical stimulation modality. Because pain is a highly subjective outcome, the lack of effective participant blinding combined with the outcome assessor (who was the treating therapist) being unblinded introduces a high risk of measurement/detection bias.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
Justification: The outcomes reported in the results (pain intensity via VAS at specified time points) match those described in the methods section. There is no indication of selective reporting of multiple pain scales or selectively reporting time points to favor the intervention. The analysis was straightforward and consistent with the planned methodology.

**6. Overall rating: high-risk**
Justification: The study is judged to be at high risk of bias overall due to the high risk of bias in the measurement of the outcome domain (lack of patient and assessor blinding for a subjective outcome).

1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**  
   - Allocation was generated using a computer-generated random-number sequence with block randomization and stratification by operator and centre. This is an appropriate method for generating an unpredictable allocation sequence.  
   - Allocation concealment was described: appliance assignments were placed in sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes and opened only after the participant had entered the trial. The person generating the randomization sequence was not involved in enrolment or allocation.  
   - Although the reported baseline table shows some numerical imbalance in malocclusion categories and mean age, no compelling evidence is presented that this arose from a defective randomization process. These differences may readily have occurred by chance.  
   - Neither participants nor treating clinicians could be blinded after allocation, but this does not itself compromise the randomization process, provided allocation was concealed at the time of recruitment and assignment.

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns**  
   - Participants and clinicians were necessarily aware of the allocated bracket system. This is particularly relevant because the treating clinicians made decisions throughout a prolonged course of orthodontic treatment, including archwire progression, management of complications, appointment timing, and the point at which treatment was considered complete and appliances removed.  
   - The investigators state that both operators followed a standardized bonding procedure and manufacturer-recommended archwire sequences. This reduces, but does not eliminate, the risk of differential management.  
   - Importantly, the protocol allowed exceptions to the recommended archwire sequence, and these exceptions were reportedly recorded. However, the manuscript does not report their frequency, nature, distribution by allocation group, or whether they affected treatment duration.  
   - The two appliance systems were used with different manufacturer-recommended wire sequences. This may be regarded as part of the intended appliance-system interventions, but it makes the estimate one of the effect of the two complete systems rather than of ligation mechanism alone.  
   - No information is provided on adherence to the allocated system, use of additional mechanics, treatment interruptions, appointment scheduling policies, or whether these differed between groups. In an unblinded trial of treatment duration, such omissions leave plausible scope for deviations or differential care to influence the result.

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**  
   - Of 138 randomized participants, 135 (97.8%) completed treatment and were included in the analysis. Thus, outcome data for total treatment duration were missing for only three participants (2.2%).  
   - One participant, allocated to SmartClip, declined fixed-appliance treatment after allocation and therefore did not receive the intervention. One participant in each group failed to complete treatment.  
   - The two post-treatment-initiation non-completers were balanced between groups. Given the very small proportion with unavailable treatment-duration data, it is unlikely that their exclusion materially changed the comparison of mean treatment duration.  
   - The analysis was per protocol rather than intention-to-treat. This is not ideal, especially because treatment duration is not observable after treatment non-completion, but the low and balanced attrition means that missing data are unlikely to have caused important bias in this endpoint.

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — total duration of treatment: some concerns**  
   - Treatment duration was defined objectively as the number of months from fixed-appliance placement to debonding. Dates of appliance placement and debonding should ordinarily be ascertainable from clinical records and are less vulnerable to assessor interpretation than subjective outcomes such as pain or satisfaction.  
   - Data analysis was reportedly undertaken with examiner blinding, and appliance type was omitted from data-collection sheets to reduce bias during data entry and analysis. These are useful safeguards.  
   - Nevertheless, the endpoint includes the date of debonding. Treating clinicians were not blinded to bracket allocation and had discretion in judging when treatment was complete and when to debond. The manuscript does not specify objective, pre-defined treatment-completion or debonding criteria, nor does it report blinded independent assessment that treatment goals had been equivalently achieved in both groups.  
   - Therefore, although the recorded elapsed time is objective, the clinical decision determining its endpoint was potentially influenced by knowledge of the allocated appliance. This is a particular concern in a trial explicitly evaluating “treatment efficiency.”

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
   - The manuscript states an a priori aim and null hypothesis concerning total treatment time, number of visits, and bracket bond failures. It also reports the principal treatment-duration comparison with a mean difference, confidence interval, and P value. Thus, there is no obvious evidence that the reported treatment-duration result was selected from numerous unreported analyses.  
   - However, no prospectively registered trial record, full protocol, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is provided. Consequently, it is not possible to verify whether the reported definition of treatment duration, analytic population, statistical model, handling of treatment non-completion, or any covariate-adjusted analyses were specified before outcome data became available.  
   - The choice to undertake per-protocol analysis because of “low number of dropouts” appears to have been made or at least reported post hoc; without a protocol or analysis plan, it cannot be confirmed as pre-specified.  
   - There are therefore some concerns, rather than low risk, about selective choice of the analysis reported for treatment duration.

6. **Overall rating: high risk of bias**  
   - Randomization and outcome-data completeness appear satisfactory.  
   - However, there are some concerns in multiple domains: deviations from intended interventions, measurement of total treatment duration, and selection of the reported analysis. In particular, unblinded clinicians could influence treatment conduct and the timing of debonding, while objective and blinded criteria for treatment completion were not reported. The absence of accessible prospective registration, protocol, or statistical analysis plan further limits confidence.  
   - Taken together, these multiple concerns substantially lower confidence in the estimated effect on total duration of treatment.

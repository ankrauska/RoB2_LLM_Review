# Risk of Bias Assessment: 3M SmartClip vs 3M Victory RCT
## Outcome: Total Duration of Treatment

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Low risk**

Justification: The allocation sequence was computer-generated, using block randomization stratified by operator and center. Concealment was achieved using sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes, and the person generating the randomization schedule did not participate in patient allocation. Baseline characteristics (Table 2) show some imbalance (e.g., Class I malocclusion: 12 vs 23; extraction pattern similar), but nothing suggesting a failure of randomization. Blinding of participants/clinicians to bracket type was not possible (inherent to the intervention), but this does not compromise the *randomization* process itself. I am satisfied this domain is low risk.

---

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Some concerns**

Justification: Neither clinicians nor patients could be blinded to bracket type — an unavoidable feature of this comparison — and this creates potential for performance bias, particularly around clinical decision-making (e.g., threshold for adding/changing archwires, or judging appointment intervals) that could differentially affect treatment duration. More importantly, the analysis was conducted "on a per-protocol basis" rather than intention-to-treat: 1 subject who refused treatment after allocation and 2 who failed to complete treatment were excluded from analysis. While the numbers are small, per-protocol analysis in an unblinded trial is more vulnerable to bias than ITT, since dropout/exclusion may not be independent of treatment experience. This raises some concern for this domain.

---

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: Low risk**

Justification: Of 138 randomized subjects, 135 (97.8%) completed treatment and were analyzed. Attrition was minimal, balanced in magnitude between groups (1 dropout in each group per the CONSORT diagram, plus 1 who refused treatment). This level of missingness is very unlikely to introduce meaningful bias for the treatment duration outcome.

---

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — Total Duration of Treatment: Some concerns**

Justification: Treatment duration is an objective outcome (date of appliance placement to debond) rather than a patient-reported subjective outcome, which reduces bias risk substantially compared to pain/enjoyment outcomes. However, the decision of *when* to debond a patient is a clinical judgment made by the treating (unblinded) clinician, who was aware of the bracket type in use throughout treatment. This introduces potential for subtle, unconscious bias in decisions about treatment endpoints (e.g., a clinician convinced of self-ligating superiority might debond earlier, or vice versa). The authors attempted to mitigate this by removing bracket-type labels from data collection sheets and performing statistical analysis blind — but this blinding occurs only at the analysis stage, not at the point of the actual clinical outcome-generating decision (debond timing). Therefore, this domain is best rated "some concerns" rather than "low risk."

---

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Some concerns**

Justification: No trial registration number or a priori published protocol is cited or referenced in the manuscript, making it impossible to fully verify that the treatment duration outcome, its analysis method, and reporting were pre-specified without modification. The Methods section does prospectively define "total duration of treatment" as a primary efficiency outcome, and the results reported (means, 95% CI, p-values) match what is described in Methods, which is reassuring. However, absence of registry/protocol verification prevents a "low risk" judgment; selective analysis or unreported comparisons (e.g., only Welch t-test used without justification, subgroup analyses by operator/center appear post hoc) cannot be excluded.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **Some concerns**

Justification: No single domain reaches "high risk," and the randomization and missing-data domains are low risk. However, three domains (deviations from intended intervention via per-protocol analysis, outcome measurement due to lack of blinding of the clinician determining debond timing, and selection of the reported result due to absence of verifiable pre-registration) each carry "some concerns." Cumulatively, these concerns — particularly the unblinded assessment of a moderately subjective clinical endpoint (timing of debond) combined with per-protocol analysis — are sufficient to lower confidence in the treatment duration result, but not to the point of "high risk," as the deviations are plausible but not clearly demonstrated to have introduced substantial bias. I therefore rate the overall risk of bias for this outcome as **"some concerns."**

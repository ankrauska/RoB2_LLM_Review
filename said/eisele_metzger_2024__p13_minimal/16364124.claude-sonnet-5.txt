# Risk of Bias Assessment: Emollients in Propanol-Based Hand Rub Trial

## 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
**Low risk.** The randomization sequence was generated using a validated software (RANCODE 3.6) with randomly permuted blocks, performed by an independent statistics department (proDERM). Allocation concealment appears adequate: test products were coded as A/B by the manufacturer, and "blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database." This is a within-subject, paired design (left vs. right cubital fossa), which further reduces confounding from randomization since each subject serves as their own control. No evidence of baseline imbalance is reported that would suggest a failure of randomization.

## 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions
**Some concerns.** The trial is described as double-blind, and products were coded to maintain blinding. However, it is not explicitly stated whether the study nurse who applied the formulations twice daily was blinded to the product code (only the assessing dermatologist's blinding is explicitly confirmed). Additionally, 3 of 35 participants had protocol violations during the trial — notably, 2 participants performed "disinfection of fossa cubitalis" themselves, which is a direct interference with the test site and the intervention itself, potentially contaminating the outcome measurement in the affected arm(s). While these devi613ations were identified and a per-protocol analysis was performed alongside ITT, the fact that self-directed disinfection occurred at the very anatomical site being scored for irritation is a meaningful deviation from intended intervention conditions.

## 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
**Low risk.** Of 35 randomized subjects, all 35 were included in the ITT analysis; missing values for 3 participants were imputed using Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF), a pre-specified and transparent method. The per-protocol population (n=32) was also presented, allowing cross-comparison. Dropout (3/35, ~8.5%) was low, and reasons for missingness (protocol deviations) appear unrelated to the outcome trajectory in a way that would bias the result. Sensitivity between ITT and PP results does not show meaningfully divergent conclusions.

## 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — Visual assessment of erythema and dryness
**Low risk.** This outcome was assessor-reported (by a dermatologist), not participant-reported, which mitigates the primary concern of unblinded subjective bias by the patient. Critically, the assessments were performed by a single investigator "blindly" for all subjects, and product codes remained concealed until database lock. Use of a standardized, pre-defined ordinal scale (modified from Fitzpatrick) for both erythema and dryness reduces measurement subjectivity. The main limitation is reliance on a single rater with no reported inter- or intra-rater reliability data, and no mention of confirmation that the assessor could not infer allocation from application-site cues (e.g., residual product texture/smell), which could theoretically unblind assessment despite coding — a minor but non-negligible concern. Overall, the design features (single blinded assessor, standardized scale, product coding) support a low risk of bias for this outcome.

## 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
**Some concerns.** No trial registration number or publicly available protocol is referenced in the manuscript, making it impossible to verify a priori whether the sum score of erythema and dryness was indeed pre-specified as the sole primary outcome, or whether other outcomes/analyses were tried and not reported. The statistical methods section does describe a pre-specified non-inferiority design with a defined margin (1.2) and analysis method (Fieller's confidence interval), which is reassuring and suggests some pre-planning. However, the additional switch to a superiority claim (via Wilcoxon test, p=0.022) after establishing non-inferiority, while statistically justified in the text (citing CPMP guidance), is a post-hoc analytical decision that could be viewed as selective emphasis on the more favorable outcome framing. Without access to a registered protocol, full pre-specification cannot be verified with confidence.

## 6. Overall rating: **Some concerns**

**Justification:** While randomization, missing data handling, and outcome measurement (for the erythema/dryness endpoint) show low risk of bias due to good methodological safeguards (validated randomization, blinded single assessor, standardized scales, ITT with transparent imputation), there are legitimate concerns regarding (a) protocol deviations involving self-administered disinfection at the test site in a subset of participants, and (b) the absence of a verifiable public protocol/registration to confirm outcome pre-specification and rule out selective reporting/analysis switching. These concerns, while not individually severe, are sufficient to prevent a "low risk" overall rating but do not rise to the level of "high risk," as no single domain shows a fundamental flaw likely to invalidate the result.

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: high-risk**  
The study states that the randomization procedure involved "picking the name of the orthosis from a bag including the names of both orthoses." This method does not provide adequate allocation concealment, as the person drawing the names could potentially see or feel the contents, or influence the draw. Additionally, the baseline characteristics show a near-significant difference in age (p = 0.06) and unequal group sizes (10 vs 8), raising concerns about the integrity of the randomization and concealment process. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**  
This was an immediate-effect study where participants wore the assigned orthosis for a 1.5-hour adaptation period followed by immediate laboratory testing. Given the highly controlled, short-term laboratory setting of the intervention, it is highly unlikely that there were any substantial deviations from the intended intervention that would impact the results. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**  
The study recruited 18 participants, and all 18 completed the testing procedure. Outcome data were available for all randomized subjects, meaning there is no risk of bias due to missing data.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**  
The endpoint, balance score, was measured using a computerized and objective posturography system (NeuroCom EquiTest system). Because this is an automated, machine-derived metric assessing the center of mass, the measurement is objective and not influenced by the lack of participant blinding to the type of orthosis they were wearing. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
There is no mention of a prospective clinical trial registration (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov) or a published protocol available prior to the study's commencement. Although the outcomes reported match those described in the methods section, the lack of a pre-specified, publicly accessible protocol makes it impossible to definitively rule out selective reporting of analyses or outcomes.

**6. Overall rating: high-risk**  
The overall risk of bias is judged to be high. While the outcome measurement, missing data, and intervention adherence domains are at low risk of bias, the method used for randomization and allocation concealment (drawing names from a bag) is fundamentally flawed and poses a high risk of selection bias, which substantially lowers confidence in the results.

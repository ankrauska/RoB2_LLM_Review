# Risk of Bias Assessment: MAS Item 6 (Upper Arm Function) Outcome

## 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
**Low risk.** The manuscript and protocol both describe a computer-generated random allocation sequence (1:1) prepared by an offsite/independent investigator, with allocation concealed in consecutively numbered opaque envelopes collected only after baseline assessment by the (unblinded) tRNS intervener. This is a robust concealment mechanism. Baseline characteristics (Table 2/3) show substantial heterogeneity between the two active and two sham participants (e.g., mRS 3,3 vs 3,5; asymmetry index 0.02, 0.11 vs severe/severe), but this reflects the play of chance in an extremely small sample (n=4) rather than a flaw in the randomization process itself. Sequence generation and allocation concealment are both adequately described and implemented as intended.

## 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions
**Low risk.** All four participants received their allocated intervention as randomised; no cross-overs or discontinuations occurred. All 12 training sessions were completed by all participants with no sessions missed. Sham/active tRNS were delivered identically in appearance (electrode placement, participant instructions that stimulation "may or may not be felt"), minimising the risk of participants or trainers behaving differently based on guessed allocation. Analysis was per intended groups (case-by-case, consistent with intention-to-treat given no missing data).

## 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
**Low risk.** MAS6 data are reported for all 4 participants at all three time points (baseline, post-training, follow-up) in Table 3, with no missing data and no dropouts. Given the extremely small sample, complete data availability across all timepoints supports a low-risk judgement for this domain.

## 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (MAS6)
**Some concerns.** MAS6 is a performance-based, clinician-administered outcome rather than a fully objective (e.g., biomechanical) measure, and therefore carries some subjectivity in scoring. The manuscript states assessors were blinded ("triple-blind" design, blinding maintained "for the duration of the study," with the blinding code only revealed after all follow-up assessments were completed), which mitigates much of this risk. However, several factors introduce residual concern: (a) with only 4 participants, even minor unblinding cues (e.g., participant remarks, differences in tRNS device behaviour) could disproportionately influence assessor rating due to lack of statistical averaging across a larger sample; (b) no formal check on the success of blinding (e.g., end-of-study guess of allocation by assessors or participants) is reported; (c) the intervener applying tRNS was unblinded and worked in proximity to the training/assessment environment, creating a plausible route for information leakage in a small, single-site study. These residual concerns, combined with the inherent subjectivity of MAS6 scoring, justify "some concerns" rather than "low risk."

## 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
**High risk.** Multiple discrepancies exist between the registered protocol and the published manuscript:
- The registered **primary outcome** was a **composite score of MAS items 6–8**, whereas the manuscript designates **MAS6 alone** as the primary outcome, with items 7 and 8 relegated to secondary outcomes. This is a substantive post-hoc change in primary outcome definition.
- The registered inclusion criterion specified stroke onset **6–24 months**, while the manuscript enrolled participants **6 months to 5 years** post-stroke — a substantial protocol deviation affecting the study population without clear documentation of amendment or rationale.
- The registered target sample size was **10** (with an "actual" enrolment of 8 recorded in the registry metadata), yet the published manuscript reports only **4** participants, with no explanation of this major discrepancy or of what happened to the additional participants.
- Several registered secondary outcomes (Quality of Life via SSQOL, VAS pain, functional connectivity/fMRI) are entirely **absent from the published results**, while structural CST integrity (DTI) — a registered secondary outcome — is emphasized heavily. Selective non-reporting of pre-specified outcomes, especially QoL and fMRI connectivity data, raises strong concern about selective reporting based on the outcomes obtained.
- The pre-specified statistical analysis plan (non-parametric mixed-effects model, 2×3 design) was **abandoned** in favour of purely descriptive, case-by-case reporting, which is a substantial deviation from the registered analytic approach and increases the potential for flexible, outcome-dependent presentation of results.

These cumulative deviations — changed primary outcome, changed eligibility criteria, unexplained major reduction in sample size, dropped secondary outcomes, and abandoned statistical plan — constitute a high risk of selective outcome reporting for the MAS6 result.

## 6. Overall rating: **High risk**

**Justification:** While randomization, adherence to intervention, and completeness of outcome data for MAS6 are all low risk, and outcome measurement carries only some concerns, the domain of selective reporting shows multiple serious, unexplained deviations from the registered protocol (primary outcome redefinition, altered eligibility criteria, drastic unexplained sample size reduction from a targeted/achieved 8–10 to an analysed 4, dropped secondary outcomes, and abandonment of the pre-specified statistical model). Per the rating rules, a "high risk" judgement in even one domain — here, selection of the reported result — is sufficient to rate the overall risk of bias for the MAS6 outcome as **high risk**. This is compounded by the "some concerns" rating in the outcome measurement domain, which further erodes confidence in the reliability of this result.

1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns (The study used computer-generated random numbers for sequence generation, which is appropriate. However, allocation concealment was performed using "sealed envelopes." Since it is not specified whether these envelopes were sequentially numbered and opaque, there is a possibility that allocation concealment could have been compromised, leading to some concerns.)

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns (The study appears to be open-label, as no blinding of participants or personnel is mentioned. While the outcome of clinical pregnancy is objective, the lack of blinding could have influenced the clinicians' decisions when individually adjusting the gonadotrophin doses based on ultrasound and estradiol monitoring. This potential for performance bias raises some concerns.)

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk (The number of patients randomized (n=241) matches the number of patients analyzed in the results and tables [n=81, 80, and 80 in the respective groups]. There are no apparent post-randomization dropouts or exclusions reported, meaning all missing data concerns are negligible.)

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low risk (The endpoint of clinical pregnancy rate was determined objectively via transvaginal ultrasound at 6 weeks post-IUI. Given the highly objective nature of this outcome, the lack of blinding of the outcome assessors is highly unlikely to have introduced bias in its measurement.)

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low risk (The methods section clearly defines the clinical pregnancy rate as the primary outcome measure, and it is reported appropriately in the results section in accordance with the pre-specified plan. There is no evidence of selective outcome reporting.)

6. Overall rating: some concerns (The trial is judged to have some concerns overall. While missing data, outcome measurement, and reporting are at low risk of bias, there are some concerns regarding the adequacy of allocation concealment using unspecified sealed envelopes, as well as the open-label nature of the study which may have influenced unblinded gonadotrophin dose adjustments.)

Based on the provided manuscript and trial registration details, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **attack number** (relapse rate).

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
The investigators used a computer-based blocking method (four blocks) to generate the random allocation sequence. Allocation concealment was ensured as separate physicians were assigned to randomize patients, and treatments were provided in visually identical packages. The baseline characteristics (Table 1) were well-balanced between the simvastatin and placebo groups, indicating that the randomization was successful.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
The trial was double-blinded. Both the patients and the evaluating neurologists were blinded to the group assignment, and all study medications (simvastatin and placebo) were delivered in identical packages every 3 months. There is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended interventions occurred due to a lack of blinding or that any non-protocol interventions were administered.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: some concerns**
Of the 85 patients enrolled, 14 withdrew from the study (5 refused to cooperate early on, 6 withdrew due to experiencing two relapses, 1 due to chronic exacerbation, and 2 for personal reasons). Dropouts due to exacerbation or multiple relapses directly relate to the outcome being measured (attack number). Although the authors attempted to handle missing data using Intention-to-Treat (ITT) principles and Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) for those who completed at least 3 to 6 months of the trial, the dropout rate and the fact that several patients withdrew specifically due to disease activity raises some concerns that missingness could introduce bias.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
The outcome (attack number/relapse rate) was assessed using a standardized clinical definition ("any new or worsening neurological deficit in the absence of high body temperature that lasted for more than 24 h after 1-month stability"). The neurologists evaluating the relapses and determining the need for steroid treatment were blinded to the intervention arm. Therefore, the measurement of the outcome is highly unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
The reported outcome "attack number" perfectly matches the primary outcome prespecified in the trial protocol (NCT00668343: "attack number, time frame: 1 year"). The data analysis corresponds to the intended measures, and there is no evidence of selective reporting or multiple analysis methods being manipulated to find a favorable result.

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
The trial is judged to have 'some concerns' overall, driven primarily by the risk of bias due to missing outcome data, as several patients dropped out specifically due to experiencing the primary outcome (disease exacerbation/relapses). All other domains are at low risk of bias.

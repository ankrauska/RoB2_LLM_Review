# Risk of Bias Assessment: WRAP Trial – Endpoint: Reduction of Psychiatric Symptom Severity (BSI)

**Reviewer: Sir Ian Chalmers**

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Low risk**

Randomization was executed by independent Survey Research Laboratory staff using a random allocation sequence embedded in CAPI software, providing genuine allocation concealment "up to the point of assignment" — neither interviewers nor participants could anticipate assignment. Baseline comparisons (Table 1) show no significant differences across a large number of demographic, clinical, and service-use variables, consistent with successful randomization. I have no concerns here.

---

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Some concerns**

Several features raise concern. First, this is an unblinded behavioral intervention — inherently impossible to mask from participants — so this criterion must be judged on deviations rather than blinding itself. Of 276 assigned to WRAP, only 233 (84%) actually received any sessions, and mean attendance was just 5 of 8 classes; this is a substantial degree of non-adherence to the assigned intervention. Second, the control ("usual care/wait-list") arm was substantially contaminated: 42–45% of control participants reported attending peer-led support groups during the trial, which share active ingredients (peer support, self-management skills) with WRAP. The authors acknowledge this may have caused convergence between conditions. While an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was used (appropriately estimating the effect of being assigned to the intervention), the magnitude of non-receipt and contamination is high enough to introduce some concern about whether observed effects reflect the intervention itself versus general peer contact, and dilutes/biases the true intervention effect in an unpredictable direction.

---

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: Low risk**

Attrition was modest and balanced: 89.2% vs 87.3% completed T2, and 87.6% vs 85.1% completed T3 in intervention versus control respectively, with no statistically significant between-group differences (p=.29, p=.23). Reasons for loss (death, illness, relocation, withdrawal) appear unrelated to outcome values, and the majority of drop-outs in the experimental arm were non-attenders unlikely to differentially bias the true effect. Overall attrition (~12-14%) is not trivial but is unlikely to induce meaningful bias given its balance across arms and plausible reasons.

---

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (BSI Global Symptom Severity / Positive Symptom Total): High risk**

This is a patient self-report instrument used to measure a distinctly subjective construct (perceived symptom bother). Participants were **not and could not be blinded** to their allocation — those attending 8 weeks of group sessions clearly knew they were in the active arm, while wait-listed controls knew they had not yet received treatment (with an explicit "anticipation effect" acknowledged by the authors themselves regarding controls' improvement). This creates high potential for differential reporting bias: participants motivated by group cohesion, social desirability toward peer facilitators, or expectation of benefit from a program they invested 2.5 hours/week in are prone to reporting more favorable symptom change than an unblinded, less-invested wait-list group experiencing "usual care." The authors' own discussion acknowledges this limitation directly ("study relied on participant self-report data... uncorroborated by clinicians or objective observers"). While interviewers were blinded (reducing assessor-driven bias), the fundamental problem is respondent-reported bias on an unblindable subjective outcome — this is a high risk of bias per the specified criteria for participant-reported subjective outcomes without participant blinding.

---

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Some concerns**

Cross-checking against the ClinicalTrials.gov registration (NCT01024569): the registered primary outcome for symptom severity specifies only the "BSI Positive Symptom Score," yet the manuscript reports and emphasizes results for **both** BSI Global Symptom Severity Index *and* Positive Symptom Total as co-primary symptom measures, with the Global Severity Index given prominence in the abstract. This is an unregistered addition, raising the possibility that the Global Severity Index was included because it also reached significance, while other pre-registered outcomes (Patient Self-Advocacy, Recovery Assessment Scale) are absent from this manuscript entirely (later published separately). Additionally, the registration lists specific hypotheses regarding multiple domains (empowerment, functioning, service costs) not reported here, consistent with outcome-splitting across multiple papers — a practice that, while not necessarily nefarious, increases the risk that this particular paper selectively emphasizes the more favorable/significant symptom findings. The odds ratio for GSI reported (0.95, CI 0.91–0.98) is presented without full corresponding raw-score context in the abstract, and the manuscript itself notes symptom effects "attenuated over time," a nuance somewhat downplayed in the abstract's strong causal language.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **High risk of bias**

**Justification:** While randomization and missing data domains are rated low risk, the combination of (a) high risk of measurement bias due to unblinded, subjective, self-reported symptom assessment in a trial where blinding was structurally impossible, plus (b) substantial concerns regarding deviations from intended intervention (16% non-receipt, high contamination of the control condition via peer-support group attendance), plus (c) some concerns regarding selective outcome reporting relative to the trial registration, cumulatively and substantially lower my confidence in the validity of the reported symptom-reduction effect. Per the stated rules, a single high-risk domain (measurement) combined with additional multi-domain concerns warrants an overall judgment of **high risk of bias** for this endpoint.

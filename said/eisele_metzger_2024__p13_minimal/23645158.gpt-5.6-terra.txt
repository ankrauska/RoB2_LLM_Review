**Endpoint assessed: postoperative serum NT-proBNP level measured 24 hours after ICU arrival**

1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**  
   - The authors state that 88 participants were “randomly allocated” using a “computer random numbers table,” with 44 participants assigned to magnesium sulfate and 44 to placebo. Use of a computer-generated random sequence is, in principle, an appropriate randomization method.  
   - However, the report gives no account of **allocation concealment**. It is not stated who generated the random sequence, who held it, whether treatment assignments were placed in sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or whether an independent pharmacy prepared and dispensed study infusions. A random sequence is not sufficient if the upcoming assignment could have been predicted or manipulated before enrolment.  
   - The trial is described as double-blind and the use of equal-volume saline placebo in identical 50-mL syringes is compatible with blinding. Nevertheless, there is no explicit confirmation that treatment containers were indistinguishable, or that those enrolling participants and administering the infusions could not know assignments.  
   - Baseline characteristics reported in Table 1 appear broadly comparable, including baseline NT-proBNP concentration. This does not resolve the absence of information on allocation concealment.  
   - **Judgement: some concerns**, principally because concealment of the allocation sequence is not described.

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns**  
   - The intended interventions are reasonably clearly specified: magnesium sulfate 15 mg/kg/hour from after induction of anaesthesia until skin closure, compared with an equal volume of normal saline placebo.  
   - The authors state that anaesthesia, surgical procedures, cardiopulmonary-bypass procedures, and postoperative-care protocols were the same in both groups, and that clinicians were the same. This is reassuring.  
   - The double-blind design and placebo infusion should have limited deviations arising from clinicians’ or participants’ knowledge of treatment assignment.  
   - However, magnesium sulfate may have clinically recognizable physiological effects. The report provides no assessment of whether blinding was maintained, whether emergency unblinding occurred, or whether clinicians could correctly guess allocation.  
   - More importantly, the protocol states that patients requiring **triple inotropic-agent therapy during weaning from cardiopulmonary bypass were excluded from the study**. Such exclusion would occur after randomization and after the intervention had begun. Need for intensive inotropic support is plausibly related to postoperative cardiac dysfunction and, consequently, to postoperative NT-proBNP. Excluding such participants could bias the estimated effect if exclusions occurred differentially by treatment group.  
   - The authors state that the numbers excluded in each group were registered, but those numbers are not reported. It is therefore impossible to determine whether this post-randomization exclusion was applied, how often, or symmetrically. The tables still show 44 participants in each group, but the participant flow is inadequately reported.  
   - **Judgement: some concerns**, due to the possibility of post-randomization exclusion based on a prognostically important clinical event and insufficient information on adherence and maintenance of blinding.

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: some concerns**  
   - The report states that 88 patients were enrolled and presents NT-proBNP results for groups of 44 participants each. This superficially suggests complete outcome data.  
   - However, there is no participant-flow diagram and no explicit accounting of: numbers randomized; numbers receiving the allocated intervention; numbers excluded after randomization; deaths, withdrawals, sample-processing failures, unavailable blood specimens, or missing 24-hour NT-proBNP measurements.  
   - This omission is important because the investigators prospectively stated that patients receiving triple inotropic therapy for cardiopulmonary-bypass weaning would be excluded. The paper does not disclose whether any such exclusions occurred, whether postoperative NT-proBNP samples were available for them, or whether their exclusion differed between groups.  
   - NT-proBNP may be particularly difficult to obtain or interpret in participants with severe postoperative deterioration, death, reoperation, or prolonged ICU instability. The report does not address these possibilities.  
   - There is no analysis demonstrating that missing data were absent or unlikely to be related to the true 24-hour NT-proBNP values.  
   - **Judgement: some concerns**, because the nominal group sizes are reported but the absence, amount, reasons, and handling of missing outcome data cannot be verified.

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (NT-proBNP levels): low risk**  
   - NT-proBNP is an objective biochemical outcome, measured from blood samples using an electrochemiluminescence immunoassay on the Elecsys 2010 platform. The assay method, analytical coefficient of variation (2.2–3.2%), and detection range are reported.  
   - Blood sampling times were prespecified in the methods: before anaesthesia and 24 hours after arrival in the ICU. The specimen processing procedure—EDTA samples, centrifugation, freezing, and storage—is described and said to have been consistent throughout the study.  
   - Unlike subjective outcomes such as pain, measurement of a laboratory analyte is unlikely to be materially influenced by whether participants or treating staff knew treatment allocation.  
   - The report does not explicitly state that laboratory personnel were blinded to treatment group. This is a reporting deficiency, but it is unlikely that knowledge of allocation could influence an automated electrochemiluminescence assay or the numerical NT-proBNP result.  
   - There is no indication that measurement procedures differed between groups.  
   - **Judgement: low risk**, because the outcome is objectively measured using a standardized automated assay, and differential outcome measurement is unlikely.

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
   - The trial is stated to have been registered prospectively at the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT201205069650N1). This is potentially reassuring.  
   - However, the registry record and a full prespecified protocol are not provided here. Consequently, it cannot be verified whether postoperative NT-proBNP at exactly 24 hours after ICU arrival was the prespecified primary outcome, whether other NT-proBNP time points were planned, or whether the chosen analysis was specified before outcome data were available.  
   - The paper reports baseline and one postoperative NT-proBNP measurement, while acknowledging that repeated measurements, trends, and area-under-the-curve analyses were not undertaken. This may simply reflect the study design, but without the protocol or accessible registry details it cannot be determined whether other planned measurements or analyses were omitted.  
   - The statistical approach is minimally described as use of Student’s *t* test. There is no prespecified estimand, no adjustment for baseline NT-proBNP, no discussion of distributional assumptions despite NT-proBNP values often being skewed, and no confidence intervals. These issues do not themselves prove selective reporting, but they leave uncertainty about whether the reported statistically significant comparison was selected from multiple possible analyses.  
   - **Judgement: some concerns**, because registration is cited but the prespecified outcome definition, time point, and analysis plan cannot be checked against the reported result.

6. **Overall rating: some concerns**  
   - The NT-proBNP result has **low risk of bias in outcome measurement**, because it is an objectively measured laboratory endpoint.  
   - Nevertheless, there are **some concerns** in several other domains: allocation concealment is not reported; post-randomization exclusion of participants requiring triple inotropic support may have occurred without adequate reporting; completeness of outcome data is not demonstrable; and selective reporting cannot be adequately assessed without the registry record or protocol.  
   - These limitations reduce confidence in the reported lower 24-hour NT-proBNP level with magnesium sulfate, although the available report does not establish a high risk of bias.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: Topiramate for Olanzapine-Related Weight Gain
## Outcome: Weight

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Some concerns**
Justification: The paper states "randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration" but provides no description of the actual sequence generation method (e.g., computer-generated, random number table) or the specific allocation concealment mechanism beyond the vague term "confidentially." Numbered boxes are mentioned for tablet dispensing, which is reassuring, but insufficient detail is given to confirm concealment was robust. Additionally, there is a discrepancy between the abstract/methods (assigned n=25 topiramate vs n=24 placebo) and the final analyzed groups (25 vs 18), with baseline imbalances not statistically tested (e.g., differing psychiatric diagnosis distribution, slightly different baseline weight/olanzapine dose). No baseline comparison p-values are given to confirm the randomization achieved balance. This lack of transparency raises some concern regarding the integrity of the randomization process.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Some concerns**
Justification: Both patients and clinicians were blinded, and olanzapine dosing was reportedly kept constant. However, concomitant psychotropic medications (benzodiazepines, various antidepressants) differed considerably between groups in type and dose range, introducing potential confounding of the weight outcome (many of these agents have their own effects on weight/appetite). There is no mention of whether an intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was used; instead, the six dropouts (all placebo) were excluded entirely from analysis, indicating a per-protocol approach was used for the primary analysis. This deviates from the ideal handling of intended interventions/analysis and could bias the weight effect estimate.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: High risk**
Justification: Six of 61 eligible subjects (and 6/24 randomized to placebo, i.e., 25% of that arm) dropped out for "failing to appear for more than one evaluation," and notably **all** dropouts came from the placebo group, with **zero** dropouts in the topiramate group. This asymmetric attrition is highly suspicious — it suggests differential engagement/motivation (possibly related to lack of perceived benefit in the placebo arm) that could plausibly relate to the weight outcome itself. No sensitivity analysis, ITT analysis, or imputation for missing weight data was performed; missing participants were simply excluded. This differential and outcome-related attrition is a serious threat to the validity of the weight comparison.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (Weight): Low risk**
Justification: Weight is an objective, physically measured outcome (using a scale), not a participant-reported subjective measure. Blinding of participants and clinicians was reported, minimizing the likelihood of differential measurement bias by assessors. However, it should be noted that topiramate causes visible, sometimes rapid weight loss, and known side effects (paresthesia) could have functionally unblinded some participants or raters toward the end of the study, but because the outcome is objectively measured (not self-reported/subjectively rated), this is unlikely to introduce meaningful measurement bias into the recorded weight value itself.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Some concerns**
Justification: No trial registration number, published protocol, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is referenced anywhere in the manuscript. While weight is plausibly a genuine primary outcome (declared as such in the abstract and consistent with the study rationale), the absence of any prospectively registered protocol makes it impossible to verify whether other weight-related outcome measures (e.g., BMI, waist circumference) were collected but not reported, or whether the analysis method (Mann-Whitney U, specific timepoints reported) was pre-specified versus chosen post hoc. This lack of independent verification introduces some concern.

## 6. Overall Rating: **High risk of bias**

Justification: While the outcome measurement itself (objective weight) is low risk, the combination of (a) inadequately described randomization/allocation concealment, (b) concomitant medication differences and per-protocol handling of deviations, (c) markedly asymmetric and outcome-relevant missing data (all dropouts in the placebo arm, unaddressed by ITT), and (d) absence of a registered protocol for verifying outcome-selection integrity, cumulatively and substantially lower confidence in the reported weight-loss effect of topiramate. The differential attrition alone (domain 3, "high risk") is sufficient to drive the overall rating to **high risk**, and this is compounded by multiple "some concerns" domains.

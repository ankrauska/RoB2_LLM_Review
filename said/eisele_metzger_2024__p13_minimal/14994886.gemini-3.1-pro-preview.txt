**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
Justification: The manuscript states that "patients were randomly selected for insertion of the 0.014 or 0.016 inch wires", but it fails to provide any details on the method of random sequence generation or whether allocation was adequately concealed prior to assignment. Without this information, it is impossible to completely rule out selection bias during the enrollment process.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns**
Justification: The study does not mention whether the patients or the treating clinicians were blinded to the intervention (wire size). While the two wires (0.014 and 0.016 inch) might be visually difficult for patients to distinguish, the lack of explicit blinding procedures for patients and personnel raises some concerns about potential deviations from intended interventions, particularly regarding concomitant care (such as recommendations for pain relief).

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
Justification: The study states that "The questionnaires were completed by all 109 patients and returned at the following appointment." Since there was no loss to follow-up or missing data for the assessment of initial pain, the risk of bias in this domain is low.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: high-risk**
Justification: Initial pain is a highly subjective, participant-reported outcome. The manuscript does not state that patients were blinded to their group assignment. According to the criteria, for subjective outcomes such as pain, the risk of bias is potentially high if the patients were not explicitly blinded to the intervention, as knowledge of receiving a thicker or thinner wire could influence their pain perception and reporting. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
Justification: There is no mention of a pre-published trial protocol or a clinical trials registry entry. Without a pre-specified analysis plan, we cannot completely rule out the possibility of selective reporting of the results, although the outcomes described in the methods section were comprehensively reported in the results.

**6. Overall rating: high-risk**
Justification: The study is judged to be at high risk of bias overall because the measurement of the outcome domain is rated as high risk (due to a subjective self-reported outcome without clear participant blinding). Additionally, there are some concerns regarding the randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, and selection of the reported results.

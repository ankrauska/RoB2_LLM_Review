**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**  
Justification: Patients were randomly assigned using a centralized 24-hour automated telephone randomization system administered by the University of Leeds Clinical Trials Research Unit. This ensures adequate allocation concealment. A minimization algorithm incorporating a random element was used to balance prognostic factors (consultant breast surgeon, age, breast density). Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between the two groups, indicating that the randomization process was successful. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**  
Justification: The trial was open-label by nature, as masking the imaging intervention is not feasible. However, the trial was pragmatic, and clinical management was based on the assigned imaging modalities. The analysis was conducted on an intention-to-treat basis. A small number of patients crossed over or did not receive the intended intervention (e.g., 9 assigned to no MRI received an MRI, and 55 assigned to MRI did not receive an MRI with interpretable results), but they were correctly analyzed in their randomly assigned groups. There is no evidence of substantial deviations from the intended interventions that would bias the intention-to-treat effect.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**  
Justification: The primary endpoint (reoperation rate) was assessed for nearly all randomized patients. As shown in the trial profile (Figure 1), only 4 patients in the MRI group and 6 patients in the no-MRI group were lost to follow-up. These missing participants represent a tiny fraction (less than 1%) of the total sample of 1623 randomized patients. Furthermore, patients lost to follow-up were classified as not having a primary-endpoint event according to a predefined rule, which is acceptable given the very low number of missing data.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**  
Justification: The primary endpoint is the reoperation rate (proportion of patients undergoing a repeat operation or further mastectomy within 6 months, or a pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation). Reoperation is an objective, standardized clinical event recorded in patient notes and driven by histopathological assessment of clear margins (which was standardized according to NHS BSP guidelines). Additionally, the criteria for "pathologically avoidable mastectomy" were pre-specified and adjudicated by an independent Data Monitoring and Ethics Committee (DMEC). Although the trial was not blinded to clinicians and patients, the outcome measurement is objective enough to be resistant to assessor bias. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**  
Justification: The trial was prospectively registered (ISRCTN 57474502) and a detailed protocol was written beforehand (as well as published as a full HTA monograph). The primary outcome reported in the manuscript (reoperation rate) strictly aligns with the pre-specified primary endpoint. The statistical analysis plan was followed, and there is no evidence of selective reporting of multiple analyses or outcome measurements.

**6. Overall rating: low risk**  
Justification: All assessed domains (randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, and selection of the reported result) were judged to be at a low risk of bias.

1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns** (Patients were said to have been “allocated at random,” using sealed envelopes. However, the report does not state how the random sequence was generated, whether envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque and tamper-proof, or who prepared and opened them. Thus, allocation concealment cannot be verified. This is particularly important because the assigned FIO2 was necessarily apparent to the anaesthetic team. There are also relevant baseline imbalances in this very small trial: group 1 had higher baseline mean pulmonary artery pressure (25 versus 19 mm Hg), substantially longer CPB time (116 versus 84 min), more valve operations (9 versus 6), and a markedly different sex distribution. These may be chance imbalances, but they undermine confidence that randomization achieved comparable groups. No adjusted analysis was reported.)

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns** (The intervention—FIO2 1.0 versus 0.35 after CPB—could not realistically be blinded from the clinicians delivering ventilation. The manuscript reports that anaesthesia, CPB methods, ventilation mode apart from FIO2, postoperative ventilation, sedation and extubation criteria were standardized, which is reassuring. Nevertheless, it does not document adherence to allocated FIO2, actual delivered FIO2 over time, protocol deviations, crossovers, or whether other intraoperative/post-CPB management decisions were comparable by group. Since clinicians knew allocation and could potentially alter care in response to oxygenation, some concern remains. There is no indication that analysis departed from the groups originally allocated, although this is not explicitly stated.)

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: high risk** (The report does not clearly account for venous ET-1 measurements for every randomized participant at every scheduled time point. Most importantly, one participant in the moderate-FIO2 group had high arterial ET-1 values throughout the study and was excluded from the statistical analysis “to simplify statistical interpretation,” despite the investigators reporting no clinical explanation for the finding. Excluding an apparently valid randomized participant because their outcome values are inconvenient is inappropriate and creates a serious risk of bias. Although the text identifies the outlier as having high *arterial* ET-1 values, it does not make clear whether that patient’s mixed-venous ET-1 values were also excluded, nor does it present a complete-participant accounting. No sensitivity analysis including the excluded participant was reported. With only 10 participants per group, exclusion of even one participant can materially alter results.)

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome—venous concentrations of ET-1: low risk** (Mixed-venous blood was obtained from the distal lumen of a pulmonary-artery catheter at prespecified study time points. Samples were handled using described procedures—EDTA anticoagulation, immediate cooling, centrifugation within one hour, storage at −70°C—and ET-1 was measured in duplicate with a commercial sandwich enzyme immunoassay. Reported intra- and interassay coefficients of variation and the detection limit suggest a technically credible laboratory measurement. Venous ET-1 concentration is an objective biochemical outcome, unlikely to be materially influenced by lack of blinding of patients or treating clinicians. The manuscript does not state whether laboratory personnel were blinded to treatment allocation or sampling time, which would have been preferable; however, there is no clear mechanism by which awareness of allocation would substantially influence an automated/standardized duplicate immunoassay result. This judgement concerns measurement itself, not the subsequent unjustified exclusion of an outlying result, which is addressed under missing data and selective reporting.)

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: high risk** (No trial registration, protocol, prespecified statistical analysis plan, or prespecified primary endpoint is provided. The investigators measured arterial and mixed-venous ET-1 and Big ET-1, venous admixture, oxygenation indices, haemodynamic variables, and multiple time points, and conducted multiple between-group, within-group and correlation analyses. There is therefore considerable scope to select favourable comparisons. More seriously, the authors explicitly excluded an unexplained participant with high ET-1 values from statistical analysis “to simplify statistical interpretation.” This is direct evidence of outcome-data-dependent analytical selection. The report does not provide analyses retaining all randomized participants, nor does it establish that the reported mixed-venous ET-1 comparison and time point were specified in advance.)

6. **Overall rating: high risk of bias** (The result for venous ET-1 concentration is at high risk of bias because of the unjustified exclusion of an outcome outlier without a prespecified, clinically or technically defensible rule, combined with absent protocol/registration information and substantial opportunity for selective selection among outcomes, time points and analyses. Concerns about allocation concealment and small-sample baseline imbalance further reduce confidence.)

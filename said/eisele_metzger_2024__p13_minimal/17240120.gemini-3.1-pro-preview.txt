Based on the provided manuscript, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the endpoint **time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment**, using the specified criteria.

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
**Justification:** The allocation sequence was adequately generated using a "computer-generated randomisation list." Allocation concealment was appropriate as the sequence was "contained in a set of sealed opaque envelopes" and unknown to the investigators. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) and clinical parameters (Table 2) were well-balanced between the escitalopram and placebo groups, indicating that the randomization was successfully implemented. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
**Justification:** Both participants and study personnel were adequately blinded to the intervention ("All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study"). The study medications were identical in appearance, taste, and smell to preserve the blind. The primary efficacy analysis correctly used an intention-to-treat (ITT) approach, which included all randomized patients who took at least one dose of the trial medication and had at least one post-randomization efficacy assessment. There is no evidence of substantial deviations from the intended interventions that would impact the effect estimate.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
**Justification:** The withdrawal rates (excluding relapses) were low and balanced between the two groups (7.9% for escitalopram and 8.9% for placebo). Survival analysis (time-to-event) appropriately handles censored data for patients who withdrew prior to experiencing a relapse. Given the similar and low proportions of missing data across arms, missingness is unlikely to depend on the true outcome, thus posing a low risk of bias.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
**Justification:** The outcome (time to relapse) was defined primarily by an increase in the Y-BOCS total score of ≥5 points, which relies on clinician-administered scales. Because all study personnel, including outcome assessors, were firmly blinded to treatment allocation, there is very little risk that knowledge of the assigned intervention could have influenced the assessment of the outcome. Standardized rater training was also employed to ensure inter-rater reliability.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
**Justification:** The manuscript explicitly states that "The prospectively defined primary analysis of efficacy was the time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment." The reported results match the prespecified primary endpoint and the statistical methods (log-rank test, Cox proportional hazards model, Kaplan-Meier curves) align with standard practices for survival data. There is no evidence of selective reporting or data dredging.

**6. Overall rating: low risk**
**Justification:** All individual domains are judged to be at a low risk of bias. The study utilized rigorous methods for sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding, and intention-to-treat analysis. Missing data was minimal and well-balanced, and the reported primary outcome matched the prospectively defined protocol.

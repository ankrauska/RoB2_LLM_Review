# Risk of Bias Assessment: COMICE Trial (Reoperation Rate Outcome)

## 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
**Low risk.** Randomization was conducted via an independent, automated 24-hour telephone system administered by the University of Leeds Clinical Trials Research Unit, using a minimisation algorithm incorporating a random element (minimisation factors: surgeon, age, breast density). This method ensures adequate allocation concealment, as the surgeon/nurse could not predict or manipulate assignment before enrolment—they had to phone in after the patient consented. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) are well balanced between groups, supporting effective randomization. Although this is an open-label trial (no blinding), for the objective, hard outcome of reoperation rate, lack of blinding of participants/clinicians does not directly compromise the randomization process itself. I have some residual concern that 2 patients were "randomised after surgery," suggesting a minor procedural violation, but this is very small in magnitude and unlikely to bias the overall result.

## 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions
**Some concerns.** This is an open-label trial with no blinding of participants, treating clinicians, or surgeons to allocation. For reoperation rate—a decision partly driven by clinician judgement and surgical decision-making (e.g., decisions to biopsy contralateral lesions, proceed to mastectomy based on MRI findings, or patient choice)—lack of blinding could plausibly influence intervention fidelity and subsequent management decisions in ways that don't perfectly reflect the "MRI vs no MRI" comparison in real-world equipoise. Notably, several protocol deviations occurred: 9 patients in the no-MRI group received MRI (6 patient request, 3 clinician request), and 53 in the MRI group did not have interpretable/available MRI before surgery. Additionally, 3 patients chose mastectomy electively, which was counted as a "reoperation" event, introducing a management pathway not strictly attributable to imaging modality. These deviations were relatively modest in number and analysis was done by intention-to-treat, which is appropriate and reduces (but does not eliminate) this concern.

## 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
**Low risk.** Missing data were minimal: 4 lost to follow-up in the MRI group and 6 in the no-MRI group (out of 816 and 807, respectively), representing <1% of participants. All randomised patients were analysed in their allocated group (816 vs 807) using intention-to-treat, with those lost to follow-up conservatively classified as not having a primary-endpoint event—a pre-specified rule stated in the methods. Given the very low attrition, this is unlikely to bias the treatment effect estimate for reoperation rate.

## 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — Reoperation rate
**Low risk.** Reoperation rate is an objective, well-defined outcome ascertained via surgical and histopathological records (repeat wide local excision, further mastectomy, or pathologically avoidable mastectomy), not a patient-reported or subjective outcome. It is unlikely that lack of blinding of participants would influence whether a reoperation occurred, since this is a hard clinical/surgical event, not a self-reported symptom. However, ascertainment of "pathologically avoidable mastectomy" required judgement calls by the DMEC based on pre-specified criteria, and outcome assessors (surgeons/multidisciplinary teams) were not blinded to allocation (i.e., they knew if MRI results existed and could act on them, which is inherent to the intervention). This lack of blinding in outcome determination for the "avoidable mastectomy" component introduces a modest risk of bias, but this was mitigated by having a pre-specified definition of "pathologically avoidable mastectomy" adjudicated by an independent DMEC. Overall the outcome measurement process is objective and mitigated by pre-specified adjudication rules — low risk.

## 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
**Low risk.** The primary outcome (reoperation rate, as defined) is registered (ISRCTN57474502) and is reported in the primary results paper exactly as pre-specified in the Methods (proportion undergoing repeat operation or further mastectomy within 6 months, or pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation). The statistical analysis plan (logistic regression adjusting for minimisation factors), sample size calculation, and pre-specified subgroup analyses (lobular carcinoma, age) are all described in the Methods and reported in the Results in a manner consistent with pre-specification. There is no evidence of selective reporting for this particular outcome—it is reported fully with the pre-specified adjustment and definition, matching the a priori HTA protocol referenced (reference 13).

## 6. Overall Rating: **Some concerns**

**Justification:** The reoperation rate outcome benefits from robust randomization, minimal missing data, objective outcome ascertainment, and transparent, pre-specified reporting. However, the open-label design introduces a plausible pathway for deviations from intended intervention (cross-over in 9 patients, patient-choice mastectomies counted as events, and non-interpretable/unavailable MRI in 53 patients) that could subtly affect the causal interpretability of the intervention effect on this outcome, even though ITT analysis was correctly applied. This single domain of "some concerns" is sufficient to elevate the overall rating from "low risk," but does not reach the threshold of "high risk" given the outcome's objectivity and the trial's otherwise rigorous conduct.

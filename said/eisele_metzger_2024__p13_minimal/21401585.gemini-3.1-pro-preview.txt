Based on the provided manuscript and criteria, here is the risk of bias evaluation for the endpoint **quality of movement of the impaired upper limb by MUUL**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
Children were matched in pairs according to key prognostic variables and then randomized using a computer-generated list of random numbers. Allocation concealment was ensured by using concealed envelopes opened by non-study personnel. Baseline characteristics (Table I) were well-balanced between the two groups, indicating that the randomization process was successful and true random assignment was achieved without foreknowledge of allocation.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
While blinding of participants and care providers to the intervention group is impossible given the nature of the interventions (constraint-induced movement therapy vs. bimanual training), there is no evidence of significant deviations from the intended interventions. Compliance was reported as 100% for the CIMT group and 97% for the BIM group. Furthermore, data analysis was conducted on an intention-to-treat basis according to CONSORT guidelines, minimizing any bias from protocol deviations or dropouts.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
The trial had excellent retention. Out of 64 children randomized, 62 received their allocated intervention, with only one reported dropout early in the camp (BIM training group) due to pre-existing emotional and behavioural difficulties. Such a low rate of missing data is highly unlikely to have a clinically meaningful impact on the study's effect estimates.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
The MUUL (Melbourne Assessment of Unilateral Upper Limb Function) was used to measure the quality of movement. To eliminate measurement bias that could arise from the unblinded nature of the intervention camps, the primary outcomes (including MUUL) were videotaped and subsequently scored in a random order by trained occupational therapists who were masked (blinded) to the participants' group allocations. This rigorous procedure adequately protects the endpoint from assessment bias.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
The MUUL was clearly designated as a primary outcome measure in the methods section. The reported results correspond to the planned measurements and follow-up time points (3 weeks and 26 weeks). Both point estimates (estimated mean differences) and 95% confidence intervals were appropriately reported for the prespecified analyses without selective reporting of positive findings.

**6. Overall rating: low risk**
All evaluated domains are judged to be at low risk of bias. The trial was well-designed, securely randomized, adequately blinded at the outcome assessment level, and completely reported.

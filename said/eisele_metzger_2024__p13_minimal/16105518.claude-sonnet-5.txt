# Risk of Bias Assessment: Change in Operation-Specific Checklist Scores

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process:** Some concerns (The manuscript states participants were "randomly assigned to three experimental groups" but provides no detail on the randomization method - e.g., sequence generation, allocation concealment. There is no mention of whether allocation was concealed from the assessors or the person assigning groups. No baseline comparison table is provided to verify that randomization produced balanced groups across the three arms (Functional-order, Random, Blocked) at pre-test, which is particularly important given the small sample size of 28 participants split three ways. Without this information, I cannot rule out selection bias in group assignment.)

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions:** Some concerns (This is an unblinded behavioral/educational intervention, which is expected given the nature of practice schedule research - participants necessarily knew what practice condition they were in. However, there is no description of whether groups received differential attention beyond the assigned practice schedule (e.g., feedback delivery mode, time-on-task equivalence is stated as controlled - 3 groups all practiced each skill 3 times for 2:10, though total session time differs by structure: 60 min for Functional/Random via three 20-min sessions vs. five 12-min sessions for Blocked). There's no explicit mention of an intention-to-treat analysis or confirmation that all randomized participants received their assigned intervention as planned. No mention of protocol deviations, e.g., whether any participant switched groups or received a different amount of instructor feedback.)

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data:** Some concerns (The manuscript does not explicitly report the number of participants analyzed for the checklist outcome relative to the 28 randomized, nor does it report any dropouts/withdrawals. There is no CONSORT-style flow diagram or accounting of participants at pre-test, immediate post-test, and delayed post-test. Without this information, it is unclear whether the analysis included all 28 participants or a subset, and whether any missingness was related to the outcome (e.g., participants unable to complete testing due to fatigue or scheduling issues around the 30-minute delayed post-test).)

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (checklist scores):** Some concerns (The checklist is an operation-specific, objective procedural checklist rated by evaluators - "two experienced orthopedic surgeons" - rather than a participant-reported outcome, which reduces concern about participant-blinding influencing the outcome directly. However: (a) it is not stated whether the raters were blinded to group assignment when scoring, which is critical since group allocation could plausibly be inferred from performance patterns or context, introducing assessor detection bias; (b) inter-rater reliability/agreement between the two raters is not reported; (c) the checklist was developed post hoc for this study by a panel of surgeons without described validation, and there is no mention of the assessors being blinded to test phase (pre/post) ordering, video/live scoring method, or standardized scoring protocol beyond checklist item selection. Given these gaps — particularly the lack of stated rater blinding — there is real risk of measurement bias.)

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result:** Some concerns (There is no trial registration or a priori published protocol referenced, so it is not possible to confirm that the checklist outcome, the specific comparisons (Tukey post hoc pairwise contrasts), and the analysis approach (difference scores, mixed ANOVA) were pre-specified rather than chosen after seeing the data. Four outcome measures are reported (checklist, final product analysis, global rating, drilling time) with the checklist and final product analysis being the ones that reached significance and are emphasized in the conclusions, while global rating and drilling time were non-significant. This selective emphasis pattern, in the absence of a protocol confirming pre-specification of primary/secondary outcomes and analysis plan, raises concern about selective outcome reporting/analysis flexibility.)

**6. Overall rating for this outcome: High risk of bias**

*Justification:* While no single domain rises to a definitive "high risk" based on explicit stated flaws, there are "some concerns" across all five domains simultaneously: unclear randomization/allocation concealment, unblinded intervention with no ITT confirmation, unreported missing data/flow, unclear rater blinding for an evaluator-assessed outcome with unreported inter-rater reliability, and no protocol/registration to verify absence of selective outcome reporting. The cumulative effect of "some concerns" across all domains, particularly the combination of unclear assessor blinding (domain 4) and lack of a pre-registered protocol (domain 5) in a small (n=28, split into three groups) unblinded behavioral trial, substantially lowers confidence in the checklist-score result and warrants an overall "high risk" judgment per the specified rules.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: Prevalence of Depressive Symptoms (EPDS ≥ 11)

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Some concerns**
The randomization was performed by an independent researcher and stratified by EPDS score and history of depression, and allocation was described as blinded to participants and study staff. However, no details are provided on the actual method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number tables) or on the concealment mechanism (e.g., sealed opaque envelopes, central allocation). Furthermore, Table 1 shows a notable baseline imbalance in skin color between groups (40.6% white in fish oil vs. 17.9% in control, borderline p=0.055), which the authors themselves flag as an exception to otherwise balanced groups. In a sample of only 60 participants, such an imbalance raises concern about whether randomization concealment was adequately maintained. This combination of insufficient methodological detail and observed baseline imbalance justifies "some concerns."

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Some concerns**
The trial was double-blinded with identical placebo/intervention capsules, and compliance was verified both by capsule counts and serum fatty acid biomarkers, which is a methodological strength. However, compliance averaged only ~74% in both arms, and a substantial number of randomized participants (6 in fish oil, 4 in control) never received the intervention for reasons stated as unrelated to the trial, with additional dropouts after initiation (7 vs. 5). While ITT analysis was appropriately used to preserve the randomized comparison, the magnitude of non-adherence and early dropout, combined with lack of clarity on how missing intervention data were handled analytically for the ITT prevalence outcome (i.e., whether non-attenders were treated as missing or given values), raises some concern that deviations could have influenced the effect estimate.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: High risk**
Attrition was substantial: only 32 of 60 randomized participants (53%) completed the trial through the postpartum assessment (T3), the time point of the primary outcome. Reasons for missingness (transfer of care, miscarriage/stillbirth, dropout, "lost capsules," missed visits) were reported, but differed somewhat in nature between groups, and no formal sensitivity analyses (e.g., multiple imputation, worst-case/best-case scenario, or evaluation of whether missingness was related to the outcome) were reported for the primary binary outcome. Given that nearly half the randomized sample did not contribute data at the primary endpoint, and no rigorous methodology was used to address this, missing data plausibly could bias the result in either direction.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (EPDS ≥ 11): Low risk**
EPDS is a validated, self-reported (participant-reported) subjective outcome measure. Critically, the double-blind design was maintained (participants, research assistants, and technicians were all blinded to allocation), and there is no indication of unblinding during the trial. Since the outcome assessment method was identical across groups and blinding was preserved for both participants (who completed the EPDS) and outcome assessors, the risk of bias in measurement of this specific outcome is low.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: High risk**
The trial was retrospectively registered (24 May 2012), well after enrollment began (November 2009) and after supplementation had already commenced for many participants, precluding verification that outcomes, subgroups, and analyses were pre-specified rather than data-driven. Moreover, the available registry entry (NCT01660165) describes this as an *observational cohort study* with a primary outcome of "Major depressive episode" — not the EPDS ≥11 prevalence outcome reported here as primary in the manuscript. This discrepancy between the registered primary outcome and the outcome reported as primary in the manuscript strongly suggests potential outcome switching. Additionally, multiple subgroup analyses (women with prior history of depression) and multiple statistical models (logistic regression, LME, per-protocol vs. ITT) were performed, increasing the risk that the significant secondary findings (highlighted in the abstract and discussion) were selectively emphasized in the absence of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan.

## 6. Overall Rating: **High risk**

**Justification:** While the outcome measurement itself (EPDS, blinded participants/assessors) was of low risk, the combination of unclear randomization/concealment procedures with an observed baseline imbalance, substantial and inadequately addressed missing outcome data (~47% attrition at the primary endpoint), and a serious discrepancy between the registered outcome and the outcome reported as primary in the manuscript (with retrospective registration precluding verification of pre-specification) collectively raise substantial concern about the trustworthiness of the reported result for this outcome. According to the rating rules, a "high risk" judgment is warranted given the high-risk domains (missing data, selective reporting) combined with additional concerns in the randomization domain.

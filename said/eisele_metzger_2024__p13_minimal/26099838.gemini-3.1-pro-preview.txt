Based on the provided manuscript, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **performance (sum scores of correct answers)** using the requested criteria:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
Justification: The manuscript states that "Students were randomized into three groups using a computer program," which indicates a truly random allocation sequence. However, there is no description of whether the allocation sequence was adequately concealed before assignment. Because the nature of the teaching modules (CT, 3D images, 3D printed models) makes blinding of participants and instructors impossible, the lack of explicit allocation concealment raises some concerns that the randomization process could have been compromised, even though baseline characteristics (age, sex, school age) appear balanced. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
Justification: Due to the educational nature of the interventions, neither the students nor the teachers could be blinded. However, the interventions were administered in a single class session, and the manuscript clearly states that "a total of 120 medical students... participated, and no one dropped out." There is no indication that any deviations from the intended interventions occurred that would impact the outcome.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
Justification: The study reports a 100% completion rate. The authors state that all 120 randomized students participated, no one dropped out, and data for all 120 students are accounted for in the final analysis and tables. Therefore, there is no missing outcome data.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
Justification: The performance outcome was measured using the sum score of 10 key anatomical questions answered via an examination paper. Although the students were aware of the intervention they received, the performance questions (identifying anatomical structures, fracture types, etc.) have objective, definitive correct or incorrect answers (as detailed in Table 1). Therefore, the measurement of this outcome is highly objective and unlikely to be influenced by the lack of blinding.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
Justification: The manuscript does not reference a published study protocol or a clinical trial registration. Without a pre-specified protocol, it is impossible to definitively rule out selective reporting, such as whether the specific questions or the grouping of the evaluation metrics were decided after the data were analyzed. While the reported outcomes align with the stated objectives, the lack of pre-registration warrants a judgment of some concerns.

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
Justification: The overall risk of bias is judged as having "some concerns." While the trial had no dropouts and utilized an objective measure for the performance outcome, the lack of clear allocation concealment details and the absence of a pre-registered protocol or statistical analysis plan raise some concerns regarding the randomization process and the selection of the reported results.

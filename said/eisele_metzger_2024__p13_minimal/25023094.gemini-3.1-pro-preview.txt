Based on the provided manuscript and criteria, here is the risk of bias assessment for the visual analogue scale (VAS) for pain.

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
(justification: The study states that "The distribution of subjects to the two treatment groups was performed using sex-stratified randomization, and the allocation sequence was blinded through the use of sealed envelopes." The groups appeared balanced at baseline with no significant differences in demographic or pre-test variables. Therefore, the randomization and allocation concealment processes were adequate.)

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
(justification: The trial describes blinding of the patients and the assessor ("with blinding of patients and blind assessor"). The control group received a sham/placebo ultrasound intervention ("ultrasound was off (sham US)"). While the physiotherapist applying the treatment was not blinded, the blinding of the patients and the use of a placebo device minimize the risk of deviations from intended interventions. There were no drop-outs or losses to follow-up, and an intention-to-treat analysis is mentioned.)

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
(justification: The manuscript explicitly states, "During the study no patient was lost or dropped out." Therefore, there is no missing outcome data.)

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (assessed for each outcome measurement separately): low-risk**
(justification: For the outcome of pain measured by VAS, which is a subjective patient-reported outcome, the patients were blinded to the intervention through the use of a sham ultrasound device. The outcome assessor was also blinded. Therefore, knowledge of the assigned intervention is highly unlikely to have influenced the outcome measurement.)

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
(justification: The manuscript does not mention a pre-published protocol or trial registry entry, which makes it difficult to verify if all planned analyses and endpoints were reported exactly as originally intended, although the reported outcomes (pain and joint mobility) match the stated objectives.)

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
(justification: The study has 'low-risk' across almost all domains, but 'some concerns' regarding the selection of the reported result due to the lack of a prospective trial registration or published protocol.)

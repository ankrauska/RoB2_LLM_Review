Here is the step-by-step bias assessment for the endpoint of **peer aggression at 12 months**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
(Justification: The study describes that patients were randomly assigned to the three study conditions (Therapist Brief Intervention [TBI], Computer-Based Brief Intervention [CBI], or control) stratified by gender and age. While the specific mechanism of random sequence generation and allocation concealment is not extensively detailed in this specific manuscript, the use of computerized assessment and immediate assignment in the Emergency Department generally implies a computer-generated randomization sequence that adequately conceals allocation prior to assignment. Baseline characteristics did not show significant differences between conditions, suggesting randomization was successful.)

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
(Justification: The authors clearly state that an intent-to-treat (ITT) approach was used. All randomly assigned participants were included in the analyses regardless of whether they received the intervention. Furthermore, the adherence to the intervention was very high, with >95% of participants receiving their assigned intervention during the ED visit. There are no indications that deviations from the intended intervention impacted the results.)

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
(Justification: The follow-up rate at 12 months was 83.6% (607/726), meaning the attrition rate was relatively low (16.4%). The researchers utilized Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) models that include all available data for participants (including those lost to attrition) under the ITT principle. This amount of missing data is unlikely to introduce significant bias, and there is no evidence that missingness was disproportionately unbalanced between the groups in a way that would alter the findings.)

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: high-risk**
(Justification: The outcome of peer aggression was assessed using self-reported measures (modified conflict tactic scale items) via a self-administered computer survey. Because participants were aware of the intervention they received (as they were not blinded to receiving therapy, a computer module, or a standard brochure) and the outcome relies entirely on subjective self-reporting of behavior, there is a high risk that knowledge of the intervention could have influenced how participants reported their aggressive behaviors (e.g., social desirability bias).)

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
(Justification: The reported outcome (violent behavior/peer aggression at 12 months) aligns with the primary outcomes and timeframes specified in the trial registration (NCT00251212) and the study's protocol. The analytical approach (GEE models, examining both specific intervention arms vs. control) was pre-specified, and there is no evidence of selective reporting or fishing for positive results among multiple undefined measures.)

**6. Overall rating: high-risk**
(Justification: Although the trial was well-conducted in terms of randomization, handling of missing data, and adherence to the intended protocol, the reliance on self-reported, subjective behavioral outcomes from non-blinded participants introduces a high risk of measurement bias. Consequently, the overall risk of bias for this specific endpoint is judged to be high.)

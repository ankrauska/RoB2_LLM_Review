1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**  
   - The authors state that 40 consecutive patients were “assigned to two groups at random,” with different detector collimations (64 × 0.6 mm versus 20 × 1.2 mm).  
   - However, they provide no information on the random-sequence generation method (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table), allocation concealment, or who assigned participants to groups. The word “random” alone is not adequate evidence that allocation was genuinely random or protected from manipulation.  
   - There is no indication that personnel assigning collimation settings were blinded to allocation. Although collimation is a technical imaging intervention and concealment may be less influential than in a treatment trial, knowledge of group allocation could still affect scanning or reconstruction choices.  
   - Baseline comparability is inadequately documented: only age is said not to differ statistically between groups. Important imaging-related characteristics, particularly calcium burden before analysis, coronary disease status, body size, and heart rate, are not reported by randomized group.  
   - Furthermore, the principal comparisons of reconstruction increment and reconstruction interval are within-patient comparisons, whereas the randomized collimation-group comparison is secondary to those repeated reconstructions. This does not remove the need for a properly described randomization process for the between-group component.

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk**  
   - The technical interventions appear to have been implemented as described: group A received 64 × 0.6 mm collimation and group B received 20 × 1.2 mm collimation; both groups underwent retrospective ECG-gated scanning, and both had reconstructions at five prespecified diastolic intervals and two prespecified reconstruction increments.  
   - Each included participant appears to have contributed all intended reconstruction datasets: 50%, 55%, 60%, 65%, and 70% of the R–R interval, reconstructed using both 3.0-mm and 1.5-mm increments. This within-person design appropriately limits confounding for the reconstruction comparisons.  
   - Heart rate was actively controlled with beta-blockers according to a stated protocol, and patients whose heart rate remained greater than 65 beats/minute were excluded before analysis. No evidence is given that beta-blocker use, acquisition parameters, or reconstruction procedures differed systematically according to reconstruction increment or interval within individuals.  
   - There is no indication of crossover, failure to implement the allocated collimation, or selective omission of particular reconstruction conditions among the analysed participants.  
   - Although operators necessarily knew the scan settings, the endpoint is based predominantly on CT-based quantification rather than participant behaviour or adherence; knowledge of allocation is therefore unlikely to have caused important deviations from the intended technical interventions.

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: high risk**  
   - Four of the 40 enrolled patients (10%) were excluded because no coronary calcium was detected on any reconstruction. Thus, the reported analyses include only 36 patients: 18 in each randomized group.  
   - This exclusion was made after imaging and was determined directly by the outcome under study—Agatston score. Such outcome-dependent exclusion is problematic. Participants with an Agatston score of zero are clinically important, especially in a study concerned with detection of small calcified plaques and the influence of reconstruction techniques.  
   - The authors do not present the results including these four patients, do not provide a sensitivity analysis, and do not explain why zero scores should be removed from analyses of mean Agatston score or detection.  
   - Although coefficients of variation may be difficult or undefined for an individual whose scores are zero at all reconstructions, this does not justify excluding those participants from analyses of the Agatston score itself, nor from analyses of whether reconstruction methods identify calcium.  
   - The exclusion may distort estimated mean scores, the apparent detectability of calcium, and the clinical implications of reconstruction increment. The direction and magnitude of the resulting bias cannot be confidently determined, but the missing/excluded data are sufficiently outcome-related to create a high risk of bias.

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — Agatston score: some concerns**  
   - **Outcome definition and acquisition:** The Agatston score was defined using a conventional threshold: more than two contiguous pixels with attenuation greater than 130 HU. Images were acquired using the same scanner platform, and all specified reconstructions were transferred to a dedicated calcium-scoring workstation. These features support reasonably standardized measurement.  
   - **Automated and manual components:** Lesions were initially identified automatically, but a radiologist and a cardiologist then differentiated calcium from noise by placing regions of interest around lesions. This manual adjudication introduces scope for subjective judgement, particularly for very small lesions near the detection threshold—the very lesions most likely to be affected by reconstruction increment or motion artefact.  
   - **Blinding:** The report does not state whether the radiologist and cardiologist were blinded to collimation group, reconstruction interval, reconstruction increment, patient identity, or other reconstructions from the same participant. Blinding is particularly relevant here because image properties can reveal the reconstruction condition and because the investigators’ hypothesis predicted advantages for overlapping reconstructions.  
   - **Potential differential measurement:** The manuscript reports that small calcified lesions were detected only with the 1.5-mm increment in two cases. This may be a genuine technical effect, but without blinded or independent reading it is not possible to exclude differential scrutiny of overlapping reconstructions or outcome assessment influenced by expectations.  
   - **Reliability information is absent:** No inter-reader agreement, intra-reader reproducibility, repeat reading, adjudication procedure, or predefined rule for resolving disagreements is reported. It is unclear whether both assessors independently scored all scans or whether one assessment was influenced by the other.  
   - **Conclusion for Agatston score measurement:** The outcome is CT-based and partly automated, so a high risk judgement is not warranted solely because assessors were apparently unblinded. Nevertheless, inadequate information on blinding, manual classification, and measurement reliability gives **some concerns**.

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
   - No prospective protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial registration is reported. Therefore, it is not possible to verify that the reported Agatston-score analyses, reconstruction intervals, summary measures, and statistical comparisons were prespecified.  
   - The study generated numerous related outcome data: Agatston score and volumetric score; five reconstruction intervals; two reconstruction increments; two collimation groups; mean scores, ranges, coefficients of variation, correlations, maximum-score intervals, lesion-detection observations, and risk-category classifications. This creates substantial opportunity for selective emphasis of statistically significant or clinically appealing findings.  
   - The methods state that mean Agatston scores at 1.5-mm and 3.0-mm increments would be compared using a sign test, and the corresponding analyses would be performed for volumetric scores. This provides some assurance that the principal increment comparisons were at least described before results were presented.  
   - However, the report does not clearly specify how observations across the five reconstruction intervals were combined to obtain the displayed group mean Agatston scores. Nor is the choice of analyses for the five interval-specific results, the correlation analysis, the maximum-score analysis, and the risk-strata analysis fully prespecified.  
   - The manuscript appropriately reports nonsignificant Agatston-score comparisons as well as significant volumetric-score comparisons, which reduces—but does not eliminate—concern about selective reporting. In the absence of a protocol or registration, **some concerns** remain.

6. **Overall rating: high risk of bias**  
   - The overall rating is **high risk of bias**, principally because 10% of enrolled participants were excluded after imaging on the basis of having no detectable coronary calcium—the outcome under investigation—with no analysis retaining these zero-score participants or sensitivity analysis.  
   - In addition, there are some concerns regarding inadequately described randomization, unreported blinding and reproducibility procedures for manual elements of Agatston-score measurement, and the absence of a protocol or registration to assess selective reporting.

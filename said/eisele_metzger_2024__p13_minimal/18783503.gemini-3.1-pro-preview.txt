**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
The study used a "randomization automated system on a one-to-one basis" which indicates appropriate random sequence generation. Allocation concealment was adequate as "the randomization list was held securely, and released only after study completion." Additionally, the authors report no significant differences between groups in baseline characteristics such as age and methadone dosage.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
The trial was double-blind, and gabapentin and placebo were dispensed in "identical appearing capsules." Patients taking placebo took the same number of capsules as those in the treatment group. Because both participants and personnel were blinded to the intervention assignment, any deviations from the intended interventions are unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of group assignment. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: high-risk**
Out of the 40 randomized participants, all 20 in the gabapentin group completed the study, whereas 3 out of 20 participants (15%) in the placebo group dropped out (one at day 14 and two at day 17). The authors explicitly state that these exclusions were due to "non-compliance with the scheduled treatment programme and self-medication with illicit and/or over-the-counter (OTC) drugs," and note that this "may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms." Because the missingness is unbalanced between groups and likely related to the unobserved true values of the outcome (missing not at random), this introduces a high risk of bias.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
The outcome evaluated is the Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale (SOWS), which is a participant-reported subjective outcome. Because the participants were adequately blinded through the use of identical-appearing capsules and matching pill counts, their knowledge of the intervention assignment could not have biased their self-assessment of withdrawal symptoms. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some-concerns**
There is no mention of a pre-registered protocol or trial registry entry, making it impossible to confidently verify whether the analysis plan and outcome reporting (such as the specific time points and sub-scores analyzed) were prespecified prior to data unblinding.

**6. Overall rating: high-risk**
The overall risk of bias is judged to be high. While the randomization, blinding, and outcome measurement processes were adequately designed and implemented, the differential and outcome-related missing data in the placebo group strongly limits confidence in the results and introduces a high risk of bias. Additionally, there are some concerns regarding the selection of the reported results due to the lack of a published protocol.

# Assessment of Risk of Bias: Nambour Skin Cancer Prevention Trial
## Outcome: Change in Photoaging (Microtopography Grade)

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Some concerns**

Justification: Randomization was performed by a computer-generated list managed by an investigator "who had no knowledge of the participants," suggesting reasonable allocation concealment for the initial randomization sequence. However, several concerns arise:
- The sunscreen intervention was explicitly unblinded (participants and those applying sunscreen knew their allocation; a placebo sunscreen was "considered unethical"). This is inherent to the trial design and cannot be avoided, but it does introduce risk since participant behavior (e.g., other sun-protection measures) could differ.
- There was a borderline imbalance in allocation to β-carotene vs. placebo (P=0.06), and inspection of Table 1 shows non-random-looking imbalances in the 2×2 factorial cross-tabulation (e.g., 238 daily sunscreen/β-carotene vs. 206 daily sunscreen/placebo; 233 discretionary/placebo vs. 206 discretionary/β-carotene). This asymmetry raises mild concern about whether true randomization was successfully achieved in the factorial allocation, though the paper does not report a formal randomization concealment failure.
- No detail is given on sequence generation method for the 2×2 factorial (block size, stratification), limiting ability to fully verify concealment robustness.

Overall, while allocation concealment for the *initial* randomization appears adequate (independent investigator, computer-generated list), the borderline imbalance and lack of detail on the factorial randomization procedure prevent a "low risk" rating.

---

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Some concerns**

Justification: 
- Sunscreen use was open-label (unblinded) by design/ethical necessity. This is a per-protocol design feature rather than a deviation, but it creates a genuine risk that behavioral co-interventions differed between groups. The authors do report similar sun-protective behaviors (shade-seeking, hat use, outdoor time) between arms, which is reassuring but based on self-report in an unblinded context.
- Adherence was imperfect: only 77% of the daily sunscreen group applied sunscreen ≥3–4 days/week by 1996, and 33% of the "discretionary" group were also applying at this frequency — substantial contamination/overlap between arms, diluting the contrast between "daily" and "discretionary" use.
- β-Carotene supplement adherence was ~68% vs 67% — similar between groups, lower concern here.
- Analysis was by intention-to-treat ("analyzed according to treatment as randomly allocated"), which is appropriate and mitigates some concern despite non-adherence, but the substantial overlap in actual sunscreen use behavior between "daily" and "discretionary" groups likely biases the effect estimate toward the null (conservative), which is an important consideration but does not eliminate the concern about deviations.

---

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: Some concerns**

Justification:
- Substantial missingness: of 903 eligible, only 604 (67%) contributed replicas at both time points; 213 had only baseline, 69 had only follow-up; 17 had no usable replica at all.
- Missingness was not random with respect to prognostic factors: participants with only one replica were more likely to have severe baseline neck photoaging and more prior sunburns — both established risk factors for the outcome itself. This raises concern that missingness may be related to the true value of the outcome (differential loss related to disease severity).
- The authors note missingness was not associated with treatment allocation, and baseline grade was not associated with missing follow-up — this is reassuring regarding differential loss by arm specifically.
- Sensitivity analyses (complete-case, multiple imputation) were conducted and yielded consistent results, which strengthens confidence that missingness did not bias the treatment effect estimate specifically, even though overall data completeness was suboptimal.

Given the substantial proportion of missing data (33%) and its association with baseline severity (even if not with treatment arm), but tempered by sensitivity analyses showing robustness, this is rated "some concerns" rather than "high risk."

---

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — Change in Photoaging: Low risk**

Justification:
- The outcome (microtopography grade from silicone skin replicas) is an objective, standardized, validated instrument (Beagley and Gibson scale), not a participant-reported subjective outcome (unlike pain or enjoyment), reducing concern about detection bias despite the unblinded nature of the sunscreen intervention itself.
- Critically, assessors grading the replicas were explicitly blinded to treatment allocation ("Experienced assessors who were unaware of treatment allocation graded replicas").
- Inter- and intra-rater reliability were high and quantitatively reported (weighted κ = 0.81 and 0.86), supporting measurement consistency.
- Participants were instructed not to use moisturizer/sunscreen on the day of replica collection, standardizing measurement conditions across arms.
- The outcome assessment method itself was identical across groups and not influenced by participant-reported perception, unlike inherently subjective outcomes.

This domain is low risk specifically because the *assessors* — not the participants — determined the outcome grade, and they were blinded.

---

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Some concerns**

Justification:
- The trial was registered retrospectively (ACTRN12610000086066), and the referenced protocol (reference 25, Green et al. 1994) predates the specific photoaging/microtopography analysis presented here by many years — it is unclear whether the specific analytic approach (GEE with proportional odds, covariate adjustment for sunburns and neck photoaging in "Model 2") was pre-specified or developed post hoc.
- Two models are presented (Model 1 unadjusted, Model 2 adjusted) — the covariates chosen for adjustment (sunburns and neck photoaging) appear to have been selected based on their association with missingness in this specific dataset, suggestive of a data-driven decision rather than a pre-specified analysis plan.
- Multiple subgroup analyses (by baseline severity grade 3-4 vs 5-6, and by 8 different baseline characteristics in Figure 2) were conducted; while described as "exploratory," the emphasis on a significant subgroup finding for β-carotene (grades 3-4) in the abstract/discussion, despite the overall null primary result, raises some concern about selective emphasis of subgroup results.
- No original full statistical analysis plan was available in the text to verify pre-specification of the primary photoaging outcome analysis method exactly as reported.
- However, on the positive side, the primary outcome (photoaging/microtopography change) is clearly stated up front and is consistent with the trial's known long-standing design (the Nambour trial), and both positive (sunscreen) and null (β-carotene) results are reported transparently, reducing suspicion of selective outcome reporting for the primary comparison itself.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **Some concerns**

**Justification:** No domain was rated "high risk" in isolation. However, multiple domains (randomization process, deviations from intended intervention, missing outcome data, and selection of reported result) were each rated "some concerns." While outcome measurement itself was low risk due to blinded objective assessment, the cumulative concerns across four of five domains — particularly substantial non-adherence/contamination between sunscreen arms, non-trivial and non-randomly distributed missing data, and ambiguity about pre-specification of the adjusted analytic model and subgroup analyses — collectively lower confidence in the robustness of the reported treatment effect (relative odds 0.76, 95% CI 0.59–0.98, a result with a confidence interval bound very close to the null). This pattern meets the criterion for "Some concerns" overall, bordering toward "high risk" given the accumulation of concerns, but not reaching the threshold of a single dominant high-risk domain.

Based on the provided manuscript and criteria, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **women’s burden and distress**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
**Justification:** The manuscript states that participants were "assigned randomly via random number tables." However, there is no description of how the allocation sequence was concealed (e.g., sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes or centralized allocation) before assignment. Without proper allocation concealment, there is a risk that group assignment could have been foreseen, potentially introducing selection bias. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: high risk**
**Justification:** Blinding of participants and therapists was not possible due to the nature of the psychological interventions, which is typical but still leaves room for bias. More concerning is that in the alcohol-focused couple therapy (AFCT) condition, only 6 of the 21 husbands engaged in treatment. The protocol stated that if the man refused to participate, the woman received the stress management treatment instead. This represents a massive deviation from the intended intervention for the majority of the AFCT arm. Furthermore, it is unclear if the analyses were strictly intention-to-treat (ITT), as they mention that missing data reduced the power of the design and analyze outcomes based on complete cases.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: high risk**
**Justification:** Sixty-one participants were randomized, but only 47 completed treatment and 44 provided data at the 6-month follow-up. The authors state that "all the women who dropped out from treatment declined further assessment." Since the dropouts were explicitly linked to the outcome (many dropped out because they left their partners, which could be related to their level of burden and distress), the missingness is likely not at random. No imputation methods were reported to handle the missing data, meaning the analyses only reect those who remained in the study, heavily biasing the results.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: high risk**
**Justification:** Women's burden and distress were measured using the Relative Stress Scale (RSS) and the General Health Questionnaire (GHQ). Both of these are subjective, participant-reported outcomes. Because the participants were aware of the intervention they were receiving (no blinding), their knowledge of treatment assignment could highly influence their self-reported stress and distress levels. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
**Justification:** There is no mention of a pre-published trial protocol or trial registry entry. The study uses multiple scales (RSS, GHQ) measured at multiple time points (pre-treatment, post-treatment, 6-month follow-up), and reports different combinations of ANOVAs. Without a pre-specied analysis plan available for comparison, we cannot rule out the possibility that the reported results were selected from multiple conducted analyses.

**6. Overall rating: high risk**
**Justification:** The study is judged to be at high risk of bias overall due to substantial deviations from the intended interventions, large amounts of informative missing outcome data without appropriate imputation, and the use of subjective self-reported outcomes in an unblinded trial.

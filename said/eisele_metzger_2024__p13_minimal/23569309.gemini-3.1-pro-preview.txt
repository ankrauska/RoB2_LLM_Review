1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk (The study states "The random permutation method was used for random assignment" and "Registration and random assignment were coordinated centrally at the KCSG datacenter." This indicates proper centralized allocation concealment and randomization.)

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk (The trial used an open-label design as it compared chemotherapy to observation, so blinding was not possible. However, analyses were performed according to the intent-to-treat principle, and deviations from intended interventions were unlikely to substantially affect the objective endpoint of PFS or were balanced / appropriately handled.)

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk (The CONSORT diagram and text indicate that all randomly assigned patients were included in the efficacy analysis according to the intent-to-treat principle. Reasons for discontinuation are documented, and drop-out rates appear balanced and low enough not to cause significant missing data bias.)

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk (Progression-free survival was assessed by investigators using RECIST version 1.0 criteria with regular 6-week radiographic evaluations. While investigators were not blinded, the use of objective radiological criteria [RECIST] limits the risk of measurement bias for PFS.)

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk (The primary endpoint in the protocol [NCT00561119] is Progression free survival, which matches the primary endpoint reported in the manuscript. The analysis methods [Kaplan-Meier, log-rank, Cox model] align with standard pre-specified methodology, and there is no evidence of selective outcome reporting.)

6. Overall rating: low-risk (All assessed domains are judged as having a low risk of bias, providing confidence in the reported results for progression-free survival.)

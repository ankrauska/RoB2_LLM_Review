Based on the provided manuscript and protocol, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the endpoint of **recurrent thrombotic events** (recurrent venous thromboembolism):

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: high risk**
*Justification:* The study states that "Random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." Generating a randomization sequence based on clinical chart numbers (which are not random and are known to the clinical staff in advance) severely compromises allocation concealment. Even with sealed envelopes, knowing the block size (10) and using chart numbers allows investigators to easily predict the group assignment of upcoming patients, potentially leading to selection bias. Therefore, the risk of bias in the randomization process is high. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk**
*Justification:* Although the trial was open-label ("Patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT"), the outcome of recurrent thrombotic events is relatively objective. The authors explicitly state that data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis. Compliance was high in the active treatment arm (only 15 of 119 patients interrupted LMWH for less than 5 days). There is no indication of substantial deviations from the intended intervention that would compromise the results.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk**
*Justification:* There is some attrition during the follow-up period (at 12 months, 91 of 119 patients in group A1 and 77 of 123 in group A2 remained). However, the primary reason for missing outcome data was patient death due to cancer progression (10.1% in group A1 and 15.4% in group A2). Because mortality is a competing risk expected in this specific advanced cancer population and was not significantly different between the groups, it is unlikely that missingness introduced significant bias in the assessment of recurrent VTE. 

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low risk**
*Justification:* Although the patients and treating physicians were unblinded to the treatment assignment, the diagnosis of the outcome (recurrent VTE) was evaluated based on objective criteria (compression ultrasonography, ventilation-perfusion scans, or CT). Furthermore, all outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central adjudication committee whose members were explicitly masked to the patient’s identity, enrolling center, RVT assay results, and treatment group assignment. This blinded central adjudication adequately protects against detection bias.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low risk**
*Justification:* The primary endpoint reported in the manuscript (recurrent venous thromboembolism) matches the primary outcome prespecified in the trial registration (NCT00450645: "Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism"). There is no evidence of selective reporting or multiple analyses of the data being cherry-picked; the results are presented comprehensively and in alignment with the planned methodology.

**6. Overall rating: high risk**
*Justification:* The overall risk of bias is judged as 'high risk' because the domain for the randomization process is assessed as high risk due to the use of clinical chart numbers for random sequence generation, which fundamentally defeats allocation concealment.

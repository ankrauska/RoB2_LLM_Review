# Risk of Bias Assessment: Surgical Time Endpoint

## 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
**Some concerns.** The manuscript states patients were allocated using a "random number table," which is an acceptable randomization method in principle. However, there is no description of allocation concealment—no mention of sealed envelopes, central allocation, or any mechanism preventing foreknowledge of group assignment. Given that the two interventions (percutaneous ablation vs. laparoscopic surgery) are entirely different procedures requiring different pre-operative preparations, if clinicians or researchers enrolling patients knew the upcoming allocation, this could have influenced enrollment decisions or subtle exclusion of certain patients into one arm. Baseline characteristics (age, sex, tumor size, nodularity) appear balanced, which is reassuring, but the absence of any concealment description remains a concern.

## 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions
**Low risk.** Surgical time is an objective, procedure-inherent measurement that is not susceptible to deviations from intended intervention in the same way subjective or behaviorally-mediated outcomes are. Both groups received their assigned procedure as described (percutaneous microwave ablation vs. laparoscopic resection), and there is no mention of crossover, non-adherence, or co-intervention that would differentially affect the recorded surgical time. This domain is essentially not applicable/low risk for this specific objective intraoperative endpoint.

## 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
**Low risk.** Table 1 reports surgical time data for all 45 patients in each group (n=45/45), with no indication of missing data, exclusions, or dropouts for this specific intraoperative measurement. Since surgical time is recorded at the time of the procedure itself, there is inherently low risk of missingness for this outcome.

## 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (Surgical Time)
**High risk.** Surgical time is an objective outcome (measured in minutes, presumably by clock/anesthesia record), which reduces subjectivity in the measurement itself. However, the critical issue is that this is fundamentally an **unblinded trial**—the surgical team, the ablationist, and any personnel recording the surgical time were necessarily aware of which procedure was being performed. There is no mention of whether the person recording/timing the procedure was independent of the treatment team or blinded to study hypothesis. While the metric itself is objective, knowledge of group assignment by those documenting start/end times (e.g., decisions about when to start the clock, how "surgical time" is operationally defined for two very different procedures) could introduce differential measurement or reporting bias. Given that outcome assessors were not blinded and no blinding was feasible or attempted, and no independent verification of timing is described, I rate this as high risk for measurement of this outcome due to lack of blinded outcome assessment in a comparison between two very different intervention types.

## 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
**Some concerns.** No trial registration, protocol, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is referenced anywhere in the manuscript. There is no way to verify whether surgical time was pre-specified as a primary or secondary outcome, whether other timing-related outcomes were measured but not reported, or whether the analysis presented (simple t-test comparison) was the pre-planned approach. The absence of any protocol/registration reference makes it impossible to rule out selective reporting, though the outcome is reported with full data (not partially presented) and appears in the a priori-stated "criteria for efficacy assessment," which offers partial reassurance.

## 6. Overall Rating: **High risk**

**Justification:** While three domains individually might only warrant "some concerns," the combination of (a) unclear randomization/allocation concealment, (b) unblinded outcome assessment for a domain where the assessor's awareness of group assignment could subtly affect measurement/reporting, and (c) absence of any protocol or registration to verify pre-specification, cumulatively and substantially lower confidence in the reported surgical time result. Additionally, the domain 4 rating of "high risk" alone is sufficient to trigger an overall "high risk" rating per the stated rules.

1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns (The authors state that randomization was completed using blocks of four and that the trial was double-blinded. However, there is no explicit mention of the method used to generate the random sequence, nor is there any description of allocation concealment prior to assignment. Without knowing whether allocation was adequately concealed, there remains a possibility that the sequence could have been predicted, raising some concerns).

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk (The trial was described as double-blinded, with both the participants and the investigators assessing the outcomes kept unaware of group assignments. Furthermore, the authors conducted a blinding validation which demonstrated that subjects were unable to guess their assigned stimulation group above chance, suggesting that the blinding was effective and deviations related to awareness of the intervention were unlikely).

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk (Out of 40 subjects recruited, 38 completed the single stimulation session. One subject in the random frequency group was removed from the analysis due to excessive EEG artifacts. The overall attrition rate is very low (3/40, 7.5%), and the reasons for missing data do not suggest systematic differences that would bias the observed outcomes).

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk (The outcome, interhemispheric functional connectivity, was measured objectively using qEEG coherence analysis. The outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention, and the data processing involved standard manual artifact rejection and filtering. The objective nature of the measurement and successful blinding ensure a low risk of bias in the assessment of this endpoint).

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns (The manuscript describes the study as exploratory and hypothesis-generating. There is no mention of a pre-published protocol or prior trial registration on a clinical trials registry. Without a pre-specified statistical analysis plan, it is impossible to verify whether the reported analyses, including the selection of specific EEG bands and electrode pairs, were planned a priori or selected based on the data, raising concerns about selective reporting).

6. Overall rating: some concerns (The study demonstrates some concerns regarding the randomization process due to the lack of details on allocation concealment, and some concerns regarding the selection of the reported result due to the absence of a pre-registered protocol for this exploratory study. The combination of these factors leads to an overall judgment of some concerns).

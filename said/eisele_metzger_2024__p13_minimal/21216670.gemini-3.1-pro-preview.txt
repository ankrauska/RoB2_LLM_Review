Based on the provided manuscript and protocol, here is the step-by-step risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
**Justification:** The trial used a computer random-number generator with a block size of four to generate the allocation sequence. Allocation was adequately concealed using sequentially numbered opaque envelopes distributed by a central pharmacist. Although there were slight baseline imbalances (e.g., slightly higher mean age, more frequent history of stroke, and higher baseline FMMS score in the fluoxetine group), the randomization process itself was robust, and the authors appropriately adjusted for these baseline imbalances in their multiple linear regression analyses.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
**Justification:** The trial was double-blinded. The placebo capsules were identical in appearance and packaging to the active drug, ensuring that participants, carers, and all study staff remained masked to the treatment assignment. Treatment compliance was carefully monitored and was nearly identical between the two groups (mean relative dose intensity ~94% in both). There is no evidence of systematic deviations from the intended interventions that would compromise the trial's validity. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
**Justification:** Out of 118 randomized patients, 113 were included in the final full analysis set (57 in the fluoxetine group and 56 in the placebo group). Only 5 patients (4.2%) were excluded (two died and three withdrew). The proportion of missing data is very low, balanced between groups (2 dropouts in the fluoxetine group, 3 in the placebo group), and the reasons for missingness (e.g., severe hypoxia, kidney tumor, septic shock) are unlikely to be related to the true outcome of the motor recovery trajectory. 

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
**Justification:** The Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale (FMMS) is a well-established, validated, and reliable tool for assessing motor recovery after stroke. The outcome was assessed by physiotherapists who were strictly blinded to the group assignment. Because both the assessors and the participants were blinded, it is highly unlikely that the measurement of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
**Justification:** The reported primary outcome (mean change in the FMMS score between inclusion [day 0] and day 90) perfectly matches the primary outcome prespecified in the registered protocol (NCT00657163). The statistical analyses were conducted as planned (using the full-analysis set), and adjustments made for baseline imbalances were transparently reported. There is no evidence of selective outcome reporting or data dredging.

**6. Overall rating: low risk**
**Justification:** All individual domains are judged to be at low risk of bias. The study utilized rigorous randomization, strict double-blinding, had minimal attrition, used objective measures assessed by blinded raters, and adhered faithfully to its preregistered protocol. Confidence in the reported results is high.

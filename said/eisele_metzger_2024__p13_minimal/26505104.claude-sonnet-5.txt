# Risk of Bias Assessment: Delayed Diagnostic Performance Outcome

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: SOME CONCERNS**
The manuscript states participants were "randomly allocated" to conditions, but no details are provided regarding the method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated randomization, block randomization) or allocation concealment. There is no description of who generated the allocation sequence or whether this person was independent from those enrolling participants. Given the reliance on convenience sampling recruited via email across two institutions and over a 3-year period (2011–2014), and no reported baseline characteristic comparison across the four groups (age, prior knowledge, GPA, etc.), I cannot rule out that allocation was improperly concealed or that baseline imbalances exist. This is compounded by unequal final group sizes (25, 21, 24, 25) arising partly from differential attrition (see below), raising suspicion regarding the integrity of the original randomization or its concealment.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: SOME CONCERNS**
The intervention (reading materials) was delivered by a standardized computer program, which is a strength. However, two participants were excluded post-hoc because they "spent less than five minutes" on the delayed diagnostic test and scored ≥3 SD below the group mean. This is a data-driven, outcome-informed exclusion rather than a pre-specified protocol deviation rule, and it was applied differentially (both exclusions came from BC and CB groups). This kind of post-hoc, outcome-linked exclusion of participants introduces risk of bias, as it could preferentially remove poor performers from specific arms, inflating between-group differences at delayed testing (the primary endpoint of interest here).

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: HIGH RISK**
Of 99 enrolled, 5 participants failed to complete the delayed session, and the dropout was concentrated entirely in the CB (n=3) and BC (n=2) arms — with zero dropout in the SC and FL groups. Because delayed diagnostic performance is the key outcome demonstrating the SC group's advantage, differential attrition selectively affecting the comparator arms (CB/BC) is a serious concern: if lower-performing or disengaged participants disproportionately dropped from CB/BC, this could artificially preserve or even improve those groups' apparent delayed performance, or conversely exaggerate the SC/FL divergence. Combined with the additional two data-driven exclusions (also from BC/CB), missingness is non-random with respect to group assignment and could materially affect the delayed diagnostic performance result.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (delayed diagnostic performance): LOW RISK**
This outcome is a diagnostic test with objectively correct/incorrect answers (multiple choice, 4 options per vignette), scored against a predetermined key rather than through subjective self-report or judgment by an unblinded assessor. Although participants could not be blinded to which learning material they received (an inherent and unavoidable feature of this type of educational intervention), the outcome itself is not vulnerable to participant expectation or effort-related reporting bias in the way a subjective symptom-report would be — a participant cannot "will" a correct diagnostic answer based on group allegiance. Scoring was presumably automated/objective (correct vs. incorrect diagnosis chosen), minimizing risk of differential outcome assessment. This domain is low risk.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: SOME CONCERNS**
No trial registration, public protocol, or a priori statistical analysis plan is referenced or available. The Methods section does describe the intended primary analysis (mixed ANCOVA on diagnostic test performance with group × time interaction), and the Results appear to correspond to this pre-specified structure, which is reassuring. However, without an independently timestamped protocol/registration, I cannot verify that this was the sole primary analysis planned in advance, that covariates were pre-specified rather than chosen post hoc (the text states covariates were "chosen based on correlations" observed in the data — a data-driven, not pre-specified, covariate selection strategy), or that no alternative outcome definitions or subgroup analyses were tried and discarded. The post hoc pairwise comparisons (LSD method) used to characterize the interaction were also not clearly pre-specified.

## 6. Overall Rating: **HIGH RISK**

**Justification:** While the outcome measurement itself (domain 4) is low risk, and randomization/intervention deviations show only some concerns, the domain of missing outcome data reaches high risk on its own due to differential, non-random attrition and post hoc, outcome-linked exclusions concentrated specifically in the comparator arms (CB, BC) relative to the arm (SC) driving the key finding. Combined with the unverifiable randomization procedure and lack of a pre-registered analysis plan (data-driven covariate selection), these concerns compound to substantially lower confidence in the reported delayed diagnostic performance result, particularly the magnitude and even the direction of the key group-by-time interaction that anchors this paper's central conclusion.

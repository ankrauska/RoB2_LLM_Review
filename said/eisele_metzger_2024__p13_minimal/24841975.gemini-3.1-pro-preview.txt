Based on the provided manuscript, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **overall survival**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
Justification: Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System, ensuring adequate sequence generation and concealment of allocation. The baseline characteristics of the treatment groups were generally balanced, indicating that the randomization was successful.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
Justification: The trial was open-label, but the primary endpoint is overall survival, which is a hard objective outcome not prone to bias from lack of blinding of patients or personnel. Six patients in the control arm received treatment not according to the prespecified investigator's choice, but this represents a very small proportion of the total sample (6/190 = 3%), and intention-to-treat analysis was performed. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
Justification: Patient disposition (Figure 2) shows that very few patients were lost to follow-up (1 patient in the elacytarabine arm, 4 in the control arm). Survival status was well-documented for the vast majority of the randomized population, leading to low risk of bias due to missing data.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
Justification: The endpoint is overall survival. Measurement of mortality is highly objective and generally unaffected by the knowledge of the assigned intervention, minimizing the risk of bias despite the open-label nature of the trial.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
Justification: The reported overall survival corresponds to the predefined primary endpoint and is analyzed according to the intention-to-treat principle. The trial was registered (NCT01147939) and the analysis matches the stated plans without evidence of selective reporting.

**6. Overall rating: low risk**
Justification: All assessed domains (randomization, deviations from intervention, missing data, outcome measurement, and selection of reported results) have a low risk of bias.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: Muscle Damage Endpoint (CK, AST)
## Sir Ian Chalmers, Clinical Trials Reviewer

I will focus my assessment on the randomized comparison relevant to this endpoint — the allopurinol vs. placebo intervention during Racing Period B — as this is the only portion of the study with random allocation. The Period A "participant vs. non-participant" comparison is non-randomized (observational) and cannot be assessed under RoB2 domains 1–3.

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: HIGH RISK**

The manuscript states only that "crew members were divided into two groups according to random numbers" — no description of the randomization sequence generation method, no mention of allocation concealment, and no detail on who performed the randomization. More concerningly, the resulting groups are severely imbalanced on a variable known to be a major determinant of the outcome: the allopurinol group contained 8 HIW/4 MIW/1 LIW while the placebo group contained only 2 HIW/4 MIW/4 LIW. Since HIW status was already shown to be the strongest predictor of CK/AST elevation (Fig. 2), this imbalance raises serious doubt about whether true randomization was achieved or maintained. Additionally, baseline CK values were already different between groups pre-intervention (445.8 vs 275.8 U/L), a large and clinically important imbalance suggesting failure of randomization to balance prognostic factors.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: SOME CONCERNS**

Participants ("sailors") were blinded to allocation, but there is no statement regarding blinding of those administering allopurinol/placebo or of trial personnel monitoring compliance. No information is given on adherence verification (e.g., pill counts, timing compliance with the "3 h before racing" dosing). Four sailors declined to participate after group assignment was presumably made, and no intention-to-treat analysis is described — an as-treated/per-protocol analysis is implied, which can introduce bias if reasons for decline relate to prognosis (e.g., anticipated poor performance or symptoms).

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: SOME CONCERNS**

Four sailors "declined to take part" in Period B; it is unclear whether this occurred before or after randomization, and no reasons for dropout are provided, nor is it stated whether dropout rates/reasons differed between allopurinol and placebo arms. No sensitivity analysis or handling of missing data is described. This is not thoroughly reported enough to rule out bias.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (Muscle damage — CK/AST): LOW RISK**

CK and AST are objective, automated spectrophotometric laboratory assays (reported intra-assay CV 4.5% and 4.2%), not subject to participant or assessor subjective interpretation. Even though it is not explicitly stated that laboratory personnel were blinded to allocation, the objective, automated nature of these biochemical assays makes outcome assessor bias unlikely to materially affect results. This differs from patient-reported outcomes (e.g., soreness), which are not the focus of this endpoint.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: SOME CONCERNS**

There is no trial registration number, no reference to a published or pre-registered protocol, and no clinicaltrials.gov identifier provided anywhere in the manuscript. Multiple outcomes (CK, AST, MDA), multiple subgroup analyses (HIW vs MIW/LIW), and multiple correlation analyses were performed and selectively highlighted where significant (e.g., CK–AST correlations reported in detail; MDA–CK correlation reported as non-significant and de-emphasized). Without a prospectively registered analysis plan, I cannot rule out selective reporting or post hoc emphasis on the statistically significant CK finding while downplaying the non-significant AST and MDA findings.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **HIGH RISK**

**Justification:** The randomization process domain is rated high risk due to substantial and outcome-relevant baseline imbalance (physical work intensity distribution, baseline CK values) combined with an inadequately described randomization procedure. This alone is sufficient for an overall "high risk" rating per the stated rules. This is compounded by "some concerns" in three further domains (deviations from intended intervention, missing data, and selective reporting), which further erodes confidence that the reported reduction in CK activity with allopurinol reflects an unbiased treatment effect rather than baseline confounding by physical workload group.

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**  
**Justification:** The study is described as a "randomised, open study with two parallel groups." However, the manuscript does not provide any details on how the randomization sequence was generated, nor does it describe whether allocation was concealed. In an open-label trial without described allocation concealment, there is a risk that the investigators could foresee assignments, leading to some concerns regarding the randomization process.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk**  
**Justification:** This was an open-label study, meaning participants and study personnel were aware of the assigned vaccine. However, deviations from the intended intervention are unlikely to occur or meaningfully affect an objective laboratory outcome such as the administration of a single booster vaccine dose. The study performed both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses, noting that the results were similar enough that only the per-protocol results were reported. The lack of blinding is unlikely to have impacted the physiological immune response (anti-HAV GMT).

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk**  
**Justification:** Of the 127 randomized subjects (64 receiving Vaqta™, 63 receiving Avaxim™), only 6 subjects were excluded from the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis (4 due to missing serological results, 2 for protocol violations). This leaves 59 in the Vaqta™ group and 62 in the Avaxim™ group for the final analysis. The proportion of missing outcome data is very small (<5%) and balanced between groups, making it highly unlikely to introduce clinically significant bias.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low risk**  
**Justification:** The endpoint is the anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) at 1 month post-vaccination. This is an objective, standardized laboratory measurement performed by Merck Research Laboratories using the modified HAVAB assay. While the study was open-label, the objective nature of an automated serological assay renders it highly resistant to assessor bias. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low risk**  
**Justification:** The reported results directly match the pre-specified primary evaluation criterion ("comparison of the anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) for both vaccines 1 month post-vaccination") outlined in the methods section. There is no evidence of selective outcome reporting or selective reporting of multiple analysis methods.

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**  
**Justification:** The overall risk of bias is judged as "some concerns" primarily due to the lack of methodological details regarding sequence generation and allocation concealment in the context of an open-label trial (Domain 1). All other domains are considered at low risk of bias.

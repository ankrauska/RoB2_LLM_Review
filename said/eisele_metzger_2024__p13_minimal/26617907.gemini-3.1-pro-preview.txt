Based on the provided manuscript, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the endpoint **Surgical time**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
**Justification:** The study states that patients were divided into two groups "by using a random number table," which constitutes an adequate method for generating a randomized allocation sequence. However, there is no mention of allocation concealment (e.g., sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes). The lack of information on how the randomization sequence was concealed until the intervention was assigned raises some concerns about potential selection bias.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk**
**Justification:** In this surgical trial, blinding of participants and healthcare providers (surgeons) is virtually impossible. However, surgical time is an inherent procedural metric rather than a downstream health outcome sensitive to placebo effects or differential non-protocol interventions. There is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that would meaningfully impact this outcome, and the analysis appears to include all randomized patients. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk**
**Justification:** The outcome data for surgical time were available for all participants randomized. According to Table 1, the sample size analyzed for this outcome is 45 in group A and 45 in group B, which perfectly matches the number of randomized patients. There is no missing data for this outcome.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low risk**
**Justification:** Surgical time is an objective, standardized metric recorded during the procedure. While the surgical staff recording the time were inherently unblinded to the intervention, it is highly unlikely that knowledge of the assigned group would systematically bias the recording of the start and end times of the surgery. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
**Justification:** There is no mention of a pre-published trial protocol or trial registry entry, which is common for studies of this type and era. Without a pre-specified statistical analysis plan, it is not possible to completely rule out selective reporting of results or selective analysis methods, although surgical time is a standard, expected outcome in surgical comparison trials. 

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
**Justification:** The overall risk of bias is judged as "some concerns" due to the lack of information regarding allocation concealment during the randomization process and the absence of a pre-registered protocol to rule out selective reporting.

Based on the provided manuscript and the specified criteria, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the endpoint **delayed diagnostic performance**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
*Justification:* The manuscript states that participants were "randomly allocated to one of four study conditions," but it does not provide any details on how the random sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed. Because the specific methods of randomization and concealment are not described, it is difficult to completely rule out baseline imbalances or selection bias, warranting some concerns.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
*Justification:* The intervention consisted of different educational texts delivered individually via a custom-designed computer program. Given the automated nature of the intervention delivery and the short timeframe (learning phase capped at 30 minutes), it is highly unlikely that there were any deviations from the intended interventions that could have systematically affected the outcomes. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
*Justification:* A total of 5 participants failed to complete the delayed testing session, and 2 participants were excluded from the analysis for apparently rushing through the test (spending less than 5 minutes, scoring very poorly). Out of the ~101 original participants, the exclusion and attrition of 7 individuals is a very small proportion (~7%). This low rate of missing data is unlikely to introduce significant bias into the outcome of delayed diagnostic performance.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
*Justification:* The outcome was measured using a diagnostic test consisting of 12 clinical vignettes where students identified the correct diagnosis from a multiple-choice list. This is an objective measure of knowledge application. The cases were created and validated by expert clinicians and had been used in previous studies. The assessments were administered by a computer program, eliminating the risk of unblinded assessor bias.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
*Justification:* There is no mention of a published protocol or a prior trial registration that would allow for a comparison between the originally intended analysis plan and the reported results. While the mixed repeated-measures ANCOVA is a standard analytical approach for this type of experimental design, the absence of a pre-registered plan means we cannot definitively rule out selective reporting or selective analysis of the data. 

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
*Justification:* The overall rating is 'some concerns' because, although the outcome measurement, intervention delivery, and missing data domains are at low risk of bias, there are some concerns regarding the lack of detail in the randomization process and the absence of a pre-registered protocol.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: Escitalopram OCD Relapse Prevention Trial

## Endpoint under review: Time to relapse from start date of double-blind treatment

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Low risk**

Justification: Randomization was via a computer-generated list, with sequence details concealed in sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes—this constitutes adequate allocation concealment. Randomization was stratified by open-label dose and used a 1:1 ratio in blocks of four per centre. Although disclosure of "blocks of four" theoretically allows some end-of-block predictability, the use of sealed opaque envelopes centrally generated mitigates this risk. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) were well balanced between arms (age, sex, race, Y-BOCS scores, illness duration), supporting successful randomization. No evidence of manipulation of allocation is apparent.

---

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Low risk**

Justification: The double-blind medication was matched for appearance/taste/smell, and a structured 1-week taper was built in for patients switching from 20 mg escitalopram to placebo, explicitly to minimize discontinuation-related confounding. Analyses were performed on the ITT population, and a per-protocol sensitivity analysis (excluding "major protocol deviations") corroborated the primary result, and sensitivity analyses censoring for 7/14-day discontinuation windows produced consistent findings. Non-relapse withdrawal rates were similar between arms (7.9% escitalopram vs 8.9% placebo), suggesting no differential deviation from assigned treatment. No unplanned co-interventions are reported.

---

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: Low risk**

Justification: Of 320 randomized patients, the ITT analysis set essentially retained the full randomized cohort (with only 2 patients excluded for not meeting entry criteria). Withdrawal rates for reasons other than relapse were low and balanced between groups (13/163 escitalopram vs 14/157 placebo). Because the primary outcome is time-to-event (relapse), appropriate censoring methodology (Kaplan-Meier/Cox) accounts for those who did not relapse but discontinued, and the pattern of missingness does not appear to differentially favor one arm. No large or asymmetric loss to follow-up threatens the estimate.

---

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — Time to relapse: Some concerns**

Justification: Relapse was defined predominantly by an increase of ≥5 points on the Y-BOCS (accounting for 97% of relapses), a semi-structured, clinician-administered but substantially patient-report-dependent instrument, and additionally by "lack of efficacy as judged by investigator"—an explicitly subjective criterion. Although raters were nominally blinded and rater-training was employed to standardize scoring, blinding integrity is questionable: the double-blind period showed a statistically significant excess of dizziness and nausea in the placebo group during the taper/early weeks (Table 3), a recognized SSRI-discontinuation signature. This differential side-effect profile creates realistic potential for both patients and raters to infer treatment allocation, which could influence symptom reporting/scoring on this partly subjective scale, and could also bias the "investigator judged lack of efficacy" component of the relapse definition. While the taper strategy and sensitivity analyses (censoring early relapses) were sensible attempts to address this, they cannot fully exclude that unblinding influenced ascertainment of relapse, particularly through the subjective judgment component and self-reported symptom severity feeding into Y-BOCS scoring.

---

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Low risk**

Justification: The manuscript explicitly states the "prospectively defined primary analysis" was time to relapse, matching the statistical analysis plan (log-rank test, Cox model, sample size calculation based on this outcome) described in the Methods. Secondary outcomes (response, remission, Y-BOCS/NIMH-OCS/CGI changes) were also pre-specified and reported comprehensively, including sensitivity analyses (per-protocol, censored discontinuation windows, subgroup analyses) rather than a single cherry-picked result. There is no evident discrepancy between planned and reported analyses, though the absence of an independently accessible trial registration/protocol document for cross-checking (this appears to be a pre-registration-era trial) limits complete verification.

---

## 6. Overall rating: **Some concerns**

Justification: While randomization, deviation-management, and missing-data handling are low risk, and outcome reporting appears complete and pre-specified, the measurement of the primary outcome (time to relapse) carries "some concerns" due to (a) the semi-subjective nature of the relapse definition (including explicit investigator judgment of "lack of efficacy"), and (b) a detectable differential adverse-event signature (dizziness/nausea) between arms that plausibly compromised blinding integrity, particularly in the early double-blind/taper period. Per the stated rules, one domain with "some concerns" is sufficient to preclude an overall "low risk" rating, and since only a single domain is affected without compounding across multiple domains, "high risk" is not warranted. The overall trial is judged **some concerns**.

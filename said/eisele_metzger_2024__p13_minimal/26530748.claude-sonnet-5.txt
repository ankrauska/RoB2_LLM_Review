# Risk of Bias Assessment: EORTC QLQ-C30 Global Health Endpoint
## PORTEC-2 Long-Term HRQL Analysis (7- and 10-year follow-up)

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Some concerns**

Justification: This paper is a secondary long-term QoL analysis of the PORTEC-2 trial; the randomization procedure itself (sequence generation, allocation concealment) is not described in this manuscript and refers only to prior publications (ref. 6, 13). No information is given here regarding concealment of allocation prior to assignment, nor on whether baseline characteristics were balanced at the time of the current analysis (baseline characteristics are only reported for the QoL-responder subset, not for the full randomized cohort in this paper). Given the open-label nature of EBRT vs VBT (impossible to blinded due to differing treatment modalities), there is no participant/assessor blinding to allocation — this is inherent to the trial design and cannot be considered a deviation, but it does mean there was no concealment of group assignment throughout follow-up, which is relevant to interpreting all subsequently collected patient-reported data.

---

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: Low risk**

Justification: No deviations from allocated treatment, crossovers, or non-adherence to EBRT/VBT are reported. The analysis is presented as an intention-to-treat-consistent longitudinal QoL follow-up of the originally randomized groups. There is no indication that treatment deviations occurred or that per-protocol analyses were substituted without justification.

---

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: Some concerns**

Justification: Substantial attrition is present at multiple levels. Of the original 427 randomized patients, only 348 (81%) contributed baseline plus ≥1 follow-up questionnaire. For the present long-term analysis, questionnaires were sent to only 265 "eligible" (alive, disease-free) patients, with a 77% response rate at 7 years (205/265) and only 67% (80/119) at 10 years. Additionally, within returned questionnaires, only 76.2% had complete QLQ-C30 items. Responders at 7 years were "slightly younger and had fewer comorbidities" than the overall PORTEC-2 cohort — a systematic difference that could bias global health/QoL estimates toward more favorable results, particularly if sicker/older patients (potentially with worse global health) were disproportionately lost to follow-up or death. Although the authors cite prior analyses showing no differences between responders and non-responders at 5 years, the demonstrated differences at 7–10 years (age, comorbidity) are a source for concern regarding missing data being related to the true outcome value.

---

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome — EORTC QLQ-C30 Global Health: Some concerns**

Justification: The QLQ-C30 Global Health scale is a subjective, participant-reported outcome. As this is an open-label trial (EBRT vs. VBT — physically and logistically distinct treatments), patients could not be blinded to their treatment allocation. Knowledge of treatment assignment could plausibly influence self-reported global health perception (e.g., patients receiving the more intensive/invasive EBRT might be prone to differential reporting bias in either direction — either overreporting due to negative expectation or underreporting due to perceived justification of a "more thorough" treatment). While the outcome itself (global health) showed no significant difference between arms, the lack of blinding remains a structural source of measurement bias per the specified criteria, independent of the null result. This is compounded by the reliance on patient recall/self-report over long intervals (7–10 years) without an objective corroborating measure.

---

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Some concerns**

Justification: The methods reference established EORTC QoL Group guidelines and pre-specified statistical models (linear mixed models, ordinal logistic regression) with a multiplicity-corrected threshold (P<.01), which supports some pre-specification of analytic approach. However, there is no explicit trial registration or published statistical analysis plan cited specifically for this 7-/10-year exploratory QoL sub-study (as distinct from the main PORTEC-2 trial registration for oncologic outcomes), making it impossible to verify whether Global Health was pre-specified as a primary/secondary long-term outcome, or whether it was one of several HRQL domains analyzed with potential for selective emphasis in reporting (particularly as bowel/urinary symptom differences are highlighted with more granularity and lower p-value thresholds than the global health/functioning scales, which are reported more briefly and mainly in the negative). The absence of a visible pre-registered analysis plan specific to this long-term follow-up analysis leaves some uncertainty as to whether outcome/analysis selection was fully pre-specified.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **Some concerns**

Justification: No single domain reaches "high risk," but multiple domains (randomization process transparency, missing outcome data, outcome measurement due to lack of blinding on a subjective PRO, and selection of the reported result) show "some concerns." While each individually might not be severe enough to undermine confidence dramatically, the accumulation of these concerns — particularly the substantial and non-random-appearing attrition (younger, healthier responders) combined with unblinded self-report of a subjective global health outcome — cumulatively lowers confidence in the reported finding of "no difference in global health" between EBRT and VBT arms. This does not reach "high risk" as no single domain shows a clear, severe flaw, but the combination is sufficient to warrant an overall "some concerns" rating rather than "low risk."

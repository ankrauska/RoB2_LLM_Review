Based on the provided manuscript, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint "effects of supervision on patient care, measured by a team checklist score":

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
*Justification:* The manuscript states that "Residents were randomized to one of three levels of supervision" but provides no details regarding the methods used for random sequence generation or allocation concealment. While the baseline characteristics (Table 1) appear reasonably well-balanced, the lack of information on how the randomization was performed and concealed introduces some concerns about potential selection bias.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns**
*Justification:* Due to the nature of the intervention (physical proximity of the supervisor), neither the residents nor the supervising fellows could be blinded to the group assignment. The manuscript also notes deviations from the intended interventions (e.g., "During immediately supervised scenarios, two residents did not call their supervisor, four residents obtained advice from their supervisor by phone only"). Because participants were aware of their assignments and deviations occurred, there are some concerns regarding bias in deviations from the intended intervention.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk**
*Justification:* A total of 53 residents were recruited. Based on Table 2, all 53 participants (18 in Direct, 16 in Immediately Available, and 19 in Distant) were included in the analysis for the supervised scenarios. There does not appear to be any missing outcome data.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
*Justification:* The outcome (team checklist score) was assessed by two independent raters who reviewed videotapes of the simulation scenarios. While the use of independent raters and a third assessor for discrepancies is a strong methodological approach, the raters were not blinded to the intervention because the physical presence or absence of the supervisor would be clearly visible on the video recordings. Although checklist items are relatively objective, the lack of blinding of the outcome assessors leaves room for some measurement bias.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
*Justification:* The manuscript does not mention a pre-registered trial protocol or an analysis plan available a priori. While the reported outcomes match those described in the methods section and there is no obvious evidence of selective reporting or p-hacking, the absence of a registered protocol means that it is not possible to definitively rule out selective reporting.

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
*Justification:* The study is rated as having "some concerns" overall. This is driven by the lack of methodological details regarding randomization and allocation concealment, the inability to blind participants, personnel, and outcome assessors due to the nature of the intervention, and the lack of a pre-registered protocol.

1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns (The allocation was performed using sealed, nontransparent envelopes drawn by the patients or caregivers. While the envelopes were sealed and nontransparent, drawing from a single pool of envelopes does not guarantee strict allocation concealment. Furthermore, the randomization ratio was altered for the final 14 patients from 1:1 to 2:1 in favor of the intervention, which introduces some concerns regarding the robustness and predictability of the randomization sequence.)

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk (The nature of the intervention meant that participants and personnel could not be blinded. However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended intervention occurred that would systematically bias the results. The intervention was delivered as intended, and there were no drop-outs during the study period.)

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk (The authors state that there were no drop-outs from the study during the 2-year follow-up. Furthermore, the outcome data were obtained from the register of the Social Insurance Institution, ensuring complete data capture for institutionalization and mortality. Therefore, the risk of bias due to missing outcome data is low.)

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk (The outcome, time to institutionalization, was assessed by a group of experts in the Finnish local social and healthcare system. The authors explicitly state that "These groups of experts were blind to the allocation and generally unaware that a patient was even participating in the study." Given that the outcome assessors were blinded and the outcome is an objective event [institutionalization], the risk of bias in the measurement of the outcome is low.)

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk (The reported outcome, time to institutionalization, was pre-specified as the main outcome measure. The statistical methods [Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and time-dependent Cox regression] are appropriate for this type of data, and the results are reported fully and consistently with the aims of the study. There is no indication of selective reporting.)

6. Overall rating: some concerns (The study has some concerns regarding the randomization process, specifically due to the method of drawing envelopes from a pool and the mid-trial change in the randomization ratio. All other domains are judged to be at low risk of bias. Consequently, the overall risk of bias is rated as 'some concerns'.)

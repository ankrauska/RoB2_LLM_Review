Based on the provided manuscript and the specified criteria, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **rate of emergency hemostasis**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
Justification: The study states that patients were randomly divided into a control group and a therapy group following a random number table method produced through Microsoft Excel. However, there is no mention of allocation concealment (e.g., using sealed, opaque envelopes or a centralized randomization system). Without concealed allocation, there is a risk that investigators could have foreseen group assignments and potentially introduced selection bias, even though the baseline characteristics appear balanced.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
Justification: The nature of the interventions (simultaneous vs. delayed endoscopic variceal ligation) makes blinding of the patients and the care providers impossible. However, the study does not suggest that there were substantial deviations from the intended interventions that would impact the initial assessment of emergency hemostasis. The use of immediate EVL in the control group as a rescue therapy for patients whose hemorrhage could not be controlled was a direct consequence of the initial treatment failure and was appropriately counted as a failure to achieve emergency hemostasis under the initial assigned intervention. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
Justification: Outcome data for the rate of emergency hemostasis was reported for all 172 randomized patients (90 in the therapy group and 82 in the control group). There is no indication of loss to follow-up or missing data for this specific immediate outcome.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
Justification: The rate of emergency hemostasis is an objective clinical outcome determined by the immediate cessation of bleeding observed during and right after the endoscopic procedure. Although the outcome assessors (the endoscopists) were not blinded to the intervention, the objective and highly visible nature of active variceal bleeding makes it unlikely that the lack of blinding introduced significant measurement bias.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
Justification: There is no pre-registered protocol available to consult. However, the reported outcomes (rate of emergency hemostasis, rate of early rebleeding, and complications) are standard, expected, and highly clinically relevant endpoints for a trial investigating acute variceal bleeding. There is no evidence that the reporting of these outcomes was selectively manipulated based on the direction or statistical significance of the results.

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
Justification: The trial is judged to have "some concerns" for risk of bias overall. This is driven entirely by the first domain, as there is no description of allocation concealment during the randomization process, which raises some concerns about potential selection bias. All other domains are rated as low risk.

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
**Justification:** The participants were randomly allocated to the study groups using an online randomization tool with permuted block randomization (randomly varying block sizes of 3, 6, and 9). Allocation concealment was properly ensured as the randomization list was managed by a non-involved person who used serially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes. Baseline characteristics were well balanced among the groups, and there is no evidence of an issue with the randomization process. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
**Justification:** Although participants were aware of the light condition they received, they were unaware of the other two light conditions, minimizing the risk of behavioral changes. The test supervisor overseeing the session was blinded to the group assignment. While there was substantial variation between the intended and actual light intensity reaching the participants' eyes, this variation reflects the practical application of the intervention rather than a systematic deviation from the intended assignment protocol, and there were no substantial non-adherences that would bias the intention-to-treat effect.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
**Justification:** A total of 74 athletes were initially randomized, and 2 were retrospectively excluded due to invalid baseline measurements (peak oxygen uptake in the screening test), leaving 72 participants for the final analysis. Missing data is very low (2 out of 74) and is balanced and unrelated to the actual experimental intervention or the outcome of acoustic reaction time. Therefore, the missing data does not introduce a risk of bias.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
**Justification:** The acoustic reaction time was measured objectively using a standardized computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task. The test supervisor was blinded to the group assignment. While the participants were not fully blinded to their own light condition (as they could see the light), they were blinded to the alternative conditions. Moreover, acoustic reaction time is an objective, automated metric (measured by pressing a space bar) that is highly unlikely to be influenced by subjective interpretation or assessor bias. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
**Justification:** Acoustic reaction time was pre-specified as a secondary outcome in the registered protocol (NCT02203539). The manuscript clearly states that the statistical methods used to compare the groups for reaction time were pre-specified. The reported results match the prespecified plan and all planned outcomes are fully reported.

**6. Overall rating: low risk**
**Justification:** All individual bias domains were assessed as having a low risk of bias. Therefore, the overall confidence in the results for the acoustic reaction time outcome is high, warranting an overall 'low risk' rating.

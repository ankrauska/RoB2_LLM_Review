1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low risk (The random sequence was computer-generated using block sizes of 6 and 4. Allocation concealment was successfully achieved using sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes. Baseline characteristics between the groups were similar, indicating that the randomization process was effective.)

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk (Although the patients and clinicians could not be blinded to the intervention due to the nature of the orthodontic appliances, there were no reported deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the experimental context. All participants received their assigned intervention and were analyzed in the groups to which they were originally randomized.)

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk (There was no loss to follow-up. All 32 randomized patients completed the trial and the assessment questionnaires on the day of appliance removal, meaning there is no missing outcome data.)

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: high risk (The outcome "patient acceptance of the appliance" was measured using a self-reported questionnaire. Since blinding of the patients to the interventions was impossible, the participants were fully aware of whether they received the standard appliance or the one with mini-implants. This lack of blinding is highly problematic for a subjective, participant-reported outcome such as acceptance or satisfaction, as knowledge of the assigned intervention could easily bias their responses.)

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns (The trial was not registered, and no pre-specified protocol was published before the study commenced. Although the authors appear to have reported the results for all the questions included in their questionnaire, the absence of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan and protocol makes it impossible to completely rule out selective reporting of the results.)

6. Overall rating: high risk (The overall risk of bias is high due to the high risk of bias in the measurement of the subjective outcome (Domain 4) caused by the lack of patient blinding, combined with some concerns regarding the selection of the reported results.)

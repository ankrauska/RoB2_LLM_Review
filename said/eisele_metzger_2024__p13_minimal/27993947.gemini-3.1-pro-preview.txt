Based on the provided manuscript and protocol, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **shared decision making score**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
Randomization was performed using a computer-generated random sequence with stratification for age, gender, and family history. Although one of the investigators generated the allocation sequence, the randomization occurred only after the participants completed their baseline visit and scheduled their second visit, ensuring that the process was random and baseline characteristics were well-balanced between the two groups. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
The trial was unblinded (as indicated by "Masking: NONE" in the protocol), which is inherent to the nature of the intervention (disclosing standard vs. genetically informed risk). There is no indication that participants deviated from the intended interventions, as all 207 randomized participants attended the genetic counselor and physician appointments.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
Out of the 207 randomized patients who completed the second study visit, 206 successfully completed the Shared Decision Making Questionnaire (SDM-Q). The amount of missing outcome data is negligible (less than 1%) and is highly unlikely to bias the results.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: high-risk**
The shared decision making score is a subjective, participant-reported outcome. Because the trial was unblinded, patients were fully aware of whether they were receiving conventional risk scores alone or with genetic risk scores. This lack of blinding in an open-label trial context can significantly influence subjective participant-reported outcomes, introducing a potentially high risk of measurement bias.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
The "Shared decision making (as assessed by survey)" score was pre-specified in the trial registration protocol as a secondary outcome. The results were reported in accordance with the pre-defined methodology and scoring scale, with no evidence of selective reporting or manipulation of the analysis plan.

**6. Overall rating: high-risk**
The overall risk of bias is judged to be high. While randomization, missing data, and selective reporting present a low risk of bias, the measurement of the outcome carries a high risk of bias because a subjective, patient-reported endpoint was assessed in a completely unblinded manner.

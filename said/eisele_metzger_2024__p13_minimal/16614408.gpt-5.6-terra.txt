**Endpoint assessed:** change from baseline to 3 months in plasma **8-iso-prostaglandin F2α (8-iso-PGF2α)** and **15-keto-dihydro-prostaglandin F2α (15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α)**.

1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**  
   - The report states that participants were “randomly assigned” first to the SAFA or MUFA diet and then, within diet groups, to fish-oil or placebo capsules. The factorial allocation is therefore described as random in principle.  
   - However, the article provides no information on the random-sequence generation method (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, blocking, stratification by centre) and no information whatever on allocation concealment. In a multicentre trial, failure to describe concealment is particularly troublesome, since recruiters could potentially foresee assignments.  
   - Baseline characteristics shown in Table 1 appear broadly comparable across the four groups. This provides some reassurance but cannot establish that allocation was adequately concealed.  
   - The placebo contained olive oil and was given as capsules in the same stated amount as fish oil, which suggests an attempt at masking for the supplement comparison. Nevertheless, the manuscript does not explicitly state whether participants, investigators, dietary staff, laboratory personnel, or analysts were blinded. Fish-oil capsules may also be recognisable because of taste, smell, or gastrointestinal effects.  
   - The dietary SAFA versus MUFA allocation could not realistically be blinded, because participants received dietary instructions and food products differing in fat composition.  

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns**  
   - Participants were instructed to follow specified SAFA or MUFA diets and to take fish-oil capsules providing 3.6 g/day of n-3 fatty acids or olive-oil placebo capsules for 90 days. Dietary records indicate that the achieved dietary fatty-acid profiles differed in the intended directions, and serum phospholipid fatty-acid composition reportedly confirmed increased EPA/DHA intake in the fish-oil groups. These findings support at least reasonable adherence.  
   - Nevertheless, adherence was assessed partly by self-reported 3-day food records, which are vulnerable to reporting error. The paper does not report capsule counts, adherence thresholds, numbers discontinuing capsules or dietary treatment, crossovers, or whether adherence differed between groups.  
   - The background-diet comparison was necessarily open-label. Knowledge of assigned diet could have prompted additional behavioural or dietary changes beyond the prescribed fat substitution. Indeed, the authors acknowledge that participation may have generally improved dietary habits. Such changes could affect oxidative-stress biomarkers.  
   - For the fish-oil comparison, use of an olive-oil capsule placebo may have limited deviations, but explicit blinding and its success are not documented.  
   - The analysis appears to compare assigned groups, but it is not explicitly stated that all randomised participants were analysed in their originally assigned groups. Thus, a strict intention-to-treat analysis cannot be confirmed.

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data:**
   - **8-iso-PGF2α: high risk**  
     - Plasma samples from the Denmark and Italy centres were declared unsuitable for isoprostane analysis because of “inappropriate sample handling and/or storage.” Thus, this primary outcome was unavailable from two of the five centres.  
     - The manuscript does not state the actual number of participants with analysable 8-iso-PGF2α measurements at baseline and follow-up, by randomised group or by centre. Yet Table 3 continues to display the originally randomised group sizes, which is inadequate and obscures the analysis population.  
     - This is not trivial missingness: exclusion of entire centres may represent a substantial fraction of the trial population and may compromise balance across treatment groups, centre-specific practices, and participant populations. The models reportedly adjusted for centre, but the unavailable centres cannot contribute data to this outcome.  
     - Although the stated reason for missingness is pre-analytical sample failure rather than participants’ measured values, the absence of participant flow, actual analysed denominators, and centre-specific results makes it impossible to judge whether missingness was unrelated to the true outcome or to treatment effect. The reported effect on 8-iso-PGF2α is therefore materially vulnerable to bias from missing outcome data.

   - **15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α: some concerns**  
     - This outcome was measured in plasma from all centres, which is reassuring.  
     - However, the report provides no CONSORT-style flow of participants, no numbers with paired baseline/follow-up measurements, no reasons for any participant-level missing samples, and no explicit statement that all 162 randomised participants were included in the analysis.  
     - There is no direct evidence of substantial or differential missingness for this outcome, but the information is insufficient to judge this confidently.

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome:**
   - **8-iso-PGF2α: some concerns**  
     - This is an objectively measured biochemical outcome, determined using a stated validated radioimmunoassay (RIA), with a reported detection limit and cross-reactivity profile. Blood was collected after an overnight fast, at baseline and 3 months, which is a reasonable standardised approach. The outcome is therefore not susceptible to bias from unblinded participant self-report.  
     - However, the article does not state whether laboratory personnel were blinded to treatment allocation, centre, or time point. While the risk of conscious influence is smaller for an assay than for a subjective outcome, laboratory procedures can still be affected by knowledge of allocation.  
     - More importantly, inappropriate handling and/or storage rendered samples from two centres unusable for this exact assay. This demonstrates that pre-analytical handling was a real vulnerability in the study. The authors do not provide sufficient information on sample processing, storage duration, batch allocation, repeat measurements, or whether the remaining three centres’ samples were handled identically and reliably.  
     - Accordingly, the assay itself seems technically credible, but confidence in consistent outcome measurement across the analysed sample is limited.

   - **15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α: low risk**  
     - This is also an objective plasma biomarker, measured in all centres with a validated RIA, reported detection limit, and documented antibody cross-reactivity.  
     - Samples were obtained under the same fasting protocol at baseline and follow-up and stored frozen until analysis. Unlike 8-iso-PGF2α, no centre-specific sample-handling failure is reported for this endpoint.  
     - Blinding of laboratory staff is not explicitly reported. Nevertheless, the objective nature of the assay and the use of a defined laboratory method make it unlikely that any lack of blinding materially influenced measured concentrations.  
     - On the information available, there is no compelling reason to suspect differential measurement error between intervention groups.

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
   - The manuscript explicitly identifies changes in 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α as the “main outcome variables,” and both outcomes are reported in Table 3. This is reassuring, as is the reporting of the null result for 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α rather than reporting only the statistically significant 8-iso-PGF2α result.  
   - However, no prospectively dated protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial registration is provided. It is therefore impossible to verify that the primary outcomes, time point, transformations, covariate adjustments, and analytic contrasts were specified before data were examined.  
   - The analysis model included treatment factors, their interaction, centre, age, sex, and baseline outcome values. The interaction term was removed when it was not statistically significant. This is a data-dependent model-selection procedure and creates scope for selective choice of analyses.  
   - Numerous biomarkers, dietary variables, within-group changes, treatment effects, and interaction tests were evaluated. No multiplicity strategy is described. The nominal P = 0.015 for 8-iso-PGF2α should therefore be interpreted cautiously.  
   - The actual analysis denominator for 8-iso-PGF2α is not reported, despite the exclusion of two centres. This further prevents verification that the reported result corresponds to a prespecified complete-case or other planned analysis.

6. **Overall rating: high risk of bias**  
   - The overall rating is **high risk of bias**, principally because the primary 8-iso-PGF2α outcome was unavailable from two entire centres owing to inadequate sample handling/storage, without reporting of the numbers analysed by group or centre and without adequate evidence that this substantial missingness could not bias the treatment estimate.  
   - In addition, there are concerns about inadequately described randomisation and allocation concealment, uncertain blinding and adherence procedures, incomplete reporting of participant-level missing data, and absence of a protocol or prespecified analysis plan.  
   - The conclusion that n-3 fatty acids reduced **8-iso-PGF2α** is therefore not sufficiently secure. The null finding for **15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α** is less affected by the centre-level assay failure, but still carries some uncertainty because participant flow and prespecification are insufficiently documented.

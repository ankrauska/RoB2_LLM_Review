1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns (The study mentions that participants were randomly assigned in a 2:2:1 ratio from each group of 5 participants, stratified by intent to use medication and order of entry. However, there is no information provided regarding whether the allocation sequence was adequately concealed before assignment. Therefore, there are some concerns about potential bias in the randomization process.)

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns (Given the nature of the contingency management interventions, neither participants nor personnel could be blinded to group assignment. While this is typical for such behavioral interventions, the lack of blinding could potentially influence participant behavior or personnel interactions, raising some concerns, although no obvious deviations from intended interventions were reported that would compromise the results.)

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk (Missing visits were common, as indicated by attendance rates. However, in contingency management studies for smoking cessation, missing a breath CO sample is typically treated as a non-abstinent sample or failure to meet the criterion, which is an appropriate conservative approach. Thus, missing data is unlikely to bias the results in favor of the intervention.)

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk (The outcome of smoking abstinence was measured objectively using a breath carbon monoxide (CO) sample cutoff of < 3 ppm. Because this is an objective physiological measure, the lack of participant and personnel blinding is highly unlikely to influence the measurement of the outcome.)

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk (Although a pre-published protocol is not referenced, the reported outcomes (abstinence rates, time to first abstinent sample, maintaining abstinence) directly align with the stated hypotheses and standard measures in contingency management smoking cessation trials. There is no evidence of selective reporting of multiple analyses or outcome measurements.)

6. Overall rating: some concerns (The trial is judged to have some concerns overall, primarily due to the lack of details regarding allocation concealment in the randomization process and the inherent lack of blinding typical of behavioral interventions.)

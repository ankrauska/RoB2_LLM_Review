1. **Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**  
   - This was a within-participant (split-body) trial: each participant received both formulations, one on each cubital fossa. This design substantially limits between-participant baseline confounding.  
   - Side allocation was generated using a randomization scheme prepared by an independent statistics/data-management department, using randomly permuted blocks and validated software (RANCODE 3.6). This is an adequately random method.  
   - The formulations were coded A and B, and the allocation/blinding code was reportedly sealed until the final database had been closed. Thus, there is reasonable evidence that allocation was concealed from investigators and participants at the point of assignment.  
   - Although the fixed block size is not reported, this is of little practical importance in this paired, coded-product design, particularly as treatment applications were performed by study nurses and the final allocations were concealed.  
   - No baseline imbalance between intervention conditions is relevant, since both treatments were applied to each participant.

2. **Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**  
   - The trial was described as double-blind. The two products were coded, and the code was maintained until database closure. Participants, study nurses applying the products, and outcome assessor were therefore intended to remain unaware of which product contained emollients.  
   - Applications were standardized: both products were applied twice daily on study days for 2 weeks; the quantity (two 0.7-mL aliquots), site, massage duration, and personnel administering treatment were specified. This minimizes differential adherence and co-interventions.  
   - Three participants had protocol violations: one took anti-inflammatory medication and two disinfected the cubital fossa. These deviations could affect skin outcomes, but they occurred in only three of 35 participants, and the primary analysis was reported in the ITT population including all 35 participants.  
   - There is no indication that protocol deviations differed systematically according to treatment side or product, nor that participants discontinued one allocated product selectively.  
   - A residual caveat is that the formulations may have differed in sensory characteristics despite coding, potentially allowing some unblinding. However, no evidence is provided that unblinding occurred or that it led to differential behaviour or treatment administration.

3. **Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: some concerns**  
   - Thirty-five participants started the study and were included in the reported ITT analysis. Thirty-two completed the study without major protocol deviations and comprised the per-protocol population.  
   - The report states that “missing values in 3 participants were replaced” using last observation carried forward (LOCF). It does not clearly specify which observations were missing, at which time-points, whether missingness concerned one or both treatment sites, or whether the three participants with missing data were the same three with protocol violations.  
   - LOCF is a potentially biased method for a repeatedly measured, cumulative irritation outcome. Skin dryness and erythema may worsen over time; carrying forward an earlier observation can underestimate later irritation and may distort the summed score.  
   - The amount of missing data appears limited—apparently involving only three of 35 participants—and both ITT and per-protocol results point in the same direction. Nevertheless, the insufficient description of missingness and reliance on LOCF preclude a low-risk judgement.

4. **Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome**

   **a. Visual assessment of erythema: low-risk**  
   - Erythema was assessed by visual inspection using a defined ordinal scale ranging from no erythema to fiery red erythema with oedema.  
   - Assessments were performed by a dermatologist, rather than by participants, and all assessments were reportedly undertaken blindly. This is important because erythema scoring entails clinical judgement and could otherwise be susceptible to expectation bias.  
   - The same blinded investigator conducted all evaluations, limiting variability arising from use of multiple assessors.  
   - Although visual erythema assessment is inherently somewhat subjective, there is no indication that the dermatologist knew product allocation or that knowledge of allocation could have influenced the measurements. Therefore, measurement bias is unlikely.

   **b. Visual assessment of dryness: low-risk**  
   - Dryness was assessed by a dermatologist using a specified ordinal scale from no dryness to severe dryness with large flakes.  
   - Dryness is a somewhat subjective clinical outcome. However, it was not participant-reported, and assessment was reported to be blinded to treatment allocation. Thus, the principal route by which subjective assessment could introduce differential bias was appropriately addressed.  
   - Use of the same assessor throughout is an advantage, though the report does not describe assessor training, duplicate ratings, or inter-rater reliability. These omissions are not sufficient to indicate that the outcome measurement was biased.  
   - There is no evidence that treatment allocation was known to the assessor or that the outcome was measured differently between treatment sides.

5. **Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
   - The manuscript describes the primary outcome as the cumulative sum of erythema and dryness scores over all scheduled assessments. The analytical strategy was also described: non-inferiority testing using a one-sided 97.5% confidence interval for the ratio of means, followed by superiority testing if non-inferiority was established.  
   - The authors report the composite primary outcome, its components (erythema and dryness), ITT and per-protocol analyses, and longitudinal mean scores in figures. This degree of reporting is reassuring.  
   - Nevertheless, no protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial-registration record is provided for independent comparison with the published report. It is consequently impossible to verify that the specified primary outcome, non-inferiority margin, switch to superiority testing, analysis population, handling of missing data, and reported subgroup analyses were finalized before outcome data were examined.  
   - The study was sponsored by the manufacturer of both products, and the corresponding author was affiliated with that manufacturer. Sponsorship is not itself evidence of selective reporting, but it strengthens the need for an accessible prospective protocol or registration record, which is absent here.  
   - Accordingly, there are some concerns rather than evidence of high risk.

6. **Overall rating: some concerns**  
   - Randomization, concealment/blinding, standardized intervention delivery, and blinded dermatologist assessment of both erythema and dryness appear sound.  
   - However, there are **some concerns** about the handling and insufficient description of missing data, particularly use of LOCF, and about selective reporting because no protocol or registration record is available to verify prespecification of outcomes and analyses.

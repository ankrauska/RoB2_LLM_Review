Based on the provided manuscript and the criteria specified, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome **venous concentrations of endothelin-1 (ET-1)**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
Justification: The study states that "sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random 20 consecutive adults". However, there is no mention of whether these envelopes were opaque and sequentially numbered to ensure adequate allocation concealment. Furthermore, Table 2 reveals notable baseline imbalances between the two small groups (e.g., sex distribution of 9/1 vs. 5/5; cardiopulmonary bypass times of 116 vs. 84 minutes; pre-bypass pulmonary artery pressures), which raises some concerns about whether the randomization process successfully prevented selection bias. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk**
Justification: The participants were anesthetized during the intervention phase and were therefore blinded to the treatment (inspired oxygen fraction). Although the clinical personnel operating the ventilators were aware of the FIO2 settings, this lack of blinding is unlikely to lead to systematic deviations from the intended interventions that would affect a physiological, laboratory-measured outcome like venous ET-1 concentrations. 

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: high risk**
Justification: The authors explicitly state in the caption for Figure 1 that "Group 2 had an outlier with high arterial ET-1 values throughout the study... These data were excluded from the statistical analysis in order to simplify statistical interpretation." Arbitrarily excluding a participant's data post-randomization simply because it is an outlier introduces a high risk of bias, as the missingness is directly related to the true value of the outcome. 

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low risk**
Justification: Venous concentrations of ET-1 were measured using an objective, standardized sandwich-type enzyme immunoassay (ELISA). Given the objective nature of this laboratory measurement, the outcome assessment is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, even if the laboratory assessors were not explicitly blinded.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**
Justification: There is no reference to a pre-published protocol or trial registry entry to confirm that the reported analyses and time points exactly match those pre-specified. The exclusion of the outlier "to simplify statistical interpretation" also indicates that the analysis plan was flexible and modified based on the acquired data. 

**6. Overall rating: high risk**
Justification: The study is judged to be at a high risk of bias overall. This is primarily driven by the deliberate exclusion of an outlier from the outcome analysis, which constitutes a high risk of bias due to missing outcome data (Domain 3). Additionally, there are some concerns regarding the randomization process and the selection of the reported result.

Based on the provided manuscript and criteria, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **Change in photoaging**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
(justification: Participants were randomly assigned using a "computer-generated randomized list" by an investigator who had no knowledge of the participants, indicating adequate sequence generation and allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics across groups were well-balanced, suggesting the randomization process was effective.)

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
(justification: Although blinding to the sunscreen intervention was not possible because using a placebo sunscreen was considered unethical, this reflects the pragmatic nature of evaluating "discretionary" vs. "daily" use. A placebo was used for the $\beta$-carotene intervention. The data were analyzed according to treatment as randomly allocated (intention-to-treat approach), minimizing bias from deviations.)

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: some concerns**
(justification: A significant portion of participants did not contribute replicas at both time points (of 886 participants with at least one good-quality replica, only 604 contributed replicas in both 1992 and 1996, meaning roughly 32% had missing data). Although the authors conducted multiple imputations and reported that missingness at follow-up was not associated with baseline grades, the substantial attrition rate raises some concerns regarding the potential for missing data to impact the true effect estimate.)

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: low-risk**
(justification: The outcome (photoaging) was assessed objectively using microtopography of skin surface replicas. These replicas were graded by experienced assessors who were entirely blinded to the participants' treatment allocation, ensuring that the measurement of the outcome was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention.)

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
(justification: The statistical analysis plan was robust (using generalized estimating equations and ordinal logistic regression), and the reported outcomes align with the pre-specified primary endpoint of the trial. There is no evidence of selective outcome reporting or multiple unadjusted analyses being cherry-picked.)

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
(justification: The overall risk of bias is judged to be 'some concerns' because, while the trial was well-designed, randomized, and featured blinded outcome assessors, there is a relatively high rate of missing outcome data (roughly 32% missing both time points) which introduces some uncertainty into the final estimates.)

Based on the provided manuscript, here is the risk of bias assessment for the endpoint **total duration of treatment**:

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: low-risk**
**Justification:** The investigators used a robust method for random sequence generation, specifically a computer-generated random number table with block randomization stratified by operator and center. Allocation concealment was properly ensured using sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes, and the person who generated the sequence was independent of the allocation process. The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics between the two groups (Table 2) were relatively well-balanced, indicating that the randomization was successful. 

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low-risk**
**Justification:** While the clinicians and patients could not be blinded to the bracket system due to the physical differences in the interventions, this is standard for orthodontic bracket trials. There were no indications of substantial deviations from the intended interventions. Although the investigators state that analysis was undertaken on a "per protocol" basis rather than intention-to-treat (ITT), the dropout rate was exceptionally low (only 3 out of 138 subjects, meaning 97.8% completed treatment). Thus, the exclusion of these three patients is highly unlikely to have biased the results.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low-risk**
**Justification:** Outcome data were available for 135 of the 138 randomized participants (97.8%). The amount of missing data is negligible and is distributed evenly between the two groups (one subject dropped out before treatment in the SmartClip group, and one from each group failed to complete treatment). This low level of attrition is very unlikely to introduce significant bias.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
**Justification:** The outcome "total duration of treatment" is defined as the time in months from fixed appliance placement to debond. The decision of when a patient's teeth are sufficiently aligned to warrant debonding is made by the clinician. Because the clinicians could not be blinded to the intervention group, there is a possibility that their subjective judgment on when to conclude treatment could have been influenced by their knowledge of the bracket system being used. Although data analysis was blinded, the actual end-point of treatment (debonding) was determined by unblinded operators, raising some concerns about measurement bias. 

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low-risk**
**Justification:** The reported outcomes (total duration of treatment, number of appointments, and bracket bond failures) correspond directly to the stated aims and methodology of the trial. The authors comprehensively reported the findings for all prespecified outcomes in the results section and provided appropriate statistical comparisons. There is no evidence of selective outcome reporting or selective analysis reporting.

**6. Overall rating: some concerns**
**Justification:** The study is generally well-designed and executed with excellent randomization, concealment, and follow-up. However, there are 'some concerns' regarding the risk of bias in the measurement of the outcome, as the unblinded clinicians were responsible for deciding when the orthodontic treatment was complete, which inherently dictates the total duration of treatment.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: KCSG-BR07-02 Trial
## Outcome: Progression-Free Survival (PFS)

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: Some concerns**

Justification: The randomization method is described as a "random permutation method" coordinated centrally at the KCSG data center, which suggests adequate allocation concealment (central randomization typically prevents foreknowledge of assignment). Stratification factors were appropriately specified (visceral disease, prior adjuvant taxane, response to induction, HR status). Baseline characteristics were well-balanced (Table 1), supporting adequate randomization. However, this is an open-label study (masking: NONE per registry), which is appropriate given the nature of maintenance chemotherapy vs. observation, and does not by itself indicate a flawed randomization process. I have no specific concerns about the sequence generation or concealment mechanism being compromised. However, no explicit description of the concealment mechanism (e.g., sequentially numbered, sealed envelopes, or central randomization at the time of allocation) is provided beyond stating it was "coordinated centrally," which leaves some ambiguity as to whether investigators could have foreseen assignments before or during recruitment. This omission of detail prevents a "low risk" judgment.

---

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: High risk**

Justification: This is an open-label trial with no blinding of patients or investigators to treatment allocation (masking: NONE). This is critical for PFS assessment because:

- PFS determination requires both radiological assessment AND investigator judgment of "progression," and investigators were aware of treatment assignment.
- Critically, 33 patients (28.4%) in the maintenance arm discontinued treatment at the *physician's discretion* without documented disease progression — this is a substantial deviation from protocol-intended continuous treatment "until progression" and could reflect non-protocol-specified stopping rules influenced by knowledge of treatment allocation.
- The asymmetry in treatment discontinuation reasons between arms (13 due to AEs and 33 due to physician discretion in the maintenance arm vs. essentially none in the observation arm) suggests differential post-randomization management that could affect the estimated treatment effect, particularly if these decisions were influenced by unblinded toxicity assessment or informal impressions of efficacy/futility.
- The systemic treatments after progression differed in choice/frequency, and while ITT analysis was used, the switching patterns and discretionary stopping represent substantial deviations that were not clearly accounted for in a per-protocol sensitivity analysis.

This combination of open-label design, discretionary early stopping without progression in nearly 30% of the treatment arm, and lack of clarity on how these deviations were handled analytically raises high risk of bias for the PFS outcome specifically.

---

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: Low risk**

Justification: All 231 randomized patients were included in the ITT efficacy analysis (116 maintenance, 115 observation), as stated in the CONSORT diagram and statistical methods. There is no indication of substantial differential loss to follow-up between arms with respect to PFS ascertainment — patients who discontinued treatment were still followed for PFS events (progression), given that PFS was defined as "documented date of disease progression or the last visit date." The sample size accounted for expected 10% loss to follow-up, and this appears consistent with what was achieved. No large or asymmetric missing data pattern is reported for the primary endpoint.

---

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (PFS): High risk**

Justification: PFS is a compound and semi-subjective outcome combining objective radiological assessment (RECIST 1.0) with investigator judgment regarding the timing/determination of progression, especially for non-measurable or symptomatic disease. Key concerns:

- **No blinding of outcome assessors**: The manuscript explicitly states "PFS and the duration of response were assessed by investigators" — not by an independent, blinded central radiology review committee. Given the open-label design, investigators assessing for progression were fully aware of treatment allocation.
- This is particularly problematic because knowledge of treatment arm can influence the timing and threshold for calling progression (e.g., investigators might be more vigilant or lenient depending on arm), introducing detection bias.
- No mention of blinded independent central review (BICR) to adjudicate radiological progression, which is now considered a methodological safeguard in trials with subjective/semi-subjective endpoints like PFS.
- The physician-driven early discontinuation in the maintenance arm (33 patients, discussed above) without documented progression further complicates ascertainment of the "true" progression date in this subgroup, as these patients may have experienced later informal or delayed capture of progression events after treatment cessation, potentially diluting or distorting the PFS estimate.

Given the combination of unblinded outcome assessment and absence of independent radiological review for what is a partially subjective endpoint, I rate this domain as **high risk**.

---

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: Low risk**

Justification: Cross-referencing the trial registry (NCT00561119) and protocol, PFS is clearly pre-specified as the primary outcome ("Progression free survival," timeframe ~4 years), consistent with the manuscript's stated primary purpose and statistical analysis plan (sample size calculation based on 6-month PFS rate difference). The manuscript reports PFS using the pre-specified method (Kaplan-Meier, log-rank test, Cox model) and the result (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.55–0.97, P=.026) appears consistent with what would be expected from the described analysis plan. No evidence of selective outcome switching, post-hoc redefinition, or suppression of the primary PFS analysis is apparent. Subgroup and forest plot analyses are appropriately labeled as exploratory/secondary considerations. I find no signal of selective reporting for this specific outcome.

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **High risk**

**Rationale:** While randomization and missing data domains show low-to-moderate concern, the combination of (a) an open-label design with no blinding of either participants or outcome assessors, (b) substantial physician-driven discretionary treatment discontinuation in nearly 30% of the maintenance arm without documented progression, and (c) reliance on unblinded investigator-assessed PFS without independent radiological review, cumulatively and substantially undermine confidence in the reported PFS treatment effect. Per the stated overall rating rules, a "high risk" judgment in Domain 4 (compounded by Domain 1 and Domain 2 concerns) is sufficient to classify the overall risk of bias for the PFS outcome as **high risk**.

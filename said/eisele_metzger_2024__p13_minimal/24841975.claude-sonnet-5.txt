# Risk of Bias Assessment: Overall Survival Endpoint
## CLAVELA Trial (Elacytarabine vs Investigator's Choice in R/R AML)

---

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: SOME CONCERNS**

*Justification:* Randomization was performed via a centralized Interactive Web Response System (IWRS) with block randomization (block size = 4), stratified by prior treatment intensity and remission status. This is an adequate allocation concealment mechanism, and the pre-specification of investigator's choice *before* randomization is a methodologically sound feature that minimizes selection bias in arm assignment. However, I am concerned by the resulting baseline imbalance: significantly more patients aged >70 years were randomized to the elacytarabine arm (30% vs 18%, P=.007), and age was not a stratification factor. Given that age is a powerful, independent prognostic factor for OS in AML, this imbalance raises the possibility that randomization, despite an adequate concealment procedure, produced a materially unbalanced comparison for the primary endpoint. The authors attempted to address this post hoc with an age-adjusted HR (0.98), which is reassuring but constitutes a secondary, non-prespecified analysis correcting for a randomization artifact.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: SOME CONCERNS**

*Justification:* This was an open-label study — no blinding of patients, treating physicians, or outcome assessors was employed or feasible given the heterogeneous control regimens. For OS, this is less concerning than for subjective endpoints, but open-label design can still influence post-progression management (e.g., decisions regarding salvage therapy, transplant referral, supportive care intensity, or hospice decisions), which could differentially affect survival times. The paper reports 6 patients in the control arm did not receive their prespecified investigator's-choice regimen; this is a minor, symmetric deviation, well documented, and unlikely to bias OS substantially. SCT rates were balanced (20 vs 21), which is reassuring regarding this key potential co-intervention. ITT analysis was used appropriately. Overall, moderate concern remains due to open-label conduct without any stated mitigation (e.g., blinded independent endpoint adjudication committee for death/censoring dates).

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: LOW RISK**

*Justification:* Missing OS data were minimal and balanced between arms (1 patient lost to follow-up in the elacytarabine arm vs 4 in the control arm, out of 381 total). Censoring rules were prespecified and applied symmetrically (censored at last known-alive date for those lost to follow-up). The vast majority of patients had confirmed vital status at the data cutoff. This is unlikely to introduce meaningful bias.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome (Overall Survival): LOW RISK**

*Justification:* OS is an objective, unambiguous, hard endpoint (date of death), which is inherently robust to lack of blinding, since ascertainment does not depend on subjective assessment by patient, clinician, or investigator. There is no indication that outcome assessors used differential methods to determine vital status between arms. Kaplan-Meier and Cox methods were applied per protocol with the same censoring approach for both arms. The lack of blinding is not expected to bias this specific outcome measurement in a meaningful way.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: LOW RISK**

*Justification:* The trial is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01147939), with OS designated a priori as the primary endpoint. This matches the primary endpoint reported and emphasized throughout the manuscript. Sample size, event-driven analysis (300/302 events), and statistical methods (log-rank test, Cox regression, HR with 95% CI) appear consistent with a prespecified statistical analysis plan. Secondary endpoints (ORR, RFS) and multiple subgroup/sensitivity analyses (age-adjusted, SCT-censored) are reported transparently, including non-significant results, without evidence of selective reporting favoring a positive outcome. The negative primary result is reported prominently and is consistent with prespecified success criteria (HR 0.70 not met).

---

## 6. Overall Rating: **SOME CONCERNS**

*Justification:* While the randomization allocation-concealment mechanism was robust and the majority of domains (missing data, outcome measurement, selective reporting) are low risk, the combination of (a) a clinically important baseline age imbalance not addressed by stratification and (b) the inherent limitations of an open-label design on post-randomization management decisions that could influence survival (crossover to transplant, differential supportive care) raises the overall bias rating to "some concerns." Neither issue alone is severe enough to constitute "high risk," and the objective nature of the OS endpoint mitigates much of the open-label concern, but together they are sufficient to lower confidence below "low risk."

**Endpoint: recurrent thrombotic events (recurrent VTE, comprising recurrent DVT and/or PE)**

The randomized comparison relevant to treatment duration is **continued LMWH (group A1; n=119) versus discontinuation of LMWH (group A2; n=123)** among patients with residual vein thrombosis (RVT). Group B (no RVT; n=105) was not randomized and therefore comparisons involving group B are prognostic/observational rather than randomized treatment comparisons.

**1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: high risk**  
The report states that patients with RVT were randomized using “a sealed-envelop technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10.” This is not an adequate description of a genuinely random sequence-generation process. Allocation based on chart number is particularly troubling: chart numbers may be predictable, sequential, or related to enrolment order, rather than randomly generated. The use of sealed envelopes does not remedy an inadequately random or potentially predictable allocation sequence.

Furthermore, there is no information that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, and opened only after irrevocable participant enrolment. Thus, allocation concealment cannot be regarded as secure. Baseline characteristics appear broadly similar between A1 and A2, but this does not establish that the allocation process was random or concealed. The randomized comparison is therefore at **high risk of bias from the randomization process**.

**2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: low risk**  
1. The interventions were clear: patients with RVT either continued nadroparin for six further months (A1) or stopped anticoagulation (A2).  
2. Participants and treating clinicians were explicitly unmasked. This was unavoidable in practical terms because one group received ongoing subcutaneous anticoagulation and the other did not.  
3. The lack of blinding could potentially influence clinical management, investigation of symptoms, or use of concomitant treatments. However, the report does not identify important treatment crossovers, systematic use of alternative anticoagulation, or differential co-interventions attributable to knowledge of allocation.  
4. Adherence in the continued-LMWH group was reported to be high: 15/119 patients interrupted LMWH for fewer than five days. There is no indication of substantial non-adherence or treatment switching sufficient to undermine the intended contrast.  
5. The analysis is stated to have been by intention to treat.

Although open-label care creates some concern for differential clinical behaviour, the available evidence does not demonstrate important deviations from the assigned intervention. This domain is judged **low risk**.

**3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: some concerns**  
1. The article reports that 42/347 patients died during follow-up, all attributed to cancer progression. Death prevents subsequent observation of recurrent VTE and is therefore a competing event, not merely an innocuous loss to follow-up.  
2. Mortality differed numerically between the randomized groups: 12/119 (10.1%) in A1 versus 19/123 (15.4%) in A2. Even though this difference was not statistically significant, differential death can affect the opportunity to detect recurrent thrombotic events.  
3. Follow-up counts declined appreciably. At 12 months after randomization, 91 A1 and 77 A2 participants were still under follow-up. The report does not fully account for all those no longer followed at each time point—for example, by clearly distinguishing deaths, recurrent events, administrative end of follow-up, withdrawals, and other losses.  
4. No explicit number of withdrawals or participants with completely unavailable recurrent-VTE outcome data is reported.  
5. The Cox-model analysis apparently censors patients, but the report does not establish that censoring was non-informative. In this cancer population, death and progression of cancer are plausibly related to thrombotic risk.

There is not enough evidence to conclude that missingness or censoring substantially biased the result, but the incomplete accounting for follow-up and competing deaths means that **some concerns** remain.

**4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: some concerns**

**4a. Recurrent DVT: some concerns**  
1. Recurrent DVT was assessed with objective compression ultrasonography, using prespecified diagnostic criteria: a previously compressible segment becoming non-compressible, or an increase of more than 4 mm in residual thrombus diameter during compression. Repeat ultrasonography or venography was planned for uncertain cases. These are strengths.  
2. Outcome events were reviewed by a central committee stated to be unaware of group assignment, RVT results, patient identity, and enrolling centre. Blinded central adjudication of the imaging and clinical evidence substantially reduces direct assessor bias.  
3. However, participants and treating clinicians were unblinded, and ultrasonography was performed only when recurrence was clinically suspected, rather than routinely at fixed intervals. Knowledge that anticoagulation had been stopped could lower the threshold for patients to report symptoms or clinicians to investigate symptoms in group A2. Conversely, symptoms in patients continuing LMWH might have been attributed to alternative explanations.  
4. The outcome is objectively confirmed once investigated, but differential symptom-triggered ascertainment remains possible.

Accordingly, recurrent DVT measurement is judged to have **some concerns**, rather than low risk.

**4b. Recurrent PE: some concerns**  
1. PE diagnoses were based on objective diagnostic algorithms using clinical probability assessment and ventilation-perfusion scanning or helical CT.  
2. A blinded central committee reviewed clinical investigations and outcome events, which protects against biased interpretation of imaging.  
3. Nevertheless, investigation for PE depended on symptoms and clinical suspicion in an unblinded setting. No routine imaging for asymptomatic PE was performed. Therefore, knowledge of whether LMWH had been continued may have influenced presentation, clinical suspicion, or referral for imaging.  
4. The authors also state that fatal PE was excluded based on absence of clinical symptoms or normal imaging findings. In a population with substantial cancer mortality, absence of symptoms before death is not a wholly secure basis for excluding fatal PE.

Thus, measurement of recurrent PE is judged to have **some concerns**.

**Overall measurement judgment for the composite recurrent-VTE endpoint: some concerns.** The objective imaging-based definitions and blinded central adjudication are important safeguards, but they do not fully eliminate bias from unblinded, symptom-triggered detection.

**5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: some concerns**  
1. The trial registration identifies recurrent DVT and/or PE as the primary outcome, consistent in broad terms with the manuscript’s recurrent-VTE endpoint.  
2. However, the registry information provided is sparse. It does not specify the primary time point, the analysis population, the exact comparison, the survival-analysis method, covariate adjustment, handling of deaths, or whether the various reported analyses were prespecified. A full protocol and statistical analysis plan are not available here.  
3. The reporting is internally difficult to reconcile. The abstract reports an adjusted hazard ratio of **1.37 (95% CI 0.7–2.5; P=.311)** for A2 versus A1, whereas the Results section reports **1.58 (95% CI 0.8–3.0; P=.175)**. These appear to concern the same randomized comparison but are materially different estimates.  
4. The manuscript reports several potentially selectable ways of presenting recurrence: overall events, events during the additional six months of LMWH, events after completion of LMWH, 12-month cumulative rates, and adjusted comparisons with the non-randomized no-RVT group. The stated primary endpoint—time to recurrent VTE from completion of LMWH—also produces different time origins for the groups, since A1 discontinued LMWH six months later than A2.  
5. The reporting therefore does not provide confidence that the reported numerical result represents a single fully prespecified analysis selected independently of the observed data.

There is insufficient evidence to prove selective reporting, but the sparse registration and discrepancies in reported estimates lead to **some concerns**.

**6. Overall rating: high risk of bias**  
The overall judgment is **high risk of bias**. The principal reason is the high risk of bias in the randomization process: allocation “based on clinical chart number,” with inadequately described concealment, is not persuasive evidence of genuinely random and concealed assignment. In addition, there are some concerns regarding outcome-data completeness/competing deaths, symptom-triggered outcome ascertainment in an open-label trial, and selection/reporting of the analysis.

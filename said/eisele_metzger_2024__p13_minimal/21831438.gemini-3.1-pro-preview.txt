1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns (The study used a mixed block randomization method, but it was an open-label study, and the manuscript does not explicitly describe whether allocation was adequately concealed. Furthermore, there was a significant baseline imbalance between the groups regarding age [51.0 vs 47.8 years, P=0.02], which may indicate potential issues with the randomization or allocation concealment process).

2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions: some concerns (Because the trial was open-label, both participants and investigators were aware of the assigned interventions. While compliance was reported to be high and an intention-to-treat approach was used for the analysis, the lack of blinding leaves room for potential deviations from the intended interventions based on treatment knowledge).

3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data: low risk (Out of 233 randomized patients, 224 were included in the intention-to-treat analysis. The dropout rates were relatively low and balanced between the treatment arms [20 discontinued in the combination group and 15 in the monotherapy group], and the reasons for discontinuation were adequately documented and similar across groups. Therefore, missing data is unlikely to have substantially biased the results).

4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome: some concerns (The outcome of interest is the change in mean sitting diastolic blood pressure. Although blood pressure was measured using a fully automated device and following a standardized protocol [averaging multiple readings after a resting period], the trial was open-label. The investigators performing the measurements were not blinded to the treatment assignment, which could introduce some observer or measurement bias, despite the use of automated devices).

5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result: low risk (The reported primary outcome—changes in the mean sitting DBP from baseline after 4 weeks of therapy—is exactly matched with the primary outcome pre-specified in the trial registration [NCT00621153]. The analysis was conducted according to the pre-specified plan without evidence of selective reporting).

6. Overall rating: some concerns (The study is judged to have 'some concerns' overall, primarily driven by its open-label design which affects multiple domains, including potential issues with allocation concealment, deviations from intended interventions, and outcome measurement).

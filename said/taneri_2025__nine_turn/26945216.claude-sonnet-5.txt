I've carefully read through this article on the comparison of TENS and interferential therapy (IFT) for myofascial pain syndrome affecting the upper trapezius muscle. Here's my understanding of the key elements relevant to a systematic review:

**Study Design & Methodology**
- Single-blind RCT, 3 arms (Control/standard care, TENS+standard care, IFT+standard care), n=105 (35/group), conducted in Sri Lanka (2012-2015)
- Standard care = hot pack + AROM exercises + myofascial release + home exercise/postural program
- 8 sessions over 4 weeks, 2x/week
- Outcomes: VAS pain (0-100mm) and cervical ROM (extension, lateral flexion, rotation) at 4 timepoints: baseline, immediately post-1st treatment, pre-8th treatment, 1-week post-8th treatment
- Sample size justified via pilot study/G*Power

**Key Findings**
- No baseline differences between groups (age, sex, pain, ROM)
- TENS > IFT > Control for both pain reduction and ROM improvement at all follow-up points (p<0.05)
- Effect appeared immediately (after first treatment) and was sustained/increased through the 1-week follow-up

**Notable Strengths I'm noting for critical appraisal**
- Adequate power calculation with pilot data
- Standardized combination therapy across groups (addressing a limitation in the prior Hou et al. study)
- CONSORT flow diagram with 0% attrition (105/105 completed)
- Randomization with allocation concealment (sealed envelopes, computerized block randomization)
- Validated outcome measures (goniometer, VAS)

**Key Limitations I'm flagging**
- Only single-blind (patients not blinded to intervention type is essentially impossible with TENS/IFT, but assessor = treating therapist = principal investigator, which is a major source of potential bias/lack of assessor blinding)
- No placebo/sham control for TENS or IFT arms
- Short follow-up (only 1 week post-treatment) — no data on medium/long-term durability
- Single-center design, single assessor/therapist (performance and detection bias risk)
- Unidimensional pain assessment (VAS only, no multidimensional pain tools)
- Possible confound: electrode placement differences between TENS (direct over MTrP, bipolar) vs IFT (quadripolar, surrounding MTrP) — authors acknowledge this as a possible explanation for TENS superiority rather than a true modality effect
- Population specific to Sri Lankan Sinhala-reading adults, limiting generalizability
- 100% completion/no attrition — unusually high, worth scrutinizing but plausible given short duration

**Relevant for GRADE/risk-of-bias assessment**
- Randomization: low risk (adequate method described)
- Allocation concealment: low risk (envelopes)
- Blinding of participants/personnel: high risk (not feasible, not attempted for personnel)
- Blinding of outcome assessment: high risk (same person = therapist/assessor)
- Incomplete outcome data: low risk (0% attrition)
- Selective reporting: appears low risk (all pre-specified outcomes reported)
- Other bias: no sham/placebo comparator, single center, single therapist

This gives me a solid basis for extracting data into a systematic review table (PICO, risk of bias, effect sizes) and for narrative synthesis alongside other TENS/IFT/MPS trials (e.g., contrasting with Hou et al. 2002, which found opposite superiority direction under non-standardized comparator conditions — an important point for discussing heterogeneity/inconsistency across the literature).

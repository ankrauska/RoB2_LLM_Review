**Outcome Name:** HDM score

1. 3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: Out of the 52 eligible participants who were randomized into the study, 11 dropped out before completion (approximately 21% missing data). This means outcome data were not available for "nearly all" (>95%) participants.

2. 3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: The authors handled missing data using a "last-observation-carried-forward procedure." According to the RoB 2 guidelines, single imputation methods like LOCF should not be assumed to correct for bias due to missing outcome data. There are no sensitivity analyses reported that evaluate the impact of this missing data under different assumptions.

3. 3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: Missingness could clearly be related to participants' health status. Two of the dropouts were explicitly for clinical reasons closely related to the outcomes being measured: one due to "increased anxiety" and another due to "increased depression." 

4. 3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The reported reasons for missing data provide direct evidence that missingness depends on the true outcome value (e.g., participants leaving due to worsening depression or anxiety). Furthermore, the authors explicitly state that "intent-to-treat analyses found differences between the last assessment HAM-D score for those who dropped out... with the dropouts tending to have higher HAM-D scores," confirming that those with worse outcomes were more likely to have missing end-of-treatment data.

Outcome Name: severity of subjective withdrawal symptoms using the Subjective Opiate Withdrawal

1. 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: While data were available for 37 out of 40 participants (92.5%) at the end-point of methadone therapy (Day 18), the missing data is not balanced. The gabapentin group had 0/20 (0%) missing data, while the placebo group had 3/20 (15%) missing data. The RoB 2 guidelines suggest that for continuous outcomes, 95% data availability is often sufficient; the 92.5% overall rate falls slightly short, and the 15% dropout rate exclusively in the control group raises concerns that the missing data could impact the estimated effect.

2. 3.2. Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

   • Response: No
   • Reason: The authors did not report using any analysis methods that correct for bias (e.g., multiple imputation) or conducting sensitivity analyses to show that the results were robust to different assumptions about the missing data. The participants who dropped out were simply excluded from the later time-point analyses.

3. 3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The authors state that one patient in the placebo group was excluded at day 14 and two others at day 17 "because of non-compliance with the scheduled treatment programme and self-medication with illicit and/or over-the-counter (OTC) drugs." Loss to follow-up due to seeking other relief directly implies that missingness could be related to participants’ actual health status, specifically the severity of their withdrawal symptoms.

4. 3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: There are multiple indicators confirming this likelihood: (1) There is a clear difference between intervention groups in the proportions of missing outcome data (0% in treatment vs. 15% in placebo). (2) The authors themselves explicitly hypothesize this relationship, stating: "The non-compliance and self-medication in the placebo group may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms." (3) The reported reasons for missing outcome data (using illicit/OTC drugs for relief) are inherently tied to experiencing unmanageable withdrawal symptoms.

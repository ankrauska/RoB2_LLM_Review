Outcome Name: effects of supervision on patient care, measured by a team checklist score

1. 4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: Although the authors explicitly state that they used a "nonvalidated assessment tool" (scenario-specific checklists without assessed construct validity), they note the checklist development process ensured high face and content validity. The instrument has not been demonstrated to have poor validity, nor is it inherently inappropriate for measuring clinical tasks completed.

2. 4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: The outcome was measured using the same method across all groups. Participants were videotaped in the simulation center using a high-fidelity simulator, and two independent raters reviewed and rated the videotaped performances for all participants using the same checklist.

3. 4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The outcome assessors (the independent raters) evaluated the performance by watching the videotapes of the simulation scenarios. Since the intervention itself is the physical proximity of the supervisor (e.g., being directly in the room versus distant/on the phone), the raters would clearly see or hear whether the supervisor was physically present, making them aware of the assigned intervention.

4. 4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: The team checklist score relies on observer judgment to determine if specific clinical tasks were performed by any member of the team. Because this involves human observation and interpretation of actions in a video, the assessors' judgments could theoretically be influenced by seeing a senior supervising fellow in the room versus a resident working alone.

5. 4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: The outcome is a count of specific, predefined clinical tasks on a checklist, which is a relatively objective measure (i.e., whether an action was performed or not). Furthermore, the study used two independent raters who underwent a training and score calibration process, and a third independent assessor was used to resolve any discrepancies by reviewing the video. This rigorous process makes it unlikely that knowledge of the intervention substantially biased the assessment.

6. Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4
   • Response: Some concerns
   • Reason: The outcome assessors were aware of the intervention received due to the visible nature of the intervention on video (4.3 is Yes), and this knowledge could theoretically influence observer-reported checklist scores (4.4 is Probably yes). While rigorous rating methods make it unlikely that the assessment was significantly biased (4.5 is Probably no), the lack of blinding for an observer-reported outcome still raises some concerns according to the RoB 2 framework.

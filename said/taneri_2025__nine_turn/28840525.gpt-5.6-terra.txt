**Outcome Name: T2D remission using four different HbA1c thresholds**

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?**

   - **Response: Yes**
   - **Reason:** The authors report that randomization used “computer-generated random number codes.” They also state that minimization was undertaken based on age, BMI, duration of T2D, insulin treatment, and ethnicity. Minimization generally includes a random component and is considered a random allocation method under RoB 2 guidance.

2. **1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   - **Response: Probably yes**
   - **Reason:** The random number codes were managed by an independent study member and were disclosed only after induction of anaesthesia. This timing means that the participant had already undergone surgery preparation and could not reasonably be enrolled selectively according to the upcoming assignment. The report does not fully describe the mechanism by which the independent member safeguarded the allocation codes before disclosure; therefore, “Probably yes,” rather than “Yes,” is appropriate.

3. **1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   - **Response: No**
   - **Reason:** The allocated group sizes were close to the intended 1:1 ratio (56 assigned to SR-LRYGB and 58 to LSG). Baseline values reported in Table 1—including age, HbA1c, body weight/BMI category, diabetes duration, insulin use, and ethnicity—do not show an important imbalance suggesting a problem with randomization. Although the proportion of females differed numerically (59% SR-LRYGB versus 45% LSG), no pattern of substantial imbalance in key prognostic factors is apparent.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Low**

**Outcome Name: T2D remission using four different HbA1c thresholds**

### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

1. **3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**

   - **Response: Probably no**
   - **Reason:** HbA1c remission data at 12 months were available for 109 of 114 randomized participants (96% overall): 56/56 participants allocated to SR-LRYGB and 53/58 allocated to LSG. However, all five participants with missing 12-month outcome data (8.6% of the LSG group) were in one intervention group. For this dichotomous outcome, five missing outcomes could plausibly affect the estimated between-group difference, particularly because the observed remission proportions were similar between groups. Therefore, the data cannot be considered available for “nearly all” participants in a way that ensures missing outcomes could make no important difference.

2. **3.2. If no/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**

   - **Response: No**
   - **Reason:** The report does not describe an analysis to address missing HbA1c/remission outcome data, such as a sensitivity analysis under plausible missing-data assumptions. The analysis appears to use available 12-month data only (56 SR-LRYGB and 53 LSG participants). No evidence is provided that the findings would be unchanged if the five missing LSG outcomes were accounted for.

3. **3.3. If no/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**

   - **Response: Probably yes**
   - **Reason:** The five LSG participants were lost to follow-up because of “failure to make contact and/or to attend appointments” after 3 months postoperatively. Failure to attend follow-up may be related to postoperative health, weight-loss progress, diabetes control, ongoing medication use, or other factors associated with the true remission outcome. The reasons reported do not establish that missingness was unrelated to HbA1c or diabetes remission.

4. **3.4. If yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**

   - **Response: Probably yes**
   - **Reason:** Missing outcome data occurred exclusively in the LSG group (5/58, 8.6%), whereas no SR-LRYGB participant was lost to follow-up. This differential missingness, combined with the reported reason of non-contact/non-attendance and the absence of analyses addressing its possible impact, makes it likely that the result may be sensitive to an association between missingness and the true remission outcome. The observed between-group differences in remission were small; consequently, the outcomes of five missing LSG participants could materially alter the comparison.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3: High risk of bias.**

**Outcome Name:** changes in levels of 8-iso-prostaglandin F2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**

   - **Response:** No information  
   - **Reason:** The article states that participants “were randomly assigned” first to the SAFA or MUFA background diet and then randomly assigned to fish-oil or placebo capsules. However, it does not report how the random allocation sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, block randomization, stratification, or minimization). Therefore, random sequence generation cannot be verified.

2. **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   - **Response:** No information  
   - **Reason:** No details are reported regarding allocation concealment. In particular, the article does not describe central randomization, an independent allocation service, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or identically packaged and sequentially numbered containers administered after enrolment. Although placebo capsules containing olive oil were used, this does not establish that the allocation sequence was concealed from personnel enrolling participants.

3. **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   - **Response:** No  
   - **Reason:** The four randomized groups were similar in size (SAFA-placebo, *n* = 42; SAFA–n-3, *n* = 41; MUFA-placebo, *n* = 40; MUFA–n-3, *n* = 39), which is consistent with the intended approximately equal allocation. The article reports that baseline clinical characteristics did not differ among groups. Baseline concentrations of the relevant outcomes showed some numerical variation, particularly for 8-iso-PGF2α, but the standard deviations were large and the article provides no evidence that these differences were statistically significant, implausibly large, or indicative of a flawed allocation process. Baseline 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α concentrations were also broadly similar across groups.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Some concerns**

- **Justification:** The study is described as randomized and there is no clear evidence from baseline group sizes or reported baseline characteristics that the randomization process failed. However, the article does not report either the method used to generate the random allocation sequence or how allocation concealment was maintained. These missing details raise some concerns regarding potential bias arising from the randomization process.

Dietary intake records and reported serum phospholipid fatty-acid changes were consistent with implementation of the assigned dietary fat quality and increased EPA/DHA intake in the fish-oil groups. ## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

1. **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**

   - **Response:** Probably no  
   - **Reason:** Data for 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α were analysed using plasma samples from all five study centres. However, 8-iso-PGF2α—the other specified outcome—was analysed only at three centres (Finland, Australia, and Sweden). The authors state that samples from Denmark and Italy “were unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage.” Therefore, 8-iso-PGF2α data were unavailable for all randomized participants at two of five centres, which is clearly more than a small proportion compatible with “nearly all.” The report does not provide the exact number of randomized participants included in the 8-iso-PGF2α analysis.

2. **3.2 If no/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**

   - **Response:** No  
   - **Reason:** The authors do not report a missing-data analysis, sensitivity analysis, or an analysis method specifically intended to assess or correct possible bias from the missing 8-iso-PGF2α measurements. They also do not provide the number of participants with analysable 8-iso-PGF2α data in each randomized group. Thus, there is no direct analytic evidence that the effect estimate was unaffected by missing outcome data.

3. **3.3 If no/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**

   - **Response:** Probably no  
   - **Reason:** The stated reason for unavailable 8-iso-PGF2α data was inappropriate sample handling and/or storage at two entire study centres. This is a technical, specimen-related reason, rather than a reason reported to be related to participants’ 8-iso-PGF2α concentration, health status, response to intervention, withdrawal, or adherence. On the information reported, there is no indication that whether an outcome measurement was unavailable depended on its true value. Although the outcome data are missing for a substantial number of participants, the reported cause of missingness does not suggest outcome-dependent missingness.

4. **3.4 If yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**

   - **Response:** Not applicable  
   - **Reason:** This question is not applicable because missingness was judged **probably not** to depend on the true value of the outcome.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3: **Low risk of bias**

- **Justification:** A substantial proportion of 8-iso-PGF2α data were unavailable because samples from two centres could not be analysed. Nevertheless, the reported reason—centre-level inappropriate sample handling and/or storage—is not indicative of missingness related to the true 8-iso-PGF2α value or to participants’ clinical status. There is no evidence that missingness differed according to outcome values or treatment response. Therefore, despite incomplete outcome availability, the risk of bias specifically due to missing outcome data is judged low.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

1. **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**

   - **Response:** Probably no  
   - **Reason:** Plasma 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α were measured using radioimmunoassays (RIAs) described by the authors as validated. The report provides assay detection limits (23 pmol/L for 8-iso-PGF2α and 45 pmol/L for 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α) and information about antibody cross-reactivity. The observed baseline and follow-up concentrations were above the stated detection limits. Nothing in the report indicates that either assay lacked validity or sensitivity for detecting relevant between-group changes.

2. **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**

   - **Response:** No  
   - **Reason:** Blood samples were collected from all participants under the same protocol—after an overnight fast at baseline and after 3 months—and the biomarkers were measured in plasma using the same RIA methods. The timing and method of measurement were not contingent on the assigned diet or capsule supplementation. Although 8-iso-PGF2α samples from two centres could not be analysed because of sample handling/storage issues, this problem was reported at the centre level and not as differing by randomized intervention group.

3. **4.3 If no/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**

   - **Response:** No information  
   - **Reason:** The article does not state whether laboratory personnel undertaking the RIAs were blinded to participants’ dietary allocation or fish-oil versus placebo allocation.

4. **4.4 If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

   - **Response:** Probably no  
   - **Reason:** Although assessor blinding was not reported, both outcomes were objectively determined biochemical concentrations measured using laboratory RIAs. Their measurement does not involve subjective clinical judgement or participant self-report. The article reports standardized assay procedures, including detection limits and cross-reactivity. Knowledge of allocation would therefore be unlikely to influence the measured analyte concentrations materially.

5. **4.5 If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

   - **Response:** Not applicable  
   - **Reason:** This question is not applicable because knowledge of intervention allocation was judged probably unable to influence objective laboratory measurement of 8-iso-PGF2α or 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: **Low risk of bias**

- **Justification:** The outcomes were measured with reported validated objective laboratory assays at the same scheduled time points across intervention groups. Blinding of laboratory assessors was not reported, but these biochemical measurements are unlikely to be materially influenced by assessor knowledge of intervention assignment.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

1. **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?**

   - **Response:** No information  
   - **Reason:** The article describes the statistical model used and identifies 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α as the main outcome variables. However, it does not report a prospectively registered trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, trial registration number, or other documentation demonstrating that the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. The referenced earlier KANWU publication describes the study design, population, and diet, but the present report provides no evidence of prospective specification of these outcomes and analyses.

2. **5.2 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**

   - **Response:** No information  
   - **Reason:** The authors report one plasma measurement method for each biomarker: 8-iso-PGF2α measured by RIA and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α measured by RIA, with measurements obtained at baseline and after 3 months. The report does not provide evidence that alternative eligible measurement methods, definitions, or time points were used and selectively omitted. However, without a pre-specified protocol or analysis plan, it cannot be confirmed that these were the only outcome measurements planned or available for reporting.

3. **5.3 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?**

   - **Response:** No information  
   - **Reason:** The reported analyses used models including diet allocation, n-3 supplementation allocation, their interaction, study centre, age, sex, and baseline outcome value; the interaction term was removed when it was not statistically significant. The report does not establish whether this modelling approach, covariate set, approach to interaction testing, transformation decisions, or reporting of pooled versus subgroup effects was specified before outcome data were examined. No protocol or statistical analysis plan is available to exclude selection from multiple eligible analyses on the basis of their results.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 5: **Some concerns**

- **Justification:** The article reports the outcomes and statistical methods used, but there is no evidence of a prospectively finalized analysis plan. Consequently, it is not possible to rule out selective reporting from alternative eligible outcome measurements or analyses. There is no direct evidence that selective reporting occurred, so the judgement is **some concerns**, rather than high risk of bias.

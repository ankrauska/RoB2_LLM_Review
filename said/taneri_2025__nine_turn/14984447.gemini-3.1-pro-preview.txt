Outcome Name: SBGM frequency and HbA1c

3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?
* Response: No
* Reason: The article reports a high dropout rate that is not balanced between the groups. Out of the 62 initially randomized patients (31 per group), only 25 remained in the no-cost (NC) group and 16 in the control (C) group at the end of the study. This corresponds to a dropout rate of 19% in the NC group and 48% in the C group, meaning data were not available for nearly all participants.

3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
* Response: No
* Reason: The authors used "last observation carried forward" (LOCF) for the intention-to-treat analysis. According to the evaluation guidelines, LOCF should not be assumed to correct for bias due to missing outcome data. There is no mention of appropriate sensitivity analyses demonstrating that the results are robust under varying assumptions about the missing data.

3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
* Response: Yes
* Reason: It is highly plausible that missingness is related to the true outcome value (SBGM frequency and HbA1c). Patients who could not afford the strips would test less frequently (poorer SBGM frequency) and might have worsening glycemic control (higher HbA1c), which could lead to discouragement and withdrawal from the study. The authors themselves acknowledge: "We cannot exclude the possibility that patients with intrinsically poorer glycaemic control became discouraged and therefore did less testing or dropped out of the study altogether."

3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
* Response: Yes
* Reason: There is a substantial difference between the intervention groups in the proportions of missing outcome data (19% in the NC group vs. 48% in the C group). The financial barrier imposed on the control group directly hindered their ability to test, inherently linking their reason for dropping out (cost/inconvenience) to the primary outcomes being measured (testing frequency and resulting HbA1c).

Domain 3 Risk of Bias Judgement: High
* Reason: There is a high amount of missing outcome data, with a severe imbalance between the two groups (19% vs. 48%). The method used to handle missing data (LOCF) is inadequate to correct for bias, and the missingness is very likely dependent on the true outcome values, as patients with poorer testing adherence and worse glycemic control (driven by the cost of strips) were more likely to drop out.

**Overall predicted direction of bias:** Unpredictable
*Reason:* The primary source of bias is the high and imbalanced attrition rate (Domain 3). In the control group, 48% of participants dropped out, likely because they could not afford the test strips (meaning their true SBGM frequency was very low and their HbA1c was likely worsening). Because the researchers used "Last Observation Carried Forward" (LOCF), early favorable results (perhaps stemming from recent baseline diabetes education) might have been artificially carried forward for these dropouts, which would make the control group look *better* than reality and bias the effect **towards the null**. Conversely, if the dropouts had terrible early values that were carried forward, it might artificially inflate the difference, favoring the experimental group. Because the exact timing and values of the LOCF data interacting with the survivor bias of the remaining highly-motivated control patients are complex, the exact direction of the bias is unpredictable, though it likely underestimates the true magnitude of the intervention.

**Overall Risk-of-bias judgement:** High
*Reason:* According to the Cochrane RoB 2 guidelines, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is assigned if at least one domain is judged to be at high risk. In this evaluation, Domain 3 (Missing outcome data) was rated as High risk due to massive, disproportionate attrition (19% vs. 48%) that was explicitly linked to the trial's context (cost of strips) and likely dependent on the true outcome values, combined with an inadequate imputation method (LOCF). Additionally, there were "Some concerns" in Domain 1 (Randomization process) and Domain 5 (Selection of the reported result).

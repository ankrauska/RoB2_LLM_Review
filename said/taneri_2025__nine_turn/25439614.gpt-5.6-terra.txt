**Outcome Name: visual acuity**

### 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Visual-acuity data at five years were not available for nearly all randomized participants. Excluding deaths, the five-year completion rate was 76% (124/163) in the prompt-laser group and 74% (111/150) in the deferred-laser group. Thus, approximately one quarter of the randomized eligible analysis population had no five-year visual-acuity assessment. This amount of missing outcome data exceeds the level generally considered unlikely to affect a continuous-outcome estimate.

### 3.2. If no/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The authors used longitudinal models including all available visit data, which may use information from participants with incomplete follow-up. However, the article does not report an analysis method specifically designed to correct for potentially informative missingness, nor does it report sensitivity analyses under plausible missing-not-at-random assumptions. The authors explicitly acknowledge that “it is not possible to know whether incomplete data were related to unobserved factors that could have affected the results in either direction.”

### 3.3. If no/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** A substantial proportion of participants did not complete the five-year assessment, primarily because years 4 and 5 were extension phases and some participants chose not to consent to longer follow-up. A participant’s decision to continue or discontinue long-term follow-up could be related to their visual outcome, ongoing DME status, treatment burden, or perceived benefit. In addition, completers and non-completers differed on some baseline characteristics—including age, prior panretinal photocoagulation, lens status, and diabetic-retinopathy severity—that may be associated with visual prognosis. The article does not provide documented reasons demonstrating that missingness was unrelated to visual acuity.

### 3.4. If yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** Although outcome-dependent missingness is plausible, the article does not provide direct evidence that it was likely. Missingness was similar between randomized groups (approximately 24% in the prompt-laser group and 26% in the deferred-laser group, excluding deaths), and no reasons for non-completion that clearly indicate poor or good visual outcomes are reported. The observed baseline differences between completers and non-completers create concern but do not establish that five-year missing visual acuity was likely determined by its unobserved value.

### Domain 3 overall risk-of-bias judgement: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Five-year visual-acuity data were missing for about one quarter of randomized participants in each group. The available-data longitudinal analysis and adjustment for selected measured characteristics do not demonstrate protection against bias from missing data, and no sensitivity analysis addressing informative missingness was reported. Missingness could plausibly depend on true visual-acuity outcomes, although the article does not provide sufficient evidence that this dependence was likely.

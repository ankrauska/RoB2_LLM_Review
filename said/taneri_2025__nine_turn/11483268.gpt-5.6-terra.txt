**1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?**

- **Response:** No information

Therefore, random sequence generation cannot be confirmed.

**1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

- **Response:** No information  
- **Reason:** The article does not report the allocation-concealment method. There is no description of central randomization, telephone/internet allocation, pharmacy-controlled allocation, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes. Consequently, it is not possible to determine whether staff enrolling participants could have known or predicted the upcoming assignment.

**1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The randomized group sizes were nearly equal (64 assigned to Vaqta™ and 63 to Avaxim™), which is compatible with the apparent intended 1:1 allocation. Reported demographic characteristics were similar between groups: mean age was 38.4 versus 38.2 years; 55% versus 52% were male; and mean weight and height were also similar.  
  Pre-booster anti-HAV GMTs were numerically different (496 mIU/mL in the Vaqta™ group and 325 mIU/mL in the Avaxim™ group), but the reported comparison was not statistically significant (*p* = 0.171), and the article does not present evidence that this difference was implausible due to chance or sufficiently large to indicate a failure of randomization. Thus, the reported baseline data do not suggest a problem with the randomization process.

### Domain 1 risk-of-bias judgement: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Although no important baseline imbalances suggesting flawed randomization were apparent, the methods for both random sequence generation and allocation concealment were not reported. Therefore, there are some concerns about bias arising from the randomization process.

- **Response:** Probably no

However, the distribution of exclusions by reason and their antibody results are not fully reported.

### Domain 2 risk-of-bias judgement: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Participants and personnel were aware of assigned vaccine because the study was open-label. No important deviations from the assigned one-dose vaccine intervention arising from the trial context were reported or strongly indicated. Although the number excluded is relatively small and a substantial effect on the result is not strongly indicated, reporting only the per-protocol analysis creates some concerns for estimation of the effect of assignment to intervention.

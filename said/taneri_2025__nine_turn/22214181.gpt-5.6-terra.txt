**Outcome Name: List of attributions task (LAT)**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** The report explicitly states that “a Microsoft Excel random number generator was used to randomize participants into the two conditions.” A computer-generated random-number process includes a random component and meets the criterion for a random allocation sequence.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response:** No information  
- **Reason:** Although the authors describe use of an Excel random-number generator, they do not report who generated or retained the allocation sequence, whether the investigator enrolling participants had access to upcoming allocations, or any concealment procedure (e.g., centralized assignment, sealed opaque envelopes, or independent allocation). Therefore, concealment before enrolment/assignment cannot be established.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The groups were equal in size (44 participants per group). The authors report no statistically significant pre-intervention differences between the fatigue-information and control groups in insomnia, fatigue, depressive symptoms, dysfunctional sleep beliefs, or the LAT outcome measures. They also report no significant differences in age or ethnicity and equal numbers of men and women in each group. No reported baseline imbalance suggests a problem with randomization.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** The allocation sequence was random, and the reported baseline data do not indicate a randomization problem. However, allocation concealment was not described. Since there is insufficient information to determine whether enrolment or assignment could have been influenced by knowledge of forthcoming allocation, the domain has **some concerns**.

**Outcome Name: List of attributions task (LAT)**

### 4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** The LAT was specifically developed to measure participants’ self-generated attributions for daytime fatigue, including whether attributions were sleep-related or non-sleep-related, their perceived frequency, and their perceived importance. This corresponds directly to the construct targeted by the intervention. Although the article does not report formal validation or reliability testing of the LAT, it does not provide evidence that the measure has poor validity or is insensitive to plausible changes in fatigue attributions.

### 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Both groups completed the same LAT before and after the intervention, during the same one-hour laboratory session. The reported procedure, instructions, scoring approach, and timing of assessment were the same for the fatigue-information and control groups. The intervention materials differed, but the method used to collect the LAT outcome did not.

### 4.3. If No/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** The LAT is a participant-reported outcome; therefore, the participants themselves were the outcome assessors. Participants were aware of the information they had received: either information about multiple non-sleep-related causes of fatigue or generic sleep-related information. In addition, the study investigator who administered the intervention was aware of intervention assignment. Although coding of the written responses was undertaken initially by a volunteer blind to the study hypotheses, participant awareness is the relevant issue for this self-reported outcome.

### 4.4. If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** The intervention directly presented the fatigue-information group with alternative, non-sleep explanations for fatigue, after which participants immediately completed the LAT again. Knowledge of receiving this information could influence which fatigue attributions participants generated, ranked, and assigned importance to. The outcome is subjective and self-reported, rather than an objective measure independent of participants’ expectations or understanding of the intervention material.

### 4.5. If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** It is likely that knowledge of the assigned intervention influenced post-intervention LAT responses because the intervention content directly supplied examples of non-sleep-related fatigue attributions, and the primary outcome immediately asked participants to list, rank, and rate such attributions. The authors themselves acknowledge that demand characteristics or priming may have influenced fatigue-information participants’ post-intervention LAT responses. The outcome assessment occurred immediately after the information session, increasing the likelihood that participants’ answers reflected the intervention content and/or perceived expected response.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: **High**

- **Reason:** The outcome was participant-reported, participants were aware of their assigned information condition, and the intervention directly targeted the precise content assessed by the LAT immediately afterwards. The authors acknowledge possible priming and demand characteristics. Consequently, it is likely that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced outcome assessment.

### Overall predicted direction of bias

- **Response:** Favours experimental  
- **Reason:** Participants in the fatigue-information intervention received explicit information about non-sleep-related explanations for fatigue immediately before completing the post-intervention LAT. Because participants knew their intervention and the LAT directly asked them to generate, rank, and assign importance to fatigue attributions, post-intervention reporting could plausibly shift toward the intervention-consistent response—fewer sleep-related and more non-sleep-related attributions. This would tend to exaggerate the apparent benefit of the fatigue-information intervention relative to the sleep-information control.

### Overall Risk-of-bias judgement

- **Response:** **High**

Under RoB 2, high risk of bias in at least one domain results in an overall judgement of **high risk of bias**. Domain 1, Domain 2, and Domain 5 also had some concerns, principally because allocation concealment, trial-context deviations, and prospective analysis pre-specification were not adequately reported.

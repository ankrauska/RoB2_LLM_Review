I've carefully read the article by Nickel et al. on the double-blind, placebo-controlled study examining topiramate's influence on olanzapine-related weight gain in women. Here's my understanding of the key elements for a systematic review:

**Study Design & Key Characteristics:**
- RCT, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 10 weeks (March 2003–June 2004)
- N=61 recruited → 55 eligible → 43 completed (25 topiramate, 18 placebo); all 6 dropouts were from placebo group
- Population: Women ≥18 years on olanzapine ≥3 months with ≥5kg weight gain
- Topiramate titrated from 50mg to 250mg/day by week 5

**Notable Methodological Points I Flagged:**
1. **Randomization imbalance**: Randomized 25 topiramate/24 placebo, but 6 placebo dropouts (25% attrition in placebo arm, 0% in topiramate) — potential attrition bias, not analyzed as ITT
2. **Sample size**: Power analysis specified 49 needed, but only 43 completed — underpowered per their own criteria
3. **Population homogeneity**: All female, moderately ill outpatients only — limits generalizability (explicitly acknowledged by authors)
4. **Short duration**: Only 10 weeks — long-term efficacy/safety unknown
5. **Concomitant medications**: Multiple different psychotropic add-ons varied between arms (not standardized), though authors state doses stayed constant during trial
6. **No blood/metabolic biomarkers**: Reliance on self-report questionnaires (SF-36, EWL-60-S, Bf-S) and weight alone — no HbA1c, lipids, waist circumference
6b. **Multiple comparisons**: Many outcomes tested (8 SF-36 subscales, 6 EWL-60-S subscales, Bf-S) without apparent correction for multiplicity
7. **Funding/COI**: Stated as "not funded" — no explicit conflict of interest statement regarding topiramate manufacturer
8. **Statistical approach**: Non-parametric tests (Mann-Whitney U) used throughout; correlations (Spearman) reported for dose-weight relationships

**Efficacy Findings:**
- Weight loss difference: 5.6 kg favoring topiramate (95% CI −8.5 to −3.0, p<0.001)
- Significant improvement on 7/8 SF-36 domains (all except ROEM/role-emotional)
- Significant improvement on all 6 EWL-60-S domains
- Significant improvement on Bf-S
- Correlations: olanzapine dose vs weight gain (R²=0.56); weight loss vs olanzapine-induced weight gain (R²=0.79); weight loss vs Bf-S change (R²=0.46)

**Safety:**
- Well tolerated; only isolated dizziness, headache, paresthesia reported
- No psychotic exacerbation or suicidality noted

If these non-responding placebo participants had remained in the study and been included in the analysis, their continued weight gain/lack of improvement would likely have *worsened* the average outcome for the placebo group, thereby *increasing* the true between-group difference favoring topiramate.

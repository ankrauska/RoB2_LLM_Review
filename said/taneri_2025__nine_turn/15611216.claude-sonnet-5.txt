Groups with more extreme/worse baseline values on a fluctuating measure are statistically more prone to regression to the mean, which would produce an apparent "improvement" independent of any true treatment effect — inflating the observed benefit in the experimental group.

2. **Domain 2 (Deviations):** The 10 participants excluded for "not being given the intervention" (due to staff being "busy" or having "more emergent needs") were removed only from the treatment-assignment pathway. If these harder-to-manage/higher-acuity participants had been retained and analyzed as randomized, they may have diluted or reduced the observed treatment effect. Their exclusion likely inflates the apparent benefit of BACE.

3. **Domain 3 (Missing data):** Similarly, attrition (12 deaths, 10 non-receipt dropouts) disproportionately removes participants from analysis in ways structurally tied to the treatment arm's implementation challenges, plausibly removing participants less likely to show improvement and thus inflating the treatment effect estimate.

4. **Domain 4 (Measurement):** Low risk — no directional concern contributed here, as blinding of assessors was robust.

5. **Domain 5 (Selective reporting):** Uncertain pre-specification of the covariate-adjustment model (added post hoc to address the baseline imbalance) introduces some indeterminate risk, but does not clearly counteract the other domains' consistent direction.

Taken together, the identified sources of bias converge in a direction that would tend to **exaggerate the beneficial effect of the BACE intervention** relative to the control condition, rather than pushing the result toward the null or in an unpredictable direction.

**Justification:** Per the Cochrane RoB 2 algorithm, if any single domain is judged "High risk," the overall study-level (outcome-level) judgement must be **High risk of bias**, regardless of the judgements in other domains.

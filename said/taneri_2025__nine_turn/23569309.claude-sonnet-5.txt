### Justification for Overall Judgement
Per the RoB 2 algorithm, an outcome is rated **High risk of bias** overall if any single domain is rated High. Here, Domains 1, 2, 3, and 5 were each judged **Low risk of bias**: randomization was adequately generated and concealed with no baseline imbalance of concern; deviations from intervention (physician discretion discontinuations, withdrawals) were consistent with routine clinical practice rather than trial-context artifacts, and an appropriate ITT analysis was used; outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants with balanced, low withdrawal rates; and there is no evidence of selective outcome or analysis reporting relative to the registered protocol.

Because investigators were aware of both treatment assignment and the study hypothesis, subjective determinations of progression timing are susceptible to systematic influence, a well-documented issue in open-label oncology trials using investigator-assessed PFS.

### Justification for Direction of Bias
The lack of blinded, independent outcome adjudication in an open-label design in which investigators had a vested interest in demonstrating the benefit of maintenance chemotherapy would most plausibly result in earlier/more liberal declarations of progression in the observation arm and/or delayed declarations of progression in the maintenance arm, thereby inflating the apparent PFS benefit of the experimental (maintenance) intervention. The predicted direction of bias is therefore **towards favouring the experimental arm**.

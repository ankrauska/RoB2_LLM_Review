**Outcome Name: treatment retention**  
**Effect of interest: effect of assignment to intervention**

### 2.1. Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** The three assigned conditions were materially different and could not realistically be blinded: routine clinic care involved once- or twice-weekly medication reporting, whereas the MMM groups had a 28-day reporting schedule, either in a clinic or a physician’s office. Participants necessarily knew their reporting frequency and treatment setting.

### 2.2. Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** Treatment staff necessarily knew which intervention participants received because they delivered distinct care arrangements. Routine-care participants continued with clinic counselors and more frequent medication pickup, while clinic-based MMM participants saw clinic physicians or senior nurses monthly and office-based MMM participants saw a physician or nurse practitioner in an office setting.

### 2.3. If yes/probably yes to 2.1 or 2.2: Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** Five of 78 randomized participants withdrew immediately after assignment to a study condition and did not enter the analyzed “intent-to-treat” sample of 73. These exclusions occurred after randomized assignment and were markedly uneven across groups: four participants withdrew after assignment to routine care and one after assignment to office-based MMM; none withdrew after assignment to clinic-based MMM. The timing (“withdrew from the study upon assignment to study condition”) and unequal distribution indicate that assignment within the trial context likely contributed to these departures from the assigned intervention.

### 2.4. If yes/probably yes to 2.3: Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** The outcome is treatment retention. Participants who withdrew immediately after allocation did not receive or continue in their assigned randomized treatment condition and were excluded from the reported retention analysis. Excluding post-randomization withdrawals directly affects estimated retention rates.

### 2.5. If yes/probably yes to 2.4: Were these deviations from intended intervention balanced between groups?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The post-randomization withdrawals were not balanced: four occurred in the routine-care group, one in the office-based MMM group, and none in the clinic-based MMM group. The differential exclusions could inflate reported retention particularly for routine care, because four participants assigned to that group were removed before the retention analysis.

### 2.6. Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Although the authors describe analyses as using an “intent-to-treat sample (n=73),” 78 participants were originally randomized. Five participants who withdrew after assignment were excluded, leaving 73 participants for the reported retention analysis. Thus, the analysis did not include all randomized participants in their originally assigned groups and was not a true intention-to-treat analysis for treatment retention.

### 2.7. If no/probably no to 2.6: Was there potential for a substantial impact on the result of the failure to analyse participants in the group to which they were randomized?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** Five of 78 randomized participants (approximately 6%) were excluded after randomization, and exclusions were strongly imbalanced toward routine care (four of the five exclusions). Because retention was high among analyzed participants (89%–92%), counting these post-randomization withdrawals as non-retained could meaningfully change the retention estimates and the between-group comparison.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 2: **High**

- **Reason:** The retention analysis excluded five participants after randomization, rather than analyzing all 78 randomized participants according to assigned group. These exclusions were unevenly distributed across intervention groups, with four exclusions from routine care, and directly concern the outcome of treatment retention.

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Treatment retention was measured as whether participants remained in their randomized treatment arm through the 6-month follow-up. This is an appropriate and objective method for assessing retention. The study reports the numbers completing 6 months and describes dropout reasons.

### 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Retention was ascertained over the same 6-month follow-up period in all three randomized groups. All groups were part of the same trial and treatment system, and the article does not report use of different retention definitions, measurement procedures, thresholds, or follow-up schedules for the groups.

### 4.3. Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The article does not explicitly identify the person who assessed or recorded retention. However, the interventions differed visibly in treatment setting, medication reporting schedule, and treating personnel. Clinic and office staff delivering the interventions would likely have known participants’ assigned treatment conditions, and no blinding of study staff is reported.

### 4.4. Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Treatment retention/completion is a largely objective outcome: participants either remained in the randomized treatment arm for 6 months or did not. Although staff likely knew treatment assignment, this knowledge would not ordinarily affect ascertainment of whether a participant completed the specified follow-up period.

### 4.5. Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response:** Not applicable  
- **Reason:** Knowledge of treatment assignment was not considered capable of influencing assessment of this objective outcome.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: **Low**

- **Reason:** Retention was measured objectively and over an equivalent 6-month period in each intervention group. While outcome-recording personnel were probably aware of treatment assignment, there is no indication that this could influence determination of whether participants remained in the randomized treatment condition.

**Outcome Name: treatment retention**

### Overall predicted direction of bias

- **Response:** Favours comparator  
- **Reason:** Four participants assigned to routine care withdrew immediately after allocation and were excluded from the reported retention analysis, compared with one excluded participant assigned to office-based MMM and none assigned to clinic-based MMM. Excluding these post-randomization withdrawals makes reported retention in the routine-care comparator group appear higher than it would have been if all randomized participants had been included. This differential exclusion is therefore likely to bias comparisons in favour of routine care relative to MMM.

### Overall Risk-of-bias judgement

- **Response:** High  
- **Reason:** The study has a high risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions for the effect of assignment to intervention (Domain 2). Although 78 participants were randomized, five participants withdrew after allocation and were excluded from the authors’ stated “intent-to-treat” analysis of 73 participants. These exclusions were unequally distributed across groups, predominantly affecting routine care (4/5 exclusions), and directly affect the retention outcome. In addition, there are some concerns regarding the randomization process and selective reporting because sequence generation, allocation concealment, and a prospectively finalized analysis plan were not reported.

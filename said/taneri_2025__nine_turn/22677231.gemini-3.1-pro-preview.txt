Based on the provided article and clinical trial protocol, here is an overview of the key elements of the systematic review data for this study:

### **Study Overview**
- **Title:** A randomized controlled trial of internet-based therapy in depression
- **Authors:** Steffen Moritz et al. (2012)
- **ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier:** NCT01401296
- **Aim:** To assess the efficacy of an online self-help program for depression (Deprexis) without therapist guidance as a stand-alone intervention.

### **Methodology**
- **Design:** Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT) with a parallel group design (Immediate treatment vs. Wait-list control).
- **Participants (n):** 210 participants randomized (105 to Wait-list, 105 to Deprexis).
- **Inclusion Criteria:** Over 18 and under 65 years old, registration on moderated internet depression forums, and absence of acute suicidal ideas/severe psychoses. 
- **Intervention:** *Deprexis*, a 10-module web-based cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) program (including psychoeducation, behavioral activation, cognitive modification, mindfulness, etc.) accessible over 3 months. No therapist guidance was provided.
- **Control:** Wait-list control group (delayed treatment after 8 weeks).
- **Primary Outcome:** Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) score at 8 weeks.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** WHO Quality of Life scale (WHOQOL-BREF), Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale (RSE), Dysfunctional Attitudes Scale (DAS), Suicide Behaviors Questionnaire-Revised (SBQ-R).

### **Key Findings**
- **Completion Rate:** High completion rate of 81.9% at the 8-week post-assessment.
- **Primary Outcome (BDI):** 
  - Both groups improved over time, but the **Deprexis group showed a significantly greater reduction in depressive symptoms** compared to the wait-list group.
  - The Intention-to-Treat (ITT) between-group effect size for the BDI total score was **d = 0.36** (small to medium effect). 
  - 24% of the treatment group showed at least a 50% symptom decline on the BDI, compared to 10% in the wait-list group.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Significant between-group improvements favoring Deprexis were seen in dysfunctional attitudes (DAS; d=0.54), self-esteem (RSE; d=0.38), and quality of life (WHOQOL-BREF; d=0.40). Suicidality (SBQ-R) did not show a significant between-group difference.
- **Moderator Effects:** The intervention was particularly effective for participants with **moderate baseline symptoms** (d = 0.70) and those not concurrently receiving face-to-face therapy (d = 0.56).

### **Conclusion**
The online, unguided self-help program *Deprexis* is an effective, feasible intervention for reducing depressive symptoms and improving quality of life and self-esteem. It is particularly beneficial for moderately depressed individuals and those not currently receiving traditional psychotherapy, making it a viable tool to bridge the treatment gap.

**Outcome Name:** Beck Depression Inventory (total score) post intervention

**Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data**

1. 3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: The overall completion rate at post-assessment was 81.9% (Table 1 shows 85.7% for the wait-list group and 78.1% for the Deprexis group). Therefore, approximately 18% of the data was missing, which does not meet the typical threshold of "nearly all" (e.g., ~95% for continuous outcomes). 

2. 3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: While the authors used multiple imputation ("ITT analyses were performed with multiple imputation using SAS PROC MI... stratified for treatment"), there is no report of sensitivity analyses examining how robust the findings are under different plausible assumptions regarding the missing data (e.g., testing if the missingness was Not At Random).

3. 3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: The study evaluates depression symptoms. In this clinical context, loss to follow-up or failure to complete the post-assessment could easily be related to the participants' health status (e.g., individuals may drop out because their depressive symptoms worsened, making them too exhausted to complete the survey, or alternatively, because they fully recovered and lost interest in the study).

4. 3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: Although it is possible (as answered in 3.3), the study provides evidence suggesting it is not highly likely to have caused substantial bias. The authors explicitly analyzed missingness and reported that "Participants who did not complete the trial were indistinguishable from non-completers on any of the background, medical or baseline psychopathological and psychological variables" except for two minor Quality of Life subscales. Additionally, the attrition rates between the two groups (14.3% in wait-list vs. 21.9% in Deprexis) were not significantly different (χ2(1) = 2.06, p > .1).

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3:** Some concerns
(Data were not available for nearly all participants, and although baseline checks were performed and multiple imputation was used, there were no explicit sensitivity analyses to definitively rule out bias from missing data mechanisms related to depression severity.)

**Outcome Name:** Beck Depression Inventory (total score) post intervention

**Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome**

1. 4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: The Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) is a widely used, standard, and validated self-report instrument for measuring the severity of depressive symptoms. The authors also reported good re-test reliability (r = .72) and internal consistency in their study.

2. 4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: Both the treatment and wait-list control groups completed the exact same online post-intervention survey using the same software platform (OPST) at the exact same time point (8 weeks after baseline).

3. 4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: Because the BDI is a participant-reported outcome, the participants themselves are the outcome assessors. As established earlier, participants were not blinded and were fully aware of whether they were receiving the Deprexis intervention immediately or waiting 8 weeks.

4. 4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: The BDI is a subjective measure of depressive symptoms. Knowledge of receiving an active treatment versus being placed on a wait-list can easily influence a participant's perception and reporting of their own symptoms due to expectancy effects or disappointment.

5. 4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: In unblinded psychological trials comparing an active intervention to a wait-list control, self-reported subjective outcomes (like depression severity) are highly susceptible to performance and reporting biases. Participants in the treatment group may over-report improvements (expectancy/placebo effect), while wait-list participants may under-report improvements, making it highly likely that the lack of blinding influenced the outcome assessment.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4:** High 
(The outcome is subjective and self-reported by unblinded participants, which inherently introduces a high risk of measurement bias in a wait-list controlled trial).

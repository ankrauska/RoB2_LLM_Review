Outcome Name: Agatston score

1. 4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: The outcome was measured using a standard, well-established threshold for coronary artery calcification (presence of more than two contiguous pixels with Hounsfield units greater than 130) evaluated on a dedicated PC-based workstation. This is the highly appropriate and standard method for computing the Agatston score.

2. 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: Both groups (A and B) had their datasets transferred to the same workstation (Syngo CaScoring Wizard) and the outcomes were assessed by the exact same personnel (a radiologist and a cardiologist), ensuring comparable measurement methods.

3. 4.3. If No/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?
   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: The article does not state that the outcome assessors (the radiologist and cardiologist) were blinded to the patient's assigned group. In standard CT imaging, DICOM metadata readily displays acquisition parameters like collimation, making it highly likely they were aware of the intervention.

4. 4.4. If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: Although the workstation automatically identified potential lesions, the methodology states that the readers manually "differentiated between calcium and noise by placing regions of interest around the CAC." This manual step involves subjective judgment, which theoretically could be influenced by knowing the imaging protocol used.

5. 4.5. If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: While there is a manual component, the baseline identification relies on a strict, objective, machine-driven threshold (>130 HU and >2 contiguous pixels). Differentiating noise from calcium is a standard radiological task, and there is no strong reason to believe the assessors would systematically alter their ROI placement simply based on knowledge of the collimation setting.

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: Some concerns
Reason: The outcome assessors were likely not blinded to the intervention groups, and the assessment involved a subjective manual step (differentiating noise from calcium). While it is not highly likely that this drastically changed the results, the lack of blinding in an outcome that involves manual reader judgment warrants at least "Some concerns" for measurement bias.

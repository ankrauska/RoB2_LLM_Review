**Outcome Name: survival time**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** The article states that patients were “randomly assigned (ratio 1:1)” to doxycycline or placebo. However, it does not describe how the allocation sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, block randomization, or another random method). The protocol confirms that the trial was randomized but likewise provides no sequence-generation method.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The article reports that “the randomisation list and the study medication were administered by the Charité, Universitätsmedizin, Berlin, Germany,” whereas the trial was conducted at the University of Göttingen. This suggests that allocation and study medication were managed externally to the enrolling study site. In addition, the trial was described as double-blind and placebo-controlled, with placebo supplied as oral hard capsules. However, the report does not explicitly state the allocation-concealment procedure, such as central randomization, sequentially numbered identical drug containers, or sealed opaque envelopes. Therefore, concealment is probable but not definitively documented.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** Baseline characteristics are presented for the analysed groups—7 doxycycline participants and 5 placebo participants—rather than clearly for all 13 randomized participants. One participant randomized to placebo was excluded from the reported survival analysis after dropping out. Thus, the available baseline table does not clearly permit assessment of balance across all randomized participants.  
  Among the analysed participants, there were notable differences: median age at onset was 58 years in the doxycycline group versus 72 years in the placebo group; median MMSE was 21 versus 16; and median time from onset to treatment/placebo was 95 versus 152 days. These differences could be important prognostic imbalances, but the extremely small sample and absence of baseline data for the placebo participant who dropped out prevent a reliable determination of whether they indicate a failure of randomization rather than chance.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

- **Judgement: Some concerns**
- **Reason:** The study is described as randomized and allocation concealment is likely because the randomization list and study medication were administered by an external institution. Nevertheless, the method used to generate the random sequence was not reported. In addition, baseline balance cannot be adequately assessed for all randomized participants because reported baseline data appear limited to the analysed sample after one placebo-group dropout.

**Outcome Name: survival time**

### 5.1. Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** A prospectively registered protocol is available (EudraCT 2006-003934-14), which was entered in the database in 2009, before recruitment began in March 2010. The protocol specifies the primary endpoint as patients’ survival time **from onset of symptoms until death**. However, the article defines survival in the randomized trial differently: follow-up “starts with the randomisation (first application of study medication)” and ends at death or study end. The reported Cox analysis and median survival comparison for the randomized trial therefore use survival from randomization/treatment initiation, rather than the protocol-specified survival time from symptom onset. No statistical analysis plan explaining or prospectively documenting this change was reported.

### 5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The protocol pre-specified survival from symptom onset as the primary outcome, while the publication reports the randomized-trial survival analysis from randomization/first study-medication administration. Both are possible measures of the survival-time domain, but they answer different questions and can produce different treatment-effect estimates, particularly because time from disease onset to treatment differed between the randomized groups (median 95 days in the doxycycline group and 152 days in the placebo group). The article does not report the protocol-specified randomized comparison of survival from symptom onset, nor does it provide a rationale for changing the survival time origin. This creates concern that the reported survival measurement was selected from more than one eligible outcome definition.

### 5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** The article reports a Cox proportional-hazards analysis for the randomized trial, but neither the publication nor the available trial-registry information provides a detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan. Therefore, it is not possible to determine whether other eligible survival analyses—such as analyses using survival from symptom onset, adjusted Cox models, alternative censoring approaches, or analyses including all randomized participants—were conducted and selectively not reported.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

- **Judgement: High**
- **Reason:** The registered protocol identifies survival from symptom onset as the primary endpoint, whereas the published randomized-trial analysis uses survival from randomization/first treatment administration. The protocol-specified randomized comparison based on symptom-onset survival was not reported, and no pre-specified justification for the different analysis is provided. This discrepancy creates a high risk that the reported survival result was selectively chosen from multiple eligible outcome definitions.

Outcome Name: visual acuity

1. 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?
   * Response: No
   * Reason: The article states that, excluding deaths, the 5-year completion rate was 76% in the prompt laser group (124 completers, 56 non-completers) and 74% in the deferred laser group (111 completers, 70 non-completers). Missing approximately 25% of the continuous outcome data at 5 years means data were not available for "nearly all" participants.

2. 3.2. Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
   * Response: Probably no
   * Reason: While the authors state they used longitudinal models to adjust for observed baseline differences between completers and non-completers, they explicitly acknowledge that "it is not possible to know whether incomplete data were related to unobserved factors that could have affected the results in either direction." There are no robust sensitivity analyses reported that simulate missing data under a range of plausible assumptions to prove the results were unaffected.

3. 3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
   * Response: Probably yes
   * Reason: Missingness was largely driven by patients choosing not to consent to the 2-year extension phase. It is plausible that a participant's decision to continue or drop out of a 5-year eye injection trial could be influenced by their visual acuity outcomes (e.g., dropping out due to frustration over lack of improvement or satisfaction with early gains).

4. 3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
   * Response: Probably no
   * Reason: The proportions of missing data were highly balanced between the two groups (24% vs 26% dropout). The primary stated reason for missing data (choosing not to re-consent for the trial extension) applied equally to both groups. Additionally, the authors adjusted the analyses for known baseline differences between completers and non-completers. Therefore, while it *could* depend on the true value, there is no strong evidence making it *likely* that missingness was driven differently or specifically by the outcome's true value across the groups.

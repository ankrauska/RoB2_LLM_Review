Based on the article provided, here is a summary of the systematic review task you requested:

**Study Aim**
The study aimed to determine whether Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy (CIMT) is more effective than Bimanual Training (BIM) for improving upper limb activity outcomes in children with congenital hemiplegia.

**Methodology**
*   **Design:** A single-blind, matched-pairs randomized clinical trial (INCITE study).
*   **Participants:** 63 children (mean age 10.2 years, SD 2.7, range 5–16 years) with congenital hemiplegia. Children were matched in pairs by age, sex, side of hemiplegia, and baseline function.
*   **Intervention:** Children were randomized into either a CIMT group or a BIM training group. Interventions were delivered in a day camp setting using a circus theme (60 hours over 10 days, 6 hours/day).
    *   *CIMT:* Wore a tailor-made glove on the unimpaired limb to force the use of the hemiplegic limb.
    *   *BIM:* Intensive bimanual training using explicit instructions on using both hands.
*   **Outcome Measures:** 
    *   *Primary:* Unimanual capacity measured by the Melbourne Assessment of Unilateral Upper Limb Function (MUUL) and bimanual coordination measured by the Assisting Hand Assessment (AHA).
    *   *Secondary:* Impairment measures (grip strength, two-point discrimination, stereognosis) and movement efficiency measured by the Jebsen Taylor Test of Hand Function (JTTHF).
*   **Assessments:** Conducted at baseline, 3 weeks, and 26 weeks post-intervention by blinded raters.

**Key Results**
*   **Baseline:** No significant baseline differences between the two groups.
*   **Unimanual Capacity (MUUL):** CIMT showed significantly superior outcomes compared to BIM for unimanual capacity at 26 weeks (Estimated Mean Difference [EMD] 4.4, p<0.001). CIMT showed immediate gains at 3 weeks that continued to improve at 26 weeks. BIM showed no significant change on the MUUL.
*   **Bimanual Performance (AHA):** Both groups demonstrated significant improvements in bimanual performance at 3 weeks. However, the gains were maintained by the BIM group at 26 weeks (EMD 2.3, p=0.008), whereas the CIMT group did not retain their initial significant gains.
*   **Movement Efficiency (JTTHF):** No significant between-group differences, but both groups showed improved movement efficiency of the impaired limb by 26 weeks.
*   **Secondary Impairments:** No significant changes in sensation or grip strength were found in either group. No adverse effects were found on the constrained (unimpaired) limb in the CIMT group.

**Conclusions & Interpretations**
Overall differences between the two training approaches were relatively small and reflected the "specificity of practice" (motor learning theory):
*   CIMT is more effective for improving *unimanual capacity* (quality and efficiency of movement of the impaired limb).
*   BIM training is more effective for sustaining improvements in *bimanual performance*.
*   The study demonstrated considerable inter-individual variation in response to both interventions.
*   The authors suggest that future research should explore a sequential approach—unimanual training (CIMT) followed by bimanual training (BIM)—to optimize outcomes.

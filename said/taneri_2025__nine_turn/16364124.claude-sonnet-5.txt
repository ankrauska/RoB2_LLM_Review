# Domain 3: Risk of Bias Due to Missing Outcome Data

**Outcome Name: visual assessment for erythema and dryness as key symptoms**

## 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** 35 participants were randomized (ITT population), but the article states: "3 subjects were excluded during the study due to protocol violations (intake of antiphlogistics for 1 panellist and disinfection of fossa cubitalis for 2 panellists) resulting in 32 subjects who finished the study per protocol." The authors state "missing values in 3 participants were replaced by the method of Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF)" to generate the ITT dataset of 35. Per RoB 2 guidance, imputed data (via LOCF) should be regarded as missing data, not actual observed outcome data. Thus, 3/35 (8.6%) of participants have genuinely missing (non-imputed) outcome data. While this is a relatively small proportion, it falls slightly below the commonly cited ~95% threshold for continuous outcomes, and the reasons for missingness (drug intake, disinfection of the test site) are directly related to the intervention/skin condition being assessed, raising some concern.

## 3.2. Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The only method used to handle missing data was LOCF imputation. There is no mention of any sensitivity analysis, multiple imputation accounting for missingness mechanisms, or other bias-correcting analytic method. As explicitly stated in the RoB 2 guidance, "LOCF... should not be assumed to correct for bias due to missing outcome data." Therefore, there is no evidence presented that the result was unaffected by the missing outcome data.

## 3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The documented reasons for exclusion/missingness are: (1) "intake of antiphlogistics" (anti-inflammatory/anti-itch medication) by 1 participant — plausibly related to experiencing skin irritation or discomfort, which is directly relevant to the erythema/dryness outcome being measured; and (2) "disinfection of fossa cubitalis" by 2 participants — a direct interference with the test site that could plausibly be driven by participants noticing irritation, itching, or discomfort at the site (i.e., related to the true value of the skin outcome) and taking independent action. These are not clearly unrelated, incidental reasons (e.g., equipment failure); they involve participant behavior at or near the test site that could plausibly be linked to the true skin condition.

## 3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

- **Response: Probably no** (i.e., "Some concerns" rather than "High risk")
- **Reason:** While the reasons for missingness are plausibly related to the outcome (as noted in 3.3), several mitigating factors are present: (1) the number of affected participants is very small (3/35, or 8.6%); (2) this is a **within-subject, paired design** where each participant received both interventions simultaneously on opposite cubital fossae — protocol violations such as antiphlogistic intake (systemic) or disinfection of the fossa cubitalis would likely have affected assessment of **both** treatment sites similarly for that participant, rather than differentially biasing the comparison between the two hand rub formulations; (3) there is no reported evidence of a differential rate or pattern of missingness between the emollient and non-emollient arms specifically (since missingness occurred at the participant level, not the intervention-arm level, in this paired design). This reduces (though does not eliminate) the likelihood that missingness differentially biased the *comparative* effect estimate between the two formulations, even though it may affect the precision/validity of the overall pooled data to a modest degree.

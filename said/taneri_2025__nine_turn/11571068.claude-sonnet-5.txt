- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** While knowledge of intervention *could* have influenced responses (per 4.4), there is no strong evidence in the article suggesting a strong directional belief among participants that any one treatment was superior, nor evidence of overt hype or expectation-inducing framing of one condition over another that would bias responding. The treatments were presented as legitimate, credible clinical options, and the study's own results showed no main effect of condition on RSS or GHQ (only a trend-level interaction), which is not strongly suggestive of pronounced expectancy-driven bias overriding a true clinical effect.

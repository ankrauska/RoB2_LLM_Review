**1. Primary Outcomes Match:**
- **Protocol Primary Outcomes:** 1) Neuropsychological test performance, 2) Diagnosis of dementia, 3) Weight loss.
- **Article Primary Outcome:** The article states the "main outcome" as neuropsychological tests. It also assesses weight loss (BMI decrease). However, the article does not report on the "Diagnosis of dementia" as an outcome measure, despite it being listed in the protocol. 

**2. Masking/Blinding:**
- **Protocol:** Specifies masking is "SINGLE" and applies to the "OUTCOMES_ASSESSOR".
- **Article:** The article mentions random allocation (1:1, randomized study), but does not explicitly state anywhere in the text that the outcomes assessors (e.g., those administering the neuropsychological tests) were blinded to the intervention groups. 

**3. Analytic Approach / Adherence to Protocol:**
- The article mentions that because both groups lost similar amounts of weight, the researchers pooled the longitudinal analysis between groups *a posteriori* to test the effects of BMI change as a continuous variable. While scientifically explained, this represents a deviation from the original parallel-group randomized controlled trial design specified in the protocol.

**4. Sample Size and Attrition:**
- **Enrollment:** 80 participants randomized (40 per arm), which perfectly matches the actual enrollment count in the protocol.
- **Attrition:** 75 completed the 12-month follow-up (5 dropped out/died). Intent-to-treat analysis does not appear to have been strictly used for the cognitive outcomes, as they analyzed the 75 completers.

Outcome Name: Neuropsychological test performance

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention

2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?
• Response: Yes
• Reason: The intervention involved attendance at 26 to 28 one-hour group meetings for nutritional counseling over 12 months, compared to conventional medical care alone. Because this is a behavioral and lifestyle intervention without a sham control, participants were undoubtedly aware of whether they were in the intensive or conventional group.

2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?
• Response: Yes
• Reason: The intervention was delivered by nutritionists leading group meetings and geriatricians providing medical care. These personnel would inherently know which participants were assigned to receive the additional nutritional counseling.

2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?
• Response: Probably yes
• Reason: The authors noted that the conventional (control) group lost a similar amount of weight as the intensive group, stating: "Because the process of recruiting volunteers included information on the risks of obesity, it is also possible that this may have increased motivation to lose weight in both groups." This suggests that the trial context (informed consent and recruitment regarding obesity risks) led the control group to adopt weight-loss behaviors not prescribed by their assigned protocol.

2.4 Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?
• Response: Yes
• Reason: The premise of the study was that weight loss improves neuropsychological test performance. Because the control group engaged in weight loss (the active mechanism being tested), this deviation directly affects the cognitive outcomes being measured.

2.5 Were these deviations from intended intervention balanced between groups?
• Response: Probably no
• Reason: The deviation was primarily contamination in the control group (i.e., the control group adopting the experimental group's targeted behavior of caloric restriction/weight loss), which is inherently an unbalanced deviation that diminishes the difference between the two assigned arms.

2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?
• Response: No
• Reason: The authors explicitly state that because both groups lost similar amounts of weight, they abandoned the intended comparison between the randomized groups for the cognitive outcomes. Instead, they state: "we decided to a posteriori pool the longitudinal analysis between groups and to test for the effects of change of BMI as a continuous variable." This is an observational, "as-treated" regression analysis rather than an intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis of the assigned groups.

2.7 Was there potential for a substantial impact (on the result) of the failure to analyse participants in the group to which they were randomized?
• Response: Yes
• Reason: By pooling the groups and analyzing the data based on continuous BMI change rather than randomized group assignment, the authors completely removed the protection against confounding provided by randomization. The results reported are based on actual weight loss rather than the assigned intervention.

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 2
• Response: High
• Reason: Participants and personnel were aware of the intervention, and the trial context led to contamination in the control group. Because the assigned intervention failed to produce a difference in weight loss between groups, the authors completely abandoned the intention-to-treat analysis and instead used an inappropriate pooled, as-treated analysis to assess the cognitive outcomes, creating a high risk of bias.

Outcome Name: Neuropsychological test performance

5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?
• Response: No
• Reason: The authors explicitly state in the text that they changed their analysis plan after seeing the data: "Because both groups lost similar amounts of weight after the intervention period... we decided to a posteriori pool the longitudinal analysis between groups and to test for the effects of change of BMI as a continuous variable." This indicates the primary results reported for the cognitive outcomes were based on a post hoc (a posteriori) analysis rather than the pre-specified parallel-group comparison plan.

5.2 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements (e.g. scales, definitions, time points) within the outcome domain?
• Response: No
• Reason: The study reported the results for all the neuropsychological tests mentioned in the methodology (Table 2 and supplemental tables). There is no evidence that they selectively withheld non-significant outcome measures within the cognitive domains.

5.3 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?
• Response: Yes
• Reason: The authors note that they initially ran the generalized estimating equation for comparisons between groups, which showed that "most of the cognitive tests improved, without difference between the groups." Because this pre-specified analysis did not yield the desired group differences (due to similar weight loss in both groups), they subsequently selected an *a posteriori* analysis (pooling the groups to analyze BMI change continuously) to report their main positive findings. This is a clear selection of an analysis based on the nature of the results.

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 5
• Response: High
• Reason: The researchers explicitly abandoned their pre-specified analysis (comparing the two randomized groups) because it failed to show a difference, and instead selected and reported an *a posteriori* analysis (pooling groups and using continuous BMI change) to demonstrate statistical significance. This constitutes a high risk of bias in the selection of the reported results.

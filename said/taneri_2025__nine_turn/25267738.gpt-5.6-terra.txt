**Outcome Name: recurrent thrombotic events**

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The report states: “Random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10.” Allocation based on clinical chart number is a predictable, non-random method. Although a block size is mentioned, the report does not state that a random sequence was generated within blocks (e.g., by computer or random-number table). Therefore, the reported method does not provide evidence of a random component in sequence generation.

**1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The allocation was determined using sealed envelopes “based on clinical chart number.” Clinical chart numbers are potentially known or foreseeable to enrolling personnel, making the upcoming allocation predictable. In addition, the article does not report that envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, tamper-proof, or opened only after irreversible enrolment. Thus, adequate allocation concealment was not demonstrated, and the described chart-number-based approach indicates that concealment was unlikely.

**1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The two randomized RVT-positive groups had similar numbers of participants (119 in continued-LMWH group A1 and 123 in discontinued-LMWH group A2). Reported baseline variables—including age, sex, body mass index, recent cancer surgery, cancer treatment, metastasis, index DVT extent, and side of DVT—did not show statistically significant differences. Cancer sites and chemotherapy distributions also appeared broadly comparable. No substantial baseline imbalance is apparent that independently suggests a problem with the randomization process.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: High**

- **Reason:** The allocation sequence was reported to be based on clinical chart number, which is predictable and not random, and allocation concealment was not adequately implemented or reported. These concerns constitute a high risk of bias arising from the randomization process, despite broadly balanced reported baseline characteristics.

### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** Although the trial reports recurrent thrombotic events and deaths in each randomized group, it does not provide a complete participant-level accounting of outcome availability or censoring throughout follow-up. Numbers remaining under follow-up declined substantially: from 119 to 91 in group A1 and from 123 to 77 in group A2 by 12 months after random assignment. The article reports 12 deaths in A1 and 19 deaths in A2, but it does not fully explain all reductions in numbers under follow-up or clearly report withdrawals/losses to follow-up separately from recurrent events, bleeding, and deaths. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that outcome data were available for all or nearly all randomized participants.

**3.2. If no/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The authors report intention-to-treat analysis and use Kaplan–Meier and Cox proportional-hazards methods, but they do not report an analysis that addresses potential bias arising from censoring or incomplete outcome information. No sensitivity analysis, analysis under alternative assumptions about missing data, or other method to demonstrate robustness to missing outcome data was reported.

**3.3. If no/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** This was a population with active cancer, and 42 participants died during follow-up because of cancer progression (12/119 in A1 and 19/123 in A2 among the randomized groups). Cancer progression and declining health may be associated with the risk of venous thromboembolism. Therefore, censoring due to death or other unreported reasons for discontinued follow-up could plausibly be related to a participant’s underlying risk of recurrent thrombotic events. In addition, the number remaining under follow-up differed between groups by 12 months (91 in A1 versus 77 in A2), although the report does not provide sufficient detail to establish the reasons for this difference.

**3.4. If yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** A substantial number of participants died from cancer progression during follow-up, particularly in the LMWH-discontinuation group (19/123, 15.4%) compared with the continued-LMWH group (12/119, 10.1%). In patients with active cancer, cancer progression is plausibly associated with thrombotic risk. The report does not show that the time-to-event analyses accounted for the potential association between censoring due to death and recurrent thrombotic events. In addition, the reasons for the full reduction in participants remaining under follow-up are not reported.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3: High risk of bias**

- **Reason:** Complete outcome-data availability is not demonstrated, the numbers remaining under follow-up declined appreciably and differently between randomized groups, and no analysis or sensitivity analysis was reported to address potential bias from censoring or missing data. In this active-cancer population, censoring due to cancer progression and death is likely related to the underlying risk of recurrent thrombotic events.

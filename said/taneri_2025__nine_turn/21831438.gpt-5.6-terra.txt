**Outcome Name: Changes in the mean sitting DBP from the baseline after 4 weeks of therapy**

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?**

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** The article explicitly reports that participants were “randomized in a 1:1 ratio by using the mixed block randomization (4 and 6 blocks) method.” Use of mixed random block sizes is a random allocation-sequence generation method.

**1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** Although the study reports mixed-block randomization, it does not describe how allocation was implemented or concealed. There is no report of centralized/telephone/web-based randomization, independent pharmacy allocation, or use of sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes or identically appearing sequentially numbered drug containers. The trial was open label, but this fact alone does not establish whether allocation was concealed before assignment.

**1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Randomized group sizes were virtually equal (117 versus 116). Baseline sitting DBP—the primary outcome measure—was closely comparable between groups (104.7 versus 104.5 mm Hg; *P* = 0.89), as was baseline SBP (161.9 versus 160.0 mm Hg; *P* = 0.26). The combination-therapy group was older on average than the monotherapy group (51.0 versus 47.8 years; *P* = 0.02), but this represents one statistically significant difference among several assessed baseline characteristics and is compatible with chance. No substantial imbalance in the baseline outcome or other reported key prognostic characteristics indicates a problematic randomization process.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Some concerns**

- **Justification:** The allocation sequence appears genuinely random; however, allocation concealment is not reported. Therefore, it cannot be established that investigators enrolling participants were unaware of forthcoming allocations.

**Outcome Name: Changes in the mean sitting DBP from the baseline after 4 weeks of therapy**

### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Sitting DBP was measured using a fully automated Omron blood-pressure device. Measurements were obtained after participants rested quietly for at least 5 minutes, with three consecutive measurements at 1-minute intervals. Where DBP differed by more than 10 mm Hg between measurements, two further readings were obtained; the highest and lowest values were discarded and the remaining three readings averaged. This is an appropriate and sufficiently sensitive method for measuring sitting DBP.

**4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The same outcome-measurement procedures were specified for both groups at baseline and week 4. BP measurements were intended to be taken at the same time of day before dosing to obtain trough values. The same fully automated device type was used, and all participants underwent the same repeated-measurement and averaging procedure. There is no evidence that outcome measurement schedules, instruments, thresholds, or procedures differed by intervention group.

**4.3. If No/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** This was an open-label trial, with no masking reported in the publication or registry record. The investigators who performed or supervised BP assessment were therefore aware of the treatment assigned to participants.

**4.4. If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** Although assessors knew treatment assignments, the outcome was an objective physiological measurement obtained with a fully automated BP device according to a detailed, standardized protocol. The reported outcome was based on averaged repeated measurements rather than subjective assessor judgment. Knowledge of the assigned treatment is therefore unlikely to have materially influenced the DBP readings.

**4.5. If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

- **Response: Not applicable**
- **Reason:** This question is not reached because knowledge of the intervention was judged unlikely to influence assessment of automated sitting DBP (question 4.4: Probably no).

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: **Low**

- **Justification:** Sitting DBP was measured objectively and comparably in both randomized groups using a standardized automated measurement procedure. Despite the open-label design and assessor awareness of assignment, the objective and automated nature of the outcome measurement makes material detection/measurement bias unlikely.

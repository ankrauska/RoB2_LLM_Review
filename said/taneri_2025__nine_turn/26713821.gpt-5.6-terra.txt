**Outcome Name: Neuropsychological test performance**

### 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** Eighty participants were randomized, and 75 completed the 12-month follow-up and were analysed (37 conventional-care and 38 intensive-intervention participants). Thus, neuropsychological outcome data were unavailable for 5/80 randomized participants (6.25%). This is below 10%, but outcome data were available for less than 95% of randomized participants. Given the small sample and the lack of a clear reported between-group cognitive effect, the missing outcomes could potentially make an important difference to the estimated group-by-time effect.

### 3.2. If no/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The report does not describe an analysis specifically intended to address possible bias from missing neuropsychological outcome data, such as multiple imputation incorporating relevant predictors of missingness, inverse-probability weighting, or a sensitivity analysis under plausible missing-not-at-random assumptions. The generalized estimating equation analysis is reported, but no information is provided to show that it corrected for potential bias caused by the five missing 12-month outcome assessments.

### 3.3. If no/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** Four participants dropped out, and one participant died from pneumonia before follow-up. Reasons for the four dropouts, their baseline cognitive status, and whether withdrawal was associated with cognitive deterioration, functional limitations, health status, or inability/willingness to complete neuropsychological testing are not reported. In an elderly population with MCI, dropout could plausibly be related to health or cognitive status, which may also be related to neuropsychological test performance. The death from pneumonia is not itself reported as related to cognitive performance, but it does not resolve the uncertainty concerning the four dropouts.

### 3.4. If yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** There is no direct evidence that missingness was related to actual neuropsychological performance. Attrition was low overall (5/80; 6.25%) and was similar between groups: 3/40 participants in the conventional-care group and 2/40 in the intensive-intervention group did not complete follow-up. The report does not provide differential reasons for missingness by group or evidence of marked baseline cognitive differences among those with missing data. Therefore, dependence of missingness on true cognitive outcome is possible but not demonstrated as likely.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data

- **Judgement:** **Some concerns**  
- **Reason:** Neuropsychological outcome data were missing for 6.25% of randomized participants, which is slightly more than the usual 95% completeness threshold. The study provides no analysis or sensitivity analysis demonstrating robustness to missing data, and dropout could plausibly be associated with health or cognitive status. However, attrition was limited and similar between randomized groups, with no direct evidence that missingness was likely determined by true neuropsychological performance.

**Outcome Name: Neuropsychological test performance**

### 5.1. Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The trial was prospectively registered (ClinicalTrials.gov **NCT01286389**) and the registry lists “Neuropsychological test performance” as a primary outcome. The publication also identifies neuropsychological tests as the main outcome. The supplied registry record does not include a detailed statistical analysis plan, specific neuropsychological test(s), analysis populations, or planned modelling strategies. However, the existence of prospective trial registration supports a **probably yes** response under the specified guidance. Importantly, the report states that the pooled analysis examining BMI change as a continuous variable was decided **a posteriori**, after the randomized groups experienced similar weight loss; this is relevant to questions 5.2 and 5.3.

### 5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** Neuropsychological test performance was assessed using numerous eligible measures across several cognitive domains: CAMcog, IQCODE, RAVLT delayed recall and total learning, digit span forward and backward, phonemic and semantic fluency, Modified Wisconsin Card Sorting Test, Trail Making Test parts A and B, and derived principal-component scores for global cognition, verbal memory, executive function, language/fluency, and attention. The trial registration identifies only the broad outcome of “Neuropsychological test performance” and does not specify a primary test, cognitive domain, time point, hierarchy of outcomes, or method for choosing among these measures. The article reports selected associations between BMI reduction and global cognition, memory, language/fluency, and executive function, while reporting that attention was not associated with BMI change (“data not shown”). This creates a substantial opportunity for selection among multiple cognitive measures and domains, particularly because the BMI–cognition analyses were conducted post hoc.

### 5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The report describes several possible analytic approaches. The initially intended randomized between-group comparison used generalized estimating equations with group, time, and group-by-time interaction. Because the groups had similar weight loss, the investigators then decided **a posteriori** to pool the groups and assess the association between continuous BMI change and cognitive change using generalized linear models. They additionally created cognitive-domain component scores by principal-components analysis and tested selected interactions involving age and APOE4 genotype. The publication acknowledges that these analyses were exploratory and that no adjustment for multiple comparisons was performed. The registry information does not pre-specify these analytic choices, including pooling randomized groups, using BMI change as the exposure, principal-components analysis, covariate adjustment, or interaction analyses. Therefore, there was substantial scope to select analyses and reported results after examining the data.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

- **Judgement:** **High**  
- **Reason:** Although the trial was prospectively registered and broadly listed neuropsychological performance as a primary outcome, the report contains numerous neuropsychological measures and multiple plausible analytic approaches. The central reported BMI–cognition findings arose from an explicitly post hoc pooled analysis after the randomized intervention comparison did not produce the intended contrast in weight loss. Selected cognitive domains and interaction analyses were reported without a pre-specified analytic plan or multiplicity adjustment, and at least one domain result was stated as not shown. These circumstances create a high risk that reported neuropsychological results were selected from multiple available outcome measurements and analyses on the basis of their results.

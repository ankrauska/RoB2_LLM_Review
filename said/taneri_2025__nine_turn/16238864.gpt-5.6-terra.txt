### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**

   - **Response:** Yes  
   - **Reason:** The article states that intervention and control booklets were arranged “in an order determined by a sequence of random integers generated by a true random number generator.” This describes use of a random component to generate the allocation sequence.

2. **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   - **Response:** No information  
   - **Reason:** The intervention and control booklets were placed in a single pile in randomized order and distributed in a lecture theatre and laboratory class. However, the report does not state that the booklets were externally identical, opaque/sealed, sequentially numbered, or distributed by someone unable to identify the forthcoming allocation. It therefore does not establish that allocation was concealed from the personnel distributing the booklets or from participants before assignment.

3. **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   - **Response:** No  
   - **Reason:** The authors report no statistically significant pre-intervention differences between intervention and control groups in cognition measures or behavioural measures. They also report no significant differences in age, gender, or follow-up dropout rate by condition. Specifically for the outcome of interest, baseline intention scores were similar (intervention mean 4.64; control mean 4.54; ANOVA *F* = 0.24). The reported data do not indicate an imbalance suggesting a problematic randomization process.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Some concerns**

- **Justification:** The allocation sequence was random, and reported baseline data do not suggest a failure of randomization. However, allocation concealment was not adequately described. The report therefore leaves uncertainty about whether the person distributing the booklets could have foreseen assignments.

**Outcome Name: intentions to consume the RDIFV**

### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

1. **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**

   - **Response:** Probably no  
   - **Reason:** Intentions to consume the RDIFV were measured using five questionnaire items on 7-point response scales, including items such as “I intend to eat the RDIFV every day over the next week.” The reported internal consistency was high at both pre-intervention and post-intervention assessment (Cronbach’s α = .90 and .93, respectively). The article does not provide evidence that the measure lacked validity or sensitivity to an intervention effect.

2. **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**

   - **Response:** No  
   - **Reason:** Both groups completed the same intention questionnaire immediately after completing their allocated booklet task. The timing, questionnaire items, response scales, and data-collection context appear comparable between groups.

3. **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**

   - **Response:** Yes  
   - **Reason:** This was a participant-reported outcome, so participants were the outcome assessors. Participants necessarily knew whether they had received the intervention materials—including persuasive messages to make a firm decision to eat the RDIFV and planning exercises—or the control task concerning their experience of completing the questionnaire.

4. **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

   - **Response:** Yes  
   - **Reason:** Intention is a subjective self-reported cognitive outcome. Participants’ answers could be influenced by their knowledge that they had just received an intervention explicitly designed to persuade them to intend to consume the RDIFV. Such knowledge may lead to expectancy effects or socially desirable responding.

5. **4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

   - **Response:** Yes  
   - **Reason:** The outcome was measured immediately after the intervention, whose explicit content instructed participants that they could eat the RDIFV and should “make a firm decision now” to do so during the following week. The primary outcome questionnaire then directly asked participants about that intended decision. Given the close temporal proximity and direct overlap between the intervention’s persuasive message and the subjective self-reported outcome, knowledge of intervention allocation was likely to influence reported intentions.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: High**

- **Justification:** Although the same apparently reliable questionnaire was administered in both groups, the outcome was subjective and self-reported by unblinded participants. The intervention explicitly aimed to change and communicate intention, and intention was measured immediately after exposure to its persuasive messages. Outcome assessment was therefore likely influenced by knowledge of assignment, creating a high risk of measurement bias.

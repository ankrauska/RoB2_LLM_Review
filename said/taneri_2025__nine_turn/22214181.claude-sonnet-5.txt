- **Reason:** The LAT was administered identically to both groups at the same time points (pre- and post-intervention), using the same paper-based chart and procedure. **4.3. Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**
- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** The LAT is a self-report/participant-generated measure, meaning the participant is effectively the "outcome assessor" for the initial generation of attributions, rankings, and importance ratings. Participants completed the post-intervention LAT immediately after personally reading/receiving the information sheet with the study investigator, and were therefore fully aware of which type of information (fatigue-related vs. generic sleep) they had just received. (Note: while the *coding* of open-ended responses was done blindly by a lab volunteer and the investigator, the initial *generation* of the outcome data by participants was inherently unblinded.)

**4.4. Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** Since the outcome is a self-generated, subjective cognitive task (listing/ranking/rating personal attributions for fatigue) completed immediately after an unblinded psychoeducational intervention explicitly describing multiple non-sleep causes of fatigue, participants' responses could plausibly be influenced by the content they were just exposed to, independent of any genuine belief change.

**4.5. Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** This is highly plausible and is explicitly acknowledged as a limitation by the study authors themselves: "it is also possible that demand characteristics or priming played a role in FI participants' post-intervention responses on the LAT... the information-based intervention may have influenced participants to respond in a certain manner." The FI group was given an explicit list of specific non-sleep causes of fatigue (e.g., caffeine rebound, boredom, physical inactivity) immediately before completing a task asking them to list/rank/rate causes of their own fatigue. It is likely that participants simply repeated or drew upon the specific content they had just been given (a demand characteristic/priming effect), rather than this reflecting a genuine, durable shift in causal beliefs. While the authors attempt to counter this concern by noting the control group showed no significant change, this argument does not eliminate the concern — it only shows the control content did not prime similarly, not that the FI results were free from priming/demand effects. No independent, objective, or blinded outcome measure was used to corroborate the self-reported attribution change.

**Justification:** Because the outcome assessor (the participant) was necessarily aware of the intervention content just received (4.3 = Yes), and because it is likely (not merely possible) that this awareness influenced their self-reported responses on the LAT given the direct content overlap between the FI intervention material and the outcome measure itself (4.5 = Yes), this domain is judged as **High risk of bias**.

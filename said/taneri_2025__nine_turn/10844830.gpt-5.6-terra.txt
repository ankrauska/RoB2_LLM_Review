**Outcome Name: venous concentrations of endothelin-1 (ET-1)**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** The article states that “sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random” 20 consecutive patients to the two groups. However, it does not describe how the allocation sequence itself was generated (e.g., computer-generated list, random-number table, coin toss, or other random component). Therefore, random sequence generation cannot be confirmed from the report.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The investigators report using sealed envelopes for allocation. This suggests an attempt to conceal assignment during enrolment. However, the report does not state whether envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, tamper-proof, or opened only after irreversible enrolment. Thus, adequate concealment cannot be definitively established, but sealed envelopes provide some support for probable allocation concealment.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Both groups contained 10 participants, consistent with the intended 1:1 allocation. There were some baseline differences, including higher mean pulmonary artery pressure in the high-\(FIO_2\) group (25 vs 19 mm Hg), which the authors report as statistically significant. There were also numerical differences in sex distribution, surgical procedure, and CPB duration. However, the article reports only one statistically significant baseline difference, which may occur by chance in a small randomized trial. The reported data do not demonstrate an excess of statistically significant imbalances, markedly unequal group sizes, or an imbalance clearly incompatible with chance.

### Domain 1 overall risk-of-bias judgement: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Although sealed envelopes suggest that allocation may have been concealed, the method used to generate the random allocation sequence was not reported. This lack of information about sequence generation raises some concerns regarding bias arising from the randomization process.

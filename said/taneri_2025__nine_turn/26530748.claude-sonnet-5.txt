I've noted the key methodological details relevant to a systematic review, including:

**Study design elements:**
- RCT with 427 patients randomized to EBRT vs VBT
- Long-term follow-up at 7 and 10 years (extending prior 5-year analysis)
- Measurement tools: EORTC QLQ-C30, custom bowel/bladder/sexual symptom subscales, IOCv2, and supplementary questions on comorbidities/incontinence
- Statistical approach: linear mixed models, ordinal logistic regression with random effects, Wald tests, with corrected significance threshold (P<.01) for multiple testing

- No difference in global QoL or functioning scales between arms

- Selection bias inherent to long-term survivor analysis (had to be alive and disease-free)

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**
- **Response:** No
- **Reason:** Data are far from complete relative to the original 427 randomized patients. Of these, only 205/265 (77%) responded at 7 years, and further, only 76.2% of these 282 evaluable questionnaires had complete QLQ-C30 items (which includes Global health). This means Global health data were available for roughly 205 × 76.2% ≈ 156 patients at 7 years — approximately **36% of the original 427 randomized participants**, far below the 95% threshold typically needed for continuous outcomes.

**3.2. Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**

This latter finding is actually a signal of difference, not equivalence, and no formal statistical correction was applied to account for this. **3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**
- **Response:** Yes
- **Reason:** Eligibility for the 7-/10-year questionnaire explicitly required patients to be "alive and disease-free." **3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**
- **Response:** Yes

Additionally, the article confirms responders differed from the overall cohort (younger, fewer comorbidities) — an empirical signal consistent with healthier patients being more likely to respond. No information is provided on whether attrition/response rates differed between the EBRT and VBT arms specifically, so it cannot be ruled out that this missingness mechanism differentially affected the between-group comparison. Given the known differential toxicity profile (EBRT causing more bowel/bladder symptoms), it is plausible that missingness patterns and their relationship to QoL could differ subtly between arms as well, though this is not explicitly reported.

- **Response:** Yes

However, there is no direct evidence (e.g., qualitative data, blinded assessment comparison) that outright rules out subtle influence, so I cannot select "No" with full confidence — hence "Probably no" rather than "No."

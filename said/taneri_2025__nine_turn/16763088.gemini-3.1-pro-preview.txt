Outcome Name: failure rate

1. 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The authors explicitly state that they used a random sequence generation method, noting, "Randomization for allocation of either of the two etch and primer systems to the upper right quadrant was undertaken using a block randomization method as described by Roberts and Torgerson (1998)."

2. 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: The article states, "The operators were unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial." While the exact mechanism of allocation concealment (e.g., sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes or a centralized system) is not explicitly described, the explicit statement that operators were blinded to the allocation sequence until trial entry supports that concealment was likely maintained.

3. 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

   • Response: No
   • Reason: The study utilizes a "split-mouth, cross-quadrant design," meaning both interventions are tested within the same patient (one on the left, one on the right). Consequently, patient-level baseline characteristics (e.g., age, gender) are perfectly balanced between the intervention groups by default. There are no reported imbalances in the text indicating a problem with the randomization process.

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Low

**Outcome Name: Change in photoaging**

### 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Change in photoaging requires photoaging measurements at both baseline (1992) and follow-up (1996). Of 903 eligible randomized participants younger than 55 years, only 604 (67%) had good-quality skin replicas at both time points. In total, 817 participants had a baseline replica and 673 had a 1996 replica; 213 contributed a baseline measurement only and 69 a follow-up measurement only. Thus, outcome data sufficient to directly assess change were unavailable for approximately one third of eligible randomized participants. This is not “all, or nearly all” participants.

### 3.2. Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** The authors conducted sensitivity analyses restricted to participants with complete photoaging grades and using multiple imputation, reporting that results were consistent with the main analysis. However, the report does not provide the numerical results of these analyses or demonstrate robustness under a range of plausible assumptions in which missingness depends on the unobserved photoaging outcome. The multiple-imputation approach alone does not establish that missing data could not have biased the result, particularly with substantial missing outcome data. Therefore, there is insufficient evidence to conclude that the result was not biased by missingness.

### 3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** Participants contributing only one skin-replica measurement were more likely than those with measurements at both time points to have severe clinical photoaging of the neck and a history of 2 to 5 sunburns. These characteristics are related to cumulative sun damage and may be associated with the outcome, photoaging. Therefore, it is possible that the probability of a missing photoaging measurement was related to participants’ actual level or progression of photoaging. Although baseline hand microtopography grade itself was not associated with missing follow-up grade, this does not exclude an association between missingness and the unobserved follow-up outcome or outcome progression.

### 3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** Missingness could be related to the true outcome because it was associated with severe clinical neck photoaging and sunburn history. However, the article reports that baseline hand microtopography grade was not associated with missing follow-up grade, and treatment allocation was not reported to be associated with missing grades. The report does not identify differential missingness between randomized sunscreen groups, nor does it provide documented reasons for missing data that clearly establish outcome-dependent missingness. Therefore, outcome-dependent missingness is possible but not shown to be likely.

### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

- **Risk-of-bias judgement:** **Some concerns**  
- **Justification:** A substantial proportion of participants lacked the paired measurements needed to directly estimate change in photoaging. Although the authors performed complete-case and multiple-imputation analyses with reportedly similar findings, the report does not provide sufficient evidence that missing-data bias was adequately addressed under plausible outcome-dependent missingness assumptions. Missingness may have been related to characteristics associated with sun damage, but the available information does not establish that it was likely dependent on the true photoaging outcome.

There is no reported evidence of group-specific measurement procedures, thresholds, timing, or outcome ascertainment intensity.

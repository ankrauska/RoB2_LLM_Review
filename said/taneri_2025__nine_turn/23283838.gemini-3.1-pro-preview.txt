2. 3.2 If No/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: The authors explicitly state that "Only those participants completing Visit 2 are analyzed," indicating a complete-case analysis. There is no mention of sensitivity analyses or appropriate imputation methods to correct for potential bias due to missing data.

3. 3.3 If No/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: Two of the missing participants "stated that they preferred not to continue in the study." In a study involving exposure therapy for severe social anxiety, choosing to discontinue could very well be related to the participant's level of subjective distress (the true value of the outcome). 

4. 3.4 If Yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: While dropping out of an anxiety study can be related to anxiety levels, the extent and reasons for missing data were perfectly balanced between the intervention groups: exactly 2 dropouts per group, with identical reasons in each group (1 changed/lost phone number, 1 preferred not to continue). Because both the proportions and the stated reasons for missingness are perfectly matched across the blinded groups, it is unlikely that the missingness introduced a systematic bias that would distort the relative intervention effect estimate.

5. Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3
   • Response: Low
   • Reason: Although not all data were available and missingness could theoretically depend on the true value of the outcome, the missing data was small (4 participants) and perfectly balanced between groups in both quantity and reasons for dropout. Therefore, it is unlikely that the missingness differentially affected the groups or substantially biased the intervention effect estimate.

**Outcome Name: weight**

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

#### 1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response:** No information  
- **Reason:** The report states that the study was “random” and that “the randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration.” However, it does not describe how the allocation sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, blocked randomization, or other random method). Therefore, random sequence generation cannot be verified.

#### 1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** Randomization was “carried out confidentially by the clinic administration,” rather than by the recruiting clinicians. In addition, treatments were supplied in numbered boxes, and both participants and clinicians were reported to be blinded to assignment. These features support allocation concealment. However, the report does not explicitly state that numbered medication boxes were sequentially allocated only after irreversible enrolment, nor does it describe who held the allocation list or the precise dispensing procedure. Thus, concealment is likely but not fully documented.

#### 1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The groups were broadly similar at baseline for age, height, initial body weight, olanzapine-associated weight gain, duration of olanzapine therapy, and current olanzapine dose. Baseline psychiatric diagnoses differed somewhat (psychosis: 52.0% in the topiramate group versus 38.9% in the placebo group), but this difference is compatible with chance in this small sample and is not sufficient, based on the reported information, to indicate a problematic randomization process. The authors also state that no essential between-group differences were found at randomization.

#### Domain 1 risk-of-bias judgement

- **Judgement:** Some concerns  
- **Reason:** Although allocation concealment appears likely and no important baseline imbalance suggests failure of randomization, the method used to generate the allocation sequence is not reported beyond the statement that the trial was randomized. This leaves some concern about whether the allocation sequence contained a genuinely random component.

**Outcome Name: weight**

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data

#### 3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Fifty-five participants were randomized/planned for allocation (25 topiramate; 24 placebo), but only 43 were included in the final analysis (25 topiramate; 18 placebo). Six placebo-group participants “failed to appear for more than one evaluation” and their data were not analysed. Thus, weight data at the final evaluation were missing for 6/49 randomized participants (12.2%), rather than being available for all or nearly all participants. Furthermore, all missing final weight data occurred in the placebo group (6/24; 25%).

#### 3.2 If No/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The authors excluded the six participants with missing evaluations from all analyses and did not report an analysis method designed to address missing outcome data. No sensitivity analyses were reported to assess how plausible outcomes among missing placebo participants could affect the estimated weight effect.

#### 3.3 If No/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The stated reason for missing data—failure to attend more than one assessment—does not establish that missingness was unrelated to weight or weight change. Attendance at repeated visits may plausibly be related to participants’ experience of treatment, weight trajectory, or other health-related factors. The report does not provide sufficient detail about the reasons for non-attendance to rule out such a relationship.

#### 3.4 If Yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** Missing final weight data were markedly and completely imbalanced between groups: 6 of 24 placebo-assigned participants had missing data, compared with none of 25 topiramate-assigned participants. The participants were excluded without outcome-data methods or sensitivity analyses, and the reasons for their non-attendance were not described beyond failure to attend assessments. This differential missingness makes the weight-effect estimate vulnerable to bias if attendance was related to actual weight or weight change.

#### Domain 3 risk-of-bias judgement

- **Judgement:** High  
- **Reason:** Final weight data were missing for 12.2% of randomized participants, all from the placebo group (25% of that group). The missing participants were excluded from analysis, no method or sensitivity analysis was used to address missing data, and missingness could plausibly—and, given the complete between-group imbalance, likely—be related to the true weight outcome.

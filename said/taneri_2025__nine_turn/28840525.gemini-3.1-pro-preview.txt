Based on the provided article and trial protocol, here is an analysis of the study comparing Laparoscopic Sleeve Gastrectomy (LSG) versus Silastic Ring Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass (SR-LRYGB):

### Study Overview
- **Design:** Prospective, randomized, parallel, 2-arm, double-blind (patient and assessor) clinical trial.
- **Population:** 114 morbidly obese adults (BMI 35-65 kg/m²) aged 20-55 years with Type 2 Diabetes (T2D) of at least 6 months duration.
- **Interventions:** 
  - Laparoscopic Sleeve Gastrectomy (LSG) (n=58)
  - Silastic Ring Laparoscopic Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass (SR-LRYGB) (n=56)
- **Primary Outcome (1-year report):** T2D remission defined by various HbA1c thresholds without diabetes medication at 1 year (primary outcome is 5-year remission < 6% without meds, but this paper reports 1-year data).
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Weight loss, quality of life, anxiety/depression, cardiovascular risk factors, metabolic medication use, postoperative complications, and mortality.

### Key Findings at 1 Year
**1. Glycemic Control (T2D Remission):**
- Rapid and sustained decrease in HbA1c in both groups.
- No statistically significant differences in T2D remission between SR-LRYGB and LSG across multiple HbA1c thresholds (e.g., HbA1c < 42 mmol/mol [6.0%]: 52% vs. 49%, p=0.85).
- Significant reduction in diabetes medication requirements in both groups with no difference between them (p=0.43).

**2. Weight Loss:**
- Significant weight loss in both groups, but SR-LRYGB achieved statistically greater weight loss than LSG.
- % Total Weight Loss: 32.2% (SR-LRYGB) vs. 27.1% (LSG), p < 0.001.

**3. Cardiovascular Risk and Medications:**
- No significant difference in blood pressure reduction between groups.
- SR-LRYGB resulted in a significantly greater reduction in Total Cholesterol and LDL cholesterol compared to LSG.
- Reduction in antihypertensive, lipid-lowering, and antiplatelet medications in both groups.

**4. Complications and Safety:**
- No deaths in either group.
- Slightly higher rate of gastrointestinal complications/reoperations in the SR-LRYGB group (e.g., 5 reoperations vs 3 in LSG). SR-LRYGB specific complications included 3 ulcers, 1 anastomotic leak, and 1 intra-abdominal bleed.

**5. Psychosocial Outcomes:**
- Significant improvements in Health-Related Quality of Life (SF-36 subscales like physical functioning, general health, etc.) in both groups, with no significant between-group differences.
- Depression scores improved in both groups; anxiety scores improved significantly after LSG (noted baseline anxiety was higher in LSG).

### Conclusion
At 1 year, both LSG and SR-LRYGB are similarly effective in achieving remission of Type 2 Diabetes and improving psychosocial outcomes in patients with morbid obesity. This occurred despite SR-LRYGB leading to significantly greater weight loss and better lipid profile improvements. Complications were slightly higher following SR-LRYGB. 

*(Note: The trial registration number is NCT01486680 and ACTRN 12611000751976).*

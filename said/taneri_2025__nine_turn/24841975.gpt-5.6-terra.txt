**Outcome Name: overall survival**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The article reports that patients were “randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System” and that stratified block randomization was used, with a block size of four within strata defined by prior treatment intensity and relapsed versus refractory AML status. Although the report does not explicitly state that the sequence was computer-generated or describe the random-number generation method, centralized stratified block randomization in this large international phase III trial strongly indicates a random allocation sequence.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** Random assignment was conducted through a centralized Interactive Web Response System. This is a remote, centrally administered allocation method and supports concealment of the allocation sequence from enrolling site personnel until assignment. In addition, investigators selected and recorded the intended control treatment before randomization, reducing the opportunity for treatment selection after knowledge of allocated study arm.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The randomized group sizes were essentially identical (191 allocated to elacytarabine and 190 to investigator’s choice), consistent with the planned 1:1 allocation. Most reported baseline characteristics were similar between groups, including sex, race, ECOG performance status, cytogenetic risk, AML type, prior therapy intensity, disease status (relapsed versus refractory), and number of prior regimens. There were more participants aged ≥70 years in the elacytarabine group than in the control group (30% versus 18%; *P* = .007). However, this was one statistically significant difference among numerous baseline comparisons and is compatible with chance. The article also reports an age-adjusted survival analysis, which produced a result similar to the unadjusted analysis (adjusted HR 0.98, 95% CI 0.79–1.28). There is no evidence of major or systematic baseline imbalance indicating a flawed randomization process.

### Domain 1 risk-of-bias judgement: **Low risk of bias**

- **Reason:** The trial used centralized, stratified block randomization through an Interactive Web Response System, allocation concealment was adequately supported, and baseline characteristics did not suggest a substantive failure of randomization.

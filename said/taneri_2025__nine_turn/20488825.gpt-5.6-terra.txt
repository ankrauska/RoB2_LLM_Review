**Outcome Name: attack number**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** The article states that “a computer-based blocking method (four blocks) was used for the randomization.” A computer-based blocking method includes a random component in sequence generation.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** The report states that “separate physicians were assigned to randomly allocate patients into two groups,” and that medications were supplied in similar packages. However, it does not describe a mechanism ensuring allocation concealment, such as central/remote allocation or sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes or drug containers. It is not stated whether the physicians responsible for allocation were independent of participant enrolment or whether enrolment personnel could foresee assignments.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The randomized group sizes were similar (simvastatin, 43; placebo, 42). The reported baseline variables—including sex, age, disease duration, prior relapse number, duration of interferon beta-1a treatment, baseline EDSS, and baseline Gd-enhancing lesions—did not differ statistically significantly between groups. Although baseline EDSS was numerically lower in the simvastatin group (1.2 versus 1.6), no substantial imbalance indicating a problematic randomization process is evident from the reported data.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** The allocation sequence was computer-generated, and no important baseline imbalance is apparent. However, allocation concealment was insufficiently described. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that treatment assignment was concealed from personnel enrolling participants before assignment.

### 2.1. Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?

- **Response: No**

### 2.2. Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?

Therefore, complete assurance is not possible.

### 2.5. If yes/probably yes to 2.4: Were these deviations from intended intervention balanced between groups?

- **Response: Not applicable**
- **Reason:** Question 2.4 is not applicable.

### 2.6. Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Although the authors state that analyses were conducted according to intention-to-treat principles, the reported primary relapse analysis did not include all 85 randomized participants. The significant Mann–Whitney analysis of total relapses included 40 simvastatin participants and 36 placebo participants, rather than the 43 and 42 randomized, respectively. The authors state that only participants who had used study medication for at least 6 months were included in the final analysis because simvastatin was assumed to require 6 months to exert an effect. Thus, participants who withdrew earlier were excluded after randomization. This is not an appropriate analysis of the effect of assignment to intervention.

### 2.7. If no/probably no to 2.6: Was there potential for a substantial impact on the result of the failure to analyse participants in the group to which they were randomized?

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** At least 9 of 85 randomized participants were excluded from the final analysis because they did not remain in the study or receive assigned treatment for 6 months. This represents approximately 11% of randomized participants. Exclusions occurred post-randomization and included participants who refused further cooperation early in follow-up. In addition, some withdrawals were related to disease exacerbation or relapse, which is directly related to the primary outcome. Excluding these participants could substantially affect the estimated between-group effect on attack number.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 2: **High**

- **Reason:** Blinding appears to have been maintained sufficiently to limit deviations caused by awareness of treatment assignment. However, the primary reported analysis excluded randomized participants after randomization on the basis of duration of treatment/follow-up, despite being labelled intention-to-treat. Because these exclusions were sufficiently numerous and could be related to relapse outcomes, they could have had a substantial impact on the result.

**Outcome Name: attack number**

### 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Eighty-five participants were randomized (43 simvastatin and 42 placebo), but the principal analysis of total relapses included only 40 participants in the simvastatin group and 36 in the placebo group (76/85 randomized participants; 89%). Therefore, outcome data contributing to the reported primary analysis were unavailable for, or excluded for, 9 randomized participants. This is not “nearly all” participants, particularly because the missing/excluded participants could plausibly have had relapse outcomes that materially affected the between-group result.

### 3.2. If No/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The authors state that intention-to-treat analysis was used and report additional per-protocol and last-observation-carried-forward (LOCF) analyses. However, the principal total-relapse analysis excluded participants who did not receive study treatment for at least 6 months. The LOCF analysis was reported for attack numbers per 3-month period and does not establish that the primary 1-year total-relapse result was robust to missing data. No appropriate missing-data adjustment or sensitivity analysis under plausible assumptions about missing outcomes was reported.

### 3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** Withdrawal could occur after two relapses during the study, and six participants withdrew for this reason (two in the simvastatin group and four in the placebo group). Since relapse occurrence was both the primary outcome and a withdrawal criterion, missingness or exclusion from the final analysis could depend on participants’ true attack number. In addition, one placebo participant withdrew because of chronic exacerbation, which may also be related to disease activity.

### 3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** The report explicitly identifies two relapses as a major withdrawal criterion, and six participants withdrew after experiencing two relapses. Thus, missingness/exclusion was directly associated with the primary outcome. Furthermore, more participants were excluded from the placebo analysis (6/42) than from the simvastatin analysis (3/43), and withdrawals for two relapses were also more frequent in the placebo group (4 versus 2). These features make it likely that missingness depended on the true outcome value and could have biased the estimated intervention effect.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3: **High**

- **Reason:** The primary total-relapse analysis excluded 9 randomized participants. There is no evidence from an appropriate missing-data analysis or sensitivity analysis that this exclusion did not bias the result. Missingness/exclusion was likely related to relapse occurrence because two relapses constituted a protocol-defined reason for withdrawal, directly linking missingness to the primary outcome.

**Outcome Name: attack number**

### 5.1. Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** The trial was registered (NCT00668343), and the registry identifies “attack number” over 1 year as the primary outcome. However, no detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan is available in the provided record. Importantly, the registry was first submitted in April 2008, after the reported actual primary-completion date of March 2008. Therefore, the available registry record does not demonstrate that the analysis plan was finalized before outcome data were available or before unblinded analysis. The article also reports several analyses of attack number, including a primary Mann–Whitney analysis, a per-protocol analysis, and a LOCF analysis, without identifying a pre-specified primary analysis approach.

### 5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** Attack number over 1 year was explicitly identified as the primary outcome in both the article and trial registry. The article reports total relapses during the 1-year study period, which corresponds to this specified outcome. Although the authors also report attack numbers per 3-month period using LOCF, this appears to be an additional analysis intended to address dropout rather than a different primary outcome measurement selected for favourable results. There is no direct evidence that the reported 1-year total attack-number result was selected from multiple measures on the basis of its result.

### 5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** The report presents more than one analysis relevant to attack number: the reported final analysis, a per-protocol analysis, and an LOCF analysis. However, results of these analyses are reported rather than only a favourable analysis being selectively presented. The total-relapse result is reported using both medians with a Mann–Whitney test (*p* = 0.01) and means with a reported *p* value of 0.007. Although the absence of a prospectively available detailed analysis plan prevents confirmation that the chosen analysis was pre-specified, there is no direct evidence that one analysis was selected and reported because it gave a more favourable result.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 5: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** The primary outcome and its 1-year timeframe were specified in the trial registry. Nevertheless, the registry was submitted after the reported primary-completion date, and neither the article nor the provided registry record supplies a statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. Consequently, selective reporting based on alternative analyses cannot be excluded, despite no direct evidence that the reported attack-number result was selected because it was favourable.

### Overall predicted direction of bias

- **Response: Unpredictable**
- **Reason:** The principal analysis excluded 9 randomized participants after randomization, based on duration of study treatment/follow-up. Although more relapse-related withdrawals occurred in the placebo group, the direction and magnitude of the resulting bias cannot be determined reliably from the available information.

### Overall Risk-of-bias judgement: **High**

- **Reason:** The study has:
  - **Some concerns** regarding the randomization process because allocation concealment was not adequately described.
  - **High risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions** for the effect of assignment to intervention, because the reported primary analysis excluded participants after randomization despite being described as intention-to-treat, and these exclusions could substantially affect the result.
  - **High risk of bias due to missing outcome data**, because data from 9 randomized participants were excluded from the principal analysis and missingness/exclusion was likely related to relapse occurrence.

  - **Some concerns regarding selection of the reported result**, because no prospectively finalized detailed statistical analysis plan was available, and trial registration occurred after the reported primary-completion date.

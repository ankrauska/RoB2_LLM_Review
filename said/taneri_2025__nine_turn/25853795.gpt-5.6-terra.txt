### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** The article explicitly reports that allocation used “block randomization” based on a “computer-generated random number table,” with stratification by operator and centre. A computer-generated random sequence includes a random component.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The assigned appliance type was placed in “sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered” envelopes. The envelopes were opened only after the patient had been accepted into the trial, and the individual who generated the randomization sequence did not participate in participant allocation. Although the report does not explicitly state that envelopes were tamper-proof or irreversibly assigned before opening, the reported procedures provide reasonable assurance of allocation concealment.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The randomized group sizes were reasonably balanced (71 allocated to Victory and 67 to SmartClip), consistent with the intended approximately 1:1 allocation. Reported baseline characteristics show some numerical differences in age and malocclusion categories, but no substantial imbalance in a clearly important prognostic factor is demonstrated, and no evidence suggests that these differences could not have occurred by chance. Extraction versus non-extraction treatment was also balanced (42 vs 43 participants).

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

- **Judgement:** Low risk of bias  
- **Reason:** The allocation sequence was computer generated, allocation was likely adequately concealed using sealed opaque sequentially numbered envelopes opened after enrolment, and there is no indication from group sizes or reported baseline characteristics that the randomization process was compromised.

### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

#### 4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Total treatment duration was defined as the time in months from placement of the fixed appliance to debonding. This is an objective and appropriate measure of treatment duration and is capable of detecting plausible differences between appliance groups.

#### 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Treatment duration was measured using the same definition in both groups: from fixed-appliance placement to debond. Both treatment groups were managed in the same hospital settings by the same two operators under a standardized protocol. There is no report of different measurement methods, thresholds, or follow-up procedures between bracket groups.

#### 4.3. If no/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The treating clinicians could not be blinded to bracket type, as explicitly stated by the authors. These clinicians would necessarily know the appliance received when managing treatment and determining when treatment was complete and debonding should occur. Although data analysis was reportedly conducted with examiner blinding and appliance type was omitted from data-collection sheets, the treatment-duration endpoint depended on dates arising during non-blinded clinical care.

#### 4.4. If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** The endpoint date was the date of debonding. The decision that treatment was sufficiently complete to debond involves clinical judgement by the treating clinician. Since clinicians knew the allocated bracket system, knowledge of intervention could potentially influence when treatment was judged complete, and therefore the recorded treatment duration.

#### 4.5. If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** The study used a standardized treatment protocol, including recommended archwire sequences, and the analysis was reportedly blinded. The report does not provide evidence that clinicians applied different completion standards or deliberately delayed or accelerated debonding according to bracket type. However, explicit objective criteria for treatment completion/debonding were not reported.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: Some concerns

- **Reason:** Treatment duration was measured appropriately and comparably across groups. However, treating clinicians knew the allocated appliance, and treatment duration depends partly on the non-blinded clinical decision to debond. The report does not describe prespecified, objective, or independently assessed criteria for treatment completion. There is no evidence that this knowledge actually influenced debonding decisions, but some concern remains.

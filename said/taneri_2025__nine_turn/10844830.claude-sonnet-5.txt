- **Type**: Randomized controlled trial
- **Population**: 20 consecutive adult cardiac surgery patients with CPB
- **Groups**: 
  - Group 1 (n=10): High FIO2 (1.0) after CPB weaning
  - Group 2 (n=10): Moderate FIO2 (0.35) after CPB weaning
- **Exposure time**: ~70 minutes at differential FIO2
- **Randomization method**: Sealed envelopes

## Key Outcomes Measured
1. Arterial/mixed venous ET-1 and Big ET-1 concentrations
2. Venous admixture (Qs/Qt) as marker of pulmonary gas exchange efficiency
3. PaO2/FIO2 ratio
4. Hemodynamic parameters (pulmonary artery pressure, right atrial pressure)
5. Clinical outcomes (extubation time, ICU discharge)

## Principal Findings
1. **Big ET-1**: Significantly higher in Group 1 (high FIO2) vs Group 2 after CPB (arterial: 2.6±1.2 vs 1.4±0.4 fmol/L)
2. **ET-1**: No significant difference between groups, though Group 1 showed increase vs pre-bypass baseline
3. **Venous admixture**: Significantly greater in Group 1 (20.9±7.5%) vs pre-bypass (8.1±3.8%); no significant change in Group 2
4. **Dissociation finding**: Reducing FIO2 from 1.0→0.6 improved venous admixture WITHOUT reducing peptide concentrations (suggesting endothelial changes may persist despite improved oxygenation)
5. **Day 1 follow-up**: Peptide concentrations equalized between groups; venous admixture remained non-significantly higher in Group 1
6. **No difference in clinical outcomes** (extubation time, ICU stay)

## Notable Methodological Points I Noted
- Statistical outlier excluded (one Group 2 patient with persistently high arterial ET-1)
- Baseline imbalance: pulmonary artery pressure differed between groups before CPB (25±5 vs 19±4 mmHg)
- Venous admixture calculated via oxygen technique (not gold-standard inert gas technique) — authors acknowledge this "shunt" includes low V/Q regions, terming it "venous admixture" rather than true shunt
- No lipid peroxidation/oxidative stress biomarkers measured directly (relies on indirect peptide-based inference)
- Correlation analyses between peptides and hemodynamics were weak/non-significant

## Questions/Considerations for Critical Appraisal
- Small sample size (n=10/group) limits power, especially for correlation analyses
- Single-center study
- Mechanism linking hyperoxia → Big ET-1 increase → venous admixture remains associative, not causally established (authors explicitly acknowledge this in discussion)
- The clinical significance of statistically significant differences in peptide concentrations without corresponding clinical outcome differences is unclear
- Randomization successful for most variables but not pulmonary artery pressure

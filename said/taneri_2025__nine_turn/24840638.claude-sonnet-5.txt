I've carefully read the article by Romanowich et al. on "The Effects of Percentile versus Fixed Criterion Schedules on Smoking with Equal Incentive Magnitude for Initial Abstinence."

I have a thorough understanding of the study's key elements:

**Study design**: A randomized trial with 93-94 hard-to-treat (HTT) smokers (defined by failure to produce any abstinent breath CO sample <3 ppm during a 5-visit trial), assigned to percentile (n=36), fixed criterion (n=40), or random incentive (n=18) schedules, with incentive magnitude for first abstinence equated at $5.00 across percentile and fixed criterion groups.

**Key manipulation**: Unlike prior Lamb et al. (2010) work, this study controlled for the confound where percentile/shaping participants typically earn larger incentives for their first abstinent sample due to escalation during the shaping phase before abstinence.

**Main findings**:
- No group differences in the proportion achieving at least one abstinent sample (~63-64% for percentile/fixed criterion; 44% for random)
- Fixed criterion participants reached first abstinence faster than percentile participants (visit 4 vs. 19)
- Fixed criterion participants produced more total criterion samples and better maintained consecutive abstinence than percentile participants
- Percentile participants earned incentives more frequently per visit but achieved abstinence less reliably
- No significant sex effects on any outcome
- This contradicts earlier findings favoring percentile schedules, suggesting the previous advantage may have been driven by incentive magnitude rather than the shaping mechanism itself

**Theoretical interpretation**: The authors propose that the small ($0.50) differential between continuing abstinence vs. reverting to shaping-level smoking under percentile schedules was insufficient to maintain abstinence, whereas the stark contingency in fixed-criterion (all-or-nothing for <3ppm) promoted stronger behavioral control.

**Limitations noted**: No percentile group with escalating shaping-criterion incentives was included for direct comparison; no formal comprehension check of contingencies; incentive magnitude itself wasn't experimentally manipulated.

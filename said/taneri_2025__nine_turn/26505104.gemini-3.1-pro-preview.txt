Outcome Name: delayed diagnostic performance

**Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data**

3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?
* Response: Probably no
* Reason: A total of 101 participants were initially enrolled (82 medical, 9 physician assistant, and 10 nursing students). The final analysis included 94 participants, meaning 7 participants (~7%) had missing outcome data. Because data availability (~93%) falls slightly below the 95% threshold commonly suggested for continuous outcomes, and because missing data were clustered rather than evenly distributed, it cannot confidently be stated that data were available for "nearly all" participants.

3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
* Response: No
* Reason: The authors did not perform any analyses (such as multiple imputation) to correct for potential bias, nor did they report sensitivity analyses evaluating whether the results would change under a range of assumptions about the missing data. 

3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
* Response: Yes
* Reason: The article explicitly states that two participants (one from the BC group and one from the CB group) were excluded because their delayed diagnostic test performance was three standard deviations below the mean (scoring 0% and 8%, respectively). Therefore, the missingness for these participants was a direct consequence of their actual outcome values.

3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
* Response: Yes
* Reason: There are two strong indicators that missingness depended on the true value. First, two participants were systematically excluded specifically because of their poor performance. Second, there are distinct differences between intervention groups in the proportions of missing outcome data: all 7 participants with missing data (5 dropouts and 2 excluded for low scores) belonged to the CB (4 participants) and BC (3 participants) groups, while the SC and FL groups experienced zero missing data. 

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3
* Response: High
* Reason: Missing outcome data affected ~7% of the randomized participants and was distinctly imbalanced across the study arms (occurring only in the BC and CB groups). Furthermore, the exclusion of participants explicitly due to their extremely low scores demonstrates that the missingness directly depended on the true outcome value. No sensitivity analyses were conducted to mitigate this, leading to a high risk of bias for this domain.

Outcome Name: delayed diagnostic performance

**Overall Predicted Direction of Bias:** Towards null
* Reason: The highest risk of bias in this study comes from missing outcome data (Domain 3). Specifically, participants who performed extremely poorly on the delayed test (scoring 0% and 8%) were intentionally excluded from the final analysis, alongside a few dropouts. All of these excluded/missing participants belonged to the comparator groups (BC and CB), while the experimental group (SC) had no missing data. By artificially removing the lowest-performing scores from the comparator groups, their average delayed performance was inflated. This artifact makes the comparator groups look better than they actually were, shrinking the difference between them and the higher-performing experimental group. Thus, the bias likely underestimates the true effect size, driving the result toward the null.

**Overall Risk-of-bias judgement:** High
* Reason: According to the Cochrane RoB 2 guidelines, if at least one domain is judged to be at a "High" risk of bias, the overall risk of bias judgment is "High." Because Domain 3 (Bias due to missing outcome data) was rated as High—due to the unbalanced missing data across groups and the explicit, systematic exclusion of participants based directly on their poor outcome scores—the overall risk of bias for the delayed diagnostic performance outcome must be considered High. Furthermore, Domains 1 and 5 were rated as having "Some concerns," further supporting an overall lack of robust methodological rigor.

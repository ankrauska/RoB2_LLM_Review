**Outcome Name: Beck Depression Inventory (total score) post intervention**

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?**

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** The report states that participants “were randomly allocated” and that allocation occurred “in consecutive order,” but it does not describe how the allocation sequence itself was generated. There is no statement of computer-generated random numbers, random-number table, blocking, coin tossing, or another random component. The protocol likewise specifies a randomized parallel design but does not report the sequence-generation method.

**1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** Participants completed the baseline online survey and provided an email address before allocation. The article states that they were then randomly allocated, and within 24 hours the intervention group received an email with the access voucher while wait-list participants were notified of delayed access. This online process makes participant foreknowledge of allocation unlikely. However, the report does not explicitly state that allocation was automated, centrally administered, or inaccessible to those responsible for enrolment; therefore, definite confirmation of allocation concealment is unavailable.

**1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Group sizes were equal at baseline (105 per group). Reported baseline demographic, treatment-related, and psychopathological variables did not differ statistically between groups. Baseline BDI total scores were similar: mean 30.02 (SD 10.18) in the wait-list group and 28.81 (SD 11.11) in the Deprexis group (*p* > .4). There is no apparent key baseline prognostic imbalance or other reported indication of a problematic randomization process.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Although baseline balance is reassuring and the allocation timing suggests that participants were allocated after enrolment, the method used to generate the allocation sequence was not reported. Consequently, the randomness of sequence generation cannot be established.

**Outcome Name: Beck Depression Inventory (total score) post intervention**

### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** BDI outcome data at eight weeks were available for 170 of 210 randomized participants (81.0%): 90/105 (85.7%) in the wait-list group and 80/105 (76.2%) in the Deprexis group. Thus, 40 participants (19.0%) had missing post-intervention BDI data. This is substantially below the approximate 95% availability generally considered “nearly all” for a continuous outcome. Imputed values are considered missing for this signalling question.

**3.2. If No/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** The investigators performed an ITT analysis using multiple imputation for missing post-treatment data. However, the reported information does not establish that the imputation model adequately addressed a possible relationship between missing BDI data and unobserved BDI outcomes. The report specifies that imputation was stratified by treatment group and gives technical details on the number of imputations and iterations, but does not report the variables included in the imputation model or provide sensitivity analyses under alternative missing-data assumptions. No analyses demonstrating robustness of the BDI result to plausible departures from missing-at-random assumptions were reported.

**3.3. If No/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The outcome was self-reported depressive symptom severity in an unguided online intervention study. Continuing or worsening depressive symptoms, low motivation, dissatisfaction with the intervention, or lack of perceived benefit could plausibly influence whether a participant completed the eight-week survey. The study does not report reasons for non-completion. Although non-completers reportedly did not differ from completers on measured baseline psychopathological variables, this does not rule out an association between missingness and unobserved post-intervention BDI scores.

**3.4. If Yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** There is no reported evidence that dropout was related to outcome values, and the authors report no significant group difference in completion rates (85.7% for wait-list versus 78.1% reported in Table 1 for Deprexis). The available report does not give reasons for missing BDI outcomes or demonstrate differential dropout related to depressive symptoms. Nevertheless, such dependence remains plausible because of the nature of the population and self-reported online outcome assessment.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Post-intervention BDI data were missing for 19% of randomized participants, with a higher proportion missing in the Deprexis group. Multiple imputation was used, but the report does not provide sufficient detail or sensitivity analyses to demonstrate that missing outcomes could not have biased the intervention-effect estimate. Missingness could plausibly be related to unobserved depressive symptom severity, but available evidence does not establish that this relationship was likely.

**Outcome Name: Beck Depression Inventory (total score) post intervention**

### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) is a 21-item self-report questionnaire designed to assess depressive symptoms and was the study’s stated primary outcome. The article describes it as a widely used depression-severity measure and reports established psychometric support, including internal consistency above .80 and correlations with clinician-rated Hamilton Depression Rating Scale scores ranging from .61 to .81. The study’s wait-list test–retest correlation for the BDI over eight weeks was .72. No information indicates that the BDI was inappropriate or insensitive for detecting plausible effects of a depression intervention.

**4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Both groups completed the same anonymous online baseline and eight-week post-intervention survey, using the same BDI instrument and assessment procedures. The outcome was assessed at the same planned time point in both groups. No group-specific measurement method, threshold, or assessment schedule was reported.

**4.3. If No/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** For this participant-reported outcome, participants are the outcome assessors. Participants knew whether they had received immediate Deprexis access or had been allocated to the wait-list condition. The immediate group received a program voucher and log-in instructions, while the wait-list group was told that program access would be delayed until after the eight-week reassessment.

**4.4. If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** The BDI is a subjective self-reported measure of depressive symptoms and related attitudes. Participants’ knowledge that they had received the active online intervention, or instead had been placed on a wait-list, could influence their symptom ratings through expectations, perceived treatment benefit, disappointment, or reporting tendencies.

**4.5. If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** This was an unblinded comparison of immediate access to a psychological self-help program against delayed access, with no attention-matched or active control. The outcome was self-reported depressive symptom severity. The article also reports treatment-group expectations and retrospective views: only 37.8% of intervention completers expected the program to help at baseline, while 64.8% said their expectations were met or exceeded at post-assessment; 47.9% reported that depressive symptoms had decreased due to Deprexis. These findings show that participants formed beliefs about the program’s effects, which could plausibly have influenced self-rated BDI scores. No blinded clinician-rated depression outcome was used to corroborate the self-report result.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: **High risk of bias**

- **Reason:** Although the same valid BDI measure was administered at the same time points in both groups, the outcome was subjective and participant reported in an unblinded immediate-treatment versus wait-list trial. Knowledge of assigned intervention could influence symptom reporting, and it is likely to have done so in this context.

**Outcome Name: delayed diagnostic performance**

### 4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** Delayed diagnostic performance was measured one week after the learning intervention using 12 clinical vignettes. For each vignette, participants selected the correct diagnosis from four listed disorders. The diagnostic cases and learning materials had been used in three previous basic-science-integration studies, and the authors state that the assessments had previously demonstrated adequate measurement properties for studying health-professions students’ learning and diagnostic reasoning. The vignettes and answer options were identical in format across groups, and the outcome is appropriate for evaluating retained diagnostic performance in this experimental educational context.

### 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** All groups completed the same type of delayed diagnostic test after the same one-week interval. Each participant completed 12 clinical vignettes with four diagnostic response options. Two alternate test versions were used and counterbalanced between immediate and delayed testing; the authors report that item analysis showed the tests had similar difficulty. There is no indication that the delayed diagnostic outcome was measured differently across the four randomized conditions.

### 4.3. If no/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?

- **Response:** No information  
- **Reason:** The article does not explicitly state whether persons scoring or overseeing the diagnostic assessments were blinded to participants’ assigned learning condition. The diagnostic testing was administered via a custom-designed computer program, but the report does not explicitly state that scoring was automated or that assessors were masked.

### 4.4. If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Delayed diagnostic performance was based on whether the participant selected the correct diagnosis from four predefined options for each clinical vignette. This is an objectively scored outcome with predetermined correct answers and does not require subjective assessor judgement. Therefore, even if an assessor had known the assigned intervention, such knowledge would not plausibly affect whether a response was scored correct.

### 4.5. If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response:** Not applicable  
- **Reason:** This question is not applicable because knowledge of intervention status was not judged capable of influencing the objective scoring of the diagnostic test.

### Domain 4 risk-of-bias judgement: **Low**

- **Justification:** Delayed diagnostic performance was assessed using the same objectively scored multiple-choice diagnostic-vignette format at the same follow-up time point in all groups. Although assessor blinding was not reported explicitly, the correct responses were predefined, so knowledge of instructional assignment could not reasonably influence outcome scoring.

### 5.1. Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?

- **Response:** No information  
- **Reason:** The article does not report a trial registration, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or other pre-specified analysis document finalized before outcome data were available. Although the authors describe their primary analysis as a mixed repeated-measures ANCOVA, this does not establish that the analysis was pre-specified. In addition, covariates were selected based on correlations observed between test performance and time variables, which indicates that at least aspects of the analysis were determined using study data.

### 5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** Delayed diagnostic performance was operationalized as performance on a 12-item diagnostic-vignette test administered after a one-week delay. The report consistently describes this outcome and reports group-level delayed diagnostic performance. Although alternate case versions were used and counterbalanced across immediate and delayed testing, these versions were intended to avoid repeat-testing effects, and the authors report that item analysis found both tests to be of similar difficulty. There is no direct evidence that the reported delayed diagnostic outcome measurement was selected from several measurements because of its result.

### 5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** No pre-specified analysis plan is available. The authors state that covariates were selected “based on the correlations between performances on the diagnostic and memory test as well as between performance and time per vignette.” Thus, selection of adjustment variables was based on observed data rather than documented pre-specification. The article also reports post hoc pairwise testing using the least significant differences method following the condition-by-time interaction. In the absence of a protocol or analysis plan, these data-informed analytic decisions create a substantial possibility that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible analyses on the basis of results.

### Domain 5 risk-of-bias judgement: **Some concerns**

- **Justification:** There is no reported pre-specified protocol or statistical analysis plan. In addition, the covariate-selection approach was based on observed correlations, and post hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted. The report does not provide definitive evidence that the delayed diagnostic-performance result was selectively chosen because it was favourable; therefore, this domain is judged as having **some concerns**, rather than high risk of bias.

Based on the provided article and the trial protocol, here is an analysis of the study.

The article reports on a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of simvastatin (40 mg/day) as an add-on therapy to interferon beta 1a (IFNβ-1a, 30 mg weekly) in patients with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS).

**Key Findings:**
- **Study Design:** 85 patients with RRMS, EDSS < 5.0, taking IFNβ-1a for at least 3 months and having had at least one relapse in the previous 6 months were randomized into two groups (simvastatin vs. placebo).
- **Primary Outcome:** The total number of relapses (attacks) was significantly lower in the simvastatin group compared to the placebo group over the 1-year follow-up (p=0.01).
- **Secondary Outcomes:** 
  - *EDSS (Expanded Disability Status Scale):* There was a trend towards lower EDSS scores in the simvastatin group, but the difference was not statistically significant after adjusting for baseline EDSS scores (p=0.07).
  - *MRI findings:* The number of new T2 lesions and Gadolinium-enhanced (Gd+) lesions showed a decreasing trend in the simvastatin group compared to placebo, but this was not statistically significant.
- **Safety:** The combination of simvastatin and IFNβ-1a was generally safe and well-tolerated. No serious adverse effects were noted. The trial monitored laboratory parameters such as AST/ALT and CPK, noting no severe complications.

**Conclusions:**
The authors conclude that simvastatin added to weekly IFNβ-1a in RRMS patients might safely reduce the relapse rate. However, larger-scale trials are needed to further substantiate these findings and to clarify the impact on MRI markers, which did not reach statistical significance in this small cohort.

The trial protocol (NCT00668343) matches the article's descriptions regarding study type, interventions, phases, masked design, and outcome measures.

Outcome Name: attack number

1. 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: A total of 85 patients were randomized. The text states that "five of these refused to cooperate without any specific reason in the first 2 months" and were completely excluded. Furthermore, while the intention-to-treat analysis purportedly included the other 9 dropouts (who withdrew later), the sample sizes reported for the Mann-Whitney U-test on the number of relapses were n=40 (simvastatin) and n=36 (placebo), totaling 76 participants out of the originally randomized 85 (10.6% missing). Given the low number of total events (means of 0.4 and 1.0 attacks), the missing data from these participants could potentially influence the outcome.

2. 3.2. Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

   • Response: No
   • Reason: The authors do not present any sensitivity analyses assessing the impact of the missing data on the results, nor do they use analysis methods that correct for bias caused by the missing outcome data for those completely excluded.

3. 3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: The study protocol explicitly designated disease deterioration ("having two relapses in the study period, developing a secondary progressive course") as a major withdrawal criterion. While the authors state that patients who withdrew were followed for the whole study period, the discrepancy in the analyzed numbers (n=76 vs. 85 randomized) leaves ambiguity about whether patients with severe disease activity were fully captured in the final count, meaning missingness could be linked to having more attacks.

4. 3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: The 5 patients who completely dropped out and were excluded did so in the first 2 months "without any specific reason," making it less likely that their missingness was directly driven by the outcome (relapses), which typically requires longer follow-up to accumulate. The 9 patients who reached the withdrawal criteria due to relapses were explicitly stated to be included in the final analysis (even if the n=76 figure in the text presents some inconsistencies with the n=80 in the flowchart), indicating the trialists made efforts not to lose the outcome data of those who worsened.

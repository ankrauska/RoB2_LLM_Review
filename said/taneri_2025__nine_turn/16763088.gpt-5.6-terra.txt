**Outcome Name: failure rate**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The authors state that allocation of the etch/primer system was undertaken using a “block randomization method” and cite a reference on randomization methods. The allocation determined whether the self-etching primer was used in the upper right or upper left quadrant, with the opposite system applied in the contralateral cross-quadrant. Although the report does not explicitly state the specific random component used to generate the block sequence (e.g., computer-generated numbers or random-number table), describing the method as block randomization provides reasonable evidence that the sequence was intended to be random.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The report states that “the operators were unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial.” This indicates that allocation was concealed from the treating operators before enrolment. However, the mechanism of concealment is not reported; for example, there is no description of central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or an independently controlled allocation list. Therefore, concealment is supported but cannot be judged definitively.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** This was a split-mouth cross-quadrant trial: each participant received both the self-etching and conventional etch-and-prime systems in allocated quadrants. Consequently, participant-level baseline characteristics, including age and gender, were inherently the same for both intervention conditions. The numbers of brackets assigned to the two systems were also essentially balanced across the tooth-location data (331 self-etching versus 330 conventional-system brackets). No baseline imbalance suggesting a problematic randomization process was reported.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

- **Judgement: Low risk of bias**
- **Reason:** The study reports block randomization, indicates that operators did not know the allocation before patient entry, and uses a split-mouth design that controls participant-level baseline characteristics between intervention conditions. Although the exact methods of sequence generation and allocation concealment were not fully described, the available information does not indicate a problem in the randomization process.

### 4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Bracket failure is a clinically observable and directly relevant outcome. The study monitored participants for 12 months and recorded the tooth and date whenever a bond failed. ### 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** Both bonding systems were used within the same participants under the split-mouth cross-quadrant design. Participants were monitored during the same 12-month follow-up period, and the authors state that, if a bond failed, the tooth and date of failure were recorded. The report does not describe different assessment methods, thresholds, visit schedules, or opportunities to identify failures between the self-etching and conventional-system brackets.

### 4.3. If No/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The article states that the operators could not be blinded to the bonding system, because the systems had different modes of application. It does not explicitly identify who assessed and recorded bracket failures or state that these assessors were blinded. Since the operators treated the patients and the intervention allocation was by quadrant, knowledge of the intervention status during follow-up is likely. There is no reported procedure to blind outcome assessment.

### 4.4. If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** The primary outcome, bracket bond failure, is an objective and readily observable clinical event: a bracket is either attached or detached from the tooth. Although knowledge of the etch/primer system could theoretically influence documentation, determining whether a bracket has failed involves little subjective judgement. The reported date of failure may reflect when the failure was detected rather than the precise time it occurred, but there is no indication that this would differ systematically by intervention group.

### 4.5. If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response: No information / Not applicable**
- **Reason:** This question is not applicable because assessment of bracket failure was judged probably not susceptible to influence from knowledge of the allocated bonding system (Question 4.4: Probably no).

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

- **Judgement: Low risk of bias**
- **Reason:** Bracket failure was measured as a clinically objective event using the same reported follow-up and recording approach for both bonding systems. Blinding of outcome assessors was not reported and is unlikely; however, outcome assessment required minimal subjective judgement, and awareness of the allocated intervention was unlikely to influence ascertainment of whether a bracket had failed.

Outcome Name: EORTC QLQ-C30 Global health

1. 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: Out of the 427 originally randomized patients, only 265 were eligible (alive and disease-free) at the 7- and 10-year marks. Of these, 282 evaluable questionnaires were returned (202 at 7 years and 80 at 10 years). Furthermore, the text states that of these 282 questionnaires, only "76.2% had completed all items of the QLQ-C30." This means a substantial portion of the original randomized cohort is missing data for this outcome.

2. 3.2. If No/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: The text does not report any sensitivity analyses or specific statistical methods designed to correct for bias due to missing data (other than using linear mixed models for longitudinal analysis). The authors note that "patients in the present analysis were slightly younger and had fewer comorbidities" than the whole PORTEC-2 cohort, indicating a recognized bias in the surviving/responding population, but no statistical correction is provided to confirm the result is unbiased.

3. 3.3. If No/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The outcome is global health (quality of life). The authors explicitly state that older patients with more comorbidities (who inherently might have lower global health scores) "were at higher risk to die of intercurrent disease" or "were not able to respond owing to other reasons, such as vision problems or cognitive disorders." Therefore, missingness is directly linked to the patients' health status.

4. 3.4. If Yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: Although missingness is related to the patients' overall health (age and comorbidities), there is no evidence or mention in the text that the missingness or the reasons for missing data differed *between the two intervention groups* (EBRT vs. VBT). Previous trials (mentioned in the introduction) showed similar rates of overall survival between the arms, suggesting that attrition due to death or aging was likely balanced between the groups, mitigating the likelihood that the missingness severely biased the comparative treatment effect estimate.

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3: Some concerns
Reason: There is a significant amount of missing data, as long-term follow-up inherently lost patients to death, disease, or non-response, and only 76.2% of responders completed the QLQ-C30 fully. While missingness is related to health status (age and comorbidities), there is no evidence provided that attrition was unequal between the treatment arms. Thus, it raises "Some concerns" rather than high risk.

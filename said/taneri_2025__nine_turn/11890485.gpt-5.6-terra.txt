### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** The allocation was made by the patient, or by the caregiver when necessary, drawing one of 100 sealed envelopes. The envelopes contained assignments to either intervention (n = 53) or control (n = 47). Drawing envelopes constitutes a random component in allocation. Although the final 14 participants were allocated using a 2:1 intervention-to-control ratio, the allocation was still conducted through the envelope-drawing procedure rather than a predictable, non-random method.

2. **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   • **Response: Probably yes**  
   • **Reason:** The report states that allocation envelopes were “sealed” and “nontransparent,” and that participants/caregivers drew an envelope for allocation. This indicates that the forthcoming allocation was likely not visible before an envelope was drawn. However, the report does not state that envelopes were sequentially numbered, tamper-proof, or prepared/held by a person independent of enrolment and intervention delivery. Notably, the dementia family care coordinator prepared the envelopes and delivered the intervention. Nevertheless, no explicit evidence indicates that allocations were known or could be manipulated before assignment.

3. **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** Baseline characteristics were reported for all 100 randomized participants. There were no statistically significant differences between groups in patient age, sex, diagnosis, dementia severity, MMSE score, Barthel Index score, or living arrangement. Caregiver characteristics were also similar between groups. There was a numerical imbalance in severe dementia (36% intervention vs 24% control), but this was not statistically significant (P = .244) and is compatible with chance in a small trial. The group-size difference (53 vs 47) was explained by the deliberate 2:1 allocation of the final 14 participants, rather than indicating an unexplained problem with randomization.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Low risk of bias**

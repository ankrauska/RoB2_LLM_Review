**Outcome Name: Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** The article explicitly reports that randomisation was based on “a block size of four, generated with a computer random-number generator by the pharmacist at the coordinating centre.” Computer-generated random numbers constitute a random allocation sequence.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** Allocation was reported to be concealed using “sequentially numbered opaque envelopes.” The pharmacist at the coordinating centre assigned participants according to the randomisation list, and the study capsules were prepared and distributed by the pharmacy. However, the report does not state that envelopes were sealed/tamper-proof or that they were opened only after irreversible enrolment. Thus, the available description supports allocation concealment, but does not provide all details required for a definite “Yes.”

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The groups had equal numbers randomly allocated (59 per group), consistent with the planned 1:1 allocation. Some baseline differences were present: participants allocated to fluoxetine were somewhat older (mean 66.4 versus 62.9 years), more frequently had previous stroke (17% versus 7%), and had a somewhat higher mean baseline FMMS score (17.1 versus 13.4). However, the article describes the groups as otherwise well balanced in demographic characteristics and stroke severity, and there is no indication of a substantial excess of important baseline imbalances or an imbalance so extreme that it is unlikely to be attributable to chance. The investigators adjusted the primary analysis for age, previous stroke, and baseline FMMS score.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

- **Judgement: Low risk of bias**

- **Reason:** The allocation sequence was explicitly computer-generated, and the reported use of sequentially numbered opaque envelopes provides reasonably strong evidence that allocation was concealed. Although some modest baseline differences occurred, these do not by themselves indicate that the randomisation process was compromised.

Although the full prespecified statistical analysis plan is unavailable, there is no evidence that alternative analyses were conducted and selectively withheld because they were less favourable to fluoxetine.

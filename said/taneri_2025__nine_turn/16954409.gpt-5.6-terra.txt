**Outcome Name: clinical pregnancy rate**

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** The article states that the 241 participants were “prospectively randomized using computer-generated random numbers” into the three intervention groups. Computer-generated random numbers constitute a random component in sequence generation.

2. **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   • **Response: Probably no**  
   • **Reason:** The report states that women were allocated on day 2 or 3 of menstruation “with sealed envelopes by specialist nurse.” However, it does not state that envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, tamper-proof, or opened only after the participant had been irreversibly enrolled. Therefore, the reported information does not adequately demonstrate that allocation could not have been foreseen or manipulated by enrolment personnel.

3. **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** The allocated group sizes were similar (81, 80, and 80 participants). Reported baseline characteristics—including age, duration of infertility, BMI, and BMI categories—were similar across groups, with no statistically significant differences reported. There is no apparent baseline imbalance suggesting a problem with randomization.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Some concerns**

**Justification:** Although sequence generation was adequately random, allocation concealment was insufficiently reported. The use of sealed envelopes without essential safeguards—such as opacity, sequential numbering, and tamper-proof sealing—raises concerns that the upcoming allocation could potentially have been predicted or influenced.

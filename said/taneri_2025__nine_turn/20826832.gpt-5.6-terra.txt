### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** After enrolment and baseline assessment, the 500 participants were allocated using “a stratified randomization method blocking by age (50–59; 60–70)” and “a computerized random generator program.” Computer-generated allocation has a random component.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** Women were recruited, consented, and completed the baseline interview before allocation. The complete baseline database was then sent to the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center coordination centre in the United States, which conducted the computerized allocation. Thus, allocation was performed centrally after enrolment rather than by the personnel enrolling participants.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The group sizes were consistent with the intended approximately equal allocation ratio (standard care: 167; high-intensity: 167; low-intensity: 166). The article reports that no significant differences in demographic characteristics were observed between groups at baseline. All enrolled women also met the same key eligibility criterion of no mammogram in the preceding two years according to electronic records. No reported imbalance suggests a problem with randomization.

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

- **Judgement:** Low risk of bias  
- **Reason:** The allocation sequence was computer-generated with age stratification, allocation occurred centrally after enrolment and baseline data collection, and there were no reported baseline imbalances or group-size discrepancies indicating a compromised randomization process.

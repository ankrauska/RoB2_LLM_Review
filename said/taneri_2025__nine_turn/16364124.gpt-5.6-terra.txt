**Outcome Name: visual assessment for erythema and dryness as key symptoms**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** The study reports that allocation of the two coded products to the left and right cubital fossae was conducted according to a randomization scheme prepared by the Department of Statistics and Data Management at proDERM. Randomization used “randomly permuted blocks of fixed size” with validated software (RANCODE 3.6). This is an explicitly random sequence-generation method.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** The products were coded as A and B, and the blinding codes were reportedly kept sealed until final database closure. However, the report does not state how the side-allocation randomization list was concealed from personnel enrolling participants or from the study nurse who applied the products. In particular, it does not describe central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, sequentially numbered containers, or another process ensuring that the forthcoming left/right allocation could not be known before participant assignment. Blinding of product identity does not, by itself, establish concealment of the allocation sequence.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response: No information**
- **Reason:** This was a within-participant paired design: every participant received the emollient-containing hand rub on one cubital fossa and the non-emollient hand rub on the opposite fossa. Therefore, participant-level characteristics (e.g., age, sex, skin type, and atopic status) were inherently the same across the two intervention conditions. However, the article does not provide baseline erythema or dryness scores separately according to the allocated treatment side, nor other side-specific baseline data. Consequently, it is not possible to assess whether there were imbalances between the left/right treatment areas at baseline that might indicate a problem with allocation.

### Domain 1 risk-of-bias judgement: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Random sequence generation was adequately described. However, there is insufficient information to determine whether the allocation sequence was concealed from those enrolling participants and assigning the intervention sides. In addition, baseline outcome data by treatment side were not reported, so side-specific baseline balance cannot be evaluated.

**Outcome Name: visual assessment for erythema and dryness as key symptoms**

### 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** Thirty-five participants were randomized and included in the ITT population. However, the report states that “missing values in 3 participants were replaced by the method of Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF).” Imputed values are considered missing outcome data for this signalling question. Thus, observed outcome data were unavailable for 3/35 participants (8.6%), leaving directly observed data for 32/35 (91.4%). This is below the commonly sufficient threshold of approximately 95% for a continuous outcome, and the report does not establish that the missing data could not materially affect the result.

### 3.2. Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** The authors used LOCF for the three participants with missing values. LOCF does not reliably correct potential bias from missing outcome data and should not be assumed to do so under RoB 2 guidance. The article does not report a sensitivity analysis under plausible missing-data assumptions, nor another analysis method designed to address potential bias from missingness.

### 3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The three participants who did not complete the study per protocol were excluded because of protocol violations: one took antiphlogistics and two disinfected the cubital fossa. Both anti-inflammatory medication and application of an additional disinfectant to the treated skin could be related to, or could affect, the visual signs being measured (erythema and dryness). Therefore, it is plausible that missing outcome data could be associated with the true outcome values.

### 3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** Although missingness could be related to the outcome, the report does not show that participants withdrew or had missing data because they developed worse erythema or dryness. The documented reasons were protocol violations rather than reported skin reactions or deterioration in the study outcome. Furthermore, this was a paired within-participant trial, in which each participant received both products, so participant-level missingness would affect both intervention conditions rather than clearly causing differential missingness between products. Nevertheless, the report does not provide sufficient details to exclude a relationship between missingness and true outcome values.

### Domain 3 risk-of-bias judgement: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Outcome data were imputed using LOCF for 3/35 randomized participants, and no analysis or sensitivity analysis was reported to demonstrate that the result was robust to missing data. Missingness could plausibly be associated with the true erythema or dryness outcome because protocol violations involved anti-inflammatory treatment or use of disinfectant on the treated area. However, there is not enough evidence that missingness was likely driven by outcome severity or that it differed between the paired intervention conditions.

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** The article reports that an offsite investigator prepared the concealed randomization using a **computer-generated random number sequence**, with a 1:1 allocation to active tRNS plus reaching training or sham tRNS plus reaching training. The trial registry reports the same method.

2. **1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   • **Response: Probably yes**  
   • **Reason:** Allocation was prepared by an offsite investigator, and consecutively numbered opaque envelopes containing allocation assignments were collected by the tRNS intervener only **after the initial assessment**. This supports that allocation was concealed from personnel undertaking enrolment, assessment, and training until after baseline assessment. Although the report does not explicitly state that the envelopes were sealed and tamper-proof, the described offsite preparation and use of consecutively numbered opaque envelopes provide reasonable evidence of allocation concealment.

3. **1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** The intended 1:1 allocation was achieved: two participants received active tRNS and two received sham tRNS. Individual participant baseline characteristics varied, including age, mobility, disability, and baseline MAS6 scores, but the sample comprised only four participants. The article does not report an imbalance that clearly indicates a non-random or compromised allocation process. Differences observed in such an extremely small pilot sample are compatible with chance.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Low risk of bias**  
• The allocation sequence was computer-generated, allocation concealment was likely adequate, and there is no persuasive evidence from the baseline data that the randomization process was compromised.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
### Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention

The effect of **assignment to active tRNS plus reaching training versus sham tRNS plus reaching training** is assessed. This is appropriate because the study randomized participants to intervention groups and described group-specific outcomes.

2. **2.2. Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** The tRNS intervener was necessarily unblinded and accessed the allocation envelopes after baseline assessment. This person delivered active tRNS at 2 mA or sham tRNS at 0 mA and maintained a separate tRNS log. Although the assessor and reaching-training personnel were blinded, at least one person involved in delivering the allocated stimulation knew group assignment.

4. **2.4. If yes/probably yes to 2.3: Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?**

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** This question cannot be meaningfully assessed because the presence and nature of trial-context deviations were not reported.

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** This question cannot be meaningfully assessed because there is no information indicating whether relevant deviations occurred or whether any such deviations differed between active and sham tRNS groups.

6. **2.6. Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**

   • **Response: Probably no**  
   • **Reason:** Although all four randomized participants completed training and follow-up, the publication reports results as four individual cases rather than conducting a comparison of MAS6 outcomes between randomized groups. The protocol specified an intention-to-treat, two-group by three-phase mixed-effects analysis followed by between-group comparisons. This planned analysis was not reported in the article. Consequently, the analysis presented does not appropriately estimate the effect of assignment to active versus sham tRNS on MAS6.

The article provides no evidence of treatment crossover, exclusion after randomization, or analysis of participants in the wrong group. Therefore, there is no reported failure to retain participants in their randomized groups that would itself be likely to have substantially affected the result. The concern is instead that no formal randomized between-group effect estimate was presented.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 2: Some concerns**

• Participants and the reaching-training personnel were blinded, and completion was complete, with no reported crossover or dropout. However, the tRNS intervener was unblinded, information about possible deviations arising from this trial context is insufficient, and the publication did not report the protocol-specified intention-to-treat between-group analysis for MAS6.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

1. **5.1. Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** The trial was prospectively registered (ACTRN12614000952640), and the registry specified a primary outcome of a **composite score of MAS items 6–8**. It also specified an intention-to-treat, non-parametric mixed-effects analysis in a two-group (active/sham) × three-timepoint design, followed by between-group comparisons. In contrast, the publication identifies MAS6 alone as the primary clinical outcome and presents individual participant MAS6 values and within-person changes. It does not report the registered composite MAS6–8 outcome or the pre-specified randomized between-group mixed-effects analysis. Thus, the reported MAS6 result was not analysed in accordance with the available pre-specified analysis plan.

2. **5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**

   • **Response: Probably yes**  
   • **Reason:** The prospectively registered primary outcome was the composite score from MAS items 6, 7, and 8. The publication instead designates and reports MAS6 alone as the primary outcome. MAS item 7 (hand movements), MAS item 8 (advanced hand activities), and the registered composite MAS6–8 score were all eligible measures of upper-limb motor function within the relevant outcome domain. The publication does not provide the MAS7 or MAS8 results, the composite score, or an explanation for changing from the registered composite primary outcome to MAS6 alone. This unexplained outcome-measurement discrepancy raises a substantial concern that MAS6 was selected from multiple eligible measurements.

3. **5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?**

   • **Response: Probably yes**  
   • **Reason:** The protocol specified an intention-to-treat mixed-effects analysis with a comparison between the active and sham groups over three time points. The publication instead reports individual case results and descriptive within-participant changes, without the pre-specified between-group analysis. The report gives no rationale for this change in analysis approach. Although the very small recruited sample may have affected feasibility of the planned analysis, the available report does not explain why the planned analysis was not conducted or provide the results of that analysis. This leaves a substantial possibility that the analysis reported was selected after examining the data.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 5: High risk of bias**

• The prospectively registered primary outcome and analysis plan differ materially from the outcome and analysis reported in the publication. The registered composite MAS6–8 outcome and planned randomized between-group analysis were not reported, while MAS6 was presented alone using descriptive individual-case results without explanation for these changes.

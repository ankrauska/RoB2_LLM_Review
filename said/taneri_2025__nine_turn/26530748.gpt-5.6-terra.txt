### 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Global-health outcome data were not available for all or nearly all 427 randomized participants. The long-term analysis was restricted to prior questionnaire responders who were alive and disease-free according to the trial database. Of 427 randomized patients, baseline and at least one follow-up questionnaire were available for 348 (81%). At 7 years, 202 evaluable questionnaires were available; at 10 years, 80 questionnaires were returned. Thus, substantial outcome-data attrition occurred relative to the randomized population, well beyond the level generally regarded as “nearly all” for a continuous outcome.

### 3.2. If no/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The authors used linear mixed models for longitudinal EORTC QLQ-C30 analyses, but the report does not describe methods designed to correct possible bias from missing Global health data under plausible missing-not-at-random mechanisms. No sensitivity analyses assessing the robustness of the Global health findings under alternative assumptions about missing data are reported. The analysis eligibility criterion also excluded patients who were not prior responders and those who were not alive and disease-free at long-term follow-up.

### 3.3. If no/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** Missingness could plausibly be associated with true Global health status. Such events may be associated with poorer health-related quality of life, including Global health. Moreover, compared with the full PORTEC-2 cohort, long-term responders were reported to be slightly younger and to have fewer comorbidities, characteristics that may be related to Global health scores. Non-response may also be related to poor health, impaired function, cognitive problems, or inability to complete questionnaires.

These factors are likely to be associated with poorer Global health. The article does not report sensitivity analyses or other analyses demonstrating that the missing-data mechanism did not affect the estimated treatment effect.

### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

The report provides no analysis or sensitivity analysis demonstrating that missing data did not bias the result.

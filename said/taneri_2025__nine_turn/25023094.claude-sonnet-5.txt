- **Response: No information**

The term "sex-stratified randomization" describes a stratification approach but does not clarify how the sequence itself was randomly generated. Per the guideline, a bare statement that the study is "randomized" (or in this case, a description of stratification without generation detail) is insufficient to answer "Yes." Therefore, "No information" is the appropriate judgment.

## 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The article states, "the allocation sequence was blinded through the use of sealed envelopes." This indicates an attempt at concealment consistent with common allocation concealment practice. However, the article does not explicitly confirm that the envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, or tamper-proof, nor does it describe who opened the envelopes and when in relation to enrollment. Given the partial information provided (sealed envelopes used specifically for concealment purposes) but lack of full procedural detail, "Probably yes" is judged more appropriate than "Yes."

## 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response: No**

No imbalance appears large enough to suggest a flawed randomization process for the VAS outcome specifically.

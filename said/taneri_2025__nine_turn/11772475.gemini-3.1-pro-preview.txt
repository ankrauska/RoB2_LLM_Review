Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention

1. 2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?
   * Response: Yes
   * Reason: The nature of the interventions (routine care vs. clinic-based 28-day methadone vs. office-based 28-day methadone) makes it impossible to blind participants. Participants were aware of their reporting schedules and dispensing locations.

2. 2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?
   * Response: Yes
   * Reason: Blinding the personnel delivering the interventions was impossible given the distinct treatment settings (clinic vs. physician's office) and differing reporting schedules (routine vs. 28-day). 

3. 2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?
   * Response: Probably yes
   * Reason: The text explicitly notes that 3 patients dropped out due to "dislike of the frequency of the recall procedure," which is a trial-specific monitoring context. Additionally, 5 patients withdrew immediately "upon assignment to study condition," suggesting disappointment or disagreement with their randomized allocation. 

4. 2.4 Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?
   * Response: Yes
   * Reason: The outcome being evaluated is "treatment retention". Withdrawing or dropping out of the study directly dictates the outcome of treatment retention.

5. 2.5 Were these deviations from intended intervention balanced between groups?
   * Response: No
   * Reason: The immediate post-randomization withdrawals were unbalanced: 4 were assigned to the routine treatment condition and 1 to the office-based condition (0 in clinic-based). 

6. 2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?
   * Response: No
   * Reason: The authors report an "intent-to-treat sample (n=73)". However, they state that "A total of 78 patients were randomized into the study... A total of five patients withdrew from the study upon assignment to study condition". The RoB 2 guidelines state that analyses excluding eligible trial participants post-randomization should be considered inappropriate. 

7. 2.7 Was there potential for a substantial impact (on the result) of the failure to analyse participants in the group to which they were randomized?
   * Response: Yes
   * Reason: Excluding the 5 participants who immediately withdrew upon assignment (4 of whom were in the routine group of 25 randomized) artificially inflates the retention rate in the routine arm. Since 16% of the routine arm was excluded post-randomization before calculating the retention outcome, this failure has a substantial potential impact on the comparative results.

Risk of Bias Judgment for Domain 2 (Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions): High
* Reason: Participants and personnel were not blinded, there were deviations (withdrawals) directly tied to the trial context, the post-randomization exclusions were unbalanced, and an inappropriate modified ITT analysis was used which likely impacted the treatment retention outcome.

Outcome Name: treatment retention

* Overall predicted direction of bias: Favours comparator
* Reason: The post-randomization exclusion of 5 participants who withdrew immediately upon group assignment disproportionately removed dropouts from the routine care (comparator) arm (4 out of 5 exclusions). If these participants had been properly analyzed using true intention-to-treat (ITT) principles, the retention rate for the routine care group would have been substantially lower (19/25 = 76% instead of the reported 19/21 = 90%), while the experimental groups' retention rates would have remained relatively similar (clinic-based: 24/27 = 89%; office-based: 23/26 = 88%). Therefore, the inappropriate exclusions artificially inflated the performance of the comparator group, biasing the results in its favor.

* Overall Risk-of-bias judgement: High
* Reason: The study is rated at High risk of bias overall because it carries a High risk of bias in Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions) and Domain 3 (Missing outcome data) due to the unbalanced post-randomization exclusion of participants directly tied to their failure to be retained in the assigned treatment. Additionally, there are "Some concerns" in Domain 1 (Randomization process) and Domain 5 (Selection of the reported result).

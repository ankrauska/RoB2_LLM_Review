Based on the article provided, here is an analysis of the key elements related to a systematic review:

### Study Overview
- **Purpose:** To evaluate the difference in spherical aberration (SA) induced by aspheric (Aberration Smart Ablation [ASA]) vs. non-aspheric (Tissue Saving Ablation [TSA]) myopic ablation profiles using the MEL80 excimer laser.
- **Design:** Prospective, randomized, paired-eye controlled study.
- **Participants:** 15 patients (30 eyes) undergoing myopic LASIK.
- **Intervention:** LASIK using the MEL80 excimer laser and Hansatome microkeratome. One eye treated with ASA, the fellow eye with TSA (randomly assigned). Target refraction was plano with a 6 mm optical zone.

### Key Findings
1. **Refractive Outcomes:**
   - Both groups showed excellent and comparable safety, efficacy, and predictability.
   - Mean preoperative spherical equivalent (SEQ): ASA = -2.43 ± 1.53 D; TSA = -2.54 ± 1.47 D (p = 0.87).
   - Efficacy: 100% of eyes were 20/25 or better in both groups.
   - Safety: No eyes lost two or more lines of CDVA.

2. **Spherical Aberration (SA) Induction:**
   - **ASA Group:** SA increased by 33% at the 6 mm zone (increase of 0.032 µm). Induction per diopter was 0.024 µm/D.
   - **TSA Group:** SA increased by 44% at the 6 mm zone (increase of 0.069 µm). Induction per diopter was 0.035 µm/D.
   - **Comparison:** There was statistically significantly less induction of SA in the ASA group for analysis zones from 4.5 to 7.0 mm (p < 0.05) compared to the TSA group.

3. **RAWS Parameter (Area under the SA curve):**
   - ASA group: 34.8% increase.
   - TSA group: 74.4% increase.
   - This indicates a greater overall exposure to SA across varying pupil sizes in the non-aspheric group.

4. **Other Aberrations:**
   - No statistically significant difference in coma (RMS) changes for either group at the 6 mm zone.

### Methodological Quality & Limitations (for Systematic Review Assessment)
- **Strengths:** 
  - Paired-eye design minimizes inter-subject confounding factors (e.g., wound healing, age, baseline characteristics).
  - Randomized allocation of the treatment to the eyes prevents selection bias.
  - Standardized optical zone (6 mm) and target refraction across all eyes.
- **Limitations:**
  - Small sample size (15 patients / 30 eyes), though post hoc power analysis showed 0.60–0.85 power.
  - Relatively low magnitude of myopia treated (range: -0.50 to -5.00 D), which may limit the generalizability to higher degrees of myopia where SA induction is expected to be more pronounced.
  - Lack of subjective psychophysical assessments (e.g., contrast sensitivity, subjective night vision questionnaires), so the clinical/symptomatic impact of this objective difference in SA is not directly measured.

### Conclusion of the Article
The aspheric ablation profile (ASA) induces significantly less spherical aberration compared to the non-aspheric profile (TSA) following myopic LASIK, which may translate to a lower risk of night vision disturbances, particularly in patients with larger pupils or those requiring higher myopic corrections.

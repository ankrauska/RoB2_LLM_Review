1. 4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: The List of attributions task (LAT) was designed specifically to capture the participants' self-generated attributions for fatigue, their rankings, and their perceived likelihoods. There is no evidence that this method is fundamentally inappropriate or lacks sensitivity to detect changes in self-reported attributions.

2. 4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: Both the fatigue information group and the control group completed the exact same LAT measure at the same time points (pre- and post-intervention) within the identical 1-hour laboratory session context.

3. 4.3 If No/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: For the LAT, which is a participant-reported outcome, the outcome assessor is the study participant. The participants were aware of the intervention they received because they had just read the information sheet provided to them. (Note: While the volunteer who coded the responses was blinded, the participants generating the outcome data were not).

4. 4.4 If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: Participant-reported attributions can easily be influenced by the knowledge of the intervention, especially since the intervention explicitly provided information about the causes of fatigue. The authors themselves note in the discussion that "it is also possible that demand characteristics or priming played a role in FI participants' post-intervention responses on the LAT."

5. 4.5 If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The trial involved participants reading a psychoeducational sheet about causes of fatigue and then immediately filling out a chart asking them to list the causes of their fatigue. This design makes it highly likely that the assessment of the outcome was directly influenced by the knowledge of the intervention received (via priming or demand characteristics), as they were essentially asked to report on the very concepts they had just been taught. 

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: High

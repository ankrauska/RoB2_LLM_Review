**1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The article states that randomization sequences were placed in "individual presealed envelopes" and that "the pharmacy implemented masking and random assignment of participants to sertraline or placebo conditions and was responsible for dispensing medications with identical markings to the project physicians for distribution." This indicates that allocation was controlled by an independent pharmacy, separate from the enrolling clinicians/researchers, and medications were identically packaged (25-mg quantities with same markings). This suggests adequate concealment; however, the article does not explicitly confirm the envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, or tamper-proof, so I cannot select a definitive "Yes."

**1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** There is an internal inconsistency in the article regarding baseline balance. In the Results section, the authors state: "No significant differences were found between drug and placebo groups on sex; age; education; race; income; etiology of injury; time since injury; injury severity; or baseline measures of depression, anxiety, or QOL." However, the **Abstract** explicitly states: "The income level of those receiving placebo was significantly lower than those participants receiving medication," and this is reiterated in the **Discussion** section, which extensively discusses how lower socioeconomic status (SES) may have affected "prestudy access to regular medical care, including treatment for depression." This is a **key prognostic factor** for depression outcomes (as the authors themselves argue in the Discussion when explaining group differences), and its significant imbalance directly contradicts the Results-section claim of "no significant differences." Additionally, Table 1 shows other notable (though not formally tested) differences that could be clinically meaningful, such as mild TBI representation (20% sertraline vs. 50% placebo) and sex distribution (45.5% vs. 63.2% male), which are not trivial given that injury severity and sex could plausibly interact with depression treatment response. Given this documented significant imbalance in a prognostic factor (income) that the authors themselves link to depression outcomes, this raises genuine concern that randomization may not have fully balanced groups on factors relevant to the outcome.

## Domain-Level Judgement for Domain 1: **Some concerns**

**Justification:** The randomization sequence generation method (1.1) was adequately described as random (computer-generated), and the allocation concealment process (1.2) appears reasonably robust (independent pharmacy control, presealed envelopes, identical packaging), warranting a "Probably yes."

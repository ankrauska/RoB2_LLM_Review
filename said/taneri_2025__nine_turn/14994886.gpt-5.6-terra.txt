**Outcome Name: initial pain**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response:** No information  
- **Reason:** The article states that “the patients were randomly selected for insertion of the 0.014 or 0.016 inch wires.” However, it does not describe how the allocation sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, sealed-envelope draw, coin toss). Therefore, the statement alone is insufficient to confirm that a random component was used.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response:** No information  
- **Reason:** The report provides no information on allocation concealment. It does not state that allocation was centrally administered, performed using opaque sequentially numbered sealed envelopes, or otherwise protected from knowledge by the personnel enrolling participants or assigning the initial archwire.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response:** No information  
- **Reason:** The intervention groups were of similar size (0.014-inch group: 56 participants; 0.016-inch group: 53 participants), which does not itself suggest an important allocation imbalance. Sex distribution and the numbers receiving wires in one versus both arches are reported for each group, but comprehensive baseline characteristics—particularly characteristics potentially relevant to initial pain, such as malocclusion severity/crowding—were not reported by intervention group. Indeed, the authors state that dental crowding was not evaluated. Thus, there is insufficient baseline information to determine whether there were meaningful imbalances indicative of a problematic randomization process.

### Domain 1 risk-of-bias judgement: **Some concerns**

- **Reason:** Although participants were described as “randomly selected,” the method used to generate the allocation sequence was not reported, and allocation concealment was not described. The available group sizes do not themselves indicate a randomization problem, but the lack of adequate information about sequence generation and concealment gives rise to some concerns about bias arising from the randomization process.

No information in the article indicates that the questionnaire or VAS was incapable of detecting relevant differences between the two wire groups.

### 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** Both intervention groups received the same questionnaire after placement of their assigned archwire, were instructed how to complete it, and reported the time at which initial pain was first perceived using the same response options. The outcome was assessed over the same period after archwire insertion in both groups. The article does not report different outcome-assessment procedures, thresholds, timing, or follow-up methods between groups.

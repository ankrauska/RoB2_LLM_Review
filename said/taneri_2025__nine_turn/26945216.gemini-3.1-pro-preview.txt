Outcome Name: Pain intensity

4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?
*   Response: No
*   Reason: Pain intensity was measured using a 100-mm visual analog scale (VAS). The VAS is a widely used and validated tool for assessing pain, and the authors provide a reference supporting its reliability.

4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?
*   Response: No
*   Reason: The study states that all measurements were taken by the principal investigator at the exact same specified time points (baseline, immediately after the first treatment, before the eighth treatment, and 1 wk after the eighth treatment) for all three groups using the same method.

4.3 If No/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?
*   Response: Yes
*   Reason: For a participant-reported outcome like the VAS, the participant is the outcome assessor. As evaluated previously, participants were aware of their interventions because the control group did not receive a sham device, and the active groups felt a strong electrical sensation. Furthermore, the article explicitly states, "The principal investigator acted as both outcome assessor, taking all study measurements, and as therapist administering all study treatment," meaning the investigator recording the outcomes was also fully unblinded.

4.4 If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
*   Response: Yes
*   Reason: Pain intensity is a highly subjective, participant-reported outcome. Knowledge of receiving an active electrical stimulation device (TENS or IFT) compared to just standard care can easily influence a participant's perception and reporting of pain due to expectation or placebo effects.

4.5 If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
*   Response: Yes
*   Reason: The control group received no device (no placebo/sham), while the intervention groups received a device producing a "strong but nonpainful" sensation. This lack of blinding for a subjective symptom (pain) makes it highly likely that expectations influenced the reporting. Additionally, the principal investigator, who may have had a vested interest in the therapy's success, administered the treatments and personally collected the VAS measurements from the patients, which creates a high-risk environment for influencing the outcome assessment.

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4
*   Response: High
*   Reason: Both the participants (who report the subjective pain score) and the single investigator (who administered treatment and collected the data) were aware of the assigned interventions. Because the outcome is subjective and no sham device was used for the control group, it is highly likely that knowledge of the intervention influenced the outcome assessment.

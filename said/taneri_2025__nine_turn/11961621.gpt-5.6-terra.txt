**Outcome Name: length of hospital stay**

### 4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Length of hospital stay was measured as the number of days from surgery to discharge. This is an appropriate and directly observable measure for the specified outcome. The article reports hospital stay in days for both intervention groups.

### 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?

- **Response: Probably no**
- **Reason:** The outcome was the duration of the same index hospital admission in both groups, reported in days. The article does not describe different measurement methods, different discharge-data sources, or different outcome-assessment time points between the LAVH and AH groups.

### 4.3. Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** Blinding of participants and clinical staff was not feasible for visibly different surgical procedures. Further, the article explicitly states that “each patient decided for herself when she was ready to be discharged from hospital.” Thus, participants—who contributed directly to determination of discharge timing—were aware of the procedure received. The treating clinicians also necessarily knew the surgical approach.

### 4.4. Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** Hospital discharge was not determined solely using an objective prespecified discharge criterion: each participant decided when she was ready for discharge. A participant’s perception of recovery and readiness for discharge, and clinicians’ agreement with discharge, could be influenced by knowledge of whether she had received LAVH or abdominal hysterectomy.

### 4.5. Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The trial was unblinded, and discharge timing depended explicitly on the participant’s own decision about readiness. The authors also report substantially lower postoperative pain after LAVH and note that patients could not be blinded and that expectations may have influenced recuperation or satisfaction. Given the participant-driven discharge decision, knowledge of the surgical procedure was likely capable of influencing the recorded hospital stay.

### Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

- **Judgement: High risk of bias**
- **Justification:** Although length of hospital stay is recorded in days using a comparable method in both groups, the timing of discharge was explicitly determined by unblinded participants’ perceived readiness. Because knowledge of the received procedure could influence this judgment-based discharge decision, outcome assessment was likely influenced by intervention awareness.

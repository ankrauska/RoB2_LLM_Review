Outcome Name: women’s burden and distress

**Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data**

3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?
* Response: No
* Reason: The article states that 61 participants were randomized, but only 44 participants provided assessed data at the 6-month follow-up. This represents an overall attrition of approximately 28% (17 out of 61) by the follow-up period, meaning data were not available for all or nearly all participants.

3.2 If No/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
* Response: No
* Reason: The authors did not report conducting any sensitivity analyses or using advanced analytical methods (such as multiple imputation or inverse probability weighting) to correct for bias or demonstrate that the results were robust to plausible assumptions about the missing data. They merely analyzed the available data using repeated-measures ANOVAs.

3.3 If No/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
* Response: Yes
* Reason: The outcome being assessed is the women's burden and distress. The authors reported that most women who dropped out did so because they "left their partners soon after beginning the programme" and "declined further assessment." Experiencing a major life event like leaving a severe problem-drinker partner is highly likely to affect a person's level of distress and burden, meaning the missingness could easily be related to the true value of the outcome.

3.4 If Yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
* Response: Yes
* Reason: The specific reason provided for the missing data (women leaving their marriages) provides direct evidence that the missingness is related to the outcome. The authors themselves acknowledge this by stating, "It would be helpful to know if the women who terminated their relationships reported better mental health and quality of life after enacting their decision." Because these women left a highly distressing environment but were excluded from the analysis, it is highly likely that their missing distress scores (which might have improved significantly) depend on their true values, systematically skewing the results of the remaining sample.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 3:** High

**Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome**

4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?
* Response: No
* Reason: The outcome was measured using the Relative Stress Scale (RSS) and the General Health Questionnaire (GHQ). The article states both instruments have established validity and high reliability for assessing burden and psychological distress, making them appropriate measures.

4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?
* Response: No
* Reason: All three treatment conditions were assessed using the same standard self-report questionnaires (RSS and GHQ) at the exact same time points (pre-treatment, post-treatment, and at 6-month follow-up). There is no indication that the method of data collection differed across the groups.

4.3 If No/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?
* Response: Yes
* Reason: Because the RSS and GHQ are self-reported measures, the outcome assessors are the study participants themselves. As established in Domain 2, the participants were aware of the behavioral and psychological interventions they were receiving, as blinding was not possible.

4.4 If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
* Response: Yes
* Reason: Burden and psychological distress are subjective outcomes. Knowledge of receiving a specific active treatment versus a supportive control could potentially influence how participants perceive and report their own stress levels (e.g., due to expectancy effects). 

4.5 If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
* Response: Probably no
* Reason: Although the participants were unblinded, the study utilized "supportive counselling" as a "plausible contact control" to establish specific treatment effects. Participants in all three conditions received the same high level of therapist contact (15 1-hour sessions). Because all participants were engaged in an extensive, credible therapeutic process, it is unlikely that the mere knowledge of their specific group assignment substantially and differentially biased their self-reported distress scores, unlike what might be expected if they were compared to a waitlist or no-treatment control group.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4:** Some concerns

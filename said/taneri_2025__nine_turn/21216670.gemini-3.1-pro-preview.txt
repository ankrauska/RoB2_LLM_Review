Outcome Name: Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale

1. 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The authors state in the Methods section that "Randomisation was balanced by centre, with an allocation sequence based on a block size of four, generated with a computer random-number generator by the pharmacist at the coordinating centre". This confirms a true random element was used.

2. 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?
   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The text explicitly states under Randomisation and masking that "Allocation was concealed by use of sequentially numbered opaque envelopes." Additionally, the pharmacist assigned the participants in accordance with the randomisation list, and the placebo was identical in appearance and packaging to the active drug, ensuring proper concealment before assignment.

3. 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: Although the authors report some baseline imbalances ("mean age was slightly higher and previous history of stroke was more frequent in the fluoxetine group... FMMS score at inclusion was higher in the fluoxetine group"), these differences in a relatively small sample size (n=118) are fully compatible with chance. Given that the trial used robust methods for random sequence generation and allocation concealment, these imbalances do not suggest a systematic flaw or failure in the randomization process itself.

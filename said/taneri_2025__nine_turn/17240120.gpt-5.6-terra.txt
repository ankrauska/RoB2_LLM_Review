### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?**

   - **Response: Yes**
   - **Reason:** The article explicitly reports that, after the open-label phase, eligible participants were assigned to escitalopram or placebo “according to a **computer-generated randomisation list**.” Randomization was conducted in blocks of four, which does not negate the reported random sequence generation.

2. **1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   - **Response: Probably yes**
   - **Reason:** The report states that the randomization-series details were unknown to investigators and were kept in “a set of **sealed opaque envelopes**.” At each centre, sequentially enrolled participants were assigned the lowest available randomization number. These features support allocation concealment. However, the report does not explicitly state that envelopes were sequentially numbered, tamper-proof, or opened only after irreversible participant enrolment; therefore, “Probably yes,” rather than “Yes,” is appropriate.

3. **1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   - **Response: No**
   - **Reason:** The double-blind groups were similar in size (placebo: 157; escitalopram: 163), consistent with the intended 1:1 allocation. The authors report no differences between groups in demographic or clinical parameters at randomization. The reported baseline characteristics—including age, sex, race, OCD onset/duration, and symptom measures at randomization—do not indicate an important imbalance suggesting a flawed randomization process.

### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

1. **3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**

   - **Response: Probably no**
   - **Reason:** The time-to-relapse analysis included 320 randomized participants. During the double-blind phase, 13/163 (7.9%) escitalopram participants and 14/157 (8.9%) placebo participants withdrew for reasons other than relapse. These participants would have had their time-to-relapse data censored at withdrawal and therefore had incomplete outcome follow-up. Thus, outcome data were not available for all participants. Although the proportion censored for non-relapse reasons was relatively modest and similar between groups, the report does not provide enough detail to establish that the missing observations could not have made an important difference to the estimated effect.

2. **3.2. If no/probably no to 3.1: Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**

   - **Response: No**
   - **Reason:** The article reports the numbers withdrawing for reasons other than relapse but does not report analyses specifically addressing the possible effect of censoring due to these withdrawals. The reported sensitivity analyses censored relapses occurring in the first 7 or 14 days after randomization to investigate possible discontinuation effects; they did not assess whether censoring from withdrawal or loss to follow-up biased the time-to-relapse comparison. No sensitivity analysis under plausible informative-censoring assumptions was reported.

3. **3.3. If no/probably no to 3.2: Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**

   - **Response: Yes**
   - **Reason:** In this time-to-event analysis, participants who withdrew before relapse were censored and therefore had incomplete relapse follow-up. In an OCD relapse-prevention trial, withdrawal may plausibly be related to emerging or worsening obsessive-compulsive symptoms, perceived lack of benefit, or other health-related factors associated with subsequent relapse risk. The article does not provide sufficiently detailed withdrawal reasons or evidence to rule out such a relationship.

4. **3.4. If yes/probably yes to 3.3: Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**

   - **Response: Probably no**
   - **Reason:** The proportions withdrawing for reasons other than relapse were low and closely similar between groups: 7.9% with escitalopram and 8.9% with placebo. However, the available information remains limited.

**Outcome Name: occurrence of cardiac death**

### 1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The study states that, after successful guidewire crossing, participants were “randomly assigned” in a 1:1 ratio to IVUS-guided or angiography-guided intervention using an “interactive web-based response system,” with stratification by participating centre. Although the specific random-sequence generation method (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, block randomization) was not reported, use of a centralized interactive web-based randomization system and centre stratification provides reasonable support that a random allocation component was used.

### 1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?

- **Response:** Yes  
- **Reason:** Allocation was conducted using an interactive web-based response system after participants had undergone successful guidewire crossing and met eligibility criteria. This is a centrally administered allocation method, which would conceal the upcoming allocation from personnel enrolling participants until assignment.

### 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The intervention groups had identical sample sizes (201 per group), consistent with the intended 1:1 allocation. Reported baseline clinical and angiographic characteristics were generally balanced, with no statistically significant differences. The distribution of one-, two-, and three-vessel disease differed numerically but was not statistically significant (P=0.12), and no important imbalance in a reported prognostic factor or outcome-related baseline measure suggests a flawed randomization process. Differences in post-randomization procedural management, such as more frequent high-pressure post-stent dilation in the IVUS group, are not baseline differences and do not indicate a problem with randomization.

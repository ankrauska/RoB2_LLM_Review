**Outcome Name: reoperation rate**

### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1. Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** The primary outcome was clearly defined as repeat wide local excision, mastectomy at further operation within 6 months of randomization, or a pathologically avoidable mastectomy at the initial operation. Repeat operations are objectively identifiable from surgical and clinical follow-up records. Pathologically avoidable initial mastectomies were assessed using pre-established criteria based on MRI findings and histopathology. These are appropriate methods for ascertaining the outcome.

**4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**

- **Response:** No  
- **Reason:** Clinical follow-up data were collected at the same scheduled intervals for both groups—at 6 and 12 months after surgery and annually thereafter. The main outcome components, including further surgery within 6 months and initial surgery type, were obtained from surgical and clinical records in both groups. Although MRI findings could change clinical management in the MRI group, this represents an effect of the assigned strategy on surgical decision-making rather than use of a different method or threshold for outcome ascertainment between groups.

**4.3. If no/probably no to 4.1 and 4.2: Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The trial was open label, and the outcome included surgery and reoperation decisions made by treating clinical teams who would generally know whether MRI had been performed. In addition, the Data Monitoring and Ethics Committee reviewed imaging and histopathological findings for participants undergoing initial mastectomy to identify pathologically avoidable mastectomy. The report does not state that this committee was blinded to intervention assignment; assessment of MRI-related avoidable mastectomy inherently required knowledge of MRI findings.

**4.4. If yes/probably yes to 4.3: Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** Most primary-outcome events—further wide local excision or mastectomy within 6 months—are objective surgical events documented in clinical records. Although clinical teams were aware of MRI status and their surgical decisions could be influenced by MRI findings, this is part of the intended effect of preoperative MRI on management, rather than biased measurement of whether subsequent surgery occurred. The avoidable-mastectomy component was determined using specified pathological and imaging criteria, reducing scope for subjective outcome assessment.

**4.5. If yes/probably yes to 4.4: Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

- **Response:** Not applicable  
- **Reason:** Assessment of the outcome was judged probably not to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment (Question 4.4).

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: Low risk of bias**

- **Justification:** Reoperation and mastectomy are objective, clinically documented outcomes, collected during comparable follow-up in both randomized groups. While the trial was open label and assessors were probably aware of MRI assignment, there is no evidence that this knowledge materially biased ascertainment of the primary outcome.

### Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1. Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?**

- **Response:** Probably yes  
- **Reason:** The trial was registered (ISRCTN 57474502), and the report states that the COMICE protocol was written in 2001, before recruitment began in February 2002. The article explicitly identifies the reoperation-rate composite as the primary clinical outcome and describes the planned primary analysis: logistic regression adjusted for the minimisation factors, analysed according to intention to treat. However, the full dated protocol and/or statistical analysis plan is not included in the provided article, so it cannot be directly verified that the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data became available.

**5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** The primary outcome was explicitly defined as the proportion of participants who underwent further wide local excision or mastectomy within 6 months of randomisation, or had a pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial surgery. The same composite outcome definition is consistently described in the Methods, Findings, Results, and Discussion. The report also presents the individual surgical components of the composite outcome in Table 3. There is no indication that the authors measured several alternative reoperation-rate definitions or time points and selectively reported the most favourable result.

**5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?**

- **Response:** Probably no  
- **Reason:** The analysis method was specified in the Methods: intention-to-treat logistic regression adjusted for the minimisation factors. The reported effect estimate for the primary outcome corresponds to this stated analysis (odds ratio 0.96, 95% CI 0.75–1.24; p=0.77). The article does not suggest that multiple alternative primary analyses were conducted and that only a selected favourable analysis was reported. Although the full statistical analysis plan is not available in the article, the reported analysis is consistent with the stated pre-specified analytical approach.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 5: Low risk of bias**

- **Justification:** The primary reoperation-rate outcome, its composite definition, its 6-month time frame, and the intention-to-treat adjusted logistic-regression analysis were described in the report and were consistent with the stated trial protocol and registration. The full protocol/analysis plan is not provided, but there is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple outcome measurements or analyses based on the findings.

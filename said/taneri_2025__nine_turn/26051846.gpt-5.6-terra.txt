**Outcome Name: pain VAS scale post-treatment**

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** The article states that each participant was allocated randomly using “a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method,” and that this process was undertaken by a statistician. Random permuted blocks include a random component in sequence generation and are an acceptable random allocation method.

2. **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** Although the allocation list was generated by a statistician who was not involved in data collection or analysis, the report does not describe how allocation was implemented or concealed. There is no report of central/remote allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, restricted access to the allocation list, or another procedure ensuring that enrolling personnel could not foresee upcoming assignments. Independence of the statistician who generated the sequence does not itself establish allocation concealment.

3. **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** Group sizes were close to the intended 1:1:1 allocation ratio (ultrasound, 32; phonophoresis, 33; iontophoresis, 34). The reported baseline demographic and clinical characteristics showed no statistically significant differences between groups: age (*P* = 0.42), sex (*P* = 0.67), symptom duration (*P* = 0.51), dominant-side involvement (*P* = 0.75), and lesion type (*P* = 0.60). Baseline VAS scores were also reasonably similar: 5.7 (SD 1.8), 5.7 (SD 1.5), and 6.3 (SD 2.0), respectively. The reported baseline information therefore does not indicate problematic randomization.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Some concerns**

• **Reason:** The random allocation sequence was adequately generated, and reported baseline data do not suggest that randomization failed. However, allocation concealment was not described. Consequently, it cannot be established that treatment assignment was concealed from personnel enrolling participants before allocation.

### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

1. **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** Pain was measured using a 0–10 visual analogue scale (VAS), where 0 represented no pain and 10 the worst pain. This is an appropriate and commonly used measure for pain severity in people with shoulder pain. Nothing reported indicates that the scale was incapable of detecting plausible treatment effects or had poor validity for this outcome.

2. **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** VAS pain was assessed in all groups at the same scheduled time points: baseline, post-treatment, and 1 month post-treatment. The report states that all assessment visits were conducted using standardized protocols and attended by the same rehabilitation doctor. There is no indication that the VAS instrument, assessment procedures, timing, or outcome thresholds differed by intervention group.

3. **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**

   • **Response: Probably yes**  
   • **Reason:** For the self-reported VAS pain outcome, the participant is the outcome assessor. The article describes the trial as single-blind and reports that the rehabilitation doctor conducting assessments was blinded. However, it does not adequately describe a credible method by which participants were blinded across the visibly and procedurally different interventions: ultrasound or phonophoresis for 5 minutes versus iontophoresis for 20 minutes, with different equipment and treatment procedures. The trial registry states that participants were masked, but neither the registry information nor the article explains how masking was accomplished or maintained. Therefore, participant awareness of assigned intervention is probable.

4. **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** VAS pain is a subjective, participant-reported outcome. If participants knew whether they were receiving conventional ultrasound, phonophoresis with dexketoprofen, or iontophoresis with dexketoprofen, their expectations about the interventions could have affected their reported pain level.

5. **4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** Although knowledge of treatment assignment could influence self-reported pain, the report provides no evidence that participants’ pain ratings were actually influenced by this knowledge. In particular, it does not report participant expectations, beliefs about the relative effectiveness of the interventions, breaches of masking, or other evidence indicating that such influence was likely.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: Some concerns**

• **Reason:** The VAS was an appropriate outcome measure and was collected comparably across groups. However, the outcome was participant reported, and participants were probably aware of their assigned intervention despite the registry’s unsupported statement that participants were masked. Since awareness could influence subjective pain assessment, but there is no evidence that it likely did so, the domain is judged as having **some concerns**.

• **Response: Unpredictable**

• **Reason:** Several sources of potential bias could operate in different directions. • **Response: Some concerns**

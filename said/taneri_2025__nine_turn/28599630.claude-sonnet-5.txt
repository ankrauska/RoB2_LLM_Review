# Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**Outcome Name: Prevalence of depressive symptoms (EPDS score ≥ 11)**

## 3.1. Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** Of the 60 women randomized (32 fish oil, 28 control), only 32 women (53.3%) completed the trial through the postpartum assessment (T3), as explicitly stated: "A total of 32 women completed the trial." The attrition pathway is substantial and multi-staged: 10 women were lost immediately post-randomization before intervention began (6 fish oil + 4 control, "for reasons apparently not related to the study"), 1 additional woman declined participation, 12 women dropped out during the intervention period (7 fish oil + 5 control), 2 women were excluded (1 per group), 1 stillbirth occurred in the control group, and 2 additional women in the fish oil group missed the final postpartum appointment. This represents approximately 47% missing outcome data at the primary endpoint (T3), which is far below the threshold where missing data "could have made no important difference to the estimated effect." Given the event rate for EPDS≥11 was approximately 25–35%, this level of attrition is substantial relative to the observed event rate and could plausibly alter the effect estimate.

## 3.2. Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?

- **Response: No**
- **Reason:** While the authors used random-intercept logistic regression models (Table 5) that incorporate available data across time points (rather than a naïve completers-only comparison), this modeling approach relies on a missing-at-random (MAR) assumption, which is not explicitly tested or justified in the article. No sensitivity analyses were reported to assess how results might change under different assumptions about the missing data mechanism (e.g., missing-not-at-random scenarios). No imputation methods (e.g., multiple imputation) were used to correct for the substantial missingness. Both ITT and per-protocol analyses were reported, but the ITT analysis appears to rely only on observed cases at each timepoint (denominators shrink over time in Table 3) rather than a true correction for the bias introduced by dropout. Therefore, there is insufficient evidence that the result was protected from bias due to missing outcome data.

## 3.3. Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?

- **Response: Yes**
- **Reason:** While many reasons for dropout are documented and plausibly unrelated to depression status (e.g., transfer of prenatal care to another health center, miscarriage, stillbirth, exceeding the 13-week gestational cutoff after ultrasound, lost capsules, preeclampsia), some losses lack specified reasons — notably, "2 missed 2nd trimester visit" (control group) and "2 women in the fish oil group missed the [postpartum] appointment." In the context of postpartum depression research, missed appointments and loss to follow-up are plausibly related to the participant's underlying mental health status (e.g., depressed or symptomatic women may be less likely to attend follow-up visits due to low motivation, social withdrawal, or difficulty accessing care). Therefore, it is plausible that missingness in the EPDS outcome was related to its true value.

## 3.4. Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** Several factors support this judgment: (1) There was a numerical imbalance in the extent of missing data between groups at multiple stages (fish oil group consistently lost more participants: 6 vs. 4 pre-intervention, 7 vs. 5 during intervention, 2 vs. 0 at postpartum follow-up), raising concern that differential attrition could be related to differential treatment effects/symptom trajectories; (2) The trial context itself (screening and following women identified as being "at risk" for postpartum depression) makes it plausible that women with worsening or persistent depressive symptoms were less likely to remain engaged in follow-up visits, a phenomenon well-documented in perinatal mental health research; (3) No adjustment or accounting for participant characteristics that might explain the relationship between missingness and the outcome was reported. While the majority of documented reasons appear administrative/obstetric and unrelated to the outcome, the substantial magnitude of unexplained/undocumented loss (missed appointments) combined with the imbalance between groups is sufficient to raise this concern to "probably yes" rather than remaining at uncertain "some concerns" level.

---

## **Overall Domain 3 Risk-of-Bias Judgement: High**

**Justification:** Nearly half (47%) of randomized participants had missing outcome data at the primary postpartum endpoint, which greatly exceeds the threshold at which missingness could meaningfully bias the estimated effect, particularly given the moderate event rate (~25–35%) for this dichotomous outcome. No imputation or sensitivity analyses were performed to demonstrate robustness of the findings to this level of attrition. There is a plausible and documented pathway (missed appointments) by which missingness could be related to the participants' true depressive symptom status, compounded by an observed imbalance in attrition rates between the fish oil and control groups at multiple stages of the trial. Based on the RoB 2 algorithm, this combination of substantial missing data, lack of correction/sensitivity analysis, and plausible informative missingness warrants a judgment of **High risk of bias** for this outcome.

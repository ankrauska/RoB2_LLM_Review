**Outcome Name: pain visual analogue scale (VAS)**

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1. Was the allocation sequence random?**

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** The report describes the study as using “sex-stratified randomization” and states that participants were assigned by “simple random selection.” However, it does not report how the random sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated numbers, random-number table, coin toss, or similar method). Stratification alone does not establish that a random component was used to generate the sequence within strata.

2. **1.2. Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   • **Response: Probably yes**  
   • **Reason:** The authors state that “the allocation sequence was blinded through the use of sealed envelopes.” This supports allocation concealment. However, the report does not specify whether envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, tamper-proof, and opened only after irreversible participant enrolment. Therefore, full assurance of adequate concealment is not available, but the reported use of sealed envelopes makes adequate concealment probable.

3. **1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** Groups were equal in size (27 participants per group) and had identical sex distributions (10 men and 17 women in each group). Baseline VAS pain did not differ statistically between groups (control median 7.0, IQR 1.9; experimental median 7.4, IQR 2.3; *p* = 0.421). No statistically significant differences were reported for age or the other baseline pain measures. Some mobility variables approached conventional statistical significance (e.g., left side bending, *p* = 0.050), but the pattern does not demonstrate an excess of important baseline imbalances or a clear failure of randomization relevant to the VAS pain outcome.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Some concerns**

**Justification:** There is no indication of an important baseline imbalance that would imply a defective randomization process, and allocation concealment using sealed envelopes was probably adequate. However, the method used to generate the allocation sequence is insufficiently reported to confirm that it included a random component.

### Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Effect of interest: effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)**

1. **2.1. Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** The study is described as having “blinding of patients,” and the control group received placebo (sham) ultrasound using the same treatment sequence as the experimental group. The report does not indicate that participants were unblinded, that they recognized whether the ultrasound device was active, or that intervention-specific adverse effects occurred that could have revealed allocation.

2. **2.2. Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** The physiotherapist delivering treatment could not be blinded, as explicitly acknowledged by the authors: “The blinding of the physiotherapist who applied the treatment was not possible.” The therapist had to actively administer ultrasound in the experimental group and keep the ultrasound device off in the sham-ultrasound control group.

3. **2.3. If yes/probably yes to 2.1 or 2.2: Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?**

However, these statements do not establish whether unblinded treatment personnel delivered any care differently because of the trial context.

4. **2.4. If yes/probably yes to 2.3: Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?**

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** This question cannot be meaningfully assessed because the report does not provide information on whether deviations from the intended interventions occurred due to the trial context.

5. **2.5. If yes/probably yes to 2.4: Were these deviations from intended intervention balanced between groups?**

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** The article does not report deviations arising from the trial context; therefore, it does not provide information on whether any such deviations were balanced between treatment groups.

6. **2.6. Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** The authors explicitly state that the analysis was conducted “by intention to treat.” Furthermore, 54 participants were randomized (27 per group), and the article states that no participants were lost or dropped out during the study. Thus, there is no reported post-randomization exclusion, crossover, or loss to follow-up that would contradict an intention-to-treat analysis.

7. **2.7. If no/probably no to 2.6: Was there potential for a substantial impact on the result of the failure to analyse participants in the group to which they were randomized?**

   • **Response: Not applicable**  
   • **Reason:** An intention-to-treat analysis was reported and no post-randomization losses or exclusions were reported.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 2 (effect of assignment to intervention): Some concerns**

**Justification:** Participants were reportedly blinded and an intention-to-treat analysis was reported with no attrition. However, the treating physiotherapist was unblinded, and the article does not report sufficient information to determine whether awareness of allocation led to deviations from the protocol or differential provision of other aspects of care.

2. **5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** VAS pain was scheduled to be measured at several time points: baseline, before the sixth session, before the 11th session, and at discharge after the 25th session. This creates opportunity for selective choice among time points. Nevertheless, without a protocol or analysis plan, it cannot be determined whether the reported time points or results were selected on the basis of their findings.

3. **5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?**

   • **Response: No information**  
   • **Reason:** The authors analysed VAS improvement indices and used an independent-samples test for the reported comparison. They also report one-sided (“unilateral”) *p*-values and effect sizes. However, no pre-specified analysis plan is available to establish whether this analysis, rather than other plausible analyses (for example, analysis of post-intervention VAS values, analysis adjusted for baseline VAS, or other approaches to repeated measurements), was chosen in advance. The report does not provide direct evidence that the analysis was selected because of its results.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 5: Some concerns**

**Justification:** No prospective protocol, trial registration, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan was identified. Moreover, VAS was measured at multiple time points but not all scheduled VAS assessment results were fully reported. There is insufficient direct evidence to judge that selective reporting definitely occurred, but the absence of pre-specification leaves some concerns about selection of the reported VAS result.

## 4.2. Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?

## 4.3. Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?

**Response: Yes**

**Reason:** This was an open-label trial with no blinding ("masking": "NONE"). ## 4.4. Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?

**Response: Probably yes**

Unblinded investigators could subtly affect measurement conditions — for example, variability in ensuring the mandated 5-minute rest period was strictly followed, encouragement/reassurance given to patients, or conduct around repeat measurements when discordant readings occurred (investigator discretion to trigger 2 additional measurements when DBP differed by >10 mm Hg between readings). **Response: Probably no**

**Reason:** Although the assessors were not blinded, the outcome (DBP) was measured using an objective, automated device rather than manual reading or subjective interpretation, which substantially limits the extent to which unblinded status could meaningfully bias the numerical result. There is no evidence in the article of differential measurement conduct, selective reporting of BP values, or any indication that investigators manipulated or selectively recorded readings favoring one treatment arm. The standardized protocol (multiple readings, averaging, discarding extremes) was applied mechanically regardless of group assignment, minimizing the likelihood that unblinding translated into a meaningfully biased outcome measurement, though the theoretical possibility cannot be entirely excluded given the open-label design.

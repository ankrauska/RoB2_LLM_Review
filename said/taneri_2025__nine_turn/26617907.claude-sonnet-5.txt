**Study design**: RCT-like design (random number table allocation) with 90 liver cancer patients split into two groups of 45 (Group A = PMWA, Group B = laparoscopic resection), conducted 2010-2012, with 1-3 year follow-up.

**Key methodological details**: Inclusion/exclusion criteria (tumor ≤5cm, Child-Pugh A/B), equipment specifications, and treatment protocols for both procedures.

**Main findings**:
- Surgical time and blood loss significantly favored Group A (PMWA)
- Overall complication rate significantly lower in Group A (6.67% vs 17.78%)
- Local recurrence significantly *higher* in Group A (20.00% vs 8.89%) — a notable and somewhat counterintuitive finding
- No significant differences in total recurrence rate or 1-, 2-, 3-year survival rates

**Notable issues I've identified that may be relevant for a systematic review appraisal**:

2. **Randomization method**: "Random number table" is stated, but no allocation concealment or blinding details are provided — important for risk of bias assessment.

3. **Small sample size** and single-center design limit generalizability.

4. **Statistical inconsistencies**: Table 2 shows individual complication categories (bile leakage, pleural effusion, blood loss) as non-significant, yet the aggregate "incidence of complications" reaches significance — this is worth scrutinizing for how the composite was calculated/tested.

5. **Discussion doesn't fully reconcile** the paradox of lower complications but higher local recurrence in the PMWA group, though it offers a plausible explanation (tumor size heterogeneity).

6. **Follow-up duration variability** (1-3 years, not fixed) could introduce bias into survival comparisons.

7. **Limited reporting** on tumor staging (e.g., BCLC stage), which is critical for liver cancer prognosis comparisons.

8. **Conflict of interest/funding**: Funded by a regional government science program; no COI declared.

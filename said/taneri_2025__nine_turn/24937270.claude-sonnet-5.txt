# Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**Outcome: Interhemispheric functional connectivity**

**5.1. Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?**

- **Response: No information**

While Section 2.9 describes the statistical methods used (mixed model ANOVA, post-hoc t-tests with "a-priori specifications" for three planned comparisons: random vs. sham, 1 Hz vs. sham, 100 Hz vs. sham), there is no evidence that this plan was registered or finalized *before* unblinded outcome data were available. No protocol document or registration number is cited anywhere in the article.

**5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** The study explicitly measured interhemispheric coherence across multiple bands and sub-bands: alpha, theta, low-alpha (8–10 Hz), high-alpha (10–12 Hz), low-beta (12–20 Hz), and high-beta (20–30 Hz) — six distinct measurements within the single "functional connectivity" domain (Section 2.7). The authors themselves describe this as "an exploratory analysis to detect changes" (Abstract). Of these six measures, only theta and low-alpha showed significant/trend-level results and were emphasized as the "main finding" in the abstract, discussion, and title-adjacent highlights, while high-alpha, low-beta, and high-beta (all non-significant) received comparatively minimal discussion. This pattern—testing multiple sub-bands and prominently reporting/interpreting only the significant ones as the key result—raises concern that the reported/emphasized result was influenced by the pattern of findings.

**5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?**

- **Response: Probably yes**
- **Reason:** While three post-hoc comparisons (random vs. sham, 1 Hz vs. sham, 100 Hz vs. sham) were described as "a-priori specifications" (Section 2.9), the Results section reports additional comparisons that appear to go beyond this original plan: "Further exploratory analysis showed that the theta interhemispheric coherence was significantly higher for the random frequency group compared to 1 Hz (p = 0.038) and 100 Hz stimulation (p = 0.0013)" (Section 3.2). These additional pairwise comparisons (random vs. 1 Hz; random vs. 100 Hz) were explicitly labeled "further exploratory analysis," indicating they were not part of the original a-priori plan and were conducted after observing the initial significant result, likely to further support/strengthen the significant finding. This is a form of post-hoc, results-driven analysis selection.

---

## Domain-Level Judgement: **High risk**

**Justification:** There is no evidence of a pre-registered protocol or pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinded data analysis (No information for 5.1), which alone would typically prompt "Some concerns." Compounding this, the outcome domain (interhemispheric connectivity) was assessed across six different frequency bands/sub-bands, with selective emphasis placed on the two bands (theta and low-alpha) that yielded significant or near-significant results, while the explicit framing as "exploratory analysis" suggests a hypothesis-generating rather than hypothesis-confirming design. Furthermore, additional post-hoc pairwise comparisons (random vs. 1 Hz, random vs. 100 Hz) were conducted and reported after the initial significant finding was observed, explicitly labeled as "further exploratory analysis" — a hallmark of results-driven analytic selection. The combination of no pre-specified/registered plan, multiple outcome measurements with selective emphasis on significant ones, and additional non-pre-specified post-hoc analyses conducted after seeing results collectively indicates a **High risk** of bias in selection of the reported result for this domain.

**Outcome Name: quality of movement of the impaired upper limb by MUUL**

### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

1. **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**

   • **Response: Yes**  
   • **Reason:** After children were matched in pairs, “children were randomized to pairs using a computer generated list of random numbers.” A computer-generated random-number list includes a random component and is an acceptable random sequence-generation method.

2. **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**

   • **Response: Probably yes**  
   • **Reason:** The article states that allocation was performed using “concealed envelopes opened by non-study personnel.” This indicates that the allocation process was intended to be concealed from study personnel involved in enrolment and assessment. However, the report does not specify whether envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed, tamper-proof, or opened only after irreversible enrolment/assignment. Therefore, the evidence does not support a definite “Yes,” but it reasonably supports “Probably yes.”

3. **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**

   • **Response: No**  
   • **Reason:** The authors report that, following matching and concealed random allocation, there were no differences between groups in demographic or baseline measures. Baseline MUUL scores were reasonably similar (CIMT: mean 67.1, 95% CI 62.5–71.6; BIM: mean 70.8, 95% CI 66.7–74.9). Groups were also comparable in age, sex, MACS level, GMFCS level, hand-function classifications, sensory measures, grip strength, AHA scores, and JTTHF performance. Although four children were matched on all variables except side of hemiplegia, resulting in fewer children with right-sided hemiplegia in the BIM group, this does not indicate a substantial or implausible baseline imbalance suggesting failure of randomization.

**Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 1: Low risk of bias**  

The allocation sequence was computer-generated, allocation concealment was likely adequate although incompletely described, and baseline data do not indicate a problem with the randomization process.

**Article Title**: The effect of intravenous magnesium sulfate on serum levels of N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT pro-BNP) in elective CABG with cardiopulmonary bypass

**Authors**: The author names are not explicitly listed in the main text block, though the study is by [Author name] et al. (The corresponding author/first author name appears to have been redacted as "[Author name]" in the prompt).

**Objective**: To assess the effect of magnesium sulfate (MgSO4) solution compared with a placebo on the serum levels of NT Pro-BNP, extubation time, and postoperative pain in adult patients undergoing elective coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB).

**Study Design**: Double-blind, randomized controlled clinical trial.

**Population**: 88 adult patients aged 40–70 years undergoing elective CABG with CPB at a university hospital in Tehran, Iran (44 in the MgSO4 group and 44 in the placebo group). 

**Intervention**: Intravenous infusion of MgSO4 at a dose of 15 mg/kg/h (via 50 ml syringe) started after anesthesia induction and continued until skin closure.

**Comparator**: Placebo (same volume of normal saline administered intravenously via 50 ml syringe).

**Key Outcomes**:
- **Postoperative NT pro-BNP levels** (24 hours after ICU arrival): Significantly lower in the MgSO4 group (1566.4 ± 15.9 pg/ml) compared to the placebo group (2632.1 ± 29.2 pg/ml) (P = 0.002).
- **Postoperative Mechanical Ventilation Time**: Significantly shorter in the MgSO4 group (1.7 ± 0.6 h) compared to the placebo group (2.4 ± 0.6 h) (P = 0.001).
- **Postoperative Pain (VAS at 12 and 24 hours)**: Significantly lower in the MgSO4 group.
- **Morphine Requirement (first 24 h)**: Significantly lower in the MgSO4 group (11.3 ± 3.4 mg) vs. placebo (18.8 ± 5.3 mg) (P = 0.01).
- **Other findings**: No significant differences in ICU stay length, postoperative blood loss, or postoperative CK-MB levels between the two groups. 

**Conclusion**: Intravenous administration of magnesium sulfate in elective CABG with CPB effectively decreases postoperative NT Pro-BNP levels, reduces the duration of postoperative mechanical ventilation, lowers postoperative pain, and decreases morphine requirements.

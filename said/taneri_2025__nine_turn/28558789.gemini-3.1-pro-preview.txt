   • Response: Yes
   • Reason: The article reports, "Consecutively numbered opaque envelopes containing group allocation were collected from the offsite investigator by the tRNS intervener after initial assessment." This indicates that allocation was concealed using consecutively numbered, opaque envelopes, and allocation only occurred after baseline assessment.

3. 1.3. Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: The study only includes four participants (two in the active group, two in the sham group). While there are baseline differences among individuals (e.g., both participants in the sham group had aphasia, while neither in the active group did), such variations are entirely compatible with chance in a very small sample size of n=4. There is no evidence indicating a flaw in the randomization process itself.

Outcome Name: Motor Assessment Scale item 6 (Upper Arm Function, MAS6

1. 5.1. Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?
   • Response: No
   • Reason: A pre-specified protocol is available (ANZCTR: ACTRN12614000952640), but the data were not analyzed in accordance with it. The protocol pre-specified the primary outcome as a "Composite score of Motor Assessment Scale upper limb items 6-8" and planned a "non-parametric, mixed effects model". The published article deviated from this plan by designating MAS6 alone as the primary outcome and utilizing individual descriptive changes rather than the planned statistical model.

2. 5.2. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements (e.g. scales, definitions, time points) within the outcome domain?
   • Response: Probably yes
   • Reason: The registry protocol defined the primary outcome as a composite of MAS items 6, 7, and 8. In the published article, this was split, with MAS6 elevated to the primary outcome and items 7 and 8 downgraded to secondary outcomes. This undocumented deviation raises the possibility that the individual MAS6 score was selected for emphasis based on the collected data.

3. 5.3. Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?
   • Response: Probably no
   • Reason: Although the authors deviated from their pre-specified analysis plan (abandoning the mixed-effects model for descriptive statistics), they provided the complete raw data for MAS6 for all four participants across all timepoints in Table 3. The shift in analysis method was likely dictated by the extremely small sample size (n=4 instead of the planned target of 10) rather than a desire to selectively report a favorable analysis, and the full transparency of the raw data mitigates the risk of selective analysis reporting.

Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
   • Risk-of-bias judgement: High

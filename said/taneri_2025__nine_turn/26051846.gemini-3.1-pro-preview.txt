Outcome Name: pain VAS scale post-treatment

4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?
• Response: No
• Reason: The outcome was measured using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS), which assesses pain on a scale of 0 to 10. This is a highly standard, appropriate, and validated tool for measuring subjective pain.

4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?
• Response: No
• Reason: The article states that "All visits were attended to by the same blinded rehabilitation doctor using standardised protocols" and assessments were made at the exact same time points (baseline, post-treatment, 1 month post-treatment) for all groups.

4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?
• Response: Yes
• Reason: According to the RoB 2 guidelines, for participant-reported outcomes such as subjective pain (VAS), the study participant is considered the outcome assessor. As established previously, because the physical nature of the treatments is completely different (ultrasound wand vs. electrical iontophoresis pads) and there was no mention of sham modalities, participants were aware of the intervention they received. 

4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
• Response: Yes
• Reason: Pain is a subjective, participant-reported outcome. A participant's knowledge of which specific intervention they are receiving (especially if they realize they are receiving a drug-enhanced therapy vs. standard ultrasound) could easily influence their perception and reporting of pain due to expectations or placebo effects.

4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
• Response: Probably no
• Reason: While knowledge of the intervention *could* influence the outcome, there is no direct evidence to suggest it *likely* did to a severe degree. All three groups received active, credible, hands-on physiotherapeutic interventions alongside standardized exercise and cryotherapy. Because participants were not comparing an active treatment to a "waitlist" or "no treatment" control, differential expectation biases are less likely to be extreme enough to guarantee an influence on the outcome.

Risk-of-bias judgement for Domain 4: Some concerns
• Reason: The outcome is subjective and self-reported by participants who were aware of their assigned intervention (since treatments were physically distinguishable and no shams were used). While this introduces the possibility that knowledge of the intervention could have influenced the outcome (leading to "Some concerns"), all groups received active treatments, making it not definitively "likely" (which would result in a "High" risk of bias).

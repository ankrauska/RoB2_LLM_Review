**Article ID**: Jamil, 2015
**Outcome Name**: Interhemispheric functional connectivity
**registration number**: None

Domain 1
1.1 : Y (100%)
The article clearly states that "Randomization was completed using blocks of four," which is an appropriate and adequate method for generating the allocation sequence.
1.2 : NI (100%)
The article mentions that the study was double-blinded, but it does not provide any specific information regarding the method of allocation sequence concealment (e.g., opaque envelopes, central allocation) prior to participant assignment.
1.3 : N (95%)
The article explicitly reports, "There were no significant differences in demographic characteristics between groups (Table 1)." The baseline differences do not suggest a problem with the randomization process.
Result of the domain : Some concerns (95%)
The allocation sequence was adequately generated, and baseline characteristics were balanced, but there is no information provided about allocation sequence concealment.

Domain 2
2.1 : N (100%)
Participants were unaware of their assigned intervention, as the article states, "Both the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention," and sham stimulation involved applying stimulation for 30 s and then turning it off automatically to preserve blinding.
2.2 : N (100%)
The article indicates that the investigators assessing the outcomes were blinded, and the sham procedure was designed to maintain blinding for both participants and researchers.
2.3 : Y (95%)
There is no indication in the article of any significant non-protocol interventions that were unbalanced across the groups.
2.4 : N (95%)
Out of 40 recruited subjects, 38 completed the single stimulation session (2 withdrew before stimulation). Implementation of the single 20-minute intervention was successful for most participants.
2.5 : N (95%)
Because the intervention was a single 20-minute session and participants completed the procedure, there is no evidence of non-adherence affecting the outcomes.
2.6 : NA (100%)
Since there were no issues with non-adherence, an evaluation of the analysis method used to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention is not applicable.
Result of the domain : Low risk (95%)
Blinding was effectively implemented, and there were no significant issues with implementation or adherence to the single-session intervention.

Domain 3
3.1 : Y (100%)
Of the 38 subjects randomized and who received stimulation, 1 subject in the random frequency group was removed due to excessive artifacts in the EEG recording. Consequently, 37 out of 38 participants (97.4%) had complete outcome data, which is above the 95% threshold for continuous variables.
3.2 : Y (100%)
Missing outcome data was minimal (<5%).
3.3 : N (95%)
The single missing data point was due to "excessive artifacts in the EEG recording," which is a technical issue and unrelated to the participant's true outcome value.
3.4 : N (95%)
The missingness was due to technical artifacts, not the true value of the outcome or characteristics related to it.
Result of the domain : Low risk (100%)
Outcome data were available for nearly all participants, and the reason for the single missing data point was technical and unrelated to the outcome itself.

Domain 4
4.1 : N (100%)
The outcome was measured using high-resolution qEEG interhemispheric coherence, which is a pre-defined and reliable method for assessing functional connectivity.
4.2 : N (100%)
The outcome was measured consistently across all groups using the same 64-channel EEG device and the same protocol.
4.3 : N (100%)
The article explicitly states that "the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention."
4.4 : N (100%)
The outcome (interhemispheric functional connectivity measured via qEEG) is an objective physiological measure.
4.5 : N (100%)
There is no clear evidence to suggest that the assessment of this objective outcome could have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, especially given the double-blind design.
Result of the domain : Low risk (100%)
The outcome was measured objectively and reliably, in a consistent manner across groups, and by blinded assessors.

Domain 5
5.1 : NI (100%)
The article does not provide a trial registration number or reference a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded data were available.
5.2 : N (95%)
There is no evidence that the reported coherence results were selectively chosen from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the domain.
5.3 : N (95%)
There is no clear evidence that the reported numerical results were selectively reported from multiple eligible analyses of the data.
Result of the domain : Some concerns (95%)
While there is no clear evidence of selective reporting, the lack of a pre-specified analysis plan or trial registration prevents a high-certainty confirmation.

Overall Bias Risk for the Article : Some concerns (95%)
The study demonstrates a low risk of bias in the domains of deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, and measurement of the outcome. However, there are some concerns regarding the randomization process (due to lack of information on allocation concealment) and the selection of the reported result (due to the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan/trial registration).

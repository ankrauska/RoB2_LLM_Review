**Article ID**: Hong, 2015  
**Outcome Name**: occurrence of cardiac death  
**registration number**: NCT01563952  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** The study states that participants were “randomly assigned” using an interactive web-based response system and stratified by center. However, it does not report the method used to generate the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random-number table, or block randomization).

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation was performed using an interactive web-based response system. This is a centralized allocation method and indicates that allocation was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The IVUS-guided and angiography-guided groups had identical sample sizes (201 each), and baseline clinical and angiographic characteristics showed no statistically significant differences. The article explicitly states that groups were well matched at baseline.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation concealment was adequately implemented through a web-based response system, and no baseline imbalances suggested a problem with randomization. Although the sequence-generation method was not explicitly reported, this does not alter the domain result under the specified algorithm.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report whether participants were blinded to IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided intervention. No participant placebo or sham procedure was described.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The intervention providers necessarily knew whether IVUS was used during PCI, because IVUS guidance was performed at the discretion of physicians in the IVUS-guided group. Therefore, carers and intervention providers were aware of the assigned intervention.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
**Reason:** The article reports differences in procedural practice, including more frequent high-pressure poststent balloon dilation in the IVUS-guided group. However, it does not clearly establish whether important nonprotocol interventions received during follow-up were imbalanced between groups and prognostic for cardiac death.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** Implementation of the assigned procedural strategy was successful for nearly all participants. Procedure success occurred in 199/201 (99.0%) in the IVUS group and 197/201 (98.0%) in the angiography group. Failures were substantially below 10%.

**2.5: N (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** Adherence to prescribed dual antiplatelet therapy at last follow-up was high and balanced: 98.5% in the IVUS-guided group and 98.0% in the angiography-guided group. No important crossover or cessation of the assigned IVUS/angiography strategy was reported.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The article explicitly states that all analyses used the intention-to-treat principle. According to the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not considered an appropriate analysis for estimating the effect of adhering to intervention when relevant deviations occur.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** Participants’ blinding status was not reported, intervention providers were aware of allocation, and the study used intention-to-treat analysis rather than an analysis designed to estimate the effect of adherence. In addition, the balance of important nonprotocol interventions was insufficiently reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Cardiac death is a binary outcome. Twelve-month follow-up was completed by 200/201 participants (99.5%) in the IVUS-guided group and 201/201 participants (100%) in the angiography-guided group; thus, one participant had missing follow-up data. There were two observed cardiac deaths overall, which is not much greater than the one participant with missing outcome data.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report inverse-probability weighting, sensitivity analyses, or other analyses demonstrating that missing cardiac-death data did not bias the result. Intention-to-treat analysis alone does not correct potential bias from missing outcome data.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not state why one participant did not complete 12-month clinical follow-up. Therefore, it cannot be determined whether missingness depended on the participant’s true cardiac-death outcome.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
**Reason:** The reason for the missing follow-up outcome was not reported, and the article does not provide characteristics of the participant with missing data or analyses addressing whether missingness was related to cardiac death.

**Result of Domain 3: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** The observed number of cardiac deaths was not much greater than the number of participants with missing outcome data, no analysis was provided to demonstrate absence of missing-data bias, and the relationship between missingness and the true outcome could not be assessed.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Cardiac death was predefined as the primary endpoint. Deaths were classified as cardiac unless an unequivocal noncardiac cause was established. This is an appropriate and reliable method for measuring cardiac death.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Clinical follow-up was performed at the same scheduled time points in both groups—1, 3, 9, and 12 months—by clinic visit or telephone contact. The outcome definition was the same for both intervention groups.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** All clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical event committee blinded to randomization.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Cardiac death is an objective clinical outcome. Assessment of death is not expected to be influenced by knowledge of the assigned IVUS-guided or angiography-guided intervention.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment was likely to influence assessment of cardiac death. In addition, the clinical event committee was blinded to randomization.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Cardiac death was objectively defined, assessed comparably between groups, and adjudicated by a clinical event committee blinded to randomization.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was registered as NCT01563952. The registry specified cardiac death within 12 months as the primary outcome. The article prespecified cardiac death as the primary endpoint and reported the 12-month cardiac-death result using the stated time-to-event analytical approach.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** Cardiac death was measured using a single prespecified clinical definition and reported at the prespecified 12-month follow-up. There is no evidence that the reported cardiac-death result was selected from multiple eligible outcome definitions or time points.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
**Reason:** The statistical methods were prespecified in the Methods section: Kaplan–Meier estimation, log-rank testing, and Cox proportional-hazards analysis. The cardiac-death result was reported consistently with these planned analyses. No evidence indicates selective reporting from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was registered, cardiac death at 12 months was prospectively specified, and the reported outcome and analysis were consistent with the stated protocol and Methods section.

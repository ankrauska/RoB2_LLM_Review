# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Rodebaugh, 2014 (A High-Throughput Clinical Assay for Testing Drug Facilitation of Exposure Therapy)
**Outcome Name**: Between-session habituation
**Registration number**: Not reported in the article

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (90% Certainty)
The article states participants were "randomly assigned" but does not describe a specific method (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random number table, block randomization). No further detail is provided to upgrade this answer.

**1.2**: Y (85% Certainty)
"Participants were randomly assigned to 250mg of DCS or placebo in identical-appearing capsules by a data manager who had no interaction with participants." This describes a specific concealment mechanism (identical-appearing capsules allocated by an independent data manager), analogous to sequentially numbered, identical containers.

**1.3**: Y (85% Certainty)
A statistically significant baseline difference in age was found (p = .019; DCS ~49 years vs. placebo ~38 years). This is a notable and statistically significant baseline imbalance not explicitly attributed to random error by the authors (they instead statistically controlled for it post hoc).

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (85% Certainty)
Because 1.2 = Y, 1.1 = NI, and 1.3 = Y, this pattern maps to "Some concerns."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: N (90% Certainty)
Placebo (identical-appearing capsule) was used, and blinding was formally tested: "neither participants nor experimenters were able to discern assignment (ps > .15)."

**2.2**: N (90% Certainty)
Same rationale as above; care providers/experimenters were also blinded and unable to discern assignment.

**2.3**: Y (75% Certainty)
Medication use (antidepressants, benzodiazepines) was assessed and found not to differ significantly between groups; these were also examined as covariates with no substantive effect on results.

**2.4**: Y (90% Certainty)
4 of 34 randomized participants (11.8%) did not complete Visit 2, exceeding the 10% threshold for implementation failure.

**2.5**: N (60% Certainty)
No specific non-adherence to the single-dose ingestion protocol was reported for those who completed the study (single supervised dosing).

**2.6**: N (90% Certainty)
The study analyzed only completers ("Only those participants completing Visit 2 are analyzed"), which is a per-protocol/completer-only analysis — an inappropriate method per guidance, rather than an appropriate adjustment technique (e.g., IPW, instrumental variable).

**Result of Domain 2**: Some concerns (75% Certainty)
Although blinding was adequately achieved (2.1 = N, 2.2 = N), there was a meaningful implementation failure (>10% dropout, 2.4 = Y) and the analysis was restricted to completers without appropriate correction (2.6 = N), raising concern that the reported effect may not reflect the effect of assignment to intervention in all randomized participants.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: Y (75% Certainty)
The outcome (between-session habituation) is a binary/categorical variable. 30/34 (88.2%) participants had complete data. Response counts among completers (~12 DCS responders, ~5 placebo responders = ~17 events) are much greater than the missing cases (4), satisfying the "events >> missing" criterion for binary outcomes.

**Result of Domain 3**: Low risk (80% Certainty)
Since 3.1 = Y, Domain 3 is rated "Low risk" per the decision rule.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90% Certainty)
The SUDS is a standard, validated behavioral research scale with excellent internal consistency (Cronbach's α > .85) reported in this study.

**4.2**: N (90% Certainty)
The outcome was measured identically for both groups, at the same time points, using the same scale and thresholds.

**4.3**: N (85% Certainty)
The outcome (SUDS) is patient-reported, and participants were blinded to allocation (confirmed via post-hoc guess testing, ps > .15); thus assessors/participants were not aware of assignment.

**4.4**: Y (90% Certainty)
The outcome is a subjective, patient-reported measure (SUDS ratings of anxiety), which inherently carries potential for influence by knowledge of intervention.

**4.5**: N (75% Certainty)
No clear evidence indicates that outcome assessment was influenced by knowledge of allocation; blinding integrity was tested and confirmed successful.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (85% Certainty)
4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = N maps directly to "Low risk."

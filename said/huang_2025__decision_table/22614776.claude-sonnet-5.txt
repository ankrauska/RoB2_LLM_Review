## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (90% certainty)
The article states participants "were randomly selected (stratified by gender and age...), and assigned to 1 of the 3 study conditions." No specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, block randomization) is described anywhere in the methods.

**1.2**: NI (90% certainty)
There is no mention of allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes) anywhere in the article.

**1.3**: N (90% certainty)
The article explicitly states: "There were no significant differences between conditions on age, race, gender." Baseline characteristics reported in Table 1 also appear reasonably balanced across groups, with no indication of randomization failure.

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (85% certainty)
Since 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = N, per the algorithm this yields "Some concerns."

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (80% certainty)
Although not explicitly stated, participants would necessarily know whether they received a therapist-delivered session, a computer-delivered session, or a brochure — these are overtly distinct delivery modes that cannot be masked from participants.

**2.2**: Y (85% certainty)
Therapists delivering the TBI necessarily knew the participants' assigned condition (they administered the therapist-assisted intervention themselves); computer-delivered and control conditions involved no live interventionist, but staff distributing conditions would know assignment.

**2.3**: NI (70% certainty)
The article mentions a baseline imbalance in "dropping out of school" between conditions (controlled for in analysis), but does not provide clear information on other non-protocol co-interventions (e.g., outside counseling, other services) and whether these were balanced.

**2.4**: N (90% certainty)
">95% received their assigned intervention during the ED visit," indicating implementation failure was under 10%.

**2.5**: N (85% certainty)
Given the single-session nature of the intervention and that >95% received the assigned intervention, non-adherence appears minimal (<10%).

**2.6**: N (95% certainty)
The article explicitly states "An intent-to-treat approach was used," which per the guidance is an inappropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to intervention, so this is scored N.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (85% certainty)
2.1/2.2 = Y (at least one "Y/PY/NI"), 2.3 = NI, and 2.6 = N → per the algorithm ("one of 2.1/2.2 is Y/PY/NI, 2.3 is NI/N/PN, and 2.6 is NI/N/PN") → High risk.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: Y (75% certainty)
This is a binary outcome. 12-month follow-up was 83.6% (607/726), with 119 participants missing. The number of observed events for severe peer aggression at 12 months (79+98+104 = 281) is substantially greater than the number of participants with missing data (119), consistent with the "Y" criterion for binary outcomes.

**Result of Domain 3**: Low risk (75% certainty)
Since 3.1 = Y, Domain 3 = Low risk.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90% certainty)
Peer aggression was measured using the Conflict Tactics Scale, a well-established and validated psychometric tool; the method is appropriate and predefined.

**4.2**: N (85% certainty)
All groups completed identical computerized self-administered surveys at the same time points (baseline, 3, 6, 12 months), with the same measurement thresholds.

**4.3**: Y (85% certainty)
This outcome is patient-reported (self-administered computer survey), and participants were aware of their own assigned intervention (therapist, computer, or brochure control) — meeting the criterion for "Y."

**4.4**: Y (90% certainty)
Peer aggression is a subjective, patient-reported outcome (self-report survey), meeting the criterion for "Y."

**4.5**: N (70% certainty)
While the article acknowledges potential self-report bias, it also notes efforts to increase validity (confidentiality assurance, blinded staff to condition, computer self-administration). There is no clear, direct evidence that assessment was actually influenced by knowledge of intervention status.

**Result of Domain 4**: Some concerns (80% certainty)
4.1=N, 4.2=N, 4.3=Y, 4.4=Y, 4.5=N → matches the rule for "Some concerns."

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: Y (85% certainty)
The trial is registered (NCT00251212), and the primary outcomes (violence and alcohol-related variables at 12 months) reported in the article match those specified in the registration and prior protocol papers referenced (e.g., Walton et al., 2010).

**5.2**: N (80% certainty)
Peer aggression was measured using a single defined outcome variable derived from the Conflict Tactic Scale (severe peer aggression, binary variable), consistently used across the SafERteens series of papers—no evidence of selection from multiple eligible measurements.

**5.3**: N (80% certainty)
A single prespecified GEE analytic approach was used consistently to test group-by-time interactions for this outcome, with no evidence of multiple analyses being conducted and selectively reported.

**Article ID**: Kulasegaram, 2015
**Outcome Name**: delayed diagnostic performance
**registration number**: None reported

Domain 1
1.1 : NI (100%)
The article states that participants were randomly allocated to one of four study conditions but does not describe the specific method used to generate the allocation sequence.
1.2 : NI (100%)
The article does not provide any information regarding whether the allocation sequence was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned.
1.3 : N (95%)
The article reports the distribution of different student types (medical students, nursing students, physician assistants) across the groups and mentions the materials used, but there is no indication of significant baseline differences that would suggest a problem with the randomization process.
Result of the domain (100%)
Some concerns
Reason you summarized: The generation of the allocation sequence and allocation concealment are not described, leading to some concerns, although baseline differences do not indicate a failure of randomization.

Domain 2
2.1 : NI (90%)
The intervention was delivered via a computer program. It is unclear if participants were aware of the specific conditions they were assigned to compared to other possible conditions, as no information regarding participant blinding to the study hypotheses or other groups is provided.
2.2 : N (95%)
The intervention was delivered entirely via a custom-designed computer program without direct human administration during the learning and testing phases, ensuring that intervention providers (the computer system) were unaware of the assignment in a way that could introduce bias.
2.3 : Y (90%)
There is no indication in the text of any significant non-protocol interventions that were imbalanced between the groups.
2.4 : N (95%)
A total of 101 participants were recruited, and 94 were included in the final analysis. Only 5 participants failed to complete the delayed session, which is less than 10% of the sample, indicating successful implementation for most participants.
2.5 : N (95%)
Two participants were excluded for spending less than five minutes on the delayed test (non-adherence), which, combined with the 5 who dropped out, is still less than 10% of the total randomized participants.
2.6 : NI (90%)
The article does not explicitly report using intention-to-treat or specific methods to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention, as it simply analyzed the remaining 94 participants.
Result of the domain (90%)
Low risk
Reason you summarized: Although participant blinding is unclear, the intervention was computer-delivered, non-protocol interventions were balanced, and dropouts/non-adherence were below 10%, indicating a low risk of bias in this domain.

Domain 3
3.1 : PN (95%)
A total of 101 participants were recruited and randomized, and 94 were included in the final analysis. This means approximately 93% of the randomized participants had complete outcome data, which falls into the 90% to 95% range for a continuous outcome.
3.2 : N (95%)
The article does not provide information regarding methods used to correct for bias due to missing data (e.g., inverse probability weighting or sensitivity analysis).
3.3 : PY (80%)
Two participants were excluded due to extremely low time spent and very low performance scores, which may relate to their understanding or health/cognitive status during the test, suggesting dropouts could potentially be related to the outcome.
3.4 : N (85%)
The overall proportion of missing data is small and evenly distributed among the groups (3 from CB, 2 from BC, plus 1 from each for low time), making it unlikely that missingness significantly biased the results based on true values.
Result of the domain (90%)
Some concerns
Reason you summarized: The completion rate was between 90-95%, no bias correction methods were reported for the missing data, and while there were some dropouts, it is unlikely to have severely impacted the results, leading to some concerns.

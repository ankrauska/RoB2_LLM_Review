# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Elman, 2015 (DRCR.net Protocol I, 5-year results)
**Outcome Name**: Visual acuity
**Registration number**: Not explicitly stated in article (referenced as DRCR.net protocol; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00444600, not stated in text)

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (85%)
The article states "Random assignment to ranibizumab every 4 weeks... and either prompt or deferred (≥24 weeks) focal/grid laser treatment," but no specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated numbers, block randomization) is described in this article.

**1.2**: NI (90%)
No mention of allocation concealment mechanisms (central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, opaque envelopes) is provided in this article.

**1.3**: NI (85%)
No baseline comparison between the originally randomized prompt vs. deferred groups is presented in this article; only completers vs. non-completers within each group are compared (Table 1).

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (85%)
Rationale: 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = NI (i.e., N/PN/NI) → per algorithm, this combination yields "Some concerns."

---

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (80%)
Laser treatment is a visible, in-office procedure; participants in the prompt-laser group would know they received laser, whereas most deferred-group participants would know they did not (56% never received laser). This is not a blinded intervention for participants.

**2.2**: Y (85%)
Treating physicians/care providers necessarily knew whether laser was administered, as this was an active procedural decision at each visit.

**2.3**: N (80%)
Nonprotocol treatment for DME (bevacizumab, triamcinolone, vitrectomy) was received by 7% of completers in the prompt group and 0% in the deferred group — an imbalance in nonprotocol intervention that is prognostic for the VA outcome.

**2.4**: Y (90%)
Excluding deaths, only 76% (prompt) and 74% (deferred) of originally randomized participants completed 5 years — more than 10% failure/withdrawal in both groups.

**2.5**: N (70%)
Cessation/reduction of injections (e.g., no injections in >50% of eyes by year 5) was per protocol-specified retreatment criteria (based on OCT/VA response), not non-adherence to assigned regimen.

**2.6**: N (75%)
The longitudinal analysis used "all available visit data" across all randomized eyes with adjustment for confounders — functionally an ITT-type approach rather than an instrumental variable or inverse-probability-weighted analysis of adherence.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (80%)
Rationale: One of 2.1/2.2 = Y; 2.3 = N; and 2.6 = N → per algorithm, this combination yields "High risk."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: N (90%)
Continuous outcome (VA letter score); only 76% (prompt) and 74% (deferred) of originally randomized (excluding deaths) participants had 5-year data — below the 90% threshold.

**3.2**: Y (75%)
The authors performed a longitudinal mixed-model analysis using all available data and a sensitivity analysis restricted to 5-year completers (Figure 2), explicitly stating "Results appeared similar," providing evidence that results were not substantially biased by missing data.

**3.3**: N (70%)
Reasons for missingness were largely due to participants declining to consent to the extension phase, not directly tied to visual acuity status; some baseline imbalances (age, PRP, lens status, DR severity) were noted but not clearly outcome-dependent.

**3.4**: N (70%)
Not applicable/not needed since 3.2 = Y drives the domain result, but based on similar sensitivity analysis results, no strong signal of differential dependency was found.

**Result of Domain 3**: Low risk (80%)
Rationale: 3.1 = N, but 3.2 = Y → per algorithm, "Low risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90%)
Best-corrected visual acuity was measured using the standardized E-ETDRS protocol, a validated and reliable method.

**4.2**: N (85%)
Both groups followed the same visit schedule and measurement protocol (visits every 4 weeks through year 1, then 4–16 weeks based on identical protocol-defined criteria).

**4.3**: N (85%)
The article states "masking of the visual acuity examiners," and VA is examiner-assessed (not patient-reported); thus assessors were unaware of group assignment.

**4.4**: Y (70%)
Visual acuity, although examiner-assessed under standardized protocol, retains a subjective/performance-based component; per criteria, subjective measures assessed by an assessor default to Y.

**4.5**: N (85%)
No evidence suggests assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention, especially given explicit masking of VA examiners.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (85%)
Rationale: 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = N → per algorithm, "Low risk" (first rule satisfied).

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: Y (80%)
The protocol was pre-specified and publicly available (www.drcr.net); measurement and analysis methods described in Methods (longitudinal model, binary outcome models) match those reported in Results.

**5.2**: N (85%)
Visual acuity was measured by a single standardized protocol (E-ETDRS) at all pre-specified visits; no evidence of selective reporting among multiple outcome measures.

**5.3**: N (80%)
Both the longitudinal analysis and cross-sectional 5-year analysis were pre-specified and both fully reported, with consistent results; no evidence of selective reporting of analyses.

**Result of Domain 5**: Low risk (85%)
Rationale: 5.2 = N, 5.3 = N, 5.1 = Y → "Low risk."

**Outcome Name**: Acustic reaction time  
**registration number**: NCT02203539  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Participants were randomly allocated using an online tool (randomization.com) that applied permuted block randomization with randomly varying block sizes of 3, 6, and 9.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The randomization list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes. This is a specific allocation-concealment method.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The analyzed groups were equal in size (24 participants each in BRIGHT, BLUE, and CONTROL). Reported participant characteristics did not indicate a baseline imbalance suggesting a problem with randomization.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Allocation sequence generation and concealment were adequately described, and there was no indication that baseline differences reflected a problem with the randomization process.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Participants knew the light condition to which they were exposed, although they did not know the other two light conditions.

**2.2: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
The test supervisor was blinded to the participants’ light condition. However, the article does not clearly state whether the person or persons delivering or setting up the light interventions were blinded to participant allocation.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
No important non-protocol interventions or co-interventions that could affect acoustic reaction time were reported, and no imbalance in such interventions between groups was described.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Two of 74 randomized participants were retrospectively excluded because of invalid peak oxygen-uptake measurements. This represents 2.7% of randomized participants, which is below 10%; there was no indication of widespread implementation failures.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 90%)**  
The article did not report participant adherence, discontinuation of assigned light exposure, crossover between light conditions, or switching to another intervention.

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 88%)**  
The reported analyses were analysis of covariance and linear regression analyses. The article did not report an analysis specifically designed to estimate the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention regimen, such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Participants were aware of their assigned light condition, adherence information was not reported, and no appropriate analysis to estimate the effect of adherence to the assigned intervention was reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Acoustic reaction-time data were analyzed for 72 of 74 randomized participants (97.3%). Acoustic reaction time is a continuous outcome, and complete outcome data were available for more than 95% of randomized participants.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The article did not report sensitivity analyses, inverse-probability weighting, or another method specifically demonstrating that the result was not biased by missing outcome data. Intention-to-treat analysis was not reported as a method for correcting missing-data bias.

**3.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
The two exclusions were attributed to invalid peak oxygen-uptake measurements during the cardiorespiratory fitness test, rather than acoustic reaction-time performance. The reported reason does not suggest that missing acoustic reaction-time data depended on their true acoustic reaction-time values.

**3.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The reported reason for exclusion—invalid peak oxygen-uptake measurement—does not indicate a relationship between missing acoustic reaction-time data and the true acoustic reaction-time outcome. Exclusions were also balanced between BRIGHT and CONTROL (one participant in each group).

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Outcome data were available for 97.3% of randomized participants, exceeding the 95% threshold for a continuous outcome.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Acoustic reaction time was measured using a computer-based simple acoustic reaction-time task. Acoustic signals occurred randomly at 3–7-second intervals over 5 minutes, and participants responded by pressing the space bar. The median reaction time was calculated for each participant. This is an objective and appropriate measurement method.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
The same computer-based acoustic reaction-time task was performed by participants in all three groups immediately before and immediately after light exposure under room-light conditions.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The acoustic reaction-time outcome was computer based rather than assessor reported. In addition, the test supervisor was blinded to the light condition received by each participant.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Acoustic reaction time was an objective computerized performance measure based on the time taken to press a keyboard key after an acoustic signal. It was not a subjective patient-reported or assessor-judged outcome.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
There was no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention status influenced assessment of the computer-recorded acoustic reaction-time outcome.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Acoustic reaction time was measured objectively and comparably between groups, and outcome assessment was not likely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
The study was prospectively registered (NCT02203539), and reaction time was listed as a secondary outcome. The article stated that all statistical methods used to compare groups for reaction time were pre-specified and registered. The reported outcome and analysis using analysis of covariance adjusted for pre-exposure reaction time were consistent with the stated methods.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
The acoustic reaction-time measurement procedure was specified: a computer-based task administered immediately before and immediately after light exposure, with median reaction time calculated for each participant. Results for the planned before- and after-exposure measurements and adjusted between-group comparisons were reported.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 83%)**  
The article reported the planned analysis of covariance comparing groups after light exposure adjusted for baseline reaction time. It also reported the additional linear-regression analysis of actual light intensity, as described in the Methods section. There was no clear evidence that only a selectively favorable analysis was reported.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
The outcome was registered as a secondary outcome, the reported measurement and analyses were described as pre-specified, and there was no clear evidence of selective selection from multiple outcome measurements or analyses.

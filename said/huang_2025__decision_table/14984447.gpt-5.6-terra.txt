## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1 Was the allocation sequence adequately generated?**: **NI (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason**: The article states that participants were “randomly assigned” after stratification by income, but it does not describe a specific random-sequence generation method (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, block randomization, or coin tossing).

**1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**: **PY (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason**: Participants were “randomly assigned in a patient-blinded fashion.” This indicates that allocation was described as masked from patients at assignment, but the specific concealment mechanism (e.g., central allocation or sequentially numbered opaque envelopes) was not reported.

**1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**: **N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason**: Baseline characteristics were comparable between the no-cost and control groups, including age, sex, education, income, insurance status, diabetes type, duration of diabetes, insulin treatment duration, baseline SBGM frequency, HbA1c, random glucose, and insulin injections. The article states that the groups were well matched.

**Result of Domain 1**: **Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason**: Allocation was described as patient-blinded, baseline characteristics did not indicate a randomization problem, and no evidence suggested an inadequate allocation sequence, although the exact sequence-generation and concealment methods were not reported.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**: **Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason**: Participants in the no-cost group received 100 free reagent strips per month, whereas participants in the control group received one initial package and were instructed to purchase further strips. Therefore, participants could identify whether they had been assigned to the free-strip or purchase-strip group.

**2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial?**: **Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason**: The research nurse supplied glucometers and reagent strips, including the monthly strip supply for the no-cost group. Therefore, the intervention provider was aware of group assignment.

**2.3 Were important nonprotocol interventions balanced across intervention groups?**: **N (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
**Reason**: Insulin dose did not change significantly in the no-cost group but increased approximately 1.5-fold in the control group during the study. Insulin-dose changes are prognostic for HbA1c and were imbalanced between groups.

**2.4 Were there failures in implementing the intervention that could have affected the outcome?**: **Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
**Reason**: There was substantial withdrawal during follow-up: 19% (6/31) of participants in the no-cost group and 48% (15/31) in the control group withdrew by the end of the study. These proportions exceeded 10%.

**2.5 Was there non-adherence to the assigned intervention regimen that could have affected participants’ outcomes?**: **NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
**Reason**: The article reports SBGM frequency and dropout but does not report the proportion of participants who did not adhere to their assigned strip-access regimen, such as no-cost participants failing to use available strips or control participants obtaining strips through other means.

**2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention?**: **N (High Certainty, 100%)**  
**Reason**: The study used intention-to-treat analysis with last observation carried forward (LOCF) and also conducted repeated-measures analysis among completers. According to the specified criteria, ITT, per-protocol/completer, and LOCF-based approaches are not appropriate methods for estimating the effect of adherence to a sustained intervention.

**Result of Domain 2**: **High risk (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason**: Participants and intervention providers were aware of allocation; insulin-dose changes differed between groups; dropout exceeded 10%; adherence was insufficiently reported; and the analyses used ITT with LOCF and completer analyses rather than an appropriate adherence-effect analysis.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**: **N (High Certainty, 100%)**  
**Reason**: Sixty-two participants started the study, but only 41 remained at 12 months: 25/31 (80.6%) in the no-cost group and 16/31 (51.6%) in the control group. Thus, complete end-of-study SBGM-frequency and HbA1c data were available for 41/62 participants (66.1%), which is below 90% for these continuous outcomes.

**3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**: **N (High Certainty, 100%)**  
**Reason**: The study used LOCF for its intention-to-treat analysis. LOCF is not an appropriate method for correcting bias caused by missing outcome data. No inverse-probability weighting, multiple-imputation approach appropriate to the missingness mechanism, or sensitivity analysis demonstrating robustness to missing data was reported.

**3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**: **NI (Moderate Certainty, 65%)**  
**Reason**: The article reports substantial dropout and discusses possible reasons, including cost of strips and inconvenience. However, it does not provide participant-level reasons for withdrawal or establish whether missing SBGM-frequency or HbA1c data depended on the true values of these outcomes.

**3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**: **NI (Moderate Certainty, 65%)**  
**Reason**: Missing outcome data were markedly different between groups (19% dropout in the no-cost group versus 48% in the control group). However, the article does not provide sufficient information to establish that dropout was directly related to true SBGM frequency or HbA1c values.

**Result of Domain 3**: **High risk (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason**: Complete outcome data were available for only 66.1% of randomized participants, LOCF was used, and there was substantial differential dropout between groups without adequate evidence that missingness was unrelated to SBGM frequency or HbA1c.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**: **N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason**: SBGM frequency was determined by downloading glucometer memory, which is an objective recording method. HbA1c was measured using a DCA 2000 Analyzer, and blood glucose was measured using a YSI2300 glucose analyzer. These are appropriate objective measurement methods.

**4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**: **N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason**: Both groups attended visits at the same 2-month intervals. SBGM frequency was downloaded from the same glucometer model, and HbA1c was measured using the same method at the same scheduled visits for both groups.

**4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**: **Y (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason**: The research nurse distributed the reagent strips and conducted study visits, including glucometer-memory downloading. Therefore, the nurse was likely aware of whether participants were in the no-cost or control group.

**4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: **N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason**: Both outcomes were objective. SBGM frequency was electronically recorded in glucometer memory, and HbA1c was laboratory measured using an analyzer. These measurements are not subjective assessor judgments.

**4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: **N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason**: There is no clear evidence that knowledge of group assignment influenced electronically downloaded SBGM frequency or analyzer-based HbA1c measurements.

**Result of Domain 4**: **Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason**: SBGM frequency and HbA1c were measured with appropriate, objective, and comparable methods across groups. Although the research nurse likely knew group assignment, knowledge of allocation was unlikely to influence these objective measurements.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**Reason**: No trial registration number, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or prospectively finalized analysis plan was reported. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data became available.

**5.2 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**: **N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason**: SBGM frequency was consistently measured as mean tests per day from glucometer-memory downloads, and HbA1c was consistently measured using the DCA 2000 Analyzer. The study reports the longitudinal results graphically and reports the overall group effect from the General Linear Model. No clear evidence indicates selective reporting from alternative measurement scales or definitions.

**5.3 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?**: **Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason**: The Methods report multiple possible analyses, including General Linear Model analysis using ITT data with LOCF and repeated-measures analysis restricted to participants completing the entire study. The Results emphasize the General Linear Model findings, while complete results from the repeated-measures completer analysis are not reported. This provides evidence of multiple eligible analyses with only selected analyses fully reported.

**Result of Domain 5**: **High risk (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason**: The study was not registered and did not provide a pre-specified analysis plan. In addition, multiple analysis approaches were described, but only selected analysis results were fully reported.

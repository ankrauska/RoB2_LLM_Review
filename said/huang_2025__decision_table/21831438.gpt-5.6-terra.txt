## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
The article states that patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio using a “mixed block randomization (4 and 6 blocks) method,” which is an adequate random-sequence generation method.

**1.2: NI (98% High Certainty)**  
Neither the article nor the provided registry record describes allocation concealment procedures such as central randomization, sequentially numbered identical drug containers, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes. The study being randomized or open-label does not establish allocation concealment.

**1.3: Y (98% High Certainty)**  
There was a statistically significant baseline difference in age between groups: mean age was 51.0 years in the candesartan-HCT group and 47.8 years in the candesartan monotherapy group (P = 0.02). This baseline imbalance suggests a potential problem with the randomization process according to the specified criteria.

**Result of Domain 1: High risk (98% High Certainty)**  
Allocation concealment was not reported, and a statistically significant baseline age imbalance was present between randomized groups.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (95% High Certainty)**  
The registered protocol identifies the study as open-label with no masking. Under the specified decision rule, an open-label experiment is classified as N for this question.

**2.2: Y (98% High Certainty)**  
The study was open-label with no masking. Therefore, carers and personnel delivering the interventions were aware of the assigned intervention.

**2.3: NI (85% Moderate Certainty)**  
The article states that concomitant medication not permitted by the protocol was considered a major protocol violation, but it does not report whether important nonprotocol interventions occurred or whether they were balanced between treatment groups.

**2.4: Y (96% High Certainty)**  
Among 233 randomized participants, 35 discontinued the study (20 in the candesartan-HCT group and 15 in the monotherapy group), representing 15.0% of randomized participants. This exceeds the specified 10% threshold for failures in implementing the intervention.

**2.5: NI (88% Moderate Certainty)**  
Compliance was assessed by interview and pill counts, and median compliance was high (98.6% and 97.8%). However, the article does not report the number or proportion of participants with less than 75% compliance, treatment cessation, crossover, or switching that would allow assessment against the 10% non-adherence threshold.

**2.6: N (99% High Certainty)**  
The efficacy analysis used an intention-to-treat population, and a per-protocol analysis was also reported. According to the specified criteria, ITT and per-protocol analyses are inappropriate methods for estimating the effect of adhering to the intervention regimen.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (93% Moderate Certainty)**  
Intervention personnel were aware of treatment allocation; information on balance of nonprotocol interventions was unavailable; more than 10% of randomized participants discontinued; and the adherence-effect analysis was not appropriate under the specified criteria.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
The primary 4-week sitting DBP analysis included 224 participants: 111 in the candesartan-HCT group and 113 in the candesartan monotherapy group. Of 233 randomized participants, this represents 224/233 = 96.1% with observed outcome data, exceeding the 95% threshold for a continuous outcome.

**3.2: N (97% High Certainty)**  
The article reports ITT and per-protocol analyses but does not report inverse-probability weighting, an appropriate missing-data adjustment, or sensitivity analyses demonstrating that missing data did not bias the result. ITT alone does not correct missing-data bias.

**3.3: NI (90% Moderate Certainty)**  
The article does not provide reasons for the nine participants without primary-outcome data at week 4, nor does it establish whether missingness was related to the true sitting DBP outcome.

**3.4: NI (90% Moderate Certainty)**  
There is no reported analysis of characteristics or reasons for the missing 4-week DBP data that would determine whether missingness was likely related to the true outcome value.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (99% High Certainty)**  
Observed 4-week sitting DBP data were available for 96.1% of randomized participants, meeting the specified threshold for low risk due to missing outcome data.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (99% High Certainty)**  
Sitting DBP was measured using a fully automated Omron device. Participants rested for at least 5 minutes, three consecutive measurements were taken at 1-minute intervals, and the mean of the retained readings was used. This is an appropriate and reliable method for measuring sitting DBP.

**4.2: N (97% High Certainty)**  
The same sitting BP measurement procedure, automated device, timing before dosing, and visit schedule were used in both intervention groups. Measurements were therefore obtained comparably between groups.

**4.3: Y (96% High Certainty)**  
The study was open-label without masking. The investigators measured BP at study visits and were therefore aware of the intervention received by participants.

**4.4: N (98% High Certainty)**  
Sitting DBP is an objective physiological measurement obtained using an automated BP device. Under the specified criteria, objective outcomes are not considered susceptible to influence from knowledge of treatment allocation.

**4.5: N (95% High Certainty)**  
There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced measurement of the objectively measured sitting DBP outcome.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (97% High Certainty)**  
The outcome was measured objectively using a standardized automated procedure applied comparably in both groups. Although assessors were aware of assignment in this open-label study, there is no clear evidence that this influenced the objectively measured DBP outcome.

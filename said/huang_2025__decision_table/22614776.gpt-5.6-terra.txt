**Article ID**: Cunningham, 2012  
**Outcome Name**: peer aggression 12 months  
**registration number**: NCT00251212  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** Participants were described as “randomly selected” and assigned after stratification by gender and age. However, the article does not describe a specific method for generating the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, block randomization, or minimization).

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report allocation concealment procedures, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or identical numbered intervention containers.

**1.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The article reports initial differences between conditions in dropping out of school. Although the authors stated that adjustment for this variable did not alter findings, this represents a baseline difference between groups.

**Result of the domain: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation concealment was not reported, and an initial between-group baseline difference in school dropout was identified.

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Participants could distinguish their assigned condition: therapist-assisted computer brief intervention, computer-only brief intervention, or control brochure. Thus, participants were aware of their assigned intervention.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Therapists delivering the therapist-assisted intervention necessarily knew which participants received that intervention. Personnel providing the study interventions were aware of intervention assignment.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not provide information showing whether important nonprotocol interventions, including use of community resources or other violence-related services, were balanced across groups.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Only 607 of 726 randomized participants completed the 12-month follow-up; therefore, 119/726 (16.4%) did not complete follow-up. This exceeds 10%.

**2.5: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The article states that more than 95% of randomly assigned participants received their assigned intervention during the ED visit. Non-adherence was therefore below 10%.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The analysis was described as intention-to-treat. Under the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** Participants and intervention providers were aware of assigned intervention, information on balance of nonprotocol interventions was unavailable, more than 10% of participants did not complete follow-up, and intention-to-treat analysis was used rather than an analysis estimating the effect of adherence.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** Peer aggression was binary. At 12 months, 281 participants reported severe peer aggression (79 therapist, 98 computer, and 104 control), which is substantially greater than the approximately 119 participants missing follow-up data (726 randomized minus 607 completing 12-month follow-up).

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** The study used GEE analyses with all available data and an intention-to-treat approach, but it did not report inverse probability weighting, sensitivity analyses, or another method demonstrating that missing outcome data did not bias the result.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report reasons for missing 12-month peer-aggression data that would allow determination of whether missingness depended on the true value of peer aggression.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The article provides no reported reasons for missing peer-aggression outcomes by intervention group and no analysis of characteristics associated with missing outcome data.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** For this binary outcome, the observed number of severe peer-aggression events was substantially greater than the number of participants with missing follow-up data.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** Peer aggression was measured using four items from the Conflict Tactics Scale, producing a predefined binary severe peer-aggression outcome. This is an established measurement approach for aggression-related behaviors.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** Peer aggression was assessed using the same self-administered computerized survey approach at baseline and 12 months for participants in all intervention groups.

**4.3: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Peer aggression was self-reported by participants. Participants were aware of whether they received a therapist-assisted intervention, computer intervention, or control brochure.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Peer aggression was a subjective, participant-reported outcome. Self-reported aggressive behavior could be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** Although participants knew their intervention assignment, the article provides no clear evidence that this knowledge was likely to influence their computerized self-report of peer aggression. Surveys were self-administered, and the article notes procedures intended to support privacy and confidentiality.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** The subjective peer-aggression outcome was self-reported by participants who were aware of their intervention assignment, although there was no clear evidence that knowledge of assignment was likely to influence assessment.

---

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was registered prospectively (NCT00251212; first posted in 2005). The protocol identified violent behaviors as primary outcomes at 12 months. The article prespecified peer aggression measurement using Conflict Tactics Scale items and reported 12-month peer-aggression results using the stated GEE analysis approach.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** The article defined severe peer aggression using four Conflict Tactics Scale items and reported this prespecified binary peer-aggression outcome at 12 months. There is no clear evidence that the reported 12-month peer-aggression result was selected from multiple unreported eligible outcome measurements.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section specified GEE models for 12-month outcomes, and the Results section reported GEE estimates, P values, and odds ratios for severe peer aggression. There is no clear evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible analyses based on the results.

**Result of the domain: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** The outcome and analytical approach were described in the registered trial and article Methods, and the reported peer-aggression result corresponded to the specified binary outcome and GEE analysis.

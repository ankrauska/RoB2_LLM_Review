## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
The allocation sequence was generated using “a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method,” applied by a statistician.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The article does not describe a specific allocation-concealment method, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or identical numbered treatment containers. The statistician’s lack of involvement in data collection or analysis does not establish concealment until enrolment and assignment.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Baseline demographic characteristics were homogeneous across groups, with no statistically significant differences. Baseline VAS scores were also similar across groups (ultrasound 5.7, phonophoresis 5.7, iontophoresis 6.3).

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
The random sequence was adequately generated and baseline characteristics did not suggest a randomization problem. However, allocation concealment was not reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Ninety-nine participants were randomized (32 ultrasound, 33 phonophoresis, and 34 iontophoresis), and the article states that all participants completed the 20 treatment sessions. Post-treatment VAS results are reported for all three randomized groups. Thus, complete post-treatment VAS data were available for 99/99 participants (100%).

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
No sensitivity analysis, inverse probability weighting, or other method specifically addressing possible bias from missing outcome data was reported. However, outcome data were reported as available for all randomized participants.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The article does not report missing post-treatment VAS data or reasons for missingness. Therefore, it is not possible to determine whether missingness, if any, depended on the true pain outcome.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
No information is provided on differential missingness or reasons for any missing post-treatment VAS outcomes that would permit assessment of whether missingness was likely related to the true VAS value.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Post-treatment VAS data were available for all randomized participants; therefore, missing outcome data are unlikely to bias the result.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Pain was measured using a predefined 0–10 visual analogue scale (VAS), where 0 indicated no pain and 10 indicated the worst pain. VAS is an appropriate and established measure for patient-reported pain intensity.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
VAS was measured for all groups at the same scheduled time points: baseline, post-treatment, and one month post-treatment. The same blinded rehabilitation doctor attended all assessment visits using standardized protocols.

**4.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
VAS is patient-reported. The trial registration record states that participants were masked; therefore, participants providing the pain ratings were intended to be unaware of their assigned intervention.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
VAS pain is a subjective patient-reported outcome. Assessment could potentially be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention allocation was likely to influence post-treatment VAS assessment. Participants were registered as masked, and outcome assessments followed standardized procedures.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
VAS is an appropriate and consistently applied measurement method. Although pain is subjective, participants were registered as masked and there is no clear evidence that knowledge of allocation likely influenced pain assessment.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The study was registered (NCT01748188), and the registry identified VAS pain as the primary outcome. The published Methods and Results are consistent in reporting VAS at baseline, post-treatment, and one month post-treatment. However, the provided registry information does not include a detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The Methods prespecified VAS assessment at baseline, post-treatment, and one month post-treatment. The Results report VAS findings at all these time points. There is no indication that the post-treatment VAS result was selectively chosen from multiple unreported pain measures or time points.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
The Methods specified repeated-measures ANOVA for within-group change over time and ANOVA with post-hoc Scheffé testing for comparisons between treatment groups. The Results report the planned post-treatment between-group and within-group VAS analyses. No clear evidence indicates selective reporting from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
The outcome and its measurement time points were consistently reported, and no clear selective outcome measurement or analysis was identified. However, a sufficiently detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data analysis was not available.

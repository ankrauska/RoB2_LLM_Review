## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
**Reason:** The article states that eligible participants were “randomly assigned” to four groups, but does not describe a sequence-generation method (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random-number table, block randomization, or envelopes). No further sequence-generation method was reported.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report any allocation-concealment method, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or identical numbered containers. The registry describes single masking of participants, but does not describe concealment of the allocation sequence before enrolment and assignment.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The article reports that initial pretest analyses found no statistically significant differences among the four study groups in the outcomes of interest. The randomized group sizes were also similar (22, 21, 20, and 22 participants).

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation concealment was not reported (1.2 = NI), while baseline differences did not suggest a problem with randomization (1.3 = N). According to the specified algorithm, this results in some concerns.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** Participants received visibly different educational formats: a community video, knowledge video, both videos, or a pictorial pamphlet. Therefore, participants were aware of the intervention they received.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Carers or personnel delivering the intervention necessarily knew the assigned intervention because they administered or facilitated viewing of specific videos or reading of the pamphlet.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not provide sufficient information on potentially important nonprotocol interventions received during follow-up, such as additional asthma education, changes in clinical care, or unequal co-interventions between groups.

**2.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** Of 92 enrolled intervention participants, 85 completed the study. Seven participants did not complete the study (approximately 7.6%), which is below the specified threshold of more than 10% for important failures in implementation.

**2.5: NI (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** The article reports study discontinuations but does not provide sufficient information about adherence to the assigned educational regimen among participants who remained in the study, including whether participants fully watched the assigned videos or read the pamphlet as intended.

**2.6: NI (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** The analyses used ANOVA and ANCOVA to compare outcomes. The article does not report an analysis specifically estimating the effect of adherence to the assigned intervention, such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting. It also does not explicitly report an intention-to-treat, per-protocol, as-treated, or treatment-received analysis.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** Participants and intervention providers were aware of the assigned intervention (2.1 = Y; 2.2 = Y). Information on nonprotocol interventions was unavailable (2.3 = NI), and the analysis estimating the effect of adherence was not reported (2.6 = NI). Under the specified algorithm, this results in high risk.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: PN (Moderate Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** A total of 92 participants were enrolled in the educational intervention, and 85 completed the study. Thus, complete follow-up data were available for approximately 92.4% (85/92) of randomized participants. Inhaler-use technique was measured as a continuous score on a nine-step checklist; therefore, 90% to <95% complete outcome data corresponds to PN.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report sensitivity analyses, inverse-probability weighting, or another method specifically addressing bias caused by missing inhaler-technique outcome data. ANOVA/ANCOVA does not constitute evidence that missing outcome data did not bias the result.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 77%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report reasons for the missing inhaler-technique outcome data among the seven participants who did not complete the study. Therefore, it cannot be determined whether missingness depended on true inhaler-use technique.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 76%)**  
**Reason:** The article provides no group-specific reasons for loss to follow-up and no comparison of characteristics or outcomes between participants with complete versus missing inhaler-technique data. Therefore, it is unclear whether missingness was likely related to the true outcome value.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 89%)**  
**Reason:** Outcome data were available for approximately 92.4% of randomized participants (3.1 = PN). According to the specified algorithm, this gives a low-risk judgment for this domain.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (Moderate Certainty, 87%)**  
**Reason:** Inhaler-use technique was measured using a standardized nine-step observational checklist. The score ranged from 0 to 9, with higher scores representing more correctly performed inhaler-use steps. This is an appropriate direct observational method for assessing inhaler technique.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** The same inhaler-technique checklist and assessment procedures were used across all intervention groups during the in-person interviews at baseline and follow-up. There is no reported evidence that the measurement method, timing, or threshold differed between groups.

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 81%)**  
**Reason:** Inhaler technique was assessed by the interviewer and research coordinator. However, the article does not state that these outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention received, nor does it explicitly state that they were aware of allocation. Therefore, assessor awareness is unclear.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** Proper inhaler technique was assessed through direct observation using a checklist. Although structured, assessment required observer judgment regarding whether each inhaler-use step was correctly performed. Thus, knowledge of intervention assignment could potentially influence the assessment.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention status was likely to influence assessment of the standardized inhaler-technique checklist. The outcome was based on observed performance rather than patient-reported improvement, and two observers assessed inhaler technique.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
**Reason:** The measurement method was appropriate (4.1 = N) and comparable between groups (4.2 = N). However, assessor awareness was not reported (4.3 = NI), and the observational assessment could potentially be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment (4.4 = Y), although there was no clear evidence that such influence was likely (4.5 = N). Under the specified algorithm, this results in some concerns.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was registered (NCT01474928), but the registry does not provide a pre-specified analysis plan for inhaler-use technique. The registered primary outcome was medication adherence, and inhaler-use technique was not clearly identified as a registered primary or secondary outcome. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported inhaler-technique analysis followed a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available.

**5.2: Y (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** Inhaler-use technique was measured at pretest, immediate post-test, and 3-month follow-up. However, the article states that it summarizes analyses from pretest and 3-month follow-up data and does not fully report the immediate post-test inhaler-technique results. This indicates that multiple eligible time points were measured but only selected results were fully reported.

**5.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods state that ANOVA was initially conducted across the four groups and that ANCOVA was subsequently applied to compare print and video methods while adjusting for covariates. The report does not fully present all eligible analyses for inhaler-use technique, including the initially described ANOVA results and all planned adjusted analyses. Thus, the reported numerical result may have been selected from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** There is evidence that results may have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements/time points (5.2 = Y) and multiple eligible analyses (5.3 = Y). According to the specified algorithm, this results in high risk.

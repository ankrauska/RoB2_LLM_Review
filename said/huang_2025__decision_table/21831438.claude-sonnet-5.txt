# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Lee, 2011
**Outcome Name**: Changes in the mean sitting DBP from the baseline after 4 weeks of therapy
**Registration number**: NCT00621153

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: **Y** (95% High Certainty)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Study patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio by using the mixed block randomization (4 and 6 blocks) method," which is an acceptable method of sequence generation.

**1.2**: **NI** (90% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: The article does not mention any allocation concealment mechanism (e.g., central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, or opaque envelopes). The protocol confirms "masking: NONE" (open-label design), but concealment of the *allocation sequence prior to enrollment* is not described.

**1.3**: **Y** (90% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: Despite randomization, there was a statistically significant difference in age between groups (51.0 [10.2] years in combination group vs. 47.8 [10.7] years in monotherapy group; P=0.02), explicitly noted by the authors as occurring "despite random allocation."

**Result of Domain 1**: **High risk** (90% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: Per the algorithm, when 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = Y, the domain is rated High risk.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: **N** (95% High Certainty)
Reason: The study is explicitly described as "open-label" (per protocol title and article discussion: "the open study design could be the potential source of bias"). Per the specific instruction, open-label studies are coded N for this question.

**2.2**: **Y** (90% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: This is an open-label trial with no blinding of investigators/care providers; treatment allocation (monotherapy vs. combination pill) was known to those administering treatment.

**2.3**: **Y** (75% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: No significant imbalance in nonprotocol interventions was reported; concomitant antihypertensive medication was prohibited during the washout/treatment period, and no evidence indicates imbalance in permitted medications between groups.

**2.4**: **Y** (85% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: Of 233 randomized patients, 35 discontinued overall (20 in combination group, 15 in monotherapy group) = ~15%, exceeding the 10% threshold for implementation failures.

**2.5**: **N** (75% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: Compliance was reported as high in both groups (98.6% and 97.8% median compliance), with no evidence of substantial (>10%) non-adherence.

**2.6**: **N** (95% High Certainty)
Reason: The primary efficacy analysis used "Intention-to-treat (ITT)" analysis with ANCOVA; per the override instruction, ITT analysis is coded as N for this question.

**Result of Domain 2**: **High risk** (85% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: 2.2 = Y (satisfies "one of 2.1/2.2 is Y/PY/NI"), 2.3 = Y (satisfies NA/Y/PY), 2.4 = Y (satisfies "one of 2.4/2.5 is Y/PY/NI"), and 2.6 = N (satisfies NI/N/PN) → algorithm dictates High risk.

**Result of Domain 3**: **Low risk** (90% Moderate Certainty)

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: **N** (90% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: DBP was measured using a validated, fully automated device (Omron) with a standardized protocol (triplicate measurements, averaging, discarding extremes), which is an appropriate and reliable method.

**4.2**: **N** (90% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: BP was measured identically in both groups—same device, same visit schedule, same methodology, same investigator per patient throughout the study.

**4.3**: **Y** (85% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: The outcome (DBP) is not patient-reported; it is assessor-measured. Since this is an open-label trial, the outcome assessors/investigators were aware of participants' group assignment.

**4.4**: **N** (75% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: DBP measurement via a standardized automated device is an objective outcome measure, not subject to subjective interpretation by the assessor.

**Result of Domain 4**: **Low risk** (85% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: 4.1 = N/PN/NI, 4.2 = N/PN, 4.3 = Y/PY/NI, and 4.4 = N/PN → algorithm dictates Low risk.

**5.2**: **N** (85% Moderate Certainty)

**5.3**: **N** (80% Moderate Certainty)

## Overall Bias Risk Assessment Result for the Article

**Overall Result**: **High Risk** (90% Moderate Certainty)

Reason: Per the algorithm, since Domain 1 (randomization process) and Domain 2 (deviations from intended interventions) were both rated **High risk**, the overall risk of bias for this outcome (change in mean sitting DBP from baseline after 4 weeks) is **High Risk**. This is primarily driven by (1) an unexplained, statistically significant baseline age imbalance despite randomization coupled with no described allocation concealment, and (2) the open-label design combined with a >10% dropout rate and use of ITT (rather than an appropriate adherence-adjusted) analysis.

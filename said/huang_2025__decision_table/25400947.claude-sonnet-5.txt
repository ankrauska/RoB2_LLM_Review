## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (75%)
The article states subjects were "divided into two groups by simple random sampling" but does not specify the actual method used (e.g., computer-generated numbers, random number tables, block randomization, coin toss, etc.). No further methodological detail is provided anywhere in the manuscript to allow upgrading to "Y."

**1.2**: NI (90%)
No mention of allocation concealment methods (no reference to central allocation, sealed/opaque envelopes, sequentially numbered containers, etc.) anywhere in the Methods section.

**1.3**: Y (80%)
While parity, history of preterm labor, and age showed no significant differences (Table 2), Table 3 shows a statistically significant difference in baseline cervical effacement between groups (P ≤ 0.0001), a key prognostic factor for preterm delivery outcomes. This imbalance in an important prognostic baseline variable suggests a possible problem with the randomization process.

**Domain 1 Result: High risk (85%)**
Rationale: 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = Y → per rule, this combination yields "High risk."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (80%)
Given the study is described as "single blinded," and the two arms received medications via different routes (IV MgSO4 vs. oral celecoxib) with different monitoring protocols (deep tendon reflex/urine output for MgSO4 vs. nausea/skin rash checks for celecoxib), participants were very likely aware of their assigned treatment.

**2.2**: Y (85%)
Care providers administering IV MgSO4 versus oral celecoxib capsules, and performing route-specific monitoring, would necessarily know participants' allocation.

**2.3**: Y (75%)
All patients received the same background maintenance therapy (bed rest, hydration, corticosteroids) prior to randomization; no evidence of imbalanced non-protocol interventions.

**2.4**: N (70%)
Table 3 shows n=300 per group analyzed, matching original randomized allocation (600 total); no substantial withdrawal/implementation failure reported.

**2.5**: NI (70%)
No specific information on adherence/compliance rates or crossovers is reported.

**2.6**: NI (70%)
No explicit description of analysis approach (ITT, per-protocol, etc.) for handling adherence deviations.

**Domain 2 Result: High risk (80%)**
Rationale: One of 2.1/2.2 = Y; 2.3 = Y; one of 2.4/2.5 (2.5) = NI; 2.6 = NI → per rule, this combination yields "High risk."

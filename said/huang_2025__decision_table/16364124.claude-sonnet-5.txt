**Outcome Name**: Visual assessment for erythema and dryness as key symptoms

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1** – **Y** (95%)
The article explicitly states: "Randomization was performed via randomly permuted blocks of fixed size using validated software RANCODE 3.6." This is a clearly described, adequate method of sequence generation (block randomization via validated software).

**1.2** – **Y** (85%)
"Both hand rubs...were randomly assigned a letter A or B for blinding of the test formulations. Blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database." This describes a specific concealment mechanism (sealed blinding codes maintained until database closure), preventing participants, care providers, and assessors from knowing allocation in advance.

**1.3** – **N** (90%)
This is a within-subject, split-body design (each subject received both interventions on left/right cubital fossa). No baseline imbalance between "groups" is reported or plausible, as both interventions were applied to the same 35 subjects.

**Domain 1 Result: Low risk (90%)**
1.2 = Y, 1.1 = Y, 1.3 = N → meets criteria for "Low risk."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1** – **N** (90%)
Double-blind design with sealed blinding codes; no indication participants were aware of allocation.

**2.2** – **N** (85%)
Study nurses applied coded (A/B) products with blinding codes sealed until database closure; no indication of unblinding of intervention providers.

**2.3** – **Y** (75%)
No significant non-protocol interventions reported that were imbalanced between the two products (both products applied under identical procedures/timing to the same subjects).

**2.4** – **N** (85%)
3/35 (8.6%) subjects were excluded due to protocol violations (antiphlogistic intake, disinfection of the fossa cubitalis) — below the 10% threshold for implementation failure.

**2.5** – **N** (75%)
No substantial non-adherence reported beyond the same 3 protocol-violation cases (<10%).

**2.6** – **N** (90%)
The article states "Results are presented for the intention-to-treat (ITT) population" and also a per-protocol (PP) analysis — both are inappropriate methods for estimating the effect of adherence per the guideline.

**Domain 2 Result: Low risk (85%)**
2.1 = N, 2.2 = N, 2.4 = N, 2.5 = N → meets criteria for "Low risk."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1** – **PN** (85%)
Continuous outcome; 32/35 (91.4%) of randomized participants had complete (non-imputed) outcome data (3 participants required LOCF imputation), falling within the 90–95% range.

**3.2** – **N** (90%)
LOCF (Last Observation Carried Forward) was used to handle missing data — this is explicitly identified as an inappropriate method for correcting bias due to missing data (per instructions, ITT/LOCF do not qualify as bias-correcting methods).

**3.3** – **PY** (70%)
The 3 missing participants were excluded due to protocol violations: intake of antiphlogistics (which could directly reduce erythema, the outcome itself) and disinfection of the fossa cubitalis (which could directly alter the skin's dryness/erythema status). This suggests a plausible relationship between reasons for missingness and the true outcome value, though not explicitly confirmed.

**3.4** – **Y** (70%)
The specific reasons for dropout (antiphlogistic use affecting erythema; disinfectant application affecting skin condition) are mechanistically linked to the outcome domain being measured (erythema/dryness), suggesting missingness likely depended on the true outcome value.

**Domain 3 Result: High risk (75%)**
3.1 = PN, 3.2 = N, 3.3 = PY, 3.4 = Y → meets criteria for "High risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1** – **N** (85%)
A standardized, predefined visual scale (modified from Fitzpatrick) was used for both erythema and dryness — a reliable, validated method for this type of outcome.

**4.2** – **N** (85%)
Both preparations were assessed identically — same assessor, same time points, same scale — no differential measurement between groups.

**4.3** – **N** (90%)
Outcome was assessor-reported (dermatologist), not patient-reported. "All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator," indicating the assessor was unaware of allocation.

**4.4** – **Y** (90%)
The outcome (visual assessment of erythema/dryness) is a subjective measure assessed by an outcome assessor.

**4.5** – **N** (85%)
No clear evidence assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention, as the assessor was blinded via sealed coding (A/B) throughout the trial until database closure.

**Domain 4 Result: Low risk (85%)**
4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = N → meets criteria for "Low risk."

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1** – **NI** (90%)
No trial registration number is provided in the article, and no pre-specified/published analysis plan is referenced. Per instructions, absence of registration/protocol publication → NI.

**5.2** – **N** (80%)
The methods section pre-specifies the sum score (erythema + dryness) as the primary outcome; results report this sum score as well as the individual erythema and dryness components (Table 1, Figures 2–3) consistently with the described outcome domain — no evidence of selective reporting from multiple eligible measures.

**5.3** – **N** (80%)
The statistical methods (Fieller's CI for non-inferiority, Wilcoxon signed-rank test for superiority, ITT and PP analyses) are all pre-specified in the Methods and fully reported in the Results without evidence of selective analysis reporting.

**Domain 5 Result: Some concerns (80%)**
5.2 = N, 5.3 = N, 5.1 = NI → meets criteria for "Some concerns."

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (85%)
The article states patients were "randomly assigned in a 3:2 ratio... stratified according to clinical center and ischemic status with the use of an algorithm that ensured near balance in each stratum," but no specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, block randomization) is described in the article or the protocol.

**1.2**: NI (85%)
No mention of allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., central allocation, sealed envelopes, sequentially numbered containers) is provided anywhere in the article or protocol.

**1.3**: N (90%)
Table 1 shows baseline demographic, clinical, echocardiographic, and medication characteristics were similar between the CRT-ICD and ICD-only groups, with no notable imbalances reported.

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (85%)
Since 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = N, per the algorithm this yields "Some concerns."

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (90%)
This was an open-label trial (masking = NONE per protocol); no placebo/sham device was used. Given the differing devices and programming (CRT-ICD vs ICD-only), participants were aware of their assignment.

**2.2**: Y (95%)
The article explicitly states: "The treating physicians were aware of study-group assignments."

**2.3**: Y (85%)
Optimal pharmacologic therapy for heart failure was provided in both arms, and Table 1 shows similar medication use in both groups at enrollment, suggesting no important non-protocol imbalance.

**2.4**: N (85%)
Only 1.0% (CRT-ICD) and 2.6% (ICD-only) of patients failed to receive an assigned device — both under 10%.

**2.5**: Y (90%)
A total of 173 crossovers occurred: 91/731 (12.4%) of ICD-only patients crossed to CRT-ICD — exceeding the 10% threshold for non-adherence.

**2.6**: N (90%)
"Data analysis was performed according to the intention-to-treat principle" — an inappropriate method per criteria for estimating the effect of adherence.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (88%)
2.1/2.2 = Y; 2.3 = Y; one of 2.4/2.5 (2.5) = Y; 2.6 = N → High risk.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (85%)
The outcome (death, and HF events defined by predefined clinical criteria requiring decongestive therapy) was measured using a predefined, clinically reliable method, and events were adjudicated by an independent committee.

**4.2**: N (85%)
Both groups were followed with the same schedule of clinical visits (1 month post-randomization, then every 3 months), with identical HF event criteria applied to both groups.

**4.3**: Y (90%)
The outcome is not patient-reported; the article explicitly states: "The diagnosis of heart failure, which was made by physicians who were aware of study-group assignments." Although final adjudication was blinded, initial diagnosis/ascertainment was performed by unblinded assessors.

**4.4**: Y (85%)
While death is objective, the nonfatal heart-failure component is a subjective clinical determination (signs/symptoms requiring decongestive therapy) assessed by treating physicians who were aware of assignment — making this a subjective outcome assessed by an unblinded assessor.

**4.5**: Y (90%)
The article itself acknowledges this risk directly: "It is possible that the investigators' knowledge of study-group assignment contributed in some way to the lower frequency of heart failure in the CRT–ICD group." This is direct evidence of potential influence.

**Result of Domain 4**: High risk (90%)
4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = Y, 4.4 = Y, 4.5 = Y → High risk.

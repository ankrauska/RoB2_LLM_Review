**Outcome Name**: delayed diagnostic performance  
**registration number**: Not reported  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Participants were described as “randomly allocated” to four conditions, but the article did not report a method for generating the allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random-number table, block randomization, or equivalent).

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not report allocation concealment. It did not mention central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, concealed assignment procedures, or equivalent methods.

**1.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not provide a baseline-comparison table or statistical comparison of participant characteristics across the four randomized groups. The authors stated that they could not conclusively rule out imbalance between conditions or professional groups.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation concealment was not reported (1.2 = NI), and there was no reported baseline comparison demonstrating that randomization produced comparable groups (1.3 = NI).

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** Participants received visibly different instructional materials according to their assigned condition (science-based causal explanation, basic-science-first, clinical-features-first, or clinical-feature list). Therefore, participants could identify the nature of the instructional material they received.

**2.2: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The intervention was delivered through a custom-designed computer program, but the article did not report whether personnel responsible for administering the study or supporting participants were blinded to allocation.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not report sufficient information about potentially important nonprotocol interventions, activities, or exposures during the one-week follow-up period, nor whether these were balanced across groups.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Of 101 participants initially allocated (SC = 26, BC = 25, CB = 25, FL = 25), five did not complete delayed testing and two additional participants were excluded because they spent less than five minutes on the delayed diagnostic test. This represents 7/101 (6.9%), which is below the 10% threshold for implementation failures.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not report adherence to the assigned instructional regimen, crossovers, switching between study conditions, or other information allowing assessment of non-adherence.

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The reported mixed repeated-measures ANCOVA estimated differences between randomized groups, but the article did not report an analysis specifically designed to estimate the effect of adherence to the assigned intervention. No instrumental-variable analysis, inverse-probability weighting, or equivalent adherence-adjusted analysis was reported.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** Participants were aware of the different instructional materials (2.1 = Y), information on nonprotocol interventions was unavailable (2.3 = NI), and no appropriate adherence-effect analysis was reported (2.6 = NI).

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: PN (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Delayed diagnostic-performance data were analysed for 94 of 101 randomized participants. The complete-data proportion was 94/101 = 93.1%. Delayed diagnostic performance was a continuous outcome, so this corresponds to PN because complete outcome data were available for 90% to less than 95% of randomized participants.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not report inverse-probability weighting, sensitivity analyses, or other methods demonstrating that missing outcome data did not bias the delayed diagnostic-performance result. Excluding participants from the analysis does not establish absence of missing-data bias.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
**Reason:** Reasons for the five participants failing to complete the delayed session were not reported. Therefore, it is not possible to determine whether their missing delayed diagnostic-performance outcomes depended on their true outcome values.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 72%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not provide reasons for non-completion by intervention group or participant characteristics that would permit assessment of whether missingness was likely related to delayed diagnostic performance.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Complete delayed diagnostic-performance data were available for 93.1% of randomized participants (3.1 = PN), which meets the specified criterion for low risk.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** Delayed diagnostic performance was measured with 12 clinical vignettes requiring selection of the correct diagnosis from four possible disorders. The cases were developed by an expert clinician, validated by another independent clinical expert, had been used in three prior studies, and were reported to have adequate measurement properties.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** All groups completed the same form of delayed diagnostic assessment after the same one-week delay. Two diagnostic-test versions were counterbalanced between immediate and delayed testing, and the article reported that the tests had similar difficulty.

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** Delayed diagnostic performance was based on responses to multiple-choice clinical vignettes, but the article did not explicitly state whether outcome scoring or outcome assessors were blinded to intervention allocation.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** The outcome was the score on a structured diagnostic test with a correct diagnosis selected from four predefined options. This is an objective outcome measure, with limited scope for subjective assessor influence.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** There was no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced assessment of delayed diagnostic performance. The outcome was based on objectively scored responses to standardized diagnostic vignettes.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** The diagnostic-test measure was appropriate and supported by prior validation evidence (4.1 = N), measurement was comparable across groups (4.2 = N), and the objective scored outcome was not likely influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment (4.4 = N; 4.5 = N).

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** No trial registration number, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or prospectively finalized prespecified analysis plan was reported. Therefore, it cannot be established that the reported delayed diagnostic-performance analysis was prespecified before unblinded outcome data became available.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** The delayed diagnostic-performance outcome was measured using the prespecified delayed diagnostic test. The article reported the delayed diagnostic-performance results for all four intervention groups. Although alternate test versions were counterbalanced, the use of the alternate version at delayed testing was described in the Methods section.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section prespecified a mixed repeated-measures ANCOVA for diagnostic-test performance, with group as the between-subject factor and test timing as the repeated factor. The Results section reported this ANCOVA analysis, including the time effect and condition-by-time interaction. There was no clear evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of Domain 5: Some concerns (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The reported outcome measurement and analysis were consistent with the Methods section (5.2 = N; 5.3 = N), but no registration, protocol, or finalized prespecified analysis plan was available (5.1 = NI).

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: Y (95%)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Individuals were randomized by a computer-generated list," which is a clearly described adequate method of sequence generation.

**1.2**: Y (85%)
Reason: The article states that the randomization list "was used for packaging of the tablets and then maintained by a third party that was not involved in the conduction of the study. Marked tablet packs were designated for each patient." Combined with identical appearance, packaging, labeling, and taste of active and placebo tablets, this describes a sequentially numbered, identical-appearing container system managed by an independent third party, consistent with adequate concealment.

**1.3**: N (95%)
Reason: The article explicitly states "There was no significant difference in any baseline parameter between the two groups," and further clarifies that minor differences (e.g., heart failure, cerebrovascular disease prevalence) were not statistically significant.

**Result of Domain 1**: Low risk (90%)
Reason: 1.2 = Y, 1.1 = Y, 1.3 = N, satisfying the criteria for "Low risk."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions

**2.1**: N (95%)
Reason: The study used identical-appearing placebo tablets with matched taste ("Both kinds of tablets had artificial mint flavor to give an identical taste") and "Both patients and investigators were blinded." This is consistent with placebo-controlled blinding of participants.

**2.2**: N (95%)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Both patients and investigators were blinded," indicating that carers/intervention providers were unaware of allocation.

**2.3**: NI (70%)
Reason: The article does not provide clear information about non-protocol interventions (e.g., additional nutritional supplementation, dietary counseling changes) that could differentially affect the groups beyond the standard care described.

**2.4**: Y (85%)
Reason: Study medication was discontinued in 4/30 treatment patients (dyspepsia/dizziness) and 3/30 placebo patients (dyspepsia/skin rash), totaling 7/60 (11.7%) of participants — exceeding the 10% threshold for implementation failure.

**2.5**: Y (80%)
Reason: The same 7/60 (11.7%) patients who discontinued study medication represent non-adherence to the assigned intervention regimen, exceeding the 10% threshold, despite good compliance (>90%) in the remaining participants.

**2.6**: N (95%)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Analyses were intention to treat irrespective of adherence to treatment regimen," which is an inappropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to intervention per the guideline definitions.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (75%)
Reason: Although 2.1 and 2.2 indicate successful blinding (N/N), there were substantial deviations from intended intervention (2.4 = Y, 2.5 = Y, both >10%) that were not addressed with an appropriate analytic approach (2.6 = N, ITT was used instead of an appropriate adherence-corrected analysis). Given these unaddressed deviations affecting more than 10% of participants without corrective analysis, this pattern is best classified as High risk, consistent with the logic underlying the provided decision rules regarding inappropriate handling of intervention deviations.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: N (85%)
Reason: 60 patients were randomized. By the end of the 52-week follow-up, 7 patients died (2 treatment, 5 placebo), 2 patients underwent technique failure (transfer to hemodialysis), and 1 patient received a kidney transplant — a total of 10/60 (16.7%) patients with incomplete outcome data at final follow-up. This falls below the 90% threshold for complete continuous outcome data.

**3.2**: N (95%)
Reason: No sensitivity analysis, inverse probability weighting, or other bias-correction method was reported to address missing SGA data due to death/dropout. (Per instructions, ITT analysis does not qualify as a correction method for missing data bias.)

**3.3**: PY (70%)
Reason: Missingness was largely due to death and technique failure, which are health-status-related events. While mortality/technique failure are plausibly linked to poor nutritional status (and thus SGA score), the article does not explicitly state the relationship between the specific cause of missingness and the SGA outcome value, warranting a "Probably Yes."

**3.4**: Y (75%)
Reason: There was a numerical imbalance in mortality between groups (5 placebo deaths vs. 2 treatment deaths), and poor nutritional status (reflected by SGA) is a well-established risk factor for mortality and technique failure in dialysis patients. This suggests that missingness in the SGA outcome was likely related to the true (poor) value of the outcome, particularly in the placebo group.

**Result of Domain 3**: High risk (75%)
Reason: 3.1 = N, 3.2 = N, 3.3 = PY, 3.4 = Y, satisfying the criteria for "High risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90%)
Reason: SGA is a validated, standardized four-item seven-point instrument with documented excellent inter-observer reliability in this study (Cohen's kappa = 0.84), indicating an appropriate and reliable measurement method.

**4.2**: N (90%)
Reason: SGA was measured using the same method, tool, and time points (0, 12, 24, 36, 52 weeks) in both groups by the same trained observers.

**4.3**: N (85%)
Reason: SGA, though inherently somewhat subjective, was assessor-administered (not patient self-reported), and the article explicitly states assessors were "blinded from the treatment group allocation and biochemical results of the patients."

**4.4**: Y (90%)
Reason: SGA is a subjective outcome measure assessed by an outcome assessor (involving clinical judgment regarding weight change, anorexia, subcutaneous tissue, and muscle mass), which inherently carries potential for subjective influence despite assessor blinding.

**4.5**: N (80%)
Reason: There is no clear evidence suggesting that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention allocation; assessors were explicitly blinded to both treatment allocation and biochemical results, minimizing the likelihood of biased assessment.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (85%)
Reason: 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = N, satisfying the criteria for "Low risk."

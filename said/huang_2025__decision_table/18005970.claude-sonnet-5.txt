**1.1**: Result: **Y** (90% Moderate-High Certainty)
Reason: The article states "students enrolled in the research project were consequently randomised with a randomisation table," and Phase 2 groups were "randomly selected at each centre using a randomisation table." Use of a randomisation table is an acceptable method of sequence generation.

**1.2**: Result: **NI** (95% High Certainty)
Reason: No information is provided regarding concealment of the allocation sequence (no mention of central allocation, sealed envelopes, or similar safeguards).

**1.3**: Result: **N** (85% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: Table 2 footnote (b) explicitly states "n.s. = no significant difference between Trial and Control groups in each group, p>0.05." The significant differences reported (previous work experience duration, voluntary work, clinical training type) are between the two Faculties (Trento vs Udine), not between trial/control arms within each randomized cohort. Thus, no evidence that randomization within each site was compromised.

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Result: **Y** (90% Moderate-High Certainty)
Reason: This is an educational/tutorial intervention (laboratory sessions, intensive tutoring, weekly tutorials, routine tutoring); students inherently know which tutorial modality they are receiving. No blinding of participants is possible or reported.

**2.2**: Result: **Y** (90% Moderate-High Certainty)
Reason: Tutors/teachers delivering the differentiated tutorial strategies necessarily knew which intervention they were delivering; no blinding of care providers is feasible for this type of intervention.

**2.3**: Result: **NI** (60% Low Certainty)
Reason: The article mentions systemic changes (Trento experienced "cut-backs" reducing tutor numbers during the study, introducing routine tutorials), but it is unclear whether this constitutes an unbalanced non-protocol co-intervention rather than intrinsic to the design comparison itself. Insufficient explicit information to judge balance.

**2.4**: Result: **N** (85% Moderate Certainty)

**2.5**: Result: **NI** (80% Moderate Certainty)

**2.6**: Result: **NI** (75% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: No specific analysis (e.g., instrumental variable, IPW) addressing adherence-effect estimation is described; only standard univariate/multivariate association analyses were used, and no adherence-related analytic method is mentioned.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: Result: **N** (85% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: The outcome measurement method was predefined and reasonably reliable: standard problems were defined a priori by a panel of 15 professional nurses using Cohen's Kappa (cutoff K≥0.80), and the same "Valentina's case" was used consistently across phases.

**4.2**: Result: **N** (85% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: All groups were assessed using the identical case, identical time allowance (20 minutes), and identical phases/timing; no differential measurement procedures between groups are described.

**4.3**: Result: **N** (90% Moderate-High Certainty)
Reason: The outcome was not patient-reported; it was assessed by independent researchers. The article states: "Two researchers did blind evaluation of the problems individualised by the students," and "data were processed blind; only after this had been done were the identities of the Schools revealed" — indicating outcome assessors were unaware of group/site assignment.

**Result of Domain 4**: **Low risk** (90% Moderate-High Certainty)

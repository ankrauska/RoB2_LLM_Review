**Article ID**: Kitchin, 2001
**Outcome Name**: anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) 1 month post-vaccination
**registration number**: None

Domain 1
1.1 : NI (95%)
The article mentions that subjects were "randomised to receive a booster dose," but it does not provide information on the specific methods used to generate the random allocation sequence.
1.2 : NI (95%)
The study is described as an "open study," and there is no mention of assignment concealment methods such as opaque envelopes or central allocation.
1.3 : N (95%)
The baseline demographic characteristics of the participants (age, sex, height, and weight) were similar between the two groups, indicating no obvious problem with the randomization process.
Result of the domain 1 : Some concerns (95%)
The randomization process has some concerns because the method of sequence generation and allocation concealment were not reported, although baseline characteristics were balanced.

Domain 2
2.1 : Y (100%)
The trial is explicitly described as a "single centre, controlled, randomised, open study," meaning participants were aware of their assigned intervention.
2.2 : Y (100%)
Since it was an open study without any blinding mentioned for the intervention providers, the carers and people delivering the interventions were aware of the participants' assigned intervention.
2.3 : NI (95%)
The article does not provide information regarding any nonprotocol interventions or whether they were balanced across groups.
2.4 : N (95%)
Implementation of the intervention was successful for most participants. Only a few participants were excluded from the per-protocol analysis (6 out of 127, which is less than 10%), indicating no significant failures in implementing the intervention.
2.5 : N (95%)
The intervention consisted of a single booster vaccination. Non-adherence was minimal, with only a small number of participants excluded (less than 10%), meaning the vast majority adhered to the single-dose regimen.
2.6 : N (95%)
The article reports that "the results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported." A per-protocol analysis is considered an inappropriate analytical method for evaluating the effectiveness of assigning interventions in this context.
Result of the domain 2 : High risk (95%)
The domain is at high risk of bias because it was an open study where participants and carers were aware of the intervention, and the primary results were based on a per-protocol analysis rather than an appropriate method like intention-to-treat.

Domain 3
3.1 : Y (95%)
A total of 127 participants were randomized, and 121 (59 in the Vaqta group and 62 in the Avaxim group) were included in the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis. This represents 95.2% of the randomized participants, which is greater than or equal to 95%.
3.2 : Y (95%)
Because the proportion of missing data was very low (<5%), it is highly unlikely that the missing data biased the outcome, and sensitivity analyses or specialized corrections are not strictly necessary to confirm lack of bias.
3.3 : N (95%)
The reasons for exclusion (prior booster, travel, lack of serological results) do not appear to be dependent on the true value of the anti-HAV antibody levels.
3.4 : N (95%)
Given the low number of missing outcomes and the reasons provided, it is unlikely that missingness depended on the true value of the outcome.
Result of the domain 3 : Low risk (95%)
There is a low risk of bias due to missing outcome data because more than 95% of the randomized participants had complete data for the continuous outcome.

Domain 4
4.1 : N (100%)
The anti-HAV antibody GMT was measured using the modified HAVAB™ assay, which is an established and reliable quantitative method.
4.2 : N (100%)
Blood samples were taken for anti-HAV antibody titres at comparable time points (prior to vaccination and 1 month post-vaccination) and measured using the same methods for both groups.
4.3 : NI (80%)
The article states that serological evaluations were performed by Merck Research Laboratories, but it does not explicitly state whether the outcome assessors (laboratory personnel) were blinded to the assigned interventions.
4.4 : N (95%)
The outcome is an objective laboratory measure (antibody titre), so the assessment of the outcome could not have been meaningfully influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
4.5 : N (95%)
There is no evidence to suggest that the assessment of the objective laboratory outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
Result of the domain 4 : Low risk (95%)
The measurement of the outcome is at low risk of bias because it is an objective laboratory measurement, and the methods were appropriate and comparable between groups.

Domain 5
5.1 : NI (95%)
The article does not provide a trial registration number or reference to a pre-specified, published protocol, so it cannot be confirmed if the analysis followed a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinding.
5.2 : N (95%)
The reported numerical result (anti-HAV antibody GMT at 1 month) corresponds directly to the clearly defined primary evaluation criterion, with no evidence of selection from multiple eligible measurements.
5.3 : N (95%)
There is only one appropriate way the geometric mean titre was analyzed and compared, leaving no opportunity to select from multiple data analyses.
Result of the domain 5 : Some concerns (95%)
There are some concerns for this domain because the trial was not registered and a pre-specified analysis plan was not available to confirm that the reported analyses strictly followed initial intentions, though selection bias appears unlikely.

Overall Bias Risk for the Article : High risk (95%)
The overall bias risk for the article is High Risk, primarily due to the high risk of bias in Domain 2 (deviations from intended interventions), which stems from the open-label design combined with the reporting of only per-protocol analysis results.

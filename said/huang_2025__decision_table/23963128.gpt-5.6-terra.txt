## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** The article states that residents “were randomized” to direct, immediately available, or distant supervision. However, it does not report a random-sequence generation method (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, block randomization, or drawing lots).

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** No information is provided on allocation concealment. The article does not report central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, identical containers, or another specific concealment procedure.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** Baseline characteristics were presented for the three groups. Group sizes were similar to the intended allocation (direct, 18; immediately available, 16; distant, 19), and the reported demographic, training, ICU-experience, simulation-experience, and confidence characteristics do not show a clear imbalance suggesting a problem with randomization.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation concealment was not reported (1.2 = NI), while baseline characteristics did not clearly suggest a randomization problem (1.3 = N). According to the specified algorithm, this results in some concerns.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Residents were necessarily aware of their assigned supervision level because the interventions differed visibly and operationally: direct supervision involved the fellow being physically present in the simulation room, immediately available supervision involved a fellow available by telephone and able to attend the bedside, and distant supervision involved telephone-only availability.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Fellows delivering supervision necessarily knew the assigned intervention because they had to provide supervision according to the assigned physical-proximity condition.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report important nonprotocol interventions that were imbalanced between groups and prognostic for the team checklist score. Fellows and confederate clinicians received standardized instructions, and the simulation structure was similar across groups.

**2.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** The assigned supervision interventions were implemented for most residents. Under immediately available supervision, two residents did not call their supervisor and four received advice by telephone only; however, the supervisor remained immediately available as allocated. There is no evidence that more than 10% experienced a failure in implementation of the assigned supervision condition.

**2.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** No substantial non-adherence to the assigned supervision regimen was reported. Although residents varied in whether and when they contacted the immediately available or distant supervisor, the allocated supervision arrangements remained available and were not switched between intervention groups.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The analysis used one-way ANOVA according to the assigned supervision level. This is an intention-to-treat-type analysis of assignment rather than an appropriate method to estimate the effect of adherence to a sustained intervention regimen.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** Although residents and fellows were aware of the assigned level of supervision, important nonprotocol interventions were not reported as imbalanced, implementation failures were not evident for more than 10% of participants, and substantial non-adherence was not reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Fifty-three residents were recruited and randomized (direct, n = 18; immediately available, n = 16; distant, n = 19). Team checklist-score results were reported for all 53 residents. Thus, complete outcome data were available for 100% of randomized participants.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report inverse-probability weighting, sensitivity analyses, or other analyses specifically demonstrating that missing outcome data did not bias the result. However, this question does not alter the domain judgment because outcome data were available for all randomized residents.

**3.3: NI (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** No missing outcome data were reported, and the article provides no information concerning whether any hypothetical missingness could have depended on the true team checklist score.

**3.4: NI (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** No missing outcome data or reasons for missing outcome data were reported.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Team checklist-score data were available for all 53 randomized residents, exceeding the 95% threshold for a continuous outcome.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (Moderate Certainty, 76%)**  
**Reason:** The team checklist score was measured as the percentage of predefined clinical tasks completed by any member of the team during the supervised simulation scenario. Although the authors acknowledge that the scenario-specific checklist was not formally validated for construct validity, they report that its development ensured face and content validity. The article does not provide evidence that the measurement method was unreliable or inappropriate for recording completion of predefined clinical tasks.

**4.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The team checklist score was measured in all three supervision groups during the supervised simulation scenario using the same outcome definition: completed checklist tasks divided by the total checklist items. There is no report that measurement frequency, thresholds, or scoring procedures differed between intervention groups.

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not clearly state whether the assessors who determined team checklist scores for supervised scenarios were blinded to the allocated supervision level. Blinding procedures are described for raters assessing videotaped resident performance during unsupervised scenarios, but not specifically for team checklist scoring during supervised scenarios.

**4.4: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The team checklist score required assessment of whether clinical tasks were completed by members of the simulation team. Although based on predefined items, this outcome involves assessor judgment regarding task completion and could potentially be influenced by knowledge of the supervision condition.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** There is no clear evidence that knowledge of the assigned supervision level actually influenced checklist-score assessment. The checklist used predefined clinical tasks, which reduces the likelihood that assessors’ knowledge of intervention status influenced the recorded score.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** Outcome assessors’ awareness of intervention assignment was not reported (4.3 = NI). The checklist outcome involved assessor judgment and therefore could potentially be influenced by knowledge of intervention status (4.4 = Y), although there was no clear evidence that such influence was likely (4.5 = N).

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** No trial registration number, protocol, or pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available is reported. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported team checklist-score analysis followed a prespecified analysis plan.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section prespecified the team checklist score during the supervised scenario as an outcome for patient care, defined as the percentage of total checklist items completed by the team. The Results section reports this outcome using the stated measurement. There is no clear evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible team checklist-score measures, scales, definitions, or time points.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section prespecified a one-way ANOVA for team checklist scores across the three supervision levels, and the Results section reports the group scores and overall p value. There is no clear evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The result does not appear to have been selected from multiple outcome measurements or analyses, but the absence of a reported registration record or pre-specified analysis plan means that selective reporting cannot be fully excluded.

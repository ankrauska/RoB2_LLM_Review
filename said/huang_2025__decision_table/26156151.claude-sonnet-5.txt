## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (90% confidence)
The article states randomization was performed "using an interactive web-based response system" and stratified by center, but does not describe the specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, block randomization). No explicit generation method is reported, so the answer is NI.

**1.2**: Y (90% confidence)
The use of an interactive web-based response system (IWRS) for randomization constitutes a specific, described method of central allocation concealment, ensuring that allocation was concealed until enrollment.

**1.3**: N (90% confidence)
Table 1 shows no statistically significant differences in baseline demographic, clinical, or angiographic characteristics between the IVUS-guided and angiography-guided groups. The only significant differences noted (high-pressure poststent dilation, postprocedural MLD) are post-randomization procedural variables, not baseline characteristics, and likely reflect intervention effects rather than randomization failure.

**Domain 1 Result: Low risk (90% confidence)**
Rationale: 1.2 = Y, 1.1 = NI, 1.3 = N → satisfies the rule for “Low risk.”

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (80% confidence)
Participants were aware of their intervention because IVUS vs. angiography guidance is a procedural intervention without placebo/sham; per the registered protocol, masking was “single” with only the investigator (assessor) blinded, implying participants were not blinded.

**2.2**: Y (95% confidence)
Interventionalists performing PCI necessarily knew whether IVUS was being used, as it is an active procedural tool requiring operator awareness.

**2.3**: Y (85% confidence)
Discharge medications (dual antiplatelet therapy, statins, beta-blockers, ACEi/ARB) were balanced between groups (Table 1), with no significant differences reported in non-protocol interventions.

**2.4**: N (90% confidence)
Only 1 of 402 patients (0.5%) failed to complete follow-up (IVUS group 99.5% completion, angiography group 100%), far below the 10% threshold.

**2.5**: N (85% confidence)
Dual antiplatelet therapy adherence at last follow-up was 98.5% and 98.0% in the two groups, indicating minimal non-adherence (<10%).

**2.6**: N (90% confidence)
The article states "the intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses," which is considered an inappropriate method for correcting bias from non-adherence per the assessment criteria, thus this is coded N.

**Domain 2 Result: Low risk (88% confidence)**
Rationale: 2.1/2.2 = Y, 2.3 = Y, 2.4 = N, 2.5 = N → satisfies “Low risk” rule (deviations were balanced and minimal, regardless of 2.6 given the pathway through 2.3/2.4/2.5).

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.2**: N (90% confidence)

**4.5**: N (85% confidence)

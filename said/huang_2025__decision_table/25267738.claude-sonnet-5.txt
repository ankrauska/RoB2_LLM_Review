## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: PN (85%)
Reason: The article states "Random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." Sequence generation was based on clinical chart number, which falls under "some rule based on hospital or clinic record number."

**1.2**: PY (75%)
Reason: The article mentions a "sealed-envelope technique" for allocation, which is a concealment method, but does not specify that envelopes were opaque or sequentially numbered, leaving some uncertainty about whether participants may have been aware of assignment.

**1.3**: N (90%)
Reason: Table 1 shows no statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics (age, sex, BMI, cancer type, metastasis, chemotherapy, DVT site, etc.) between groups A1 and A2 (all P > .05).

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (85%)
Reason: 1.2 = PY and 1.1 = PN → per rule, this combination yields "Some concerns."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (90%)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT," indicating participants were aware of their assigned intervention.

**2.2**: Y (90%)
Reason: Same statement indicates treating physicians (carers) were also aware of assignment.

**2.3**: Y (80%)
Reason: Table 1 and Table 2 show chemotherapy, radiotherapy, hormone therapy, and central venous catheter use were balanced between A1 and A2 (no significant differences), suggesting no imbalance in non-protocol interventions.

**2.4**: Y (80%)
Reason: By 12 months, substantial proportions of patients were lost to follow-up due to death from cancer progression: 12/119 (10.1%) in A1 and 19/123 (15.4%) in A2, exceeding the 10% threshold for implementation failure.

**2.5**: Y (70%)
Reason: "15 of the 119 patients interrupted LMWH for less than 5 days during study treatment" (12.6%), which exceeds the 10% threshold for non-adherence, although the interruption was brief.

**2.6**: N (95%)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis," which is a pre-specified inappropriate method for assessing the effect of adherence per the guideline.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (85%)
Reason: 2.1/2.2 = Y, 2.3 = Y, one of 2.4/2.5 = Y, and 2.6 = N → per rule, this combination yields "High risk."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**Result of Domain 3**: Low risk (85%)
Reason: Since 3.1 = Y, per the decision rule, Domain 3 is directly rated "Low risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90%)
Reason: Recurrent VTE was diagnosed using validated, reliable methods (compression ultrasonography, contrast venography, clinical probability algorithms, ventilation-perfusion scanning, or CT), which are standard and appropriate measurement tools.

**4.2**: N (80%)
Reason: All groups underwent the same follow-up visit schedule (every 3 months) with testing performed only upon clinical suspicion, using the same diagnostic criteria across groups.

**4.3**: N (90%)
Reason: "All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of the patient's name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment." Outcome assessment was not patient-reported, and assessors were blinded.

**4.4**: N (85%)
Reason: The outcome (recurrent VTE) is an objective measure confirmed by imaging (ultrasonography/venography), not a subjective patient-reported outcome.

**4.5**: N (85%)
Reason: There is no clear evidence that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention, especially given the blinded central adjudication committee.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (85%)
Reason: 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = N → per rule, this combination yields "Low risk."

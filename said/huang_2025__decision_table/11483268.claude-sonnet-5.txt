## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (90% Confidence)
The article states subjects "were randomised to receive either Vaqta™ or Avaxim™" but does not describe the specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated, block randomization, random number tables). No further detail is given anywhere in the methods.

**1.2**: NI (90% Confidence)
The study is described as an "open study" with randomisation, but no information is provided regarding allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., central allocation, sequentially numbered sealed containers, opaque envelopes).

**1.3**: N (90% Confidence)
Table 1 shows very similar demographic characteristics (age, sex, weight, height) between groups, and the text explicitly states "The two groups were similar with respect to age, sex, height and weight." The pre-booster GMT difference (496 vs. 325, p=0.171) was not statistically significant and is attributable to chance.

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (90% Confidence)
Because 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = N, per the algorithm this yields "Some concerns."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (95% Confidence)
The study is explicitly described as an "open study," meaning participants were aware of their assigned intervention.

**2.2**: Y (95% Confidence)

**2.3**: NI (85% Confidence)
No information is provided regarding non-protocol interventions occurring during the trial.

**2.4**: N (90% Confidence)
Of 127 randomized, only 6 subjects (4.7%) were excluded from the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis (previous booster, travel to endemic area, missing serology) — well below the 10% threshold.

**2.5**: N (85% Confidence)

**Article ID**: Fineberg, 2007  
**Outcome Name**: time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment  
**registration number**: Not reported  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Eligible responders were assigned to escitalopram or placebo according to a computer-generated randomisation list. Randomisation was conducted in blocks of four.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
The randomisation series was unknown to investigators and was contained in sealed opaque envelopes. Sequentially enrolled patients received the lowest available randomisation number. Study medication tablets were identical in appearance, taste, and smell.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The article reports no differences in demographic or clinical parameters between the placebo and escitalopram groups at randomisation. The randomized group sizes were also comparable: placebo, 157; escitalopram, 163.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Allocation sequence generation and concealment were adequately described, and no baseline imbalance suggesting a problem with randomization was reported.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: PY (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Participants were formally blinded and received identical study tablets. However, during the first 2 weeks after randomisation, dizziness and nausea occurred significantly more often in the placebo group during escitalopram tapering, which could have led some participants to infer their allocation.

**2.2: PY (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Investigators and study personnel were stated to be blinded. However, the differential occurrence of discontinuation-related symptoms, particularly dizziness and nausea in the placebo/taper group, could potentially have revealed treatment allocation to carers or intervention providers.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
No important imbalanced non-protocol interventions were reported. Concomitant psychotropic treatment and formal psychotherapy were restricted by the protocol.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
More than 10% of randomized participants withdrew from double-blind treatment because of relapse/worsening of OCD. There were 81 relapses among 157 placebo participants and 38 relapses among 163 escitalopram participants; relapsed participants were withdrawn according to the protocol.

**2.5: NI (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
The article does not report medication-adherence or compliance data during the double-blind period, such as missed doses, treatment discontinuation not due to relapse, or crossover between groups.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The primary analysis was conducted using the intention-to-treat population. The article did not use an instrumental-variable analysis, inverse-probability weighting, or another appropriate method to estimate the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 93%)**  
There were more than 10% intervention-implementation failures due to withdrawal after relapse, adherence data were not reported, and the primary analysis was intention-to-treat rather than an appropriate analysis estimating the effect of adherence.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
For the time-to-relapse outcome, 119 relapse events were observed among all 320 randomized participants. The number of observed relapse events was much greater than the number of participants who withdrew for reasons other than relapse (13 escitalopram participants and 14 placebo participants; 27 total).

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
No inverse-probability weighting, missing-data sensitivity analysis, or other method specifically correcting potential bias from missing outcome data was reported. Intention-to-treat analysis does not itself correct bias due to missing outcome data.

**3.3: PY (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
Some missing outcome data resulted from withdrawals related to adverse events, serious adverse events, pregnancy, and other health-related circumstances. These circumstances may be associated with participants’ health status, although their direct relationship with true relapse status was not fully established.

**3.4: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
In this time-to-event analysis, withdrawals due to adverse events and health-related reasons could be related to the participant’s health status and potentially to the likelihood of relapse. Participants withdrawing before relapse could therefore have informative censoring.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 93%)**  
Observed relapse events substantially exceeded the number of participants with missing outcome data.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Time to relapse was prospectively defined using a clinically specified relapse criterion: an increase of at least 5 points in Y-BOCS total score from randomisation or investigator-judged lack of efficacy. Y-BOCS is an established OCD rating scale, and investigators received rater training intended to increase inter-rater reliability.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The outcome was assessed using the same scheduled assessment timetable and the same relapse definition in both randomized groups. Efficacy assessments occurred at weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and then every 4 weeks until the last double-blind dose.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Outcome assessors were study investigators, and all study personnel were blinded to treatment assignment throughout the study.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Relapse determination included Y-BOCS assessment and investigator judgment of unsatisfactory treatment effect. These are subjective clinical assessments that could potentially be influenced by knowledge of treatment allocation.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 91%)**  
There is no clear evidence that outcome assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention received because investigators and study personnel were blinded to treatment allocation.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The outcome definition and measurement procedures were appropriate and comparable between groups, and outcome assessors were blinded.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
The article states that the primary outcome and primary analysis were prospectively defined. However, no trial registration number, publicly available protocol, or pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available was reported.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The primary outcome was clearly specified as time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment, using a pre-defined relapse definition. The primary time-to-relapse result was reported using this definition. The post-hoc analysis limited to the Y-BOCS relapse criterion was explicitly identified as post-hoc.

**5.3: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
The Methods section specified a two-tailed log-rank test as the primary analysis, with Kaplan–Meier curves and Cox proportional-hazards modelling as additional analyses. These analyses were reported in the Results section. No evidence indicates that only a selectively favorable analysis was reported.

**Result of Domain 5: Some concerns (High Certainty, 90%)**  
Although the reported primary outcome and analyses were consistent with the Methods section, no registered protocol or pre-specified analysis plan was available to verify that the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

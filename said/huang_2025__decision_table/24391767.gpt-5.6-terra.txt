**Outcome Name**: image quality parameters  
**registration number**: NCT00715806  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: Patients were “randomly assigned (computer-generated sequence) in a 1:1 ratio” to open or short-bore MR imaging.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: The article explicitly states that “allocation was concealed (using sealed envelopes).”

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Baseline characteristics of the 93 patients included in the spinal-image-quality analysis were well matched between groups. Table 1 showed no statistically significant baseline differences, including sex, age, body size, imaging region, claustrophobia score, and pre-imaging anxiety.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: The allocation sequence was computer-generated, allocation concealment was reported using sealed envelopes, and baseline characteristics did not suggest a problem with randomization.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: The article states that patients could not be blinded to their assigned study group because of the different MR imaging settings.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: Personnel delivering the intervention necessarily operated or arranged imaging on either the open or short-bore scanner and would have been aware of the scanner assignment.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: Cross-referral and use of conscious sedation were specified trial procedures for patients unable to tolerate their initially assigned scanner. The article does not report important nonprotocol interventions that were imbalanced between groups.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: There were failures to implement the initially assigned scanner intervention. Twenty-five examinations were performed after cross-referral to the other scanner, which exceeds 10% of the 93 patients who underwent spinal MR imaging.

**2.5: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Cross-referral from the assigned scanner to the alternative scanner represents crossover/non-adherence to the assigned intervention. This occurred in 25 examinations, exceeding 10% of participants.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: The primary image-quality analysis included examinations performed after cross-referral and compared examinations according to the scanner on which imaging was actually performed (48 short-bore and 55 open-MR examinations). This is an analysis by treatment received rather than an appropriate analysis estimating the effect of adherence to assigned intervention.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: Participants and intervention providers were aware of scanner assignment, more than 10% of patients crossed over to the alternative scanner, and the image-quality analysis was based on the scanner actually received rather than using an appropriate method to estimate the effect of adhering to assignment.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Of 106 patients with an indication for spinal MR imaging, 93 underwent imaging and were included in the image-quality analysis. The complete outcome-data proportion was therefore 93/106 = 87.7%, which is below 90% for this continuous outcome.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: No sensitivity analysis, inverse-probability weighting, or other appropriate method to assess or correct potential bias from missing image-quality data was reported.

**3.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 77%)**  
Reason: The available information indicates that imaging was missing because some patients could not undergo MR imaging owing to claustrophobia before imaging was completed or started. The article does not indicate that missing image-quality measurements depended on their unobserved true image-quality values.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
Reason: The article does not provide a comparison of missing image-quality outcomes between randomized groups or an analysis of whether the reasons for missing imaging were related to the unobserved image-quality results.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 79%)**  
Reason: Although outcome data were available for fewer than 90% of the patients with a spinal-imaging indication and no correction for missing data was reported, the reported reason for missing imaging does not establish that missingness depended on true image-quality values.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: Image quality was measured using predefined qualitative ratings, quantitative signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR), and contour-sharpness measurements. The article describes the rating scales, region-of-interest procedures, formulas, workstation, and contour-sharpness method.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: Image-quality parameters were assessed using the same qualitative and quantitative measurement approaches for images from both scanner groups. The sequence setup aimed for comparable voxel size and imaging time on both scanners.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: The article explicitly states that, at the time of image analysis, the examiners were blinded to imaging technique and patient identity.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Qualitative image-quality ratings, including contrast, contour sharpness, noise, artifacts, and overall image quality, involve assessor judgment and therefore could be influenced by knowledge of the intervention if assessors were unblinded.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: There is no clear evidence that outcome assessment was influenced by knowledge of the intervention because the image assessors were blinded to imaging technique and patient identity.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Detailed and appropriate image-quality measurement methods were used comparably across groups, and outcome assessors were blinded to scanner type.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 83%)**  
Reason: The trial was registered (NCT00715806) before recruitment, and a detailed trial protocol was published. The image-quality measurements and analysis methods described in the Methods section—qualitative image-quality ratings, SNR, CNR, and contour sharpness—were reported in the Results section.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
Reason: The study reported the intended qualitative and quantitative image-quality parameters across the available spinal sequences and anatomical regions. There is no clear evidence that only selected measurements or time points within the image-quality domain were reported.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Reason: The statistical methods described in the Methods section, including Mann–Whitney tests for qualitative outcomes and appropriate tests for continuous variables, correspond to the reported image-quality analyses. There is no clear evidence that the numerical results were selected from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
Reason: The trial was registered and supported by a published protocol, and the reported image-quality outcomes and analyses were consistent with the methods described in the article.

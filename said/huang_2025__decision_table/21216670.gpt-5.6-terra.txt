**Article ID**: Chollet, 2011  
**Outcome Name**: Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale  
**registration number**: NCT00657163  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
The allocation sequence was generated using a computer random-number generator, with balanced block randomisation (block size of four) by centre.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
Allocation was concealed using sequentially numbered opaque envelopes.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
The groups were reported to be well balanced for baseline demographic characteristics and stroke severity. Although mean age, previous stroke history, and baseline FMMS score were somewhat higher in the fluoxetine group, the article describes these as imbalances addressed in adjusted analyses and does not report statistically significant or clearly non-random baseline differences indicating a failure of randomisation.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The sequence was computer-generated, allocation was adequately concealed, and no clear evidence indicates that baseline differences resulted from a problem in the randomisation process.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 100%)**  
Participants were masked to treatment allocation. The placebo capsules were identical to fluoxetine capsules in appearance and packaging.

**2.2: N (High Certainty, 100%)**  
Carers, site study staff, investigators, and physicians assessing outcomes were masked to treatment allocation.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
All participants received physiotherapy and organised inpatient stroke care. Although rehabilitation procedures varied across centres, both treatment groups received standard centre-specific rehabilitation, and the primary analysis adjusted for centre. No important imbalance in non-protocol interventions between groups was reported.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
There were five failures to complete follow-up or treatment assessment among 118 randomised participants: two deaths and three withdrawals (4.2%). This was below 10%.

**2.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Treatment compliance was similar in both groups. Mean relative dose intensity was 94.8% in the fluoxetine group and 94.0% in the placebo group. No evidence indicates that more than 10% of participants had clinically important non-adherence, cessation, or crossover.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The primary efficacy analysis used the full-analysis set, including only patients for whom outcome data were available. This available-case approach is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adherence to the assigned intervention under the specified criteria.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Participants, carers, and outcome-related study staff were masked; no important imbalance in concomitant care or substantial implementation failures was reported; and treatment adherence was high and similar between groups. Although the available-case analysis was not an adherence-effect analysis, there was no evidence of substantial non-adherence that would require such an analysis.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
Outcome data for FMMS progression at day 90 were available for 113 of 118 randomised participants: 57/59 in the fluoxetine group and 56/59 in the placebo group. The complete-data proportion was 95.8%, which is at least 95% for this continuous outcome.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The study did not report inverse probability weighting, an appropriate missing-data sensitivity analysis, or another method demonstrating that missing data did not bias the FMMS result. The full-analysis set was based on participants with available data.

**3.3: PY (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
Missing data resulted from deaths and withdrawals due to severe hypoxia, kidney tumour, pulmonary embolism, respiratory distress, and septic shock. These reasons reflect health status and could potentially be related to functional motor recovery, although a direct relation to the true FMMS value was not established.

**3.4: Y (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
The reasons for missing data included serious medical complications and deaths. Such events may plausibly be associated with participants’ true functional status and motor recovery. The reasons and proportions of missing data also differed slightly between treatment groups.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
FMMS outcome data were available for 95.8% of all randomised participants, exceeding the 95% threshold for a continuous outcome.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 100%)**  
The outcome was measured with the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale. The article describes the FMMS as a widely used measure of post-stroke motor recovery with excellent intra-rater and inter-rater reliability and validity.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
FMMS assessments were scheduled at the same time points for both groups: baseline, day 30, and day 90. There is no evidence that measurement procedures or assessment thresholds differed between groups.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The FMMS was assessed by physiotherapists. The article states that all site study staff and investigators were masked to treatment allocation, and physicians assessing outcomes were also masked.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
FMMS motor-function scoring is an assessor-administered functional assessment. Its assessment could be influenced by knowledge of treatment allocation if assessors were aware of allocation.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention status influenced assessment, because outcome-related investigators and site study staff were masked to treatment allocation.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
FMMS is a valid and reliable outcome measure, assessments were conducted comparably between groups, and outcome assessors were masked.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
The trial was registered (NCT00657163), and the registry specified progression in the FMMS at 3 months as the primary outcome. The article also reported FMMS change from baseline to day 90 as its primary outcome. However, the registry information provided does not contain a detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data became available. In addition, the registry listed FMMS assessment at 12 months as a secondary outcome, which was not reported in this article.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 90%)**  
The assessed result was progression in FMMS from baseline to day 90, which corresponded to the pre-specified primary outcome at 3 months in both the article and trial registry. The article reported total, upper-limb, and lower-limb FMMS results and did not provide clear evidence that only favourable outcome measurements were selectively reported.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The Methods section specified the primary analysis of change in FMMS over 90 days, including multiple linear regression adjusted for centre and baseline imbalances. The reported primary adjusted FMMS result was consistent with this analysis. Although additional sensitivity analyses were reported, there is no clear evidence that the primary numerical result was selectively chosen from multiple eligible analyses on the basis of its results.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
The primary outcome and its 3-month time point were consistent with the registered outcome. However, a detailed pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available was not provided, and the registered 12-month FMMS secondary outcome was not reported in this article.

---

# Overall Bias Risk for the Article: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 80%)

The trial has low risk of bias in the randomisation process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, and outcome measurement for progression in FMMS. Some concerns remain regarding selection of the reported result because a sufficiently detailed pre-specified analysis plan was not available and a registered 12-month FMMS outcome was not reported.

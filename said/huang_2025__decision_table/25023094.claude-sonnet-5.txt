## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: **NI** (Moderate Certainty, 80%)
The article states "the distribution of subjects to the two treatment groups was performed using sex-stratified randomization" and "a non-probabilistic sampling row was used" and "assigned by simple random selection." No specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated numbers, random number table, coin toss, etc.) is described. Therefore, information is insufficient to classify as Y.

**1.2**: **PY** (Moderate Certainty, 80%)
The article states "the allocation sequence was blinded through the use of sealed envelopes." Sealed envelopes suggest a concealment mechanism was used, but the article does not specify whether the envelopes were opaque or sequentially numbered, so full safeguards cannot be confirmed. This corresponds to "PY" per the criteria (concealment mentioned, but participants may not be aware — sealed envelopes generally protect against foreknowledge).

**1.3**: **N** (High Certainty, 95%)
Table 1 shows no statistically significant differences between groups on any variable, including the pre-test VAS (p = 0.421) and age (p = 0.784) and sex distribution (p = 1.000). Descriptive differences in mobility variables were also attributed to normal variation, not randomization failure.

**Result of Domain 1**: **Low risk** (Moderate-High Certainty, 85%)
Reasoning: 1.2 = PY, 1.1 = NI, 1.3 = N → satisfies rule for "Low risk" (1.2 = Y/PY; 1.1 = Y/NI/PY; 1.3 = N/PN/NI).

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: **N** (High Certainty, 95%)
VAS is a validated, widely used, and reliable tool for pain measurement; it was predefined in the Methods section.

**4.2**: **N** (High Certainty, 90%)

**4.3**: **N** (Moderate Certainty, 80%)

Per the rule, when a placebo/sham intervention is used for participant blinding on a patient-reported outcome, the answer is "N."

**4.4**: **Y** (High Certainty, 95%) [reported for completeness though not required due to Domain 4 rule triggering earlier]

**4.5**: **N** (Moderate Certainty, 75%) [reported for completeness]
There is no clear evidence in the article suggesting that blinding of patients failed or that assessment was influenced by knowledge of allocation; the placebo control and blinded assessor support adequate blinding.

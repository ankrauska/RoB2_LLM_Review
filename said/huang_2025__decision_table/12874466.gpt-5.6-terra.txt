**Article ID**: Szeto, 2003  
**Outcome Name**: subjective global assessment (SGA) score  
**registration number**: Not reported  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: Participants were randomized using a “computer-generated list.”

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The computer-generated list was used for tablet packaging and was maintained by an independent third party not involved in conducting the study. Sodium bicarbonate and placebo tablets had identical appearance, packaging, labeling, and taste. Patients and investigators were blinded until the randomization code was broken after study completion.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: The article states that there was no significant difference in any baseline parameter between intervention groups. Although there were numerical differences in heart failure and cerebrovascular disease, these were reported as not statistically significant.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The allocation sequence was computer generated, allocation was concealed through third-party-controlled identical tablet packaging, and baseline differences did not suggest a problem with randomization.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: Participants received identical placebo tablets, and both patients and investigators were blinded to treatment allocation.

**2.2: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Investigators and personnel delivering trial-related care were blinded to allocation. The randomization code was not broken until the end of the study.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
Reason: Apart from study medication, clinical management was the same as for other patients. Both groups received conventional dextrose-based peritoneal dialysis solution, and dialysis prescriptions were changed only when there was clinical evidence of underdialysis. No important imbalanced nonprotocol interventions affecting SGA were reported.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: Study medication was stopped in seven of 60 randomized participants because of adverse effects: four in the treatment group and three in the placebo group. This represents 11.7% of randomized participants, exceeding 10%. Nutritional assessments continued despite discontinuation.

**2.5: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Seven of 60 participants stopped their assigned study medication prematurely, representing 11.7% of randomized participants. This exceeded the 10% threshold for non-adherence. Compliance among the remaining participants was over 90% by pill count.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: The study used an intention-to-treat analysis irrespective of adherence to the treatment regimen. According to the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: More than 10% of participants discontinued the assigned intervention, constituting both implementation failure and non-adherence, while the analysis used intention-to-treat analysis rather than an analysis appropriate for estimating the effect of adherence.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (Moderate Certainty, 81%)**  
Reason: Sixty participants were randomized. During follow-up, seven participants died, two transferred to long-term hemodialysis, and one received a kidney transplant. Therefore, complete 52-week SGA data could not be available for all randomized participants; available complete data were likely below 90% of randomized participants.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: No sensitivity analysis, inverse probability weighting, or other method to evaluate or correct bias due to missing SGA outcome data was reported. Intention-to-treat analysis does not itself correct bias caused by missing outcome data.

**3.3: Y (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: Missing SGA outcome data could depend on the true nutritional-status outcome because participants died or experienced clinical events such as peritonitis, vascular disease, and transfer to hemodialysis. These events may be related to nutritional and overall health status measured by SGA.

**3.4: Y (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Reason: Death, transfer to hemodialysis, and kidney transplantation are clinical events likely related to participants’ health status and potentially to their true SGA scores. The report did not provide analyses demonstrating that the missingness was unrelated to SGA outcome values.

**Result of Domain 3: High risk (High Certainty, 91%)**  
Reason: Complete SGA data were likely unavailable for more than 10% of randomized participants, no analysis addressing missing-data bias was reported, and missingness was likely related to participants’ underlying health and nutritional status.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: SGA was measured using a predefined four-item seven-point system. The two assessors were trained before the study and achieved a Cohen’s kappa coefficient of 0.84, indicating excellent interobserver agreement.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: SGA was assessed in both groups at the same prespecified time points: baseline and 12, 24, 36, and 52 weeks. The same four-item seven-point SGA system was used for both groups.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: SGA was assessed by two trained observers who were blinded to treatment-group allocation and biochemical results.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: SGA is a subjective outcome assessed by observers and incorporates clinical judgment regarding weight change, anorexia, subcutaneous tissue, and muscle mass. Therefore, assessment could in principle be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: The SGA assessors were blinded to treatment allocation. There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention status influenced outcome assessment.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: The SGA measurement method was predefined and reliable, measurements were performed comparably between groups, and the trained outcome assessors were blinded to treatment allocation.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: No trial registration number, protocol, or prospectively finalized statistical analysis plan was reported. Therefore, it cannot be established that the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
Reason: The Methods section prespecified SGA as the primary outcome and specified assessment at baseline and at 12, 24, 36, and 52 weeks. The Results section reports overall SGA scores across these stated time points, as well as the repeated-measures analysis.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
Reason: The Methods section prespecified repeated-measures ANOVA for longitudinal nutritional indices, including SGA score, with post hoc t tests using Bonferroni adjustment. The Results section reports the repeated-measures ANOVA and post hoc comparisons consistent with this plan. No clear evidence of selection from multiple analyses was reported.

**Result of Domain 5: Some concerns (High Certainty, 91%)**  
Reason: Although the reported SGA outcome measurements and analyses were consistent with the Methods section, no registration record or prespecified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome-data analysis was reported.

# Overall Bias Risk for the Article: High Risk (High Certainty, 94%)

Reason: The study is at high risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions and due to missing SGA outcome data. More than 10% of participants discontinued study medication, the analysis was intention-to-treat rather than an adherence-effect analysis, and missing SGA data were likely related to participants’ health status without reported analyses to address missing-data bias.

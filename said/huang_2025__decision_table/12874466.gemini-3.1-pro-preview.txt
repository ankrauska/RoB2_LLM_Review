**Article ID**: Szeto, 2003
**Outcome Name**: subjective global assessment (SGA) score
**registration number**: None

Domain 1
1.1 : Y (High Certainty 100%)
The article explicitly states that "Individuals were randomized by a computer-generated list."
1.2 : Y (High Certainty 100%)
The article mentions that the randomization list was "maintained by a third party that was not involved in the conduction of the study" and "Marked tablet packs were designated for each patient," ensuring allocation concealment.
1.3 : N (High Certainty 100%)
Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics of both groups, and there are no significant differences that would suggest a problem with the randomization process.
Result of the domain (High Certainty 100%)
Low risk
The allocation sequence was adequately generated and concealed, and baseline characteristics did not suggest any problems with randomization.

Domain 2
2.1 : N (High Certainty 100%)
The article states that "The appearance, packaging, and labeling of the tablets were identical. Both kinds of tablets had artificial mint flavor to give an identical taste... Both patients and investigators were blinded."
2.2 : N (High Certainty 100%)
The article confirms that "Both patients and investigators were blinded."
2.3 : Y (High Certainty 95%)
Clinical management was the same for both groups except for the study medication.
2.4 : Y (Moderate Certainty 90%)
Seven patients out of 60 (11.6%) could not tolerate the study medication and it was stopped, which is slightly more than 10%. 
2.5 : Y (Moderate Certainty 90%)
The same 7 participants (11.6%) had non-adherence as they stopped taking the medication.
2.6 : N (High Certainty 100%)
The article states "Analyses were intention to treat irrespective of adherence to treatment regimen," which is considered an inappropriate analytical method for evaluating the effectiveness of persistent interventions according to the criteria.
Result of the domain (Moderate Certainty 90%)
Low risk
Since both participants and carers/investigators were unaware of the assigned interventions (blinding was successful), the risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions is considered low.

Domain 3
3.1 : Y (High Certainty 95%)
Although 7 patients stopped medication, the article states "biochemical tests and nutritional assessment were continued for 52 wk according to the study protocol", implying that outcome data were available for all or nearly all randomized participants.
3.2 : Y (High Certainty 95%)
Outcome data were nearly complete for all randomized participants, so there is no evidence of bias driven by missing outcome data.
3.3 : N (High Certainty 95%)
There were no significant missing outcome data as assessments continued per protocol even for those who stopped the medication.
3.4 : N (High Certainty 95%)
Missingness was not a significant issue and was unlikely to depend on the true value of the outcome.
Result of the domain (High Certainty 95%)
Low risk
Data for the outcome were available for nearly all participants randomized, resulting in a low risk of bias.

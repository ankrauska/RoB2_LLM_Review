## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

Reason: The article explicitly states "Procedure type was assigned by a computer-generated randomization list."

**1.2**: **Y** (90% Certainty)

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: **N** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Length of hospital stay (discharge date) is a standard, reliable measurement method predefined as the primary outcome.

**4.2**: **N** (80% Certainty)
Reason: The same discharge criterion/measurement method (patient self-determined readiness) applied equally to both groups; no differential measurement protocol between groups.

**4.3**: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Although not strictly "patient-reported," the outcome relies on patient decision-making ("Each patient decided for herself when she was ready to be discharged from hospital"), and patients were aware of their group assignment given the unblinded nature of surgery.

**4.4**: **Y** (80% Certainty)
Reason: Because discharge timing was self-determined by the patient rather than by strict objective clinical criteria, this outcome carries a subjective component susceptible to patient expectations/awareness of intervention type.

**4.5**: **Y** (80% Certainty)
Reason: The authors explicitly acknowledge in the Discussion that "patients could not be blinded to type of surgical treatment; thus, their expectations may have influenced the duration of their recuperation," a rationale that also plausibly extends to patient-determined hospital discharge, given the same self-report mechanism.

**Result of Domain 4**: **High risk** (85% Certainty)
Reason: 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = Y, 4.4 = Y, 4.5 = Y — this combination meets the criteria for high risk of bias in outcome measurement.

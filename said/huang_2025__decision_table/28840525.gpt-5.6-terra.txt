**Article ID**: Murphy, 2018  
**Outcome Name**: T2D remission using four different HbA1c thresholds  
**registration number**: ACTRN12611000751976; NCT01486680  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Randomisation was conducted using “computer-generated random number codes” and minimization according to prespecified factors (age, BMI, duration of T2D, insulin treatment, and ethnicity).

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Random number codes were managed by an independent study member and were disclosed only after induction of anaesthesia. This describes concealment of allocation until participants had been enrolled and assigned.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, ethnicity, diabetes duration, insulin use, HbA1c, body weight, and BMI category, were generally comparable between groups. The reported baseline difference in anxiety scores does not indicate an important prognostic imbalance for the assessed outcome of T2D remission.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The sequence was computer generated, allocation was concealed until after anaesthesia induction, and baseline characteristics did not suggest a problem with the randomization process.

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** The study was described as double-blind, with participants masked to treatment allocation. Identical port placements and methylene-blue leak testing irrespective of procedure were used to support participant blinding.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Surgeons and personnel delivering the surgical procedures necessarily knew which procedure was performed (LSG or SR-LRYGB).

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** Diabetes medications were discontinued after surgery subject to endocrinologist review, and post-operative metabolic medications were restarted using a standard protocol by endocrinologists blinded to allocation. No important imbalance in non-protocol interventions was reported.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** Five of 58 participants in the LSG group were lost to follow-up (8.6%), and no participants in the SR-LRYGB group were lost to follow-up. This is below the specified 10% threshold for failures in implementing the intervention.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not report information on adherence to the assigned intervention regimen after surgery, beyond reporting follow-up losses and surgical adverse events.

**2.6: NI (Moderate Certainty, 77%)**  
**Reason:** The article reported Fisher’s exact tests and repeated-measures mixed models but did not report instrumental-variable analysis, inverse-probability weighting, or another specified analysis estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 83%)**  
**Reason:** Although participants were blinded and post-operative medication management was standardized, intervention providers were aware of allocation, adherence information was not reported, and no appropriate analysis to estimate the effect of adherence was reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** T2D remission is a binary outcome. At 1 year, outcome data were available for 56/56 SR-LRYGB participants and 53/58 LSG participants. Five LSG participants had missing outcome data. The number of remission events was substantially greater than the number with missing outcome data for every HbA1c threshold; for example, 44 remission events occurred at HbA1c ≤38 mmol/mol, compared with five missing outcomes.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** No inverse-probability weighting, missing-data sensitivity analysis, or other method specifically addressing potential bias from missing outcome data was reported.

**3.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** The five missing participants were reported as lost because of failure to make contact and/or failure to attend appointments beyond 3 months after surgery. The reported reason does not indicate that missingness depended on the true T2D-remission outcome.

**3.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 76%)**  
**Reason:** The available explanation for missingness was non-contact/non-attendance, without reported evidence that the missing outcome data were related to true HbA1c status or T2D remission.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** The outcome was binary, the number of observed remission events was much greater than the number of participants with missing outcome data, and the reported reasons for missingness did not indicate dependence on true remission status.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** T2D remission was defined using objective laboratory HbA1c thresholds (≤38 mmol/mol, <42 mmol/mol, <48 mmol/mol, and <53 mmol/mol) together with absence of glucose-lowering medication. HbA1c is a reliable measure of glycaemic control.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** HbA1c was assessed at the same scheduled follow-up time point (1 year) in both intervention groups, using the same HbA1c thresholds and medication-free requirement.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was double-blind for patients and outcome assessors. In addition, HbA1c is an objective laboratory measurement.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** T2D remission based on HbA1c concentration and glucose-lowering medication use is an objective outcome measure.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention status was likely to influence laboratory HbA1c measurement or the prespecified medication-free remission definition.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** T2D remission was measured objectively using the same HbA1c thresholds and assessment schedule in both groups, and outcome assessors were blinded.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was prospectively registered (ACTRN12611000751976), and the study protocol was reported previously. The Methods section specified T2D remission using four HbA1c thresholds without glucose-lowering therapy, and the Results section reported all four prespecified threshold-based remission results at 1 year. The stated analysis using Fisher’s exact test for between-group comparisons of remission proportions was consistent with the reported results.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** All four HbA1c threshold definitions specified for T2D remission in the Methods section—≤38 mmol/mol, <42 mmol/mol, <48 mmol/mol, and <53 mmol/mol without glucose-lowering therapy—were reported in the Results section.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section prespecified Fisher’s exact test for comparing proportions achieving T2D remission, and the Results section reported remission proportions and corresponding p-values for each HbA1c threshold. No evidence of selection from multiple eligible analyses was reported.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
**Reason:** The remission outcome definitions and the analysis approach reported in the Results were consistent with those specified in the Methods, and all four planned HbA1c threshold results were reported.

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: Allocation of etch/primer systems was undertaken using a stated block-randomization method. The report specifies that block randomization ensured that every 10 consecutive participants included five allocated to each cross-quadrant configuration.

**1.2: PY (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The operators were “unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial,” indicating that allocation was concealed until enrolment. However, the specific concealment mechanism (e.g., central allocation or sequentially numbered opaque envelopes) was not described.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Reason: This was a split-mouth cross-quadrant trial, so each participant received both etching systems. No baseline imbalance between intervention conditions was reported or suggested.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The allocation sequence was generated by block randomization, allocation was concealed until enrolment, and no baseline differences suggesting a randomization problem were identified.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 90%)**

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 92%)**

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Failure rate is a binary outcome. There were approximately 50 observed bracket failures, whereas only one participant moved away before the end of follow-up. The number of observed failure events was much greater than the number of participants with incomplete follow-up.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: No sensitivity analysis, inverse probability weighting, or other method to evaluate or correct potential bias from missing outcome data was reported.

**3.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Reason: The only reported incomplete follow-up occurred because a participant moved to another city. This reason was not reported as related to bracket failure or the true failure outcome.

**3.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Reason: The participant who moved had no bracket failures before leaving the study, and there was no indication that missingness was related to the true failure outcome.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: The number of observed bracket-failure events was substantially greater than the amount of missing outcome information, and the reported reason for incomplete follow-up was not related to bracket failure.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Bracket failure was measured by recording whether a bond failed and the date and tooth on which failure occurred during 12 months of monitoring. This is an appropriate and reliable measurement method for bond failure.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: Both intervention conditions were applied within the same participants and were monitored over the same 12-month period using the same definition of bracket failure.

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
Reason: The article did not explicitly state whether the individuals assessing or recording bracket failures were blinded to the etching/primer system used. Operators were not blinded, but the report does not explicitly identify the outcome assessor.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: Bracket failure is an objective clinical outcome: a bracket is either detached or remains bonded. It is not a subjective patient-reported outcome.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: There is no clear evidence that knowledge of the assigned etching system would be likely to influence assessment of an objectively observable bracket failure.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 91%)**  
Reason: Bracket failure was measured objectively and comparably between groups. Although assessor blinding was not explicitly reported, there is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment would influence assessment of bracket detachment.

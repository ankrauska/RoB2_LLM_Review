**Article ID**: Dissanayaka, 2015
**Outcome Name**: Pain intensity
**registration number**: None

**Domain 1**
1.1 : Y (100%)
Reason you summarized: The article explicitly states that allocation was done using "computerized block randomization".

1.2 : Y (100%)
Reason you summarized: The article explicitly states that the allocation group was "concealed in envelops".

1.3 : N (100%)
Reason you summarized: The article states "One-way ANOVA found no significant differences in baseline pain intensity levels reported among the three groups". Baseline characteristics (Table 1) also do not suggest significant differences beyond random error.

Result of the domain (100%)
Low risk
Reason you summarized: The randomization sequence was adequately generated, allocation was concealed, and baseline differences did not suggest a problem. 

**Domain 2**
2.1 : Y (100%)
Reason you summarized: Participants were not blinded to the interventions since the physical therapy modalities (TENS, IFT, or control) are active treatments and the article states "The participants were blind to the allocation to treatment groups after randomization", but given the nature of the interventions (TENS and IFT) and no sham/placebo, they would be aware of their assigned intervention.

2.2 : Y (100%)
Reason you summarized: The principal investigator acted as the therapist administering all study treatments, so they were aware of the participants' assigned interventions.

2.3 : Y (100%)
Reason you summarized: Standard care was provided to all groups and there is no evidence of significant non-protocol interventions being imbalanced across groups.

2.4 : N (100%)
Reason you summarized: The article states that all 105 recruited participants completed the study and were entered into the analysis, indicating no failure in implementing the intervention for >10%.

2.5 : N (100%)
Reason you summarized: All participants completed the study and there is no mention of non-adherence. 

2.6 : NI (100%)
Reason you summarized: The article does not provide specific information about using appropriate analytical methods like inverse probability weighting or instrumental variable analyses to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention.

Result of the domain (100%)
Some concerns
Reason you summarized: Participants and personnel were aware of the intervention, and there is no information on appropriate analysis to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention.

**Domain 4**
4.1 : N (100%)

4.2 : N (100%)
Reason you summarized: Pain intensity was measured at the same time points and using the same method for all groups.

4.3 : Y (100%)
Reason you summarized: Pain intensity is a patient-reported outcome, and participants were aware of their interventions. Additionally, the outcome assessor (principal investigator) was also the therapist and was aware of the interventions.

4.4 : Y (100%)
Reason you summarized: Pain intensity is a subjective measure reported by the patient.

4.5 : Y (100%)
Reason you summarized: Because pain intensity is subjective and both the patient and the outcome assessor were aware of the intervention, the assessment of the outcome is likely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

Result of the domain (100%)
High risk
Reason you summarized: The outcome is subjective, the assessors/patients were unblinded, and it is likely that the assessment was influenced by this knowledge.

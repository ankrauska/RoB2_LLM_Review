**Outcome Name**: Beck Depression Inventory (total score) post intervention  
**registration number**: NCT01401296  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: The article states that participants were “randomly allocated” to the Deprexis or wait-list group in consecutive order, but it does not report a specific method for generating the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random-number table, block randomization, or similar method).

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: The article does not describe allocation concealment. It does not report central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or another specific concealment procedure. The statement that participants were allocated after providing their email addresses does not establish concealment.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: Baseline characteristics were reported for the two groups. The article states that randomization was successful and that no statistically significant differences were found for background or psychopathological variables. Baseline BDI total score did not differ between groups (wait-list: 30.02; Deprexis: 28.81; p > .4).

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Allocation-sequence concealment was not reported (1.2 = NI), while baseline data did not indicate a problem with randomization (1.3 = N). According to the prespecified decision rules, this leads to some concerns.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: Participants were aware of their assigned intervention. Those allocated to immediate treatment received a Deprexis access voucher, whereas wait-list participants were explicitly informed that they were assigned to the control group and would receive access after the eight-week post-intervention survey.

**2.2: Y (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
Reason: Delivery of the intervention required allocation-specific emails and vouchers. Therefore, the study personnel or automated delivery system responsible for sending the Deprexis access code would necessarily have been aware of whether participants were assigned to immediate Deprexis access or the wait-list condition. No blinding of intervention delivery was reported.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: Concurrent psychological/psychiatric treatment and psychotropic medication were permitted. At baseline, psychotropic medication use and therapy status did not differ statistically between the groups (all reported p-values > .1). No clear evidence of an important imbalance in nonprotocol interventions between groups was reported.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: Trial completion was 78.1% in the Deprexis group, meaning that 21.9% of participants allocated to Deprexis did not complete the post-intervention assessment. This exceeds the specified 10% threshold for failures in implementing the intervention/trial procedures.

**2.5: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: Among Deprexis completers, 84.5% reported that they were able to perform the program exercises regularly. Therefore, 15.5% reported not being able to perform the exercises regularly. This exceeds the 10% threshold for non-adherence to the assigned intervention regimen.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: The study used both per-protocol and intention-to-treat analyses. Under the specified criteria, per-protocol analysis and ITT analysis are classified as inappropriate methods for estimating the effect of adhering to the intervention regimen.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Participants and intervention deliverers were aware of allocation, more than 10% of the Deprexis group did not complete the trial procedures, and more than 10% of completers reported imperfect regular adherence. The analyses used per-protocol and ITT approaches rather than an appropriate method to estimate the effect of adherence.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: BDI post-intervention data were available for 170 of 210 randomized participants: 90/105 in the wait-list group and 80/105 in the Deprexis group. The proportion with complete observed BDI outcome data was therefore 170/210 = 81.0%, which is below 90% for this continuous outcome.

**3.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
Reason: The article used multiple imputation for the ITT analysis, but it did not report an analysis specifically demonstrating that the result was not biased by missing outcome data, such as inverse probability weighting or a formal missing-data sensitivity analysis. ITT analysis alone does not establish that missing-data bias was removed.

**3.3: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: The article does not report reasons for missing BDI post-intervention data. Therefore, it is not possible to determine whether missingness depended on participants’ true depression severity.

**3.4: NI (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: The proportions of non-completion differed between groups (14.3% in the wait-list group and 21.9% in the Deprexis group), but the article does not provide reasons for missing BDI data or evidence that the differential missingness was related to BDI outcome values.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: Observed BDI outcome data were available for only 81.0% of randomized participants. The study did not provide evidence that missing-data bias was absent, and the relationship between missingness and the true BDI outcome could not be determined.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: The BDI total score was measured using the Beck Depression Inventory, a predefined and widely used 21-item self-report measure of depressive symptoms. The article reports good psychometric properties, including internal consistency above .80 and a BDI re-test reliability of r = .72 in the wait-list group.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Both groups completed the same online questionnaire battery at baseline and at the eight-week post-intervention assessment. The BDI was therefore measured using the same method and at comparable time points in both groups.

**4.3: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: The BDI is a patient-reported outcome. Participants knew whether they had immediate access to Deprexis or had been assigned to the wait-list condition. Therefore, outcome assessment was made by participants aware of their intervention assignment.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: The BDI total score is a subjective patient-reported measure of depressive symptoms. Patient reporting of subjective depressive symptoms could be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
Reason: Although the BDI is subjective and participants knew their allocation, the article does not provide clear evidence that knowledge of receiving Deprexis versus wait-list assignment actually influenced BDI assessment. Under the specified instruction, absence of clear evidence warrants an answer of N.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 91%)**  
Reason: The BDI was a reliable and appropriately applied measure, administered comparably in both groups. However, participants knew their allocation and self-reported a subjective outcome, creating some concern despite no clear evidence that knowledge of allocation influenced the assessments.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 93%)**  
Reason: The study was registered prospectively (NCT01401296), and the registry identifies depressive symptoms assessed by the BDI as the primary outcome. However, neither the registry information nor the article provides a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data became available. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported numerical BDI result was analyzed according to a pre-specified analysis plan.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The BDI total score was identified as the primary outcome in the article and in the trial registration. The post-intervention BDI total-score result was reported. Although BDI subscales were additionally reported, the specified outcome for this assessment is the BDI total score, and there is no clear evidence that the reported total-score result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements on the basis of results.

**5.3: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: The Methods section specified both per-protocol and ITT analyses, and the Results section reported both per-protocol and multiple-imputation ITT results for BDI total score. There is no clear evidence that one analysis was selectively reported from multiple eligible analyses on the basis of its results.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 91%)**  
Reason: The BDI total-score outcome and both stated analytical approaches were reported, but a finalized pre-specified statistical analysis plan before unblinded outcome-data availability was not reported.

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The article states that patients were “randomly selected” for insertion of the 0.014- or 0.016-inch wire. However, it does not describe a sequence-generation method such as computer-generated random numbers, random-number tables, block randomization, coin tossing, or envelope shuffling.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** No information was reported on allocation concealment. The article does not mention central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, identical numbered wire containers, or any other procedure to conceal allocation before enrolment and assignment.

**1.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The groups contained 56 and 53 participants, respectively, which is compatible with approximately equal allocation. However, the article does not provide a formal baseline comparison of relevant participant characteristics between the two wire groups. Therefore, baseline differences suggesting a problem with randomization cannot be fully assessed.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation concealment was not reported (1.2 = NI), and there was no reported evidence of problematic baseline imbalance (1.3 = NI).

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report whether participants were blinded to the size of the initial archwire assigned. No placebo, sham procedure, or participant-blinding procedure was described.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** The orthodontic personnel who inserted and ligated the 0.014- or 0.016-inch archwire would have been aware of the wire size delivered to each participant. No blinding of intervention providers was reported.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** No important non-protocol intervention likely to affect the timing of *initial pain* was reported as imbalanced between groups. Participants were allowed to take pain relief when necessary, but analgesic use would occur after pain was perceived and therefore would not reasonably affect the reported time at which initial pain first occurred.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** All 109 participants completed and returned the questionnaires. No failures in implementation of the assigned archwire intervention, withdrawals, or discontinuations exceeding 10% were reported.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not provide information on adherence to the assigned archwire regimen, crossovers, wire changes, or switching to another intervention during the assessment period.

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** No analysis intended to estimate the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention was reported. The article does not report instrumental-variable analysis, inverse-probability weighting, or another adherence-adjusted analysis.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** Participants and intervention providers were not shown to be blinded (2.1 = NI; 2.2 = Y). Although non-protocol interventions were not considered important for initial pain (2.3 = Y), adherence information was unavailable (2.5 = NI), and no appropriate adherence-effect analysis was reported (2.6 = NI).

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** Initial pain was measured using a predefined questionnaire asking participants when pain was first perceived after archwire insertion. Patient-reported assessment is appropriate for pain, which is a subjective experience. The questionnaire also used a visual analogue scale for pain intensity, described as sensitive and reliable.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** Both intervention groups received the same questionnaire and were asked to report the time at which initial pain was first perceived after archwire insertion. The outcome was measured in the same manner in both groups.

**4.3: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Initial pain was patient reported. The article does not state whether participants were blinded to their allocated archwire size; therefore, it is unclear whether outcome assessors/participants were aware of the assigned intervention.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Initial pain is a subjective, patient-reported outcome. Assessment of pain could be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 77%)**  
**Reason:** Although initial pain is subjective, the article provides no clear evidence that awareness of the allocated wire size was likely to influence participants’ reporting of the time at which pain first occurred.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
**Reason:** Outcome measurement was comparable between groups and appropriate (4.1 = N; 4.2 = N), but it was not reported whether participants were blinded (4.3 = NI). Initial pain is subjective and could be influenced by knowledge of intervention (4.4 = Y), although no clear evidence showed that such influence was likely (4.5 = N).

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** No trial registration, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or prespecified analysis plan finalized before outcome-data analysis was reported.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** Initial pain was assessed using one prespecified questionnaire item asking when pain was first perceived after wire insertion. The results report the distribution of initial-pain timing across the planned hourly categories. There is no clear evidence that the reported initial-pain result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements.

**5.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 72%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section lists chi-squared and Fisher’s exact tests, but the article does not clearly specify which analysis was prespecified and applied specifically to the initial-pain timing outcome. Therefore, selection from multiple eligible analyses cannot be excluded.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** No prespecified analysis plan or registration was reported (5.1 = NI), and it is unclear whether the analysis of initial pain was selected from multiple eligible analyses (5.3 = NI). There was no clear evidence of selection from multiple outcome measurements (5.2 = N).

# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Murphy, 2018 (Silastic Ring Gastric Bypass vs Sleeve Gastrectomy)
**Outcome Name**: T2D remission using four different HbA1c thresholds (≤38, <42, <48, <53 mmol/mol)
**Registration Number**: ACTRN12611000751976 (prospective); NCT01486680 (retrospective)

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: **Y** (95% Certainty)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Randomisation was by computer-generated random number codes managed by an independent study member, and included minimization based on age, BMI, time since diagnosis, insulin treatment and ethnicity." This is a clearly described adequate method.

**1.2**: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The article states allocation codes were "managed by an independent study member" and "disclosed after anaesthesia induction," indicating central allocation with concealment maintained until after participants were fully committed to the procedure (post-anaesthesia). This meets criteria for a specific, robust concealment method.

**1.3**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Table 1 shows baseline characteristics (age, sex, ethnicity, diabetes duration, insulin use, HbA1c, weight, BMI) were reasonably balanced between groups. No baseline outcome-related variable showed a problematic imbalance suggestive of a randomization failure. Minor differences (e.g., baseline anxiety scores, noted separately in Results) are attributable to chance given the minimization-based randomization process.

**Result of Domain 1**: **Low risk** (90% Certainty)
Reason: 1.2 = Y, 1.1 = Y, 1.3 = N, satisfying the rule for "Low risk."

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: **PY** (75% Certainty)
Reason: The study was described as double-blind (patient-blinded), with identical port placements for both procedures. However, SR-LRYGB-specific complications occurred (e.g., "removal of silastic ring for vomiting," ulcers, anastomotic leak) that could reveal allocation to affected participants, creating potential for partial unblinding.

**2.2**: **Y** (95% Certainty)
Reason: Surgeons performing the operations must be aware of which procedure (LSG vs SR-LRYGB) they are performing, as these are distinctly different operations. Only the assessing endocrinologists were blinded, not the surgical team.

**2.3**: **Y** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Post-operative diabetes medication was managed via "a standard medical review protocol, by endocrinologists blinded to treatment allocation," ensuring balanced non-protocol co-interventions across groups.

**2.4**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Only 5/114 (4.4%) participants were lost to follow-up (all from LSG group), which is below the 10% threshold.

**2.5**: **N** (70% Certainty)
Reason: No evidence of non-adherence to the assigned surgical intervention was reported; once surgery is performed, adherence is essentially fixed (unlike medication trials).

**2.6**: **NI** (60% Certainty)
Reason: The article does not explicitly state whether an ITT, per-protocol, or as-treated analysis was used. Analysis appears to be based on available case data (n=53 LSG, n=56 SR-LRYGB) without explicit methodological labeling.

**Result of Domain 2**: **Low risk** (80% Certainty)
Reason: 2.1 = PY (Y/PY/NI category), 2.3 = Y, 2.4 = N, 2.5 = N — satisfies the rule for "Low risk" (deviations from intervention were balanced and did not affect outcome via co-interventions or implementation failures).

---

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Of 114 randomized, 109 (95.6%) had outcome data analyzed at 1 year (56/56 SR-LRYGB, 53/58 LSG). This is a binary outcome with a high proportion of complete data, and the observed number of "remission events" (ranging 38-80% across thresholds) substantially exceeds the small number of participants with missing data (5/114).

**Result of Domain 3**: **Low risk** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Since 3.1 = Y, Domain 3 defaults to "Low risk" per the decision rules.

---

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: **N** (95% Certainty)
Reason: HbA1c is a standard, validated, reliable laboratory measure with well-established clinical utility for classifying diabetes status; this is an appropriate and predefined outcome measure.

**4.2**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Both groups underwent identical HbA1c measurement schedules (baseline, 3, 6, 9, 12 months) using the same laboratory thresholds and comparable clinical protocols.

**4.3**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: HbA1c is an objective laboratory measurement, not patient-reported. Post-operative medication management (which could influence whether patients met "remission" criteria) was conducted by "endocrinologists blinded to treatment allocation," minimizing assessor awareness bias.

**4.4**: **N** (95% Certainty)
Reason: HbA1c is an objective outcome measure (biochemical assay), not subject to subjective interpretation by assessors or patients.

**4.5**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: No clear evidence suggests that knowledge of intervention influenced the objective HbA1c measurement or the blinded post-surgical medication management protocol.

**Result of Domain 4**: **Low risk** (92% Certainty)
Reason: 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = N, satisfying the first rule for "Low risk."

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The trial was prospectively registered (ACTRN12611000751976) with a published protocol (reference 23) pre-specifying the four HbA1c thresholds for T2D remission. The methods and results sections are fully consistent, and all four pre-specified thresholds are reported in the Results (Table 2).

**5.2**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: All four pre-specified HbA1c thresholds (≤38, <42, <48, <53 mmol/mol) are fully reported in Table 2, matching the Methods section exactly with no selective reporting evident.

**5.3**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: A single, pre-specified statistical method (Fisher's exact test) was consistently used to compare remission proportions between groups, with no evidence of multiple competing analyses from which results were selectively chosen.

**Result of Domain 5**: **Low risk** (90% Certainty)
Reason: 5.2 = N, 5.3 = N, 5.1 = Y, satisfying the first rule for "Low risk."

## Overall Bias Risk Assessment for the Article

**Overall Result**: **Low Risk** (88% Certainty)

Reason: All five domains (Randomization Process, Deviations from Intended Interventions, Missing Outcome Data, Measurement of the Outcome, and Selection of the Reported Result) were assessed as "Low Risk." This well-designed, prospectively registered, double-blind RCT with robust randomization (computer-generated with minimization), appropriate allocation concealment, balanced co-interventions, minimal missing data, objective outcome measurement (HbA1c), and pre-specified/fully reported outcomes and analyses supports an overall "Low Risk" of bias rating for the T2D remission outcome across all four HbA1c thresholds.

**Article ID**: Park, 2013  
**Outcome Name**: progression free survival  
**registration number**: NCT00561119  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** The article states that “the random permutation method was used for random assignment,” which is an adequate random sequence-generation method.

**1.2: Y (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** Registration and random assignment were coordinated centrally at the KCSG data center. This constitutes central allocation.

**1.3: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Although most baseline characteristics were similar, lung hematogenous metastasis differed significantly between groups (31.9% in the maintenance group versus 16.5% in the observation group; *P* = .029). This is a statistically significant baseline difference in a potentially prognostic disease characteristic.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation sequence generation and central allocation were reported, but there was a statistically significant baseline imbalance in lung hematogenous metastasis.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was unmasked/open-label according to the registered protocol. Under the specified instruction, an open-label experiment is rated N for this question.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Personnel delivering treatment would necessarily know assignment because one group received intravenous paclitaxel/gemcitabine chemotherapy whereas the other group underwent observation without treatment.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** Hormonal therapy was prohibited in both groups between random assignment and progression. The article does not report important imbalanced nonprotocol interventions before progression that would affect PFS.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** There were substantial failures in implementation of the maintenance intervention. In the maintenance group, 33 of 116 participants (28.4%) stopped chemotherapy at physician discretion without progression; additional participants discontinued because of adverse events or withdrawal. This exceeds 10%.

**2.5: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Non-adherence to maintenance chemotherapy exceeded 10%. Specifically, 33 of 116 maintenance-group participants (28.4%) stopped study drugs without disease progression at physician discretion.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** The efficacy analysis included all randomly assigned patients according to the intention-to-treat principle. Under the specified criteria, ITT analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adherence to the intervention.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Important implementation failures and non-adherence occurred in more than 10% of participants, and the analysis used ITT rather than an appropriate adherence-effect analysis.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
**Reason:** All 231 randomly assigned participants were included in the efficacy analysis according to the intention-to-treat principle (116 maintenance; 115 observation). PFS was analyzed for the randomized population, with observations censored at last clinical contact or the analysis cutoff date.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not report inverse-probability weighting, sensitivity analyses for missing PFS data, or another method demonstrating that missing outcome data did not bias the PFS result. ITT analysis alone does not correct missing-data bias.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
**Reason:** Participants lost to follow-up were censored at their last clinical contact, but the article does not report reasons for loss to follow-up or whether missingness was related to true progression status.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 68%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not provide sufficient information on the number, distribution, or reasons for loss to follow-up to determine whether missingness was likely related to true PFS.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
**Reason:** All randomized participants were included in the PFS efficacy analysis, meeting the criterion for nearly complete outcome data.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** PFS was measured from random assignment to documented disease progression or last visit. Disease response/progression was assessed using RECIST version 1.0, a reliable and predefined radiographic measurement method.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** Radiographic studies were performed every 6 weeks under the trial protocol. The same PFS definition and RECIST-based assessment method were applied to both groups.

**4.3: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** PFS and disease progression were assessed by investigators. The registered protocol specified no masking, and investigators could therefore have been aware of assigned intervention.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** PFS depended on investigator assessment of radiographic disease progression using RECIST. Although imaging-based, assessment of progression includes investigator judgment and could be influenced by knowledge of treatment assignment.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not provide clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment actually influenced investigator assessment of progression.

**Result of Domain 4: Some concerns (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** Outcome assessors were aware of treatment assignment and assessed progression; however, there is no clear evidence that this knowledge was likely to have influenced assessment.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was registered prospectively (NCT00561119). PFS was prespecified as the primary outcome, and the article reported PFS from random assignment using the stated Kaplan-Meier, log-rank, and Cox proportional-hazards methods.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
**Reason:** PFS was consistently defined as time from random assignment to documented disease progression or last visit. The prespecified PFS outcome and its reported analysis correspond to the intended outcome measurement.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** The methods prespecified Kaplan-Meier estimation, log-rank comparison, and multivariable Cox proportional-hazards analysis. The reported PFS results correspond to these planned analyses, with no clear evidence that the reported analysis was selected from multiple eligible analyses based on its result.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
**Reason:** PFS was prospectively registered as the primary outcome, and the measurement and statistical analyses reported for PFS were consistent with the prespecified methods.

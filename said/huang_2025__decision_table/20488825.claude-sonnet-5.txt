# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Togha, 2010
**Outcome Name**: Attack number (relapse rate)
**Registration Number**: NCT00668343

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: **Y** (95% Certainty)
Reason: The article explicitly states "A computer-based blocking method (four blocks) was used for the randomization" — this is a clearly described, adequate random sequence generation method.

**1.2**: **NI** (80% Certainty)
Reason: The article states that "Separate physicians were assigned to randomly allocate patients into two groups" and that medications were given in "similar packages," but no explicit concealment mechanism (central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, or opaque envelopes) is described. The registry also does not clarify concealment procedures.

**1.3**: **N** (95% Certainty)
Reason: Table 1 shows no statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics between groups (all p>0.05), and the article explicitly states "There was no significant difference in baseline characteristics between the two groups."

**Result of Domain 1**: **Some concerns** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Since 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = N, per the algorithm this yields "Some concerns."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions

**2.1**: **N** (95% Certainty)
Reason: "The patients were unaware of their group assignment and all medications were given in similar packages." Placebo was used.

**2.2**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Registry indicates quadruple masking (participant, care provider, investigator, outcomes assessor); evaluating neurologists and MRI radiologists were explicitly blinded to treatment assignment.

**2.3**: **Y** (75% Certainty)
Reason: No evidence of imbalanced non-protocol interventions; all patients continued IFNb 1a, and relapse treatment (IV methylprednisolone) was applied uniformly per protocol.

**2.4**: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: 14/85 (16.5%) patients withdrew from the study — exceeding the 10% threshold for implementation failure.

**2.5**: **N** (70% Certainty)
Reason: The article states that dropout patients "had taken the provided medications consistently until they withdrew from the trial," suggesting good adherence while on treatment.

**2.6**: **N** (95% Certainty)
Reason: The article explicitly used intention-to-treat, per-protocol, and last-observation-carried-forward (LOCF) analyses — all classified as inappropriate methods per the criteria.

**Result of Domain 2**: **Some concerns** (65% Certainty)
Reason: Although 2.1 and 2.2 indicate adequate blinding (N), there was a notable implementation failure (2.4 = Y, >10% withdrawal) not fully covered by the "low risk" algorithm branch requiring 2.4 = N/PN/NA. Given the substantial dropout rate and use of inappropriate analytic methods (2.6 = N), this raises concerns regarding deviations from intended intervention, classified conservatively as "Some concerns."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Total randomized = 85; final analyzed for attack number = 40 (simvastatin) + 36 (placebo) = 76 (89.4%), which falls below the 90% threshold for continuous/count outcome data completeness.

**3.2**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The article relied on ITT/LOCF, which are not appropriate bias-correction methods (per instructions, ITT does not qualify as correction for missing data bias); no sensitivity analysis or inverse probability weighting was used.

**3.3**: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Withdrawal reasons included six patients having two relapses (directly related to the attack-number outcome) and one experiencing chronic exacerbation — clearly related to the outcome variable itself.

**3.4**: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The dropout due to relapses was imbalanced between groups (4 from placebo vs. 2 from simvastatin for two-relapse withdrawal), and this reason is directly tied to the attack-number outcome, indicating missingness likely dependent on the true outcome value.

**Result of Domain 3**: **High risk** (90% Certainty)
Reason: 3.1 = N, 3.2 = N, 3.3 = Y, and 3.4 = Y — matching the algorithm criterion for "High risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: **N** (90% Certainty)

**4.2**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Both groups were assessed by neurologists at identical intervals (every 3 months) using the same criteria; no differential measurement frequency or threshold reported.

**4.3**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Outcome assessment was performed by neurologists (not solely patient-reported), and these assessors were explicitly blinded to treatment allocation ("blinded to the patients' assignment to treatment group").

**Result of Domain 4**: **Low risk** (90% Certainty)

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: **Y** (80% Certainty)

**5.2**: **N** (75% Certainty)

**5.3**: **N** (75% Certainty)

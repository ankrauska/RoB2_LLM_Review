**Article ID**: Hayward, 2017  
**Outcome Name**: Motor Assessment Scale item 6 (Upper Arm Function, MAS6)  
**registration number**: ACTRN12614000952640  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
An offsite investigator generated the allocation sequence using a computer-generated random-number sequence in a 1:1 ratio.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
Allocation was concealed using consecutively numbered opaque envelopes containing group allocation. The envelopes were held by the offsite investigator and collected by the unblinded tRNS intervener only after the initial assessment.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
The group sizes were balanced (two active-tRNS and two sham-tRNS participants). Although individual baseline characteristics varied in this very small sample, the article provides no evidence that baseline differences arose from a problem in the randomization process.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 92%)**  
The allocation sequence was computer generated, allocation was adequately concealed, and there was no reported evidence that baseline differences indicated a failure of randomization.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Participants were blinded to allocation. Active and sham stimulation used the same electrode placement, and participants were informed that they might or might not feel stimulation regardless of allocation.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The independent tRNS intervener was unblinded and delivered either active tRNS at 2 mA or sham tRNS at 0 mA. Therefore, at least one person delivering the intervention was aware of assigned intervention.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
All participants received the same reaching-training programme, including outcome-triggered peripheral nerve stimulation where required. No important nonprotocol interventions or group imbalance in such interventions were reported.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
All four participants completed all 12 training sessions over four weeks, and no sessions were missed. There was no evidence of failures in implementation affecting more than 10% of participants.

**2.5: NI (Moderate Certainty, 74%)**  
The article reports complete attendance but does not provide explicit information on adherence to all assigned intervention components, including receipt of the assigned active or sham tRNS regimen.

**2.6: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
The prospectively registered protocol specified intention-to-treat analysis. Intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention regimen under the specified criteria.

**Result of the domain: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
An intervention provider was aware of allocation, adherence information was incomplete, and the prespecified intention-to-treat approach was not an appropriate analysis for estimating the effect of adherence to intervention.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
MAS6 data were available for all four randomized participants at baseline, post-training, and follow-up. Thus, 100% of randomized participants had observed MAS6 outcome data.

**3.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
No missing-data bias-correction method, such as inverse-probability weighting or sensitivity analysis, was reported. However, this is not consequential because MAS6 outcome data were complete for all participants.

**3.3: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
There were no missing MAS6 data. Therefore, missingness could not depend on the true MAS6 outcome value.

**3.4: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
There were no missing MAS6 data, no differential missingness between intervention groups, and no reported reasons for missing outcomes related to upper-arm function.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 99%)**  
All randomized participants contributed MAS6 data at all reported time points.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
MAS6 was prespecified as the primary clinical outcome measure. The Motor Assessment Scale is an established clinical measure of upper-arm function after stroke.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
MAS6 was assessed for all participants at the same scheduled time points: baseline (week 0), post-training (week 4), and follow-up (week 12). No group-specific measurement procedures were reported.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Study personnel involved in assessment were blinded to group allocation throughout the study. MAS6 is assessor-rated rather than participant-reported.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 92%)**  
MAS6 is a performance-based and assessor-rated functional outcome. Therefore, its assessment could potentially be influenced by an assessor’s knowledge of intervention allocation if blinding were broken.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 91%)**  
Outcome assessors were reported to be blinded, and the article provides no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention allocation influenced MAS6 assessment.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
MAS6 was measured using the same method and time points in both groups, and assessors were blinded to allocation.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 72%)**  
The trial was prospectively registered. The registry specified a composite MAS upper-limb score involving items 6–8 and proposed a non-parametric mixed-effects model with between-group comparisons. The article reported individual-case changes in MAS6, MAS7, and MAS8 rather than the planned mixed-effects and between-group analysis. The available information does not establish whether this change in analysis was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available or whether it was related to the results.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
MAS6 was measured at the three prespecified time points—baseline, post-training, and follow-up—and all three MAS6 values were reported for every participant in Table 3. There is no evidence that one time point or measurement was selectively reported.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 79%)**  
Although the article did not report the mixed-effects analysis described in the registry, there is no clear evidence that multiple analyses of MAS6 were undertaken and that only a selected favourable analysis was reported.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 81%)**  
There are concerns because the published analysis of MAS6 does not clearly correspond to the prospectively registered analysis plan, and it is unclear whether the analytical change occurred before unblinded data were available.

**Article ID**: Halford, 2001
**Outcome Name**: women’s burden and distress
**registration number**: None

**Domain 1**
1.1 : Y (100%)
The article states that participants were assigned randomly via random number tables to one of the treatment conditions.
1.2 : NI (100%)
The article does not provide any information regarding whether the allocation sequence was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions.
1.3 : N (100%)
The article mentions that there were no significant differences between the conditions on demographic variables, pre-treatment GHQ, RSS, reports of partner’s drinking, and KAT.
Result of the domain: Some concerns (100%)
The allocation sequence was adequately generated, but there is no information on allocation concealment, leading to some concerns despite no significant baseline differences.

**Domain 2**
2.1 : Y (100%)
Given the nature of the interventions (supportive counselling, stress management, alcohol-focused couples therapy), participants would be aware of their assigned intervention.
2.2 : Y (100%)
The clinical psychologists delivering the specific therapeutic interventions were aware of the participants' assigned interventions.
2.3 : Y (75%)
There is no indication of significant non-protocol interventions being imbalanced across the intervention groups.
2.4 : Y (100%)
The article reports an overall attrition rate of 22% of participants from the treatment phase of the study, which exceeds the 10% threshold for failures in implementation.
2.5 : Y (100%)
Just over 20% of women dropped out of treatment, indicating non-adherence for more than 10% of participants.
2.6 : N (100%)
The study analyzed data using ANOVAs based on available data, and did not report using appropriate analytical methods (like inverse probability weighting) to adjust for the effects of non-adherence or missing data.
Result of the domain: High risk (100%)
Both participants and personnel were aware of the interventions, there was more than 10% attrition/non-adherence, and inappropriate analysis methods were used to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention.

**Domain 3**
3.1 : N (100%)
Out of 61 participants randomized, 47 completed treatment, and 44 provided data at the 6-month follow-up (72%), which is less than the 90% threshold for continuous variables.
3.2 : N (100%)
There is no evidence or sensitivity analysis provided in the article to demonstrate that the result was not biased by missing outcome data.
3.3 : Y (100%)
The article notes that many women who dropped out did so because they left their partners (a decision related to their burden, distress, and relationship status), suggesting missingness could depend on the true value of the outcome.
3.4 : Y (100%)
The reported reasons for missing outcome data (ending the relationship due to severe distress/alcohol abuse) suggest a relationship with the missing outcome variable itself.
Result of the domain: High risk (100%)
A substantial proportion of data is missing, there are no appropriate corrections for missing data bias, and the missingness is likely dependent on the true value of the outcomes.

**Domain 4**
4.1 : N (100%)
The outcomes were measured using established and predefined tools (Relative Stress Scale and General Health Questionnaire).
4.2 : N (100%)
There is no evidence that the measurement of the outcome differed between the intervention groups.
4.3 : Y (100%)
The outcomes are patient-reported, and the participants were aware of their group assignments.
4.4 : Y (100%)
The outcome is a subjective measure (self-reported burden and psychological distress) reported by the patient who is aware of the intervention received.
4.5 : Y (100%)
Since the outcomes are subjective and self-reported by participants who knew their treatment assignment, it is likely that the assessment was influenced by this knowledge.
Result of the domain: High risk (100%)
The outcomes are subjective, self-reported by unblinded participants, and likely to be influenced by the knowledge of the intervention received.

**Domain 5**
5.1 : NI (100%)
The article does not provide a trial registration number or reference a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available.
5.2 : N (100%)
There is no clear evidence that the reported numerical results were selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements; all intended measurements were reported.
5.3 : N (100%)
There is no evidence that the results were selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data.
Result of the domain: Some concerns (100%)
While there is no evidence of selective reporting of measurements or analyses, the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan leads to some concerns.

**Overall Bias Risk for the Article**: High Risk (100%)
The article is rated as High Risk overall due to high risks identified in Domain 2 (deviations from intended interventions), Domain 3 (missing outcome data), and Domain 4 (measurement of the outcome).

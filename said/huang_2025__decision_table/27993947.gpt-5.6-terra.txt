**Article ID**: Jouni, 2017  
**Outcome Name**: shared decision making score  
**registration number**: NCT01936675  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Randomization was performed using a computer-generated random sequence, with stratification for age, sex, and family history of CHD using the Pocock and Simon method.

**1.2: NI (98% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Although the article states that an investigator generated the allocation sequence and study-arm assignment after the first visit, it does not report a specific allocation-concealment method, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or identical numbered containers.

**1.3: N (98% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, smoking status, family history, BMI, blood pressure, lipid measures, and education, were similar between groups, with no statistically significant baseline differences.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (98% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The random sequence was adequately generated and baseline characteristics were balanced, but allocation concealment was not reported.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Participants received different risk disclosures: conventional CHD risk only (CRS) or CHD risk incorporating a genetic risk score (CRS+GRS). The trial registry also states that masking was absent. Participants were therefore aware of the intervention received.

**2.2: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Genetic counselors and physicians delivered the risk-disclosure intervention and discussed the different risk information with participants. They were necessarily aware of participants’ assigned intervention.

**2.3: Y (80% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Both groups attended a genetic-counselor encounter and a physician visit, and both used a modified Statin Choice decision aid. No important non-protocol interventions that were imbalanced between groups were reported.

**2.4: N (96% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** All 207 randomized participants completed the second study visit, including both genetic-counselor and physician appointments. There was no evidence that more than 10% experienced failure in implementation of the assigned risk-disclosure intervention.

**2.5: NI (95% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report adherence to the assigned disclosure regimen, crossovers between risk-disclosure groups, or discontinuation/non-adherence data relevant to the intervention.

**2.6: NI (94% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article reports analyses comparing randomization groups but does not report an analysis specifically designed to estimate the effect of adherence to the assigned intervention, such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting.

**Result of the domain: High risk (92% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Participants and intervention providers were aware of assignment. In addition, adherence was not reported and no appropriate analysis estimating the effect of adherence to the assigned intervention was reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The SDM survey was completed by 206 of 207 randomized participants (99.5%). The shared decision-making score is a continuous outcome, and outcome data were available for more than 95% of randomized participants.

**3.2: N (97% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report sensitivity analyses, inverse-probability weighting, or another method to evaluate or correct potential bias from the one missing SDM outcome. Intention-to-treat analysis was not reported as a missing-data correction method.

**3.3: NI (93% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The reason for the missing SDM survey outcome was not reported. Therefore, it cannot be determined whether missingness depended on the participant’s true shared decision-making score.

**3.4: NI (93% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** There is no information regarding the reason for the missing SDM survey or characteristics of the participant with missing data that would allow assessment of whether missingness likely depended on the true outcome value.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (99% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** SDM outcome data were available for 206/207 randomized participants (99.5%), exceeding the 95% threshold for a continuous outcome.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (98% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Shared decision making was measured using the validated 11-item Shared Decision-Making Questionnaire (SDM-Q). The total score ranged from 0 to 11, with higher scores indicating more effective shared decision making.

**4.2: N (96% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The same SDM-Q survey and scoring procedure were used for participants in both intervention groups following the genetic-counselor and physician encounters.

**4.3: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The SDM outcome was patient-reported, and participants were aware of whether they received conventional CHD-risk disclosure alone or genetics-informed CHD-risk disclosure.

**4.4: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The SDM score is a subjective patient-reported measure of perceived participation, discussion, and inclusion in treatment decision making. Such assessments could be influenced by participants’ knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (80% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Although participants knew their assigned risk-disclosure intervention, the article provides no clear evidence that this knowledge was likely to have influenced their assessment of shared decision making. Both groups received structured risk disclosure, a decision aid, genetic counseling, and a physician consultation.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (90% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The validated SDM-Q was measured comparably in both groups; however, the outcome was subjective and patient-reported by participants who were aware of their intervention assignment.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (85% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The study was registered (NCT01936675), and shared decision making assessed by survey was listed as a secondary outcome. The Methods section specified the SDM-Q measurement and scoring approach, and the Results section reported the overall SDM scores and individual SDM-Q item results using the stated group-comparison analyses.

**5.2: N (90% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The patient-reported shared decision-making outcome was measured using the prespecified SDM-Q, and the overall SDM score and individual questionnaire-item results were reported. The supplementary OPTION5 video assessment was also reported rather than selectively omitted.

**5.3: N (88% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The reported SDM-score comparison was consistent with the statistical methods described in the Methods section, including two-sample t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum testing for continuous outcomes. There is no clear evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible analyses based on its results.

**Result of the domain: Low Risk (85% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Shared decision making was registered as a secondary outcome, and the reported measurement and analyses were consistent with the Methods section, without evidence of selective selection among multiple measurements or analyses.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (90% – Moderate Certainty)
The article states: "Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to, but not the other two light conditions." This indicates participants were aware of their own assigned intervention (open-label design, masking="NONE" per registry).

**2.2**: N (60% – Low Certainty)
"The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to the light condition each participant was exposed to." This suggests the person administering testing/outcome assessment was blinded; however, it is unclear who set up/administered the light exposure itself (which is visually distinguishable equipment).

**2.3**: NI (70% – Low Certainty)
No information is provided regarding non-protocol interventions (e.g., additional treatments, caffeine use, extraneous light exposure) that participants may have received.

**2.4**: N (85% – Moderate Certainty)
72 of 74 randomized participants (97.3%) had data available; the 2 exclusions were due to invalid VO2 peak measurements in the fitness test, not failures in implementing the light intervention itself. This is <10%.

**2.5**: NI (70% – Low Certainty)
The article does not report any information on adherence/non-adherence to the assigned light exposure protocol (e.g., whether participants complied with viewing instructions, gaze direction, etc.), though it does note high variability in light received, but this is not framed as "non-adherence."

**2.6**: NI (70% – Low Certainty)
No explicit statement of ITT, per-protocol, or other analysis approach specifically addressing adherence; ANCOVA was used to compare all randomized participants with available data, but no explicit mention of handling non-adherence.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (80% – Moderate Certainty)

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90% – Moderate Certainty)
The outcome was measured via a predefined, computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task, with a clear, reliable, and standardized protocol (signal randomly at 3–7 s intervals over 5 minutes; lapses >500 ms excluded). This is an objective, validated method.

**4.2**: N (90% – Moderate Certainty)
The same measurement method, timing (immediately before/after light exposure), and equipment were used identically across all three groups.

**4.3**: N (80% – Moderate Certainty)
This is an objective, computer-recorded outcome (not patient-reported), and the test supervisor administering/collecting the reaction time data was explicitly blinded to the light condition ("The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to the light condition each participant was exposed to").

**4.4**: N (90% – Moderate Certainty)
Acoustic reaction time is an objective, automatically recorded measure (computer-based, millisecond precision), not subject to subjective interpretation by assessor or patient.

**4.5**: N (85% – Moderate Certainty)
There is no clear evidence suggesting that knowledge of intervention influenced the objective computer-recorded assessment of reaction time, and the supervisor was blinded.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (90% – Moderate Certainty)

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: Y (90% – Moderate Certainty)
The study was registered (NCT02203539) prospectively, and the article states: "All statistical methods used to compare the groups for reaction time and handgrip strength in the present report were pre-specified and registered." **5.2**: N (85% – Moderate Certainty)

**5.3**: N (85% – Moderate Certainty)

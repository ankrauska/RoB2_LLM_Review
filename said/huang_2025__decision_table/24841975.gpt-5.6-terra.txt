## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
The article reports that patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio through a centralized Interactive Web Response System, with a block size of four patients within each stratum. Block randomization is an adequate random-sequence generation method.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Allocation was conducted through a centralized Interactive Web Response System after eligibility assessment. This is a specific allocation-concealment method.

**1.3: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
There was a statistically significant baseline imbalance in age: patients aged 70 years or older were more common in the elacytarabine group than in the control group (30% versus 18%; P = .007). Age is an important prognostic factor for overall survival in relapsed/refractory AML.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Although randomization and allocation concealment were adequately described, the statistically significant imbalance in the proportion of patients aged 70 years or older raises concerns regarding the randomization process according to the specified decision rules.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The study was explicitly described as open-label. According to the specified instruction for this question, an open-label experiment is rated N.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The study was open-label; therefore, carers and personnel delivering treatment were aware of the assigned intervention.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
The control treatment was selected and recorded by the investigator before randomization. Stem-cell transplantation, an important post-treatment intervention relevant to survival, was balanced between groups (20 patients in the elacytarabine arm and 21 in the control arm). No important imbalance in nonprotocol interventions was reported.

**2.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Twenty-two of 381 randomized patients did not receive treatment (5.8%), which is below the 10% threshold. In addition, 95% of patients receiving one course and 77% receiving a second course received at least 80% of assigned doses. The article does not report implementation failures affecting more than 10% of all randomized participants.

**2.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 76%)**  
Six control-arm patients received treatment not according to the prespecified investigator’s choice. This is below 10% of randomized participants. The reported proportions receiving at least 80% of assigned doses do not establish non-adherence affecting more than 10% of all randomized participants.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The primary overall-survival analysis was performed in the intention-to-treat population using Kaplan-Meier estimation, log-rank testing, and Cox proportional hazards regression. Under the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method to estimate the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention.

**Result of Domain 2: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Although treatment providers were aware of allocation in this open-label trial, important nonprotocol interventions were not reported to be imbalanced, implementation failures and non-adherence did not exceed 10% of randomized participants, and deviations were limited.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Overall survival was analyzed in all 381 randomized patients. There were 302 deaths, whereas only five patients were reported as lost to follow-up or withdrawn (one in the elacytarabine arm and four in the control arm). The number of observed deaths was much greater than the number with missing outcome data.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
The article does not report inverse probability weighting, sensitivity analyses for missing survival data, or another method specifically addressing potential bias caused by missing outcome data. Intention-to-treat analysis alone does not correct missing-data bias.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 72%)**  
The article reports that five participants were lost to follow-up or withdrawn but does not provide reasons for these missing survival data. Therefore, it cannot be determined whether missingness depended on the true overall-survival outcome.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
Reasons for loss to follow-up or withdrawal were not reported, and the article does not provide information allowing assessment of whether missingness was likely related to true survival status.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The number of observed deaths (302) was substantially greater than the number of participants with missing survival information (five).

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Overall survival was measured as time from randomization/treatment to death, with censoring at the data cutoff for patients known to be alive and at the last known alive date for those lost to follow-up. Overall survival is a reliable and appropriate outcome measure.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Overall survival was assessed using the same Kaplan-Meier and censoring approach in both treatment groups. No differential survival-measurement procedure between groups was reported.

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
The study was open-label, but the article does not specifically state whether personnel ascertaining deaths or conducting survival follow-up were aware of the assigned treatment.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Overall survival/all-cause mortality is an objective outcome. Its assessment is not expected to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment was likely to influence ascertainment of death or overall survival.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Overall survival is an objective outcome, was measured comparably between groups, and there is no evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced its assessment.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
The trial was registered (NCT01147939). Overall survival was prespecified as the primary endpoint. The Methods section specified Kaplan-Meier estimation, log-rank testing, and Cox proportional hazards regression, and the Results section reported overall survival using these methods, including median survival, hazard ratios, confidence intervals, and P values.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
The primary overall-survival result was reported for the intention-to-treat population. The additional analysis censoring patients undergoing stem-cell transplantation was also reported rather than selectively omitted. There is no clear evidence that the numerical overall-survival result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements on the basis of the results.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
The reported overall-survival analyses correspond to the analysis methods described in the Methods section: Kaplan-Meier estimation, log-rank comparison, and Cox proportional hazards regression. There is no clear evidence of selective reporting from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Overall survival was prespecified, the reported survival analyses were consistent with the stated statistical methods, and there is no clear evidence of selective outcome-measurement or analysis reporting.

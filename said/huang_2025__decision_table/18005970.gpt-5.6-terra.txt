## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The outcome was measured by comparing students’ diagnostic hypotheses for a standardized simulated case with pre-defined standard problems. The standard was independently defined by a panel of 15 professional nurses, with agreement assessed using Cohen’s kappa and a stated cut-off of kappa > 0.80. This is a reasonably reliable and appropriate method for assessing diagnostic-hypothesis errors.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The same simulated case history (“Valentina’s case”), modified only in demographic, contextual, and family details, was used across study phases to maintain comparable complexity. Students were given the same 20-minute period to formulate diagnostic hypotheses. There is no evidence that outcome measurement differed between intervention groups.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Two researchers conducted a blind evaluation of the diagnostic problems identified by students. Therefore, outcome assessors were not aware of the intervention received by participants.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 93%)**  
The outcome involved assessor judgement regarding whether student-generated diagnostic hypotheses were mistaken compared with the established standard. This is a subjective assessor-evaluated outcome and could, in principle, be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Although the outcome required assessor judgement, the article explicitly reports that two researchers performed blind evaluation. There is no clear evidence that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The outcome was measured using a standardized case and an independently developed reference standard, and the outcome assessors were blinded to group assignment.

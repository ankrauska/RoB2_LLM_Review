## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article states that 241 couples were “prospectively randomized using computer-generated random numbers” into the three intervention groups.

**1.2: N (93% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Allocation was performed using sealed envelopes by a specialist nurse. However, the article does not report that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, or otherwise protected against prediction or manipulation. Therefore, appropriate allocation concealment cannot be confirmed.

**1.3: N (96% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Baseline characteristics were reported as comparable among the three groups. There were no statistically significant differences in age, duration of infertility, BMI, or BMI categories. Group sizes were also consistent with the intended allocation ratio (81, 80, and 80 participants).

**Result of Domain 1: High risk (94% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Although the random sequence was computer-generated and baseline characteristics were comparable, allocation concealment used sealed envelopes without reported safeguards such as opacity or sequential numbering. Under the specified decision rules, an answer of N for Question 1.2 results in High risk.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: NI (82% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report whether participants were blinded to their assigned gonadotrophin preparation. No placebo or sham intervention was described.

**2.2: NI (82% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report whether specialist nurses, clinicians, or other personnel delivering the gonadotrophin interventions were blinded to allocation.

**2.3: Y (76% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article describes the same general ovarian-stimulation monitoring, hCG administration criteria, cancellation criteria, and IUI procedure for all intervention groups. No important non-protocol interventions that were imbalanced between groups were reported.

**2.4: NI (75% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Cycles could be cancelled for decreasing estradiol levels or more than three follicles of ≥16 mm on the day of hCG administration. However, the number of cancelled cycles or failures in implementing the assigned intervention was not reported; therefore, it cannot be determined whether failures affected more than 10% of participants.

**2.5: NI (90% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report adherence to the assigned gonadotrophin regimen, treatment discontinuation, crossover between preparations, or switching to another active intervention.

**2.6: NI (91% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article reports chi-square tests, t-tests, ANOVA, and Fisher’s exact tests, but does not report an analysis estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention, such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting. No ITT, per-protocol, or as-treated adherence-effect analysis was explicitly reported.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (84% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Participants and carers/providers were not reported to be blinded, adherence information was absent, and no appropriate analysis of adherence to the assigned intervention was reported. According to the specified decision rules, this results in High risk.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (96% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Clinical pregnancy data were reported for all randomized participants: 21/81 in the rFSH group, 11/80 in the uFSH group, and 10/80 in the hMG group. Thus, the reported denominators total all 241 randomized participants. For this binary outcome, 42 observed clinical pregnancies were reported and no participants with missing clinical-pregnancy outcomes were identified.

**3.2: N (88% Moderate Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report sensitivity analyses, inverse-probability weighting, or another method specifically addressing possible bias caused by missing outcome data. ITT analysis was not reported as a method for correcting missing-data bias.

**3.3: NI (90% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** No missing clinical-pregnancy outcome data were reported, and the article provides no information on whether any potential missingness could have depended on the true clinical-pregnancy outcome.

**3.4: NI (90% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** The article does not provide information on differential missingness, reasons for missing clinical-pregnancy data, or analyses evaluating whether missingness was related to clinical-pregnancy status.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (95% High Certainty)**  
**Reason:** Clinical pregnancy outcomes were available for all randomized participants according to the reported group denominators and outcome counts.

**4.2: N (92% Moderate Certainty)**

**4.4: N (96% High Certainty)**

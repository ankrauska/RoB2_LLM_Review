## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1 Was the allocation sequence adequately generated?**: **Y** (High Certainty, 99%)  
**Reason:** Participants were allocated using “a stratified randomization method blocking by age” and “a computerized random generator program.” This is an adequately generated random allocation sequence.

**1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**: **Y** (Moderate Certainty, 82%)  
**Reason:** After all 500 women had consented and completed baseline interviews, the database was sent to the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center coordination center for random allocation. This constitutes centralized allocation after enrolment.

**1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**: **N** (High Certainty, 96%)  
**Reason:** The article reports that no significant differences were observed between groups in demographic characteristics at baseline. Group sizes were also consistent with the intended allocation ratio: standard care, 167; high-intensity intervention, 167; low-intensity intervention, 166.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk** (High Certainty, 94%)  
**Reason:** The allocation sequence was computer generated, centralized allocation occurred after enrolment, and no problematic baseline imbalances were reported.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**: **Y** (High Certainty, 100%)  
**Reason:** The article explicitly states that blinding participants to their intervention group was not possible and that participants knew their allocation arms.

**2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?**: **Y** (High Certainty, 100%)  
**Reason:** The article states that blinding was not possible for interventionists. Lay health educators delivered telephone and home-visit components exclusively in the high-intensity intervention group and therefore knew participants’ allocated interventions.

**2.3 Were important nonprotocol interventions balanced across intervention groups?**: **Y** (High Certainty, 91%)  
**Reason:** Opportunistic brief advice from primary-care providers was received by 15% (73/500) of participants and was reported to have a very similar distribution across groups. Access to free mammography and medical or midwife care was equivalent across groups.

**2.4 Were there failures in implementing the intervention that could have affected the outcome?**: **N** (Moderate Certainty, 80%)  
**Reason:** All participants in the low- and high-intensity groups received the mailed intervention. In the high-intensity group, telephone/home-visit contact was provided according to the protocol to women who had not obtained mammography after the mailing. The authors reported very high intervention compliance. Loss to follow-up was below 10% in every group.

**2.5 Was there non-adherence to the assigned intervention regimen that could have affected participants’ outcomes?**: **N** (Moderate Certainty, 76%)  
**Reason:** All women assigned to the low- and high-intensity interventions received the mail contact. The telephone/home-visit component in the high-intensity group was conditional on not obtaining mammography after the mailed intervention and was implemented for those requiring this additional outreach. The article characterizes intervention compliance as very high.

**2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention?**: **N** (High Certainty, 99%)  
**Reason:** The study used an intention-to-treat analysis. Under the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention regimen.

**Result of Domain 2: Low risk** (Moderate Certainty, 81%)  
**Reason:** Although participants and intervention providers were aware of allocation, important nonprotocol care was balanced across groups, implementation was largely successful, and there was no evidence of material non-adherence affecting the intervention comparison. Question 2.6 is not determinative because substantial implementation failure or non-adherence was not identified.

**3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**: **N** (High Certainty, 91%)

**3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**: **N** (High Certainty, 91%)

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk** (High Certainty, 96%)  
**Reason:** Electronic-record mammography outcomes were reviewed for all randomized women, and analysis used the original randomized-group denominators.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**: **N** (High Certainty, 96%)  
**Reason:** Mammography screening was measured using electronic records from the affiliated radiology unit and electronic clinical charts. These records are an appropriate and reliable method for ascertaining whether a mammogram was completed. Self-report was also collected, but the intention-to-treat outcome analysis was based on electronic clinical records.

**4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**: **N** (High Certainty, 92%)  
**Reason:** Electronic clinical records were reviewed for all women in all randomized groups, using the same sources and the same six-month follow-up period. No group-specific outcome measurement procedures were reported.

**4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**: **N** (High Certainty, 97%)  
**Reason:** The study data analysis was conducted by investigators at the coordination center who were blinded to participants’ group assignments. The primary outcome was also based on electronic records rather than participant-reported screening alone.

**4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: **N** (High Certainty, 96%)  
**Reason:** Completion of mammography documented in electronic clinical records is an objective outcome. Knowledge of intervention assignment would not be expected to alter whether a mammogram was recorded as completed.

**4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: **N** (High Certainty, 95%)  
**Reason:** There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced electronic-record ascertainment of completed mammography. In addition, the data analysts were blinded to group assignment.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk** (High Certainty, 95%)  
**Reason:** Mammography completion was objectively ascertained from electronic records in a comparable manner across groups, and the data analysts were blinded to allocation.

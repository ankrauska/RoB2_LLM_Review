# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Elkordy, 2015
**Outcome Name**: Patient acceptance of the appliance
**Registration number**: Not registered (trial was not registered per article statement)

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: Y (98% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: The article explicitly states "Random sequence generation was done with a computer-generated list of random numbers obtained from an Excel spreadsheet... using the '=RAND()' function," with "blocked randomization... with block sizes of 6 and 4." This clearly meets the criteria for adequate sequence generation.

**1.2**: Y (98% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: The article explicitly states "Allocation concealment was achieved with sequentially numbered and sealed opaque envelopes that were concealed from the researchers and the assessors." This is a clear description of an appropriate concealment method.

**1.3**: N (90% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: Table I shows baseline characteristics (age, SNA, SNB, ANB, MP/SN, MMP, overjet, crowding) were similar between groups. The article states "The baseline characteristics were similar in both groups," with no statistically significant differences reported, attributable to random error.

**Result of Domain 1**: **Low risk** (95% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: 1.2 = Y, 1.1 = Y, and 1.3 = N, which satisfies the criteria for "Low risk" per the algorithm.

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (98% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: The article explicitly states "Blinding of the clinicians and patients to the interventions in each group was impossible." Patients could see/feel whether mini-implants were placed.

**2.2**: Y (98% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: The article explicitly states blinding of clinicians was impossible, as they delivered the differing interventions (mini-implant insertion vs. none).

**2.3**: Y (85% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: No significant non-protocol interventions are reported. Both groups received identical FFRD protocols, follow-up intervals (every 3-4 weeks), and the only difference was the mini-implant. No imbalanced co-interventions were noted.

**2.4**: N (95% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: The article states "No patients dropped out during the trial" and "there were no dropouts." 0% failure rate, well below the 10% threshold.

**2.5**: N (80% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: No non-adherence is reported; all 32 patients completed treatment as assigned with no crossovers or switches mentioned. The FFRD is a fixed appliance not requiring patient compliance behaviorally.

**2.6**: N (85% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: The article states "The primary analysis was planned to be an intention-to-treat analysis... all the patients were included in the analysis." Per instructions, ITT analysis is coded as "N" for this question.

**Result of Domain 2**: **Low risk** (90% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: 2.1 = Y and 2.2 = Y (satisfying "one of 2.1/2.2 is Y"), 2.3 = Y, 2.4 = N, 2.5 = N, which satisfies the criteria for "Low risk."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: Y (98% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: "The 32 surveys were retrieved from the patients; there were no dropouts." All 32/32 (100%) randomized participants had complete outcome data, far exceeding the 95% threshold for continuous/ordinal Likert data.

**Result of Domain 3**: **Low risk** (98% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: 3.1 = Y satisfies the criteria for "Low risk" directly.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (85% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: The questionnaire was modified from a previously used, validated instrument (Bowman et al.) with a standardized 5-point Likert scale and yes/no questions. This is a predefined, reasonably reliable tool for measuring patient acceptance.

**4.2**: N (90% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: Both groups were measured identically—same questionnaire, same 5-point Likert scale, same time point (day of FFRD removal), same administration procedure by the same investigator.

**4.3**: Y (95% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: The outcome (patient acceptance) is patient-reported via self-completed questionnaires, and patients were aware of their group assignment (blinding of patients was explicitly stated as "impossible," and mini-implant placement is a noticeable physical intervention).

**4.4**: Y (95% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: Patient acceptance is inherently a subjective, patient-reported outcome (Likert scale self-ratings of satisfaction, comfort, noticeability, etc.).

**4.5**: Y (80% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: There is clear evidence that patients were aware of their intervention assignment (visible/palpable mini-implants vs. none), and the primary outcome relies entirely on self-report. This creates a plausible mechanism by which knowledge of allocation (e.g., anxiety about implants, or perceived "extra treatment") could influence subjective ratings of acceptance and satisfaction.

**Result of Domain 4**: **High risk** (85% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = Y, 4.4 = Y, and 4.5 = Y, which satisfies the criteria for "High risk" per the algorithm (unblinded patient-reported subjective outcome with plausible influence from knowledge of intervention).

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: NI (95% - High Certainty)
*Reason*: The article explicitly states "This trial was not registered" and "The protocol was not published before trial commencement." Per instructions, this results in "NI."

**5.2**: N (85% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: All outcome measurements described in the Methods (14 questionnaire items covering acceptance, satisfaction, functional interference, noticeability, pain, swelling, gum problems, breakage) are fully reported in the Results (Tables III-VII), with no evidence of selective reporting from multiple eligible measures.

**5.3**: N (85% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: A single, pre-specified analytical method (ridit analysis) was consistently applied to all outcome comparisons, with no evidence of multiple analyses being conducted and selectively reported.

**Result of Domain 5**: **Some concerns** (85% - Moderate Certainty)
*Reason*: 5.2 = N, 5.3 = N, but 5.1 = NI, which satisfies the criteria for "Some concerns" per the algorithm.

## Overall Bias Risk Assessment Result for the Article

**Overall Risk of Bias**: **High Risk** (85% - Moderate Certainty)

*Reason*: Domain 4 (Measurement of the Outcome) was rated as "High risk" due to the unblindable, subjective, patient-reported nature of the primary outcome (patient acceptance) combined with patients' clear awareness of their treatment allocation (presence/absence of mini-implants). Per the overall assessment algorithm, if any single domain is rated "High risk," the overall risk of bias for the article is "High Risk," regardless of the favorable ratings in Domains 1, 2, and 3.

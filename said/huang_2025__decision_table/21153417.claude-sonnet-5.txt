## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: **NI** (80%)
*Reason*: The article states "crew members were divided into two groups according to random numbers" but does not specify the actual method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated, random number table, coin toss). No further detail is given to upgrade this to "Y".

**1.2**: **NI** (85%)
*Reason*: The article states "Sailors were blind regarding the group assignment" but does not describe any concealment mechanism (e.g., sequentially numbered containers, central allocation, sealed opaque envelopes). No information on how the sequence was concealed prior to enrollment.

**1.3**: **Y** (65%)
*Reason*: The two intervention groups (allopurinol n=13; placebo n=10) show marked imbalance in the distribution of high-intensity workers (8 HIW in allopurinol vs. 2 HIW in placebo) — a variable strongly correlated with CK/AST levels per the study's own data (Table 1, Fig. 2). Additionally, baseline CK values differ substantially between groups (445.8 U/L vs. 275.8 U/L) without statistical comparison reported. This imbalance in an important prognostic factor suggests a potential problem with the randomization process.

### **Result of Domain 1: High risk (80%)**

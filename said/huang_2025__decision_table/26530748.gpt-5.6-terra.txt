## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** The article states that 427 patients were “randomized” or “randomly allocated” to EBRT or VBT, but does not report a method for generation of the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random-number table, blocks, minimization, or similar method).

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not describe allocation concealment. There is no report of central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, identical numbered treatment containers, or another specific concealment procedure.

**1.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** The article refers to patient characteristics in Table 1 and reports that responders at 7 years were younger and had fewer comorbidities than the complete PORTEC-2 cohort. However, the provided article text does not clearly report baseline comparisons between the EBRT and VBT groups that would allow assessment of whether baseline differences suggested a problem with randomization.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Allocation concealment was not reported (1.2 = NI), and there is insufficient information about baseline balance between randomized groups (1.3 = NI).

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** Participants received distinct radiotherapy interventions—external beam radiation therapy or vaginal brachytherapy. These treatment procedures are materially different, so participants were likely aware of their assigned intervention.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Carers and clinicians delivering EBRT or vaginal brachytherapy would necessarily be aware of which radiotherapy intervention was administered.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not provide sufficient information on potentially important nonprotocol interventions received during follow-up, or whether such interventions were balanced between treatment groups.

**2.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 76%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report failures in implementation of the assigned radiotherapy intervention. Long-term questionnaire nonresponse and exclusion of patients who were dead, had recurrent disease, or were not disease-free do not establish failure in delivering the assigned intervention.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** No information is reported regarding adherence to the assigned EBRT or VBT regimen, treatment discontinuation, crossover, or switching between interventions.

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** The reported linear mixed-model and logistic-regression analyses estimated differences according to random assignment. The article does not report an analysis specifically estimating the effect of adherence to the assigned intervention, such as inverse-probability weighting or instrumental-variable analysis.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** Participants and intervention providers were aware of assigned treatment (2.1 and 2.2 = Y). Information on nonprotocol interventions was unavailable (2.3 = NI), and no appropriate adherence-effect analysis was reported (2.6 = NI).

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** EORTC QLQ-C30 Global health is a continuous outcome. Of 427 randomized patients, baseline questionnaires and at least one follow-up questionnaire were available for 348 patients (81%). This is below 90% of randomized participants. Furthermore, at the 7- and 10-year assessments, only 282 evaluable questionnaires were available, and 76.2% had complete QLQ-C30 item completion.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report inverse-probability weighting, a sensitivity analysis for missing data, or another method demonstrating that the Global health result was not biased by missing outcome data. Linear mixed models and analysis according to random assignment do not by themselves demonstrate correction for missing-data bias.

**3.3: PY (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** Long-term eligibility required participants to be alive and disease-free. Missing follow-up data could therefore be related to participants’ health status, including death, recurrence, comorbidity, or inability to complete questionnaires. However, the article does not establish that all missingness was specifically dependent on the true Global health score.

**3.4: Y (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** The long-term analysis excluded patients who were not alive or disease-free and included only prior questionnaire responders. These circumstances make it likely that missingness was related to health status and potentially to true Global health-related quality-of-life values.

**Result of Domain 3: High risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** Outcome data were available for substantially fewer than 90% of randomized participants (3.1 = N), no evidence was provided that missing-data bias was corrected (3.2 = N), and missingness was likely associated with health status and potentially with the true outcome value (3.3 = PY; 3.4 = Y).

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** Global health was measured using the EORTC QLQ-C30 version 3.0. This is a validated and widely used cancer-specific health-related quality-of-life questionnaire. The Global health/QoL scale uses items 29 and 30, scored on a 7-point response scale and transformed to a 0–100 scale.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** The article describes the same HRQL questionnaire schedule and the same EORTC QLQ-C30 measurement approach for patients in both treatment groups. No group-specific difference in Global health measurement procedures or assessment time points was reported.

**4.3: Y (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** Global health is a patient-reported outcome. Participants were likely aware of whether they received EBRT or VBT; therefore, outcome assessment was performed by participants aware of their assigned intervention.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** EORTC QLQ-C30 Global health is a subjective patient-reported quality-of-life outcome. Assessment could be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
**Reason:** Although Global health is subjective and participants likely knew their treatment allocation, the article provides no clear evidence that knowledge of treatment assignment was likely to influence the Global health assessment. The reported absence of a statistically significant difference in Global health between groups does not itself demonstrate influence by knowledge of intervention.

**Result of Domain 4: Some concerns (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** The outcome measure was appropriate and was measured comparably across groups (4.1 and 4.2 = N). However, Global health was patient reported by participants likely aware of treatment assignment (4.3 and 4.4 = Y). There was no clear evidence that this knowledge was likely to have influenced assessment (4.5 = N).

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report a trial registration number, statistical analysis plan, or prespecified HRQL analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported Global health analysis followed a prespecified plan.

**5.2: NI (Moderate Certainty, 77%)**  
**Reason:** Global health was assessed repeatedly at baseline, 6-month intervals during the first 2 years, annually until 5 years, and at 7 and 10 years. The article reports longitudinal analyses but does not provide sufficient information to determine whether all eligible Global health time-point results were fully reported or whether the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible time points.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 83%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section specifies linear mixed models for EORTC QLQ-C30 scales, including Global health, with time, random assignment, and their interaction as fixed effects. The reported Global health result is described as a longitudinal comparison between treatment groups. There is no clear evidence that one analysis was selectively reported from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of Domain 5: Some concerns (High Certainty, 90%)**  
**Reason:** The availability of a prespecified analysis plan was not established (5.1 = NI), and selective reporting from multiple eligible Global health time points cannot be excluded (5.2 = NI).

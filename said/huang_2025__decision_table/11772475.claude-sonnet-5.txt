## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (85%)
Reason: The article states patients were "stratified on the following variables... They were then randomized into one of the three study conditions," but no specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, block randomization) is described.

**1.2**: NI (90%)
Reason: The article provides no information regarding allocation concealment procedures (e.g., central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque containers, envelopes). No mention of concealment methods.

**1.3**: Y (65%)
Reason: Although demographic characteristics (age, gender, employment, income) appeared balanced across groups, a statistically significant baseline difference in self-reported marijuana use was found between groups (Table 2: 16% routine, 21% clinic-based, 0% office-based; X²=9.13, P=0.01), despite stratification on drug use. This imbalance in an important prognostic substance-use variable suggests a possible problem with the randomization/stratification process.

**Result of Domain 1**: High risk (85%)
Reason: Per the algorithm, when 1.2 = "NI" and 1.3 = "Y", the domain is rated "High risk."

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (95%)
Reason: This was an open-label study; participants were aware whether they were receiving routine clinic care, clinic-based MMM, or office-based MMM due to obvious differences in visit frequency/location.

**2.2**: Y (95%)
Reason: Clinicians/nurses/physicians administering care necessarily knew group assignment given the structurally different treatment protocols (visit frequency, location, dosing schedule).

**2.3**: Y (70%)
Reason: The "stepped care" intensification protocol was applied uniformly and based on pre-specified clinical criteria (positive urine, failed recall) across all groups; no evidence of imbalance in important non-protocol interventions.

**2.4**: N (85%)
Reason: Six-month retention rates were 90%, 89%, and 92% for routine, clinic-based, and office-based groups respectively — all with less than 10% failure to complete the intervention period.

**2.5**: Y (80%)
Reason: A "significant minority" of patients (19/73 ≈ 26%) failed at least one medication recall, indicating non-adherence exceeding the 10% threshold; additionally, one patient admitted to taking more medication than prescribed.

**2.6**: N (95%)
Reason: The data analysis was explicitly described as "intent-to-treat sample (n=73)," which is considered an inappropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to intervention per the specified criteria.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (85%)
Reason: 2.1/2.2 = Y, 2.3 = Y, one of 2.4/2.5 (2.5) = Y, and 2.6 = N → this combination corresponds to "High risk" per the algorithm.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90%)

**4.2**: N (85%)

**4.3**: Y (75%)

**4.4**: N (90%)

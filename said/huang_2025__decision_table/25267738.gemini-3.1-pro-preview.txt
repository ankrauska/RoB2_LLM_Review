**Article ID**: Siragusa, 2013
**Outcome Name**: recurrent thrombotic events
**registration number**: NCT00450645

Domain 1
1.1 : PN (100%)
The allocation sequence was generated based on the clinical chart number, which is a rule based on hospital record numbers.
1.2 : N (100%)
The assignment envelopes were used without appropriate safeguards as they were based on clinical chart numbers, and the study was open-label without proper allocation concealment.
1.3 : N (100%)
There were no statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics between the intervention groups.
Result of the domain (100%)
High risk
Reason you summarized
The sequence generation was predictable (based on chart numbers) and allocation was not adequately concealed, leading to a high risk of bias in the randomization process.

Domain 2
2.1 : Y (100%)
Patients and treating physicians were not masked to the treatment assignment.
2.2 : Y (100%)
Carers and people delivering the interventions were aware of the participants' assigned intervention because the study was unblinded.
2.3 : NI (100%)
The article does not provide information regarding important non-protocol interventions that were imbalanced across groups.
2.4 : N (100%)
Implementation of the intervention was successful for most participants, with no evidence of major failures affecting >10% of participants.
2.5 : N (100%)
Non-adherence was low; only a small number of patients (15/119) interrupted treatment for less than 5 days, which does not constitute significant non-adherence.
2.6 : N (100%)
Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat (ITT) basis, which is an inappropriate analytical method to evaluate the effect of adhering to the intervention.
Result of the domain (100%)
High risk
Reason you summarized
Participants and providers were aware of the assignment, and inappropriate analytical methods (ITT) were used to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention, leading to a high risk of bias.

Domain 3
3.1 : Y (100%)
Outcome data were available for all randomized participants, as all 242 patients assigned to the A1 and A2 groups were analyzed.
3.2 : Y (100%)
No significant missing data were reported, meaning there was no bias introduced by missing outcome data.
3.3 : N (100%)
There is no evidence that participants dropped out due to health issues related to the outcome variable.
3.4 : N (100%)
There is no evidence suggesting that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value.
Result of the domain (100%)
Low risk
Reason you summarized
Data were available for nearly all randomized participants, resulting in a low risk of bias due to missing outcome data.

Domain 4
4.1 : N (100%)
The outcome was measured using predefined and reliable methods, such as compression ultrasonography and other objective imaging tests.
4.2 : Y (100%)
Follow-up visits lasted 18 months for group A1 and 12 months for group A2, indicating differences in the measurement process between groups.
4.3 : N (100%)
Outcomes were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of group assignments.
4.4 : N (100%)
The outcome (recurrent VTE) is an objective measure confirmed by imaging tests.
4.5 : N (100%)
There is no clear evidence to suggest that knowledge of intervention status influenced the outcome assessment, especially since the central committee was blinded.
Result of the domain (100%)
High risk
Reason you summarized
Although the outcome assessors were blinded and the outcome was objective, the ascertainment process differed between groups (different follow-up durations), which introduces a high risk of bias.

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
The intervention and control booklets were placed in a single pile according to “a sequence of random integers generated by a true random number generator.” This is an adequately generated random allocation sequence.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The article does not report a specific allocation-concealment procedure, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or identical numbered containers. The use of a randomized pile of booklets does not itself establish that allocation was concealed until enrolment and assignment.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The study reported no statistically significant baseline differences between intervention and control groups in pre-intervention behaviour, cognition measures, age, or gender. The authors concluded that randomization was successful.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
The allocation sequence was adequately generated and baseline characteristics were balanced, but allocation concealment was not reported.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Participants were aware of their assigned intervention. Intervention participants received persuasive messages and completed implementation-intention planning tasks, whereas control participants completed questionnaire-related questions. These procedures were visibly different.

**2.2: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The article does not report whether the individuals distributing the booklets or otherwise delivering the intervention were blinded to participants’ assignments.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
The article instructed participants not to discuss booklet contents in order to reduce contamination, but it did not report whether non-protocol interventions or contamination were balanced between groups.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 88%)**  
For the outcome of intentions to consume the RDIFV, post-intervention cognition measures were completed immediately after the intervention/control task. The intention analysis included 218 participants, as indicated by the ANCOVA degrees of freedom, and there is no evidence of intervention-implementation failure affecting more than 10% of participants for this immediate outcome.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 90%)**  
The article does not report adherence to the assigned intervention, including whether all intervention participants completed or followed the implementation-intention planning component as intended.

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 92%)**  
No analysis specifically designed to estimate the effect of adherence to the intervention was reported. The analyses assessed differences between randomized groups using ANCOVA, rather than an adherence-adjusted method.

**Result of the domain: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Participants were aware of assignment, information on non-protocol interventions was insufficient, adherence was not reported, and no appropriate analysis estimating the effect of adhering to the intervention was reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Intentions to consume the RDIFV were assessed immediately after the intervention/control task. The post-intervention ANCOVA for intention had degrees of freedom of 1,215, consistent with analysis of all 218 randomized participants. Therefore, complete outcome data were available for at least 95% of randomized participants for this continuous outcome.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 90%)**  
The article did not report sensitivity analyses, inverse probability weighting, or another method specifically addressing possible bias due to missing outcome data. However, missing data were not substantial for the immediate post-intervention intention outcome.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
The article did not provide information about whether any missing immediate post-intervention intention data depended on participants’ true intention values.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
The article did not provide information allowing assessment of whether any missing immediate post-intervention intention data were likely related to true intention values.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Outcome data for intentions to consume the RDIFV were available for all or nearly all randomized participants.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Intentions to consume the RDIFV were measured using a predefined five-item self-report scale. Internal consistency was high (Cronbach’s α = .90 pre-intervention and .93 post-intervention), indicating reliable measurement.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The same intention measure was administered to both intervention and control groups at the same pre-intervention and immediate post-intervention time points.

**4.3: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The outcome was participant-reported, and participants were aware of their assigned intervention because intervention and control tasks were clearly different.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Intention to consume the RDIFV is a subjective, participant-reported cognitive outcome.

**4.5: Y (High Certainty, 93%)**  
Knowledge of intervention assignment was likely to influence assessment of intentions. The intervention directly included persuasive messages intended to increase intention (e.g., asking participants to make a firm decision to consume the RDIFV), and intentions were self-reported immediately afterward by unblinded participants.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The subjective self-reported intention outcome was assessed by participants who knew their assigned intervention, and the intervention directly targeted intention immediately before outcome measurement.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
No trial registration, protocol, or pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available was reported.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 83%)**  
The Methods section specified that intention was measured using five items, and the Results section reported the intended post-intervention intention outcome using this measure. There is no clear evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible intention measures, definitions, or time points on the basis of the results.

**5.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
The article reported ANCOVA analyses for post-intervention intention controlling for pre-intervention intention, but it did not provide a pre-specified analysis plan or sufficient information to determine whether the reported analysis was selected from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 90%)**  
No pre-specified analysis plan or registration was reported, and there is insufficient information to exclude selective reporting from multiple possible analyses.

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
The study used block randomization determined by a computer-generated random-number table, stratified by operator and center.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Allocation was concealed using sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes. The randomization generator did not participate in patient allocation.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 97%)**

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
The article explicitly states that it was not possible to blind patients to the type of bracket system used.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
The clinicians delivering treatment were aware of the bracket type because blinding of clinicians was not possible.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Both groups were managed using a standardized bonding procedure and standardized manufacturer-recommended archwire sequences. No important imbalanced nonprotocol interventions affecting treatment duration were reported.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Three of 138 randomized participants did not complete the study or treatment (2.2%), which is below the 10% threshold for important implementation failures.

**2.5: NI (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
The article did not report adherence to the assigned bracket-system regimen, treatment switching, crossover, or sustained non-adherence.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The analysis was performed on a per-protocol basis. According to the specified criteria, per-protocol analysis is not appropriate for estimating the effect of adhering to the intervention when non-adherence may affect outcomes.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Participants and clinicians were aware of treatment assignment. Although treatment procedures were standardized and implementation failures were limited, adherence information was not reported and a per-protocol analysis was used.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
No sensitivity analysis, inverse-probability weighting, or other method to evaluate or correct possible bias from missing outcome data was reported. Per-protocol analysis does not itself correct missing-data bias.

**3.3: PY (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
The article states that data analysis was conducted with examiner blinding, but it does not clearly state whether the persons who initially recorded treatment start and debond dates were blinded to intervention assignment.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Treatment duration is an objective outcome based on dates of appliance placement and debonding.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 97%)**

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**

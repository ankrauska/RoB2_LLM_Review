## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Participants were assigned to prompt focal/grid laser treatment or laser deferred for at least 24 weeks. The timing of laser treatment would be apparent to participants; therefore, participants were aware of their assigned intervention strategy.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Carers and intervention providers needed to administer focal/grid laser promptly or defer it according to the assigned group and protocol criteria. Therefore, they were aware of treatment assignment.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Most treatment decisions were protocol specified. Other DME treatments occurred in 9/124 (7%) prompt-laser completers and none in deferred-laser completers; seven of the nine cases were reported as per protocol, and only two were not. The report does not indicate substantial imbalanced non-protocol interventions likely to materially affect visual-acuity outcomes.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
At 5 years, outcome data were absent for approximately one quarter of randomized eyes: 39/163 (24%) in the prompt-laser group and 39/150 (26%) in the deferred-laser group, excluding deaths. This exceeds 10% and represents failure to complete the extended trial follow-up.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 92%)**  
The article does not provide sufficient information on adherence to the assigned intervention regimens. Although treatment use is reported, it does not provide a participant-level adherence assessment or quantify non-adherence/crossover exceeding 10%.

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 90%)**  
The article reports longitudinal analyses using available visit data, but does not report an instrumental-variable analysis, inverse-probability weighting for adherence-related censoring, or another specified appropriate method to estimate the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Participants and intervention providers were aware of assignment. More than 10% of randomized participants did not complete the 5-year follow-up, adherence information was insufficient, and no appropriate adherence-effect analysis was reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Visual acuity is a continuous outcome. Five-year visual-acuity data were available for 124/163 (76%) randomized prompt-laser participants and 111/150 (74%) randomized deferred-laser participants, excluding deaths. Both proportions are below 90%.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
The report used longitudinal models with available data and adjusted for measured potential confounders. However, it did not report inverse-probability weighting, a sensitivity analysis for missing visual-acuity data, or another method demonstrating that the result was not biased by missing outcome data.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 77%)**  
The principal reason for missing 5-year data was that some participants did not consent to the extension phase. The article states that it was not possible to determine whether incomplete data were related to unobserved factors that could have affected the results. Therefore, the relationship between missingness and true visual acuity cannot be determined.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The article identifies differences between completers and non-completers in age, prior panretinal photocoagulation, lens status, and diabetic-retinopathy severity. However, it explicitly states that it was not possible to know whether incomplete data were related to unobserved factors affecting results. Thus, whether missingness was likely dependent on true visual acuity is not established.

**Result of Domain 3: High risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Five-year visual-acuity data were missing for more than 10% of randomized participants in both groups. No method demonstrating that missing-data bias was corrected was reported, and the possible relationship between missingness and visual-acuity outcomes remained unresolved.

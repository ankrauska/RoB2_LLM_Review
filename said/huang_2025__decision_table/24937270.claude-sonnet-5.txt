## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: N (85% – Moderate Certainty)
Reason: The study used a sham stimulation condition (applied for 30 seconds then automatically turned off) to maintain participant blinding. Blinding validation confirmed subjects could not correctly guess their group (p = 0.679), consistent with effective blinding.

**2.2**: N (80% – Moderate Certainty)
Reason: The article states "the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention." While not explicit about the person delivering stimulation, the double-blind design and automated sham protocol suggest that those interacting with participants were also blinded.

**2.3**: NI (80% – Moderate Certainty)
Reason: No mention of any non-protocol interventions or co-interventions during the single-session study.

**2.4**: N (85% – Moderate Certainty)
Reason: Of 40 recruited participants, 2 withdrew before stimulation (5%) and 1 was excluded due to EEG artifact (2.6%), together under 10%, indicating minimal implementation failure.

**2.5**: NA/N (70% – Low-Moderate Certainty)
Reason: This was a single 20-minute session intervention; adherence issues (e.g., cessation, crossover) are not applicable to this design, and none were reported.

**2.6**: NI (65% – Low Certainty)
Reason: No explicit description of adherence-adjusted analysis; standard mixed-model ANOVA was used, not clearly ITT/per-protocol labeled.

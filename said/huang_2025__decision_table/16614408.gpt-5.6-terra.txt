**Article ID**: Helmersson, 2006  
**Outcome Name**: changes in levels of 8-iso-prostaglandin F2a and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a  
**registration number**: Not reported  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1 Was the allocation sequence adequately generated?**: NI (High Certainty, 98%)  
**Reason**: The article states that participants were “randomly assigned” to the SAFA or MUFA diet and underwent a second random assignment to fish-oil or placebo capsules. However, it does not report a sequence-generation method such as computer-generated random numbers, random-number tables, block randomization, or another specified random method.

**1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**: NI (High Certainty, 98%)  
**Reason**: No allocation-concealment procedure was described. The article does not report central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, sequentially numbered identical drug containers, or another method that concealed allocation before assignment. The use of placebo capsules alone does not establish allocation concealment.

**1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**: N (High Certainty, 96%)  
**Reason**: The article states that clinical characteristics at baseline did not differ among the four groups. Table 1 shows generally comparable baseline age, BMI, lipid levels, glucose, and blood pressure measurements across groups.

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (High Certainty, 95%)  
**Reason**: Allocation concealment was not reported, although baseline characteristics did not suggest a problem with randomization.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**: N (High Certainty, 92%)  
**Reason**: Participants assigned to fish oil received fish-oil capsules, whereas comparator participants received placebo capsules containing olive oil. The reported placebo-capsule comparison indicates that participants were intended to be unaware of the supplementation assignment.

**2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial?**: NI (Moderate Certainty, 82%)  
**Reason**: The article does not state whether the personnel supplying dietary advice, study foods, fish-oil capsules, or placebo capsules were blinded to intervention assignment.

**2.3 Were important nonprotocol interventions balanced across intervention groups?**: Y (Moderate Certainty, 79%)  
**Reason**: The dietary intervention and instructions were protocol-specified. All participants were instructed to avoid nutritional supplements, avoid regular acetylsalicylic acid use, avoid fatty fish/products, and not change lifestyle or physical activity. No important nonprotocol co-interventions reported as imbalanced between groups were identified.

**2.4 Were there failures in implementing the intervention that could have affected the outcome?**: NI (Moderate Certainty, 76%)  
**Reason**: The article reports that dietary fatty-acid changes were reflected in serum phospholipid composition, supporting implementation of the dietary and fish-oil interventions. However, it does not report the number of participants with intervention failures, withdrawals, discontinuations, or protocol deviations.

**2.5 Was there non-adherence to the assigned intervention regimen that could have affected participants’ outcomes?**: NI (High Certainty, 95%)  
**Reason**: The article does not provide participant-level adherence data, capsule compliance data, numbers discontinuing capsules, or crossover information. Serum phospholipid changes support uptake at the group level but do not provide sufficient information to determine whether more than 10% of participants were non-adherent.

**2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention?**: NI (High Certainty, 94%)  
**Reason**: The article used regression/statistical models comparing assigned treatment categories and adjusting for treatment center, age, sex, and baseline outcome values. It did not report an adherence-effect analysis using methods such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting. It also did not explicitly report an intention-to-treat, per-protocol, as-treated, or treatment-received analysis.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 81%)  
**Reason**: Information on intervention implementation and participant adherence was insufficient, and no appropriate analysis estimating the effect of adherence was reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**: NI (Moderate Certainty, 78%)  
**Reason**: For 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a, plasma samples from all centers were analyzed, and the reported group sample sizes total 162 randomized participants. However, for 8-iso-PGF2a, samples from Denmark and Italy were not analyzed because of inappropriate sample handling and/or storage. The article does not report the exact number of participants with analyzable 8-iso-PGF2a data; therefore, the proportion with complete data cannot be calculated.

**3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**: N (High Certainty, 96%)  
**Reason**: The article did not report sensitivity analyses, inverse-probability weighting, or another method to assess or correct possible bias from the unavailable 8-iso-PGF2a samples from two centers.

**3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**: N (Moderate Certainty, 83%)  
**Reason**: The missing 8-iso-PGF2a data were attributed to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage at two centers, rather than to participant health status, measured 8-iso-PGF2a levels, or 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a levels.

**3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?**: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)  
**Reason**: The stated reason for unavailable 8-iso-PGF2a samples was technical sample inadequacy at centers. The article provides no indication that missingness was related to the true levels of either lipid-peroxidation biomarker.

**Result of Domain 3**: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 82%)  
**Reason**: Although the exact amount of missing 8-iso-PGF2a data is not reported and no missing-data sensitivity analysis was conducted, the reported missingness resulted from sample handling/storage problems rather than outcome-related participant characteristics.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**: N (High Certainty, 97%)  
**Reason**: Plasma 8-iso-PGF2a was measured using a validated radioimmunoassay (RIA) with a reported detection limit of 23 pmol/L. Plasma 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a was measured using a validated RIA with a reported detection limit of 45 pmol/L. The article also reports assay cross-reactivity characteristics.

**4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**: N (High Certainty, 91%)  
**Reason**: Plasma samples were obtained after an overnight fast at baseline and after 3 months for all intervention groups. The same biomarker measurement methods were used for the relevant samples. The exclusion of two centers from 8-iso-PGF2a analysis applied to center-level sample inadequacy rather than to a specific intervention group.

**4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**: NI (Moderate Certainty, 78%)  
**Reason**: The article does not state whether laboratory staff or outcome assessors performing the RIAs were blinded to dietary and fish-oil/placebo assignments.

**4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: N (High Certainty, 96%)  
**Reason**: Both 8-iso-PGF2a and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a are objectively measured plasma biomarkers assessed by laboratory RIA, rather than participant-reported or assessor-rated subjective outcomes.

**4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: N (High Certainty, 94%)  
**Reason**: There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention allocation would influence the laboratory-based RIA measurement of plasma 8-iso-PGF2a or 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (High Certainty, 92%)  
**Reason**: Validated objective assays were used at comparable time points and there is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention allocation could influence the biomarker measurements.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis?**: NI (High Certainty, 95%)  
**Reason**: The article describes the planned primary outcomes and statistical models in the Methods section, and reports results for both primary outcomes. However, no trial registration number, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or prospectively finalized prespecified analysis plan was reported.

**5.2 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)  
**Reason**: The Methods section specifies plasma 8-iso-PGF2a and plasma 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a measured at baseline and after 3 months. The Results section reports baseline values, 3-month values, and changes for both biomarkers. No selectively omitted eligible measurement method or time point is evident from the article.

**5.3 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?**: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)  
**Reason**: The Methods section specifies the statistical approach: treatment categories, their interaction, and adjustment for treatment center, age, sex, and baseline outcome value; a limited model was used when the interaction was nonsignificant. The reported results correspond to this described analytical approach. No clear evidence indicates selection from multiple analyses based on the observed results.

**Result of Domain 5**: Some concerns (High Certainty, 91%)  
**Reason**: The article was not registered and does not provide a prospectively finalized analysis plan, although the reported outcome measurements and analyses are consistent with those described in the Methods section.

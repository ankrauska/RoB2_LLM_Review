# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Fineberg, 2007 (Escitalopram prevents relapse of obsessive-compulsive disorder)
**Outcome Name**: Time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment
**Registration number**: Not reported in the article

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: Y (95%)
Reason: "After the open label period, eligible patients were assigned to escitalopram or placebo treatment according to a computer-generated randomisation list." This explicitly describes an adequate method of sequence generation.

**1.2**: Y (95%)
Reason: "The details of the randomisation series were unknown to any of the investigators and were contained in a set of sealed opaque envelopes." This explicitly describes a specific concealment method (sequentially numbered/sealed opaque envelopes plus central control of the randomization list).

**1.3**: N (90%)
Reason: "There were no differences in demographics and clinical parameters between the two treatment groups in the randomisation period." Table 1 confirms similar baseline characteristics between groups.

**Result of Domain 1**: Low risk (95%)
Reason: 1.2 = Y, 1.1 = Y, 1.3 = N, satisfying the criteria for low risk of bias from randomization.

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from the intended interventions

**2.1**: N (90%)
Reason: Double-blind design with placebo tablets "of identical appearance, taste or smell." "All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study."

**2.2**: N (90%)
Reason: Same blinding procedures applied to investigators/care providers; identical tablets used, and randomization details were concealed from investigators.

**2.3**: NI (75%)
Reason: No specific information is provided regarding non-protocol interventions (e.g., concomitant therapies) received during the double-blind period.

**2.4**: N (85%)
Reason: "the overall withdrawal rate, excluding relapses, was comparable for both treatments: 7.9% for patients treated with escitalopram (13 patients), and 8.9% for patients treated with placebo (14 patients)." Both below 10%.

**2.5**: NI (70%)
Reason: No explicit information on medication compliance/adherence rates is reported in the article for the double-blind period.

**2.6**: N (90%)
Reason: "All efficacy analyses in the double-blind treatment period were conducted using the intention-to-treat (ITT) population." Per instructions, ITT analysis is deemed inappropriate for this question, yielding N.

**Result of Domain 2**: Low risk (80%)
Reason: 2.1 = N and 2.2 = N (participants and care providers unaware of allocation due to robust blinding with identical placebo tablets); 2.4 = N (low non-implementation rate); 2.5 = NI, but no evidence suggests non-adherence was a substantive problem given the structured double-blind taper protocol and comparable low withdrawal rates across groups. This pattern most closely aligns with a low risk of bias for this domain.

---

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: Y (90%)
Reason: 320 of 322 randomized patients were included in the ITT/primary time-to-relapse analysis (163 escitalopram, 157 placebo), representing >99% of randomized patients contributing outcome data. This is a time-to-event outcome with substantial events (119/320, 37%) relative to missing data.

**3.2**: N (80%)
Reason: No specific bias-correction method (e.g., inverse probability weighting) or sensitivity analysis addressing missing outcome data bias (beyond ITT) is reported; per instructions, ITT alone does not qualify as evidence of no bias.

**Result of Domain 3**: Low risk (90%)
Reason: Since 3.1 = Y, the outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, satisfying low risk criteria regardless of 3.2.

---

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90%)
Reason: Outcome measured via the Y-BOCS, a validated, widely used, and reliable scale for OCD severity administered by trained raters ("Only those investigators who had actively participated in rater training sessions... were allowed to rate patients").

**4.2**: N (90%)
Reason: Assessment schedule was identical for both groups ("efficacy and tolerability parameters were assessed 2, 4, 6, 8, and 12 weeks after randomisation and then every 4 weeks").

**4.3**: N (85%)
Reason: Outcome (Y-BOCS-based relapse) is not purely patient-reported; it is clinician-assessed, and outcome assessors were blinded to treatment ("All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study").

**4.4**: Y (85%)
Reason: Y-BOCS is a clinician-rated, semi-subjective outcome measure (rater-assessed severity scale), classified as a subjective measure assessed by an outcome assessor.

**4.5**: N (85%)
Reason: No clear evidence that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention; robust blinding (identical tablets, blinded raters, rater training for inter-rater reliability) minimizes this risk.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (90%)
Reason: 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = N, satisfying the low risk criteria (first rule) for this domain.

---

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: Y (80%)
Reason: "The prospectively defined primary analysis of efficacy was the time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment," consistent with the Methods and Results sections, using a two-tailed log-rank test as pre-specified.

**5.2**: N (80%)
Reason: Only one primary relapse definition was pre-specified (Y-BOCS increase ≥5 points or lack of efficacy); a post-hoc analysis using only the Y-BOCS criterion is explicitly identified as post-hoc/exploratory rather than a selectively reported alternative primary measure.

**5.3**: N (80%)
Reason: The primary analysis (log-rank test, Cox proportional hazards model) matches the pre-specified statistical analysis plan; additional sensitivity analyses (7- and 14-day censoring) are disclosed transparently as a means to rule out discontinuation-effect confounding, not as selective reporting.

**Result of Domain 5**: Low risk (80%)
Reason: 5.2 = N, 5.3 = N, and 5.1 = Y, satisfying the criteria for low risk.

---

## Overall Bias Risk Assessment Result for the Article

**Overall Risk of Bias**: Low Risk (85%)

Reason: All five domains (Randomization, Deviations from Intended Interventions, Missing Outcome Data, Measurement of the Outcome, and Selection of the Reported Result) were assessed as "Low Risk." The study employed a computer-generated randomization sequence with adequate concealment (sealed opaque envelopes), robust double-blinding with identical placebo/active tablets, minimal loss to follow-up on the primary time-to-relapse outcome, a validated and blinded outcome measurement approach, and pre-specified analysis methods consistent with reported results. Per the overall assessment rule, when all five domains are rated "Low Risk," the overall risk of bias for the article is "Low Risk."

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Reason: The study states that participants were divided into two groups by “simple random sampling.” However, it does not report a specific sequence-generation method such as computer-generated random numbers, random-number tables, block randomization, coin tossing, or sealed-envelope shuffling.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: The article does not report allocation concealment. There is no description of central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, sequentially numbered drug containers, postcards, or another concealment procedure. The statement that the trial was single blinded does not establish allocation concealment.

**1.3: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: Baseline cervical effacement differed significantly between groups (MgSO4: mean 35.93, SD 18.07; celecoxib: mean 42.00, SD 15.64; P ≤ 0.0001). Cervical effacement is an important baseline prognostic factor for preterm delivery and could affect the frequency of delivery after 48 hours.

**Result of Domain 1: High risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: Allocation concealment was not reported (1.2 = NI), and an important baseline prognostic factor, cervical effacement, differed significantly between groups (1.3 = Y).

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 90%)**  
Reason: Participants were likely aware of their assigned intervention because the interventions were visibly and practically different: one group received intravenous magnesium sulfate with continuous infusion and toxicity monitoring, whereas the other group received oral celecoxib capsules. The study does not state that matching placebo infusions or placebo capsules were used.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: Carers and personnel delivering the interventions would have been aware of allocation because magnesium sulfate was administered intravenously as a loading dose followed by an infusion, whereas celecoxib was administered orally. In addition, magnesium sulfate recipients underwent monitoring for toxicity, including urine output, deep tendon reflexes, and respiratory rate.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: Both groups received the same initial routine management for preterm labor, including bed rest, hydration, and corticosteroid therapy. The article does not report important imbalances in additional nonprotocol interventions between groups.

**2.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
Reason: The article does not report failures in delivering the allocated interventions for more than 10% of participants. Treatment was stopped when contractions did not stop, as specified in the protocol; this represents lack of treatment response rather than a reported failure to implement the allocated intervention.

**2.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
Reason: The interventions were administered according to the described regimen until uterine contractions stopped or for a maximum of 48 hours. The article does not report treatment crossover, switching between study treatments, or non-adherence exceeding 10% of participants.

**2.6: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: The article reports use of chi-square tests and independent t-tests but does not report an analysis specifically designed to estimate the effect of adherence to the intervention, such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting. It also does not explicitly report intention-to-treat, per-protocol, or as-treated analysis.

**Result of Domain 2: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
Reason: Although participants and carers were likely aware of treatment allocation, the study reports balanced routine care, no substantial implementation failures, and no reported substantial non-adherence.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 95%)**

# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Pérez-Merino, 2016
**Outcome Name**: Pain VAS scale post-treatment
**Registration number**: NCT01748188

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: Y (95%)
The article explicitly states: "Each participant was assigned to one treatment group, at random, using a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method, applied by a statistician." This is a clearly described adequate randomization method.

**1.2**: NI (90%)
The article does not describe any specific allocation concealment mechanism (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation, sequentially numbered containers). Although a statistician not involved in data collection generated the list, no information is given regarding how allocation was concealed from those enrolling participants.

**1.3**: N (95%)
Table 1 shows no statistically significant baseline differences between groups (age, sex, time from onset, dominant shoulder, tendinitis/tendinosis, partial tear; all P>0.05). The article states "no differences were found between the three groups" and "were homogenous across groups."

**Domain 1 Result: Some concerns (90%)**
Rationale: 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = N → per rules, this combination yields "Some concerns."

---

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (65%)
Although described as "single-blind," the blinding applied to the outcome assessor (rehabilitation doctor), not participants. Given the physically distinct nature of the interventions (ultrasound only vs. phonophoresis with gel vs. iontophoresis with electrical current), participants could likely perceive differences between treatments, particularly between iontophoresis (electrical stimulation) and the other two.

**2.2**: Y (95%)
Physiotherapists administering the interventions necessarily knew which treatment (ultrasound, phonophoresis, or iontophoresis) was being delivered, as different equipment/techniques were used.

**2.3**: Y (75%)
No non-protocol interventions are reported; exercise and cryotherapy were standardized and identical across groups. No evidence of imbalance in additional treatments/analgesic use is mentioned (article states groups were homogeneous for "intake of analgesics").

**2.4**: NI (70%)
The article states all participants "completed 20 treatment sessions," suggesting low dropout during treatment, but no explicit numerical dropout/withdrawal data are provided in the main text (referenced only in an online supplementary figure not available here).

**2.5**: N (90%)
"All participants completed 20 treatment sessions plus exercise therapy and cryotherapy," indicating adherence to the assigned intervention regimen.

**2.6**: NI (70%)
No explicit statement of ITT, per-protocol, or other analytic approach to account for adherence is provided; only repeated-measures ANOVA is described for comparing outcomes.

**Domain 2 Result: High risk (75%)**
Rationale: 2.1/2.2 = Y; 2.3 = Y; one of 2.4/2.5 = NI; 2.6 = NI (not Y/PY) → per rules, this combination yields "High risk."

---

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: Y (90%)
Sample sizes reported in Table 2 (Ultrasound n=32, Phonophoresis n=33, Iontophoresis n=34) match the originally randomized group sizes stated in the Participants section, and the article states "All participants completed 20 treatment sessions," indicating essentially complete outcome data (~100%) for the post-treatment VAS assessment.

**Domain 3 Result: Low risk (90%)**
Rationale: 3.1 = Y → automatically "Low risk."

---

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (95%)
VAS is a validated, standard, and reliable tool for pain measurement, predefined in the Methods section.

**4.2**: N (90%)
VAS was measured identically across all three groups at the same predefined time points (baseline, post-treatment, 1-month) by the same blinded rehabilitation doctor using standardized protocols.

**4.3**: Y (70%)
VAS is a patient-reported outcome. As determined in 2.1, participants were likely aware (at least partially) of their group assignment due to the distinct nature of the interventions (particularly the electrical sensation of iontophoresis vs. ultrasound-based treatments).

**4.4**: Y (90%)
VAS is a subjective, patient-reported outcome measure, which is inherently susceptible to influence by knowledge of intervention received.

**4.5**: Y (60%)
Given that participants were likely aware of their allocation (different physical sensations/techniques) and the outcome is entirely patient-reported (subjective), there is a plausible mechanism (e.g., expectation bias toward "active drug delivery" techniques like phonophoresis/iontophoresis vs. plain ultrasound) by which knowledge of the intervention could have influenced pain reporting.

**Domain 4 Result: High risk (80%)**
Rationale: 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = Y, 4.4 = Y, 4.5 = Y → per rules, this combination yields "High risk."

---

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: Y (85%)
The registered primary outcome (VAS) and secondary outcomes (DASH, CMS) match those reported in the Methods and Results sections, with consistent measurement time points (baseline, post-treatment, 1-month) as pre-specified in the trial registration (NCT01748188).

**5.2**: N (85%)
VAS was the only scale used for pain measurement; there is no evidence of multiple pain scales or alternative definitions from which a result was selectively reported.

**5.3**: N (80%)
The statistical analysis (repeated-measures ANOVA with post-hoc Scheffé test) was consistently applied and reported for all outcomes/timepoints without evidence of selective reporting from multiple analysis approaches.

**Domain 5 Result: Low risk (85%)**
Rationale: 5.2 = N, 5.3 = N, 5.1 = Y → per rules, "Low risk."

## Overall Bias Risk Assessment

**Summary of Domain Results:**
- Domain 1: Some concerns
- Domain 2: High risk
- Domain 3: Low risk
- Domain 4: High risk
- Domain 5: Low risk

**Overall Bias Risk for the Article: High Risk (85%)**

Rationale: Per the overall assessment rules, if one or more domain results are "High Risk," the overall assessment result for the article is "High Risk." Both Domain 2 (deviations from intended interventions) and Domain 4 (measurement of the outcome) were rated as High Risk, primarily driven by the inability to blind participants effectively given the physically distinct nature of the three interventions, combined with the subjective, patient-reported nature of the VAS pain outcome, and lack of clarity regarding intervention adherence analysis methods.

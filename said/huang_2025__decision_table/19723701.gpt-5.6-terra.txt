**Outcome Name**: all-cause death or nonfatal heart failure events  
**registration number**: NCT00180271  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Patients were randomly assigned in a 3:2 ratio and stratified by clinical center and ischemic status using an algorithm that ensured near balance within each stratum. This is a minimization-type randomization procedure.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The article does not report a method of allocation concealment, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or sequentially numbered identical device containers. The absence of blinding does not establish allocation concealment.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Baseline demographic, clinical, echocardiographic, and medication characteristics were similar between the CRT–ICD and ICD-only groups. The 3:2 allocation ratio was closely maintained (1089 versus 731 participants), with no important baseline imbalance indicating a problem with randomization.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
The allocation sequence was generated with an appropriate balancing algorithm and baseline groups were comparable; however, allocation concealment was not reported.

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
### Effect of assignment to intervention

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Participants were assigned to visibly different implanted devices (CRT–ICD versus ICD-only), and the registered study design reported no masking. Participants were therefore aware of their assigned intervention.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Treating physicians were explicitly reported to be aware of study-group assignments. Implanting clinicians and other personnel delivering treatment would also have known which device was implanted.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Both groups received optimal pharmacologic heart-failure therapy, and the article reports similar baseline medication use between groups. No important imbalance in nonprotocol cointerventions that was prognostic for death or heart-failure events was reported.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
Implementation was successful for most participants. Device implantation was achieved in 98.4% of participants, and 95.4% received their assigned device. The reported proportions not receiving an implanted device were below 10% in each group.

**2.5: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Important non-adherence occurred: 91 of 731 patients assigned to ICD-only treatment crossed over to CRT–ICD treatment (12.4%). This exceeds the specified 10% threshold. In addition, 82 CRT–ICD-assigned patients received ICD-only devices because of technical lead-placement difficulties.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
The primary analysis was conducted according to the intention-to-treat principle. Under the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention when meaningful non-adherence has occurred.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The trial was unmasked, substantial crossover occurred in the ICD-only group, and the analysis was intention-to-treat rather than an analysis designed to estimate the effect of adherence to assigned treatment.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
This was a binary time-to-first-event outcome. The 372 observed primary endpoint events were substantially greater than the number of participants reported as declining continued participation, being withdrawn, or lost to follow-up (99 participants). In addition, the registry reports that all 1820 randomized participants were included in the intention-to-treat outcome analysis.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
No inverse-probability weighting, sensitivity analysis for missing outcome data, or other method specifically demonstrating that missing data did not bias the result was reported. Intention-to-treat analysis alone does not correct bias from missing outcome data.

**3.3: PY (Moderate Certainty, 76%)**  
Some participants were withdrawn by physicians or discontinued participation. These reasons could be related to health status, but the article does not establish that missingness was specifically dependent on the true death or heart-failure outcome value.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
There were different proportions of discontinuation, withdrawal, or loss to follow-up between groups (4.0% CRT–ICD and 7.5% ICD-only), but the article does not provide sufficient information showing that these differences were related to the true outcome values or that reasons differed by treatment group in a way related to death or heart failure.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
For this binary outcome, the number of observed endpoint events was substantially greater than the number of participants with potentially missing outcome data.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The outcome was predefined as all-cause death or first nonfatal heart-failure event. Heart-failure events required prespecified clinical signs and symptoms plus specified decongestive treatment, and events were adjudicated according to prespecified criteria. All-cause death is an objective outcome.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Participants in both groups had scheduled clinical follow-up at 1 month after randomization and every 3 months thereafter. The same endpoint definition and adjudication procedures applied to both groups.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Although treating physicians were aware of treatment assignment, the mortality committee and heart-failure adjudication committee were unaware of study-group assignments. The adjudicated primary endpoint was therefore assessed by blinded outcome committees.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 91%)**  
The composite outcome included nonfatal heart-failure events. The initial diagnosis of heart failure and decisions about intravenous decongestive therapy or hospital admission were made by physicians aware of treatment assignment; thus, ascertainment of the nonfatal heart-failure component could have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
The article acknowledges that treating physicians’ knowledge of treatment assignment could possibly have contributed to lower heart-failure-event frequency in the CRT–ICD group. However, no clear evidence demonstrates that knowledge of assignment was likely to influence the blinded committee’s final adjudication of the composite endpoint.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 90%)**  
The outcome was predefined, measured comparably between groups, and adjudicated by committees unaware of treatment assignments. Although unblinded treating physicians could influence the initial ascertainment of heart-failure events, no clear evidence establishes that the final adjudicated outcome assessment was likely biased.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
The trial was prospectively registered (NCT00180271; first posted September 16, 2005), before primary completion. The registered primary outcome—mortality from any cause or first heart-failure event—matches the article’s primary endpoint. The reported time-to-first-event intention-to-treat analysis, stratified log-rank testing, and Cox proportional-hazards model were consistent with the prespecified analysis approach.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The primary outcome used one prespecified composite definition: death from any cause or first nonfatal heart-failure event, whichever occurred first. The article reported the prespecified outcome at the planned event-driven follow-up period, with no evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible definitions or time points.

**5.3: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
The primary analysis was prespecified as a stratified log-rank test with a stratified Cox proportional-hazards model. The reported primary hazard ratio and significance testing correspond to these intended analyses. There is no clear evidence that one favorable analysis was selectively reported from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The trial was prospectively registered, the reported primary outcome and analysis were consistent with the registered and published prespecified plan, and there is no evidence of selective reporting from multiple outcome measurements or analyses.

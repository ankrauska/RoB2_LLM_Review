## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: **Y** (95% Confidence)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Using the random number table method, 90 cases were divided into group A and group B." This is a clearly described method of sequence generation.

**1.2**: **NI** (95% Confidence)
Reason: The article provides no information regarding allocation concealment. There is no mention of central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, opaque envelopes, or any other concealment mechanism.

**1.3**: **N** (90% Confidence)
Reason: The article reports baseline characteristics (age, sex, tumor size, nodule number) for both groups and explicitly states "The two groups did not show significant differences in general information (P>0.05)." No evidence suggests problems with randomization.

**Result of Domain 1**: **Some concerns** (85% Confidence)
Reason: Since 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = N, per the decision rule ("If 1.2 is NI, and 1.3 is N/NI/PN, then Some concerns"), Domain 1 is rated as Some concerns.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: **Y** (90% Confidence)
Reason: The two interventions (percutaneous microwave ablation vs. laparoscopic surgical resection) are fundamentally different procedures with different physical experiences (needle puncture vs. surgical incisions under general anesthesia). Patients would inevitably be aware of which procedure they underwent; no blinding is feasible or mentioned.

**2.2**: **Y** (90% Confidence)
Reason: Surgeons/interventionists performing percutaneous ablation vs. laparoscopic resection would obviously know which procedure they are performing; these are entirely distinct clinical procedures with no blinding possible or reported.

**2.3**: **NI** (80% Confidence)
Reason: The article does not provide any information about non-protocol interventions received by participants during or after the procedures.

**2.4**: **N** (85% Confidence)
Reason: Table 1 shows n=45 in both groups, matching the original randomized sample sizes (45 each). No dropouts or implementation failures are reported for this outcome.

**2.5**: **NI** (80% Confidence)
Reason: No information is provided regarding non-adherence to the assigned surgical intervention. As this is a one-time surgical intervention (not a sustained treatment), non-adherence data is not discussed.

**2.6**: **NI** (80% Confidence)
Reason: No specific analysis method (ITT, per-protocol, etc.) is described for handling adherence. Standard t-test/chi-square analyses are reported without further methodological detail on adherence-based analysis.

**Result of Domain 2**: **High risk** (85% Confidence)
Reason: Since one of 2.1/2.2 = Y, 2.3 = NI (falls into NI/N/PN category), and 2.6 = NI (falls into NI/N/PN category), per the decision rule, Domain 2 is rated as High risk.

**Outcome Name**: visual assesment for erythema and dryness as key symptoms  
**registration number**: Not reported  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Randomization was performed using “randomly permuted blocks of fixed size” with validated RANCODE 3.6 software.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The two formulations were coded A or B, and the blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of the final database. This indicates concealment of allocation during enrolment and assignment.

**1.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
No baseline comparison according to allocated treatment site/formulation was reported. This was a within-participant trial in which each participant received both formulations on different cubital fossae.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The allocation sequence was adequately generated and concealed. No reported information suggested baseline imbalances caused by the randomization process.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Participants were unaware of their assigned formulation. Both formulations were coded A or B, and the trial was described as double-blind.

**2.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The study nurse delivering the applications was not informed of the formulation identity because products were coded and unblinding occurred only after database closure.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
No important imbalanced non-protocol interventions between the two formulations were reported. Both formulations were applied by the study nurse using the same schedule and procedure to opposite cubital fossae of each participant.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Three of 35 participants were excluded from the per-protocol population because of protocol violations, representing 8.6%, which is below 10%. All 35 participants remained in the ITT analysis.

**2.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
All applications were performed by a study nurse, indicating that adherence to the assigned application regimen was maintained. No treatment crossover or cessation of an assigned formulation was reported.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The article states that analyses were performed in the ITT population and that missing values for three participants were replaced using LOCF. Under the specified criteria, ITT analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adherence to intervention.

**Result of Domain 2: Low risk (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Participants and intervention providers were blinded, applications were administered by the study nurse, and protocol-implementation failures affected fewer than 10% of participants.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: PN (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The outcome was continuous. Thirty-five participants were randomized, and three participants had missing values replaced using LOCF. Therefore, complete non-imputed outcome data were available for 32/35 participants (91.4%), which is between 90% and 95%.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Missing values in three participants were replaced using last observation carried forward (LOCF), which is not an appropriate method for correcting possible bias caused by missing outcome data.

**3.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
The reported reasons for missing data/protocol exclusion were intake of antiphlogistics for one participant and disinfection of the cubital fossa for two participants. These reasons were protocol violations and were not reported as withdrawal due to erythema, dryness, or worsening skin condition.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
The article does not provide sufficient information to determine whether the likelihood of missingness depended on the true erythema or dryness outcomes.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Although 8.6% of participants had missing data handled with LOCF, the documented reasons for missingness were protocol violations rather than reported outcome-related deterioration.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
Erythema and dryness were assessed by visual inspection using predefined ordinal scales. Erythema was graded from 0 to 4 and dryness from 0 to 3. These are appropriate measures for visual assessment of irritant contact dermatitis symptoms.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Both formulations were assessed using the same visual scales, by the same dermatologist, at the same scheduled assessment time points.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The visual outcome assessments were conducted blindly by the same investigator. Product codes remained sealed until the final database was closed.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Erythema and dryness assessed visually by a dermatologist are subjective outcomes and could potentially be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
There is no clear evidence that outcome assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention status because the dermatologist performed all visual assessments blindly.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The outcomes were assessed using predefined scales, measured comparably for both formulations, and evaluated by a blinded dermatologist.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
No trial registration number, protocol publication, or prospectively finalized statistical analysis plan was reported. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported analyses followed a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
The Methods section specified visual assessments of erythema and dryness at defined time points and a sum score across all assessments as the primary outcome. The Results section reported the planned sum scores and displayed erythema and dryness at the scheduled assessment times.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
The primary analysis specified in the Methods section was a Fieller-method one-sided 97.5% confidence interval for non-inferiority, with superiority testing after demonstration of non-inferiority. The Results reported these analyses and additionally reported the stated Wilcoxon signed-rank test. No clear evidence indicates selective reporting from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of Domain 5: Some concerns (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The intended outcome measurements and analyses were reported consistently; however, no registration record or pre-specified analysis plan was reported.

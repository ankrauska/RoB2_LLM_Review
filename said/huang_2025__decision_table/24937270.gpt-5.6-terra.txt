## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Randomization was completed using “blocks of four,” which is an acceptable random-sequence generation method under the specified criteria.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The article reports that participants and the outcome investigator were blinded, but it does not describe allocation concealment procedures such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or sequentially numbered identical containers.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
The authors reported no significant differences in demographic characteristics between groups. Although baseline coherence values were not presented, the reported baseline demographic and behavioral characteristics do not suggest a randomization problem.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Allocation was generated using blocks of four, and no baseline imbalance suggesting a randomization problem was reported. However, allocation concealment was not described.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Subjects were blinded to the intervention. The sham condition involved 30 seconds of stimulation followed by automatic cessation, and blinding validation showed that subjects did not identify their assigned group beyond chance.

**2.2: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
The article states that the investigator assessing outcomes was blinded, but it does not report whether the personnel administering tPCS or caring for participants were blinded to assigned stimulation.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
No important non-protocol interventions that could affect interhemispheric functional connectivity were reported. Adverse effects were mild and did not significantly differ between groups.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Thirty-eight participants completed the stimulation session. One participant from the random-frequency group was excluded from analysis because of excessive EEG artifacts. This represents approximately 2.6% of randomized participants, below the 10% threshold.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The article does not report adherence information, treatment discontinuation during stimulation, crossover, switching, or other non-adherence to the assigned stimulation regimen.

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 93%)**  
No analysis specifically designed to estimate the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention, such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting for adherence, was reported.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Participants were blinded, but blinding of intervention providers was not reported. Adherence information was not reported, and no appropriate analysis addressing potential non-adherence was described.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Thirty-eight participants were randomized and completed stimulation. One participant was removed from the connectivity analysis because of excessive EEG artifacts. Therefore, interhemispheric functional-connectivity data were available for 37/38 randomized participants (97.4%), exceeding the 95% threshold for a continuous outcome.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
No inverse-probability weighting, sensitivity analysis, or other method specifically demonstrating that missing outcome data did not bias the result was reported.

**3.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
The only reported missing functional-connectivity data resulted from excessive EEG recording artifacts. This technical reason does not indicate that missingness depended on the participant’s true interhemispheric functional-connectivity value.

**3.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
The reason for missing data was specified as excessive EEG artifacts in one participant, with no indication that the missingness was related to true functional-connectivity outcomes or differed systematically between intervention groups.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Outcome data were available for 97.4% of randomized participants, which meets the criterion for nearly complete outcome data.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Interhemispheric functional connectivity was measured using magnitude-squared EEG coherence, estimated with the Welch averaged modified periodogram method from 64-channel qEEG recordings. This is an appropriate and established objective method for measuring EEG coherence.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
EEG was recorded before and after the experimental condition using the same 64-channel EEG device, acquisition procedure, filtering, artifact-rejection procedure, frequency bands, and electrode pairs across all groups.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
The outcome was not patient reported. The investigator assessing outcomes was reported to be blinded to intervention assignment.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Interhemispheric coherence is an objectively derived qEEG measure. Outcome assessment is not a subjective patient-reported or assessor-rated judgment likely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced assessment of the objective EEG coherence outcome.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The outcome was measured objectively with comparable EEG procedures between groups, and the outcome assessor was blinded.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
No trial registration number, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available was reported.

**5.2: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
The Methods section specified assessment of coherence across multiple bands/sub-bands, two electrode pairs, and eyes-open and eyes-closed conditions. Although results were reported for the stated frequency bands and sub-bands, the report does not provide sufficient information to determine whether the reported numerical results were selected from all eligible electrode-pair-specific or other possible connectivity measurements.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
The reported main analysis for interhemispheric coherence was consistent with the Methods section: a mixed-model ANOVA of post–pre coherence differences with eyes condition, intervention group, and the eyes × group interaction, followed by stated post-hoc comparisons.

**Result of Domain 5: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
No registered protocol or pre-specified analysis plan was available. In addition, the information was insufficient to exclude selection among multiple eligible connectivity measurements.

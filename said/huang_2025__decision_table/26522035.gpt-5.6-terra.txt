## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (80% Moderate Certainty)**  
Patient acceptance was assessed using a structured questionnaire with predefined questions and a 5-point Likert scale, administered identically to both groups on the day of FFRD removal. The article does not report evidence that the questionnaire was unreliable or unable to measure patient acceptance.

**4.2: N (95% High Certainty)**  
Patient acceptance was measured using the same questionnaire, scoring system, and assessment time point for both intervention groups: the day of FFRD removal.

**4.3: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
The outcome was patient reported, and patients were aware of their assigned intervention because blinding of patients was impossible.

**4.4: Y (99% High Certainty)**  
Patient acceptance, satisfaction, discomfort, pain, effects on eating and speech, and appliance noticeability are subjective patient-reported outcomes. Their assessment could be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: Y (90% High Certainty)**  
The intervention status was apparent to participants because one group received mini-implants in addition to the FFRD. Since patient acceptance was assessed through subjective self-reported questionnaires, knowledge of the intervention received was likely to influence outcome assessment.

**Result of the domain: High risk (94% Moderate Certainty)**  
The outcome was subjective and patient reported. Patients were aware of their intervention assignment, and their knowledge of receiving FFRD alone or FFRD with mini-implant anchorage was likely to influence reported appliance acceptance.

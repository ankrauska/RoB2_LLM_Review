## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1** – Result: **Y** (95% Certainty)
Reason: The article explicitly states, "one of our study investigators... randomly assigned them by using a computer-generated randomized list." This is a clearly described adequate method of sequence generation.

**1.2** – Result: **NI** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The article does not mention any concealment mechanism (e.g., central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, sealed opaque envelopes). It only states that the investigator who generated the randomization list "had no knowledge of the participants," which does not describe how the allocation sequence itself was concealed from enrollment personnel/participants. No information on concealment method is given, so NI is appropriate even after double-checking for envelopes/central allocation language (none found).

**1.3** – Result: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Table 1 shows daily sunscreen (n=442) vs. discretionary sunscreen (n=444) groups with very similar baseline characteristics (sex, age, skin color, sunburn history, phenotype, etc.). The text states "There were no differences in phenotype, sun exposure, or pretrial sunscreen use between the intervention and control groups at baseline." The only imbalance noted (P=0.06) pertains to the β-carotene randomization, not the sunscreen groups relevant to this outcome. Differences appear attributable to chance.

**Result of Domain 1**: **Some concerns** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Since 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = N, per the mapping rule ("If 1.2 is NI, and 1.3 is N/NI/PN → Some concerns"), Domain 1 is rated as Some concerns.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions

**2.1** – Result: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: This was an open-label trial; a placebo sunscreen was "considered unethical" and not used. Participants in the daily sunscreen group were instructed to apply sunscreen; those in the discretionary group used sunscreen as they wished. Participants were aware of their allocation.

**2.2** – Result: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: As an open-label design without sham/placebo sunscreen, personnel monitoring adherence (weighing bottles, tablet counts) and delivering the intervention were aware of participants' allocation.

**2.3** – Result: **Y** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Reported sun exposure and use of other sun-protection measures (shade-seeking, hat use) were similar between daily and discretionary sunscreen groups during the trial, indicating no important imbalance in non-protocol interventions.

**2.4** – Result: **N** (70% Certainty)
Reason: No specific report of >10% of participants failing to implement the intervention due to withdrawal from death, worsening condition, or doctor's advice. Missing outcome data (addressed in Domain 3) was due to failure to obtain skin replicas, not withdrawal from the intervention itself.

**2.5** – Result: **Y** (85% Certainty)
Reason: By 1996, only 77% of daily sunscreen users applied sunscreen at least 3–4 days/week (i.e., ~23% non-adherent), exceeding the 10% threshold for non-adherence to the assigned regimen.

**2.6** – Result: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The article states, "Data were analyzed according to treatment as randomly allocated," which is an intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis. Per instructions, ITT analysis is not an appropriate method to estimate the effect of adherence, so the answer is N.

**Result of Domain 2**: **High risk** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Since 2.1/2.2 = Y, 2.3 = Y (NA/Y/PY), 2.5 = Y (one of 2.4/2.5 = Y/PY/NI), and 2.6 = N (NI/N/PN), this matches the rule: "one of 2.1/2.2 = Y/PY/NI, 2.3 = NA/Y/PY, one of 2.4/2.5 = Y/PY/NI, and 2.6 = NI/N/PN → High risk."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1** – Result: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Of 903 randomized participants, only 604 contributed replicas at both 1992 and 1996 (66.9%), which is well below the 90% threshold for continuous outcome data completeness required for change-in-photoaging analysis.

**3.2** – Result: **Y** (85% Certainty)
Reason: The authors performed sensitivity analyses restricting to complete cases and using multiple imputation for missing data; they explicitly state, "estimates from multiple imputation replicated the results presented in Table 4," providing evidence that results were not biased by missing data.

**Result of Domain 3**: **Low risk** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Since 3.1 = N and 3.2 = Y, per the rule "If 3.1 is N/PN/NI, and 3.2 is Y/PY → Low risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1** – Result: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The outcome was measured using the validated Beagley and Gibson microtopography grading scale, previously shown to correlate with dermal elastosis and skin cancer risk, with high intra- and inter-rater reliability (kappa 0.81 and 0.86).

**4.2** – Result: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The measurement method (silicone replicas graded on the same scale) was identical for both groups, at the same follow-up time points (1992 and 1996), with no differential frequency of assessment reported.

**4.3** – Result: **N** (95% Certainty)
Reason: The outcome was assessor-rated (not patient-reported), and the article explicitly states, "Experienced assessors who were unaware of treatment allocation graded replicas."

**4.4** – Result: **Y** (70% Certainty)
Reason: Although objective replicas were used, grading on the microtopography scale (visual/tactile assessment of skin surface features) is inherently a subjective assessment performed by an outcome assessor.

**4.5** – Result: **N** (85% Certainty)
Reason: There is no evidence suggesting that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention, since assessors were explicitly blinded to treatment allocation ("unaware of treatment allocation").

**Result of Domain 4**: **Low risk** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Since 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, and 4.3 = N, this satisfies the rule: "If 4.1 is N/PN/NI, 4.2 is N/PN, and 4.3 is N/PN → Low risk" (regardless of 4.4/4.5 values, as this rule takes precedence).

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

The analysis methods described in the Methods section (GEE ordinal logistic regression, sensitivity analyses) match those reported in the Results section, with all pre-specified outcomes (sunscreen effect, β-carotene effect, subgroup analyses) reported.

**5.2** – Result: **N** (85% Certainty)
Reason: There is only one outcome measurement tool used for photoaging (microtopography grading via silicone replicas), with no evidence of multiple alternative scales or definitions being tested and selectively reported.

**5.3** – Result: **N** (80% Certainty)
Reason: All planned analyses (main GEE models, complete-case sensitivity analysis, multiple imputation, subgroup analyses) are fully reported and consistent with each other, with no evidence of selective reporting from multiple analytic approaches.

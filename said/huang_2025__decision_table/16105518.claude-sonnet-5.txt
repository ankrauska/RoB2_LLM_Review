## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (85% - Moderate-High Certainty)
The article states participants "were randomly assigned to three experimental groups" but does not specify the method used to generate the allocation sequence (no mention of computer-generated numbers, random number tables, coin tossing, etc.).

**1.2**: NI (90% - Moderate Certainty)
No information is provided regarding allocation concealment. No mention of central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, or opaque envelopes.

**1.3**: NI (85% - Moderate Certainty)
No baseline characteristics (e.g., demographics, pre-test scores) are compared or reported between the three groups. No information is provided regarding differences in baseline characteristics.

**Domain 1 Result: Some concerns (85% - Moderate Certainty)**
Since 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = NI (which falls under N/PN/NI), this satisfies the rule: "If 1.2 is NI, and 1.3 is N/NI/PN, then Domain 1 = Some concerns."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (85% - Moderate Certainty)
Participants were required to physically perform the practice tasks in their assigned schedule (functional-order, random, or blocked practice). By the nature of the intervention, participants were inherently aware of their assigned practice schedule.

**2.2**: Y (85% - Moderate Certainty)
"An orthopedic faculty member circulated within the group systematically to provide feedback to the trainees" during the acquisition phase. This faculty member was necessarily aware of which practice schedule the group was following.

**2.3**: NI (80% - Moderate Certainty)
The article does not provide information regarding balance of nonprotocol interventions across groups.

**2.4**: N (85% - Moderate Certainty)
All 28 participants appear to have completed the study; no dropouts or withdrawals are mentioned.

**2.5**: NI (75% - Moderate Certainty)
No information is provided regarding non-adherence to the assigned practice schedule.

**2.6**: NI (75% - Moderate Certainty)
The analysis used standard mixed ANOVAs; no specific adherence-adjusted analysis method (e.g., instrumental variable analysis) is mentioned, nor is ITT/per-protocol explicitly stated, but no correction for adherence issues is described.

**Domain 2 Result: High Risk (80% - Moderate Certainty)**
Since one of 2.1/2.2 = Y, 2.3 = NI (falls under NI/N/PN), and 2.6 = NI (falls under NI/N/PN), this satisfies the rule: "If one of 2.1/2.2 is Y/PY/NI, 2.3 is NI/N/PN, and 2.6 is NI/N/PN, then Domain 2 = High risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (85% - Moderate Certainty)
The checklist was developed by a panel of 3 orthopedic surgeons who identified necessary actions for the operative task. This is a predefined, reliable measurement tool commonly used in surgical education research.

**4.2**: N (85% - Moderate Certainty)
All groups were assessed using the same checklist at the same comparable time points (pre-test, immediate post-test, delayed post-test) with identical measurement thresholds.

**4.3**: NI (75% - Low-Moderate Certainty)
The checklist was assessed by "two experienced orthopedic surgeons" (not patient-reported). The article does not state whether these assessors were blinded to participants' group assignment.

**4.4**: Y (85% - Moderate Certainty)
The checklist outcome is a subjective measure assessed by outcome assessors (orthopedic surgeons rating performance), not an objective outcome like mortality.

**4.5**: N (70% - Low-Moderate Certainty)
There is no clear evidence in the article suggesting that the assessors' knowledge of intervention status influenced their assessment of the checklist scores.

**Domain 4 Result: Some concerns (80% - Moderate Certainty)**

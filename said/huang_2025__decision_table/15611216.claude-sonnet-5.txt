# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Richards (Kovach et al.), 2006 — "Effect of the BACE Intervention on Agitation of People With Dementia"
**Outcome Name**: Agitation Score (Table 4)
**Registration number**: Not reported

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (85% Moderate-High Certainty)
The article states "We randomly assigned participants to study groups in time blocks of 1 week" and "randomized time blocks as treatment or control condition weeks," but no specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated numbers, random number table, coin toss) is described.

**1.2**: NI (90% Moderate Certainty)
No information is provided regarding allocation concealment (no mention of central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, or sealed opaque envelopes).

**1.3**: Y (90% Moderate Certainty)
Table 1 and text indicate a statistically significant difference between groups in "time spent in arousal imbalance at pretest" (t = 2.57, p < .01) — an important prognostic factor directly related to the outcome (agitation), which the authors explicitly acknowledge: "random assignment did not equalize the groups on arousal imbalance."

**Result of Domain 1**: **High risk** (90% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = Y triggers "High risk" per the algorithm, since a baseline imbalance directly relevant to the outcome variable was identified despite an unclear concealment process.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: N (90% Moderate Certainty)
"Data collectors and subjects were blinded to treatment and control conditions" — participants were blinded.

**2.2**: Y (85% Moderate Certainty)
Nursing staff and the geriatric nurse practitioner (GNP) directly implemented individualized activity schedule changes for the BACE group and were necessarily aware of which participants were receiving the intervention (control group activity was left unaltered).

**2.3**: Y (70% Low-Moderate Certainty)
Therapeutic activity sessions were tracked and used as a covariate; no significant imbalance in non-protocol interventions was reported (though psychotropic medication use was not measured, which the authors note as a limitation).

**2.4**: N (75% Moderate Certainty)
10 of 102 participants (9.8%) were dropped due to failure to deliver any intervention — below the 10% threshold.

**2.5**: Y (90% Moderate Certainty)
Only 72% of participants received the full prescribed dose; 28% received partial (50–75%) or minimal (<50%) doses — exceeding 10% non-adherence.

**2.6**: N (85% Moderate Certainty)
The primary analysis was based on all participants who received at least one prescribed change (an "as-treated"/modified-ITT approach), not an appropriate causal-adherence method (e.g., IV analysis or IPW).

**Result of Domain 2**: **High risk** (85% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: 2.2 = Y, 2.3 = Y, 2.5 = Y (non-adherence >10%), and 2.6 = N → meets criteria for "High risk."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: N (90% Moderate Certainty)
Of 102 randomized/enrolled participants, only 78 (76.5%) had complete outcome data for the ANCOVA — below the 90% threshold for continuous outcomes.

**3.2**: N (85% Moderate Certainty)
No sensitivity analysis, inverse probability weighting, or other bias-correction method for missing data was reported.

**3.3**: PY (65% Low-Moderate Certainty)
12 participants died before follow-up; death in this severely demented, agitated population may plausibly relate to their true agitation/health status, though this is not explicitly confirmed.

**3.4**: N (70% Moderate Certainty)
Reasons for missingness (death, staffing failures to deliver intervention, data errors) appear similarly distributed and unrelated to the true agitation value differentially by group; no clear group-specific pattern connecting missingness to outcome value is reported.

**Result of Domain 3**: **Some concerns** (75% Moderate Certainty)
Reason: 3.1 = N, 3.2 = N, 3.3 = PY, 3.4 = N → meets criteria for "Some concerns."

**4.1**: N (90% Moderate Certainty)

**4.2**: N (90% Moderate Certainty)

**4.3**: N (90% Moderate Certainty)

**4.4**: Y (80% Moderate Certainty)

**4.5**: N (85% Moderate Certainty)

# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Nickel, 2005
**Outcome Name**: Weight
**Registration number**: Not reported

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (85%)
The article states "randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration" but does not describe the specific method used (e.g., computer-generated numbers, block randomization, random number tables). No further detail is provided to upgrade this to "Y".

**1.2**: Y (85%)
The article states randomization was "carried out confidentially by the clinic administration" and that "tablets were supplied in numbered boxes," indicating sequentially numbered, identical-appearing containers — a recognized allocation concealment method.

**1.3**: N (90%)
Table 1 shows comparable baseline characteristics (age, height, initial weight, weight gain, olanzapine dose/duration, diagnosis) between groups. The discussion explicitly states "no essential topiramate group versus placebo group differences were found."

**Result of Domain 1**: Low risk (85%)
Since 1.2 = Y, 1.1 = NI, and 1.3 = N, this meets the criteria for "Low risk."

---

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: N (90%)
Study is double-blind with matching placebo tablets; "both subjects and clinicians were blinded."

**2.2**: N (90%)
"Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment."

**2.3**: Y (75%)
Concomitant medications are listed for both groups and are described as remaining constant during the study, with similar classes of drugs used in both arms; no evidence of significant imbalance.

**2.4**: Y (85%)
Six of 49 randomized subjects (12.2%) dropped out ("failed to appear for more than one evaluation"), exceeding the 10% threshold, and this attrition was entirely from the placebo group.

**2.5**: N (60%)
No explicit report of non-adherence to the medication regimen itself (separate from dropout); the trial was reported "concluded according to protocol" for the remaining subjects.

**2.6**: N (80%)
Data from dropouts were excluded entirely and not analyzed ("their data were not further analyzed"), representing a complete-case/per-protocol style analysis rather than an appropriate method (e.g., inverse probability weighting) to account for non-adherence/attrition.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (75%)
Blinding was intact (2.1=N, 2.2=N), but implementation failure occurred (2.4=Y, >10% attrition, imbalanced between arms) and was not addressed with an appropriate analytic method (2.6=N), indicating the effect estimate may be biased by this unaddressed deviation.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: N (90%)
49 participants randomized (25 TG, 24 PG); 43 analyzed (25 TG, 18 PG) = 87.8% — below the 90% threshold for continuous outcomes.

**3.2**: N (95%)
No sensitivity analysis or bias-correction method (e.g., inverse probability weighting) was reported; simple exclusion of dropouts was used.

**3.3**: N (65%)
Dropout reason given was "failed to appear for more than one evaluation," a procedural/non-health-specific reason, with no indication that missingness was related to weight (the outcome) itself.

**Result of Domain 3**: Low risk (75%)
3.1=N, 3.2=N, 3.3=N meets the criteria for "Low risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.2**: N (90%)

**4.3**: N (85%)

**4.4**: N (90%)
Weight is an objective outcome measure, unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**4.5**: N (90%)
No evidence suggesting that assessment of this objective outcome (weight) was influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (90%)

**Article ID**: Napolitano, 2014  
**Outcome Name**: recurrent thrombotic events  
**registration number**: NCT00450645  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: PN (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Random assignment was “determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10.” Allocation based on clinical chart number is a non-random rule for sequence generation.

**1.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Although sealed envelopes were used, the article does not report that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, or otherwise safeguarded against foreknowledge of allocation. The described envelope allocation method therefore did not demonstrate adequate allocation concealment.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups A1 and A2. The article states that there were no statistically significant differences between groups in cancer characteristics, metastatic disease, cancer type, or ongoing chemotherapy. Table 1 also shows no statistically significant baseline differences.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Allocation concealment was inadequate because the sealed-envelope method lacked reported safeguards. In addition, the allocation sequence was based on clinical chart number.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
Patients were aware of their assigned intervention. The article explicitly states that “patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT.”

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 100%)**  
Treating physicians and carers delivering treatment were aware of allocation. The article explicitly reports that treating physicians were not masked.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The article reports baseline chemotherapy and cancer-treatment characteristics but does not provide sufficient information about important nonprotocol interventions received after random assignment or whether such interventions were balanced between randomized groups.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 93%)**  
There were failures in implementation potentially affecting the intervention. During follow-up, 12 of 119 participants in group A1 died (10.1%), and 19 of 123 participants in group A2 died (15.4%). These proportions exceed 10% and could have prevented continued implementation of assigned treatment or follow-up.

**2.5: Y (High Certainty, 91%)**  
More than 10% of participants in group A1 had interruption of the assigned LMWH regimen: 15 of 119 participants (12.6%) interrupted LMWH, although interruptions were reported as lasting fewer than 5 days.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 100%)**  
The article states that data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis. Under the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to sustained treatment when non-adherence or implementation failures are present.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Participants and treating physicians were unmasked, there was insufficient information on balance of nonprotocol interventions, implementation failures and non-adherence exceeded 10%, and the analysis was intention-to-treat rather than an appropriate adherence-effect analysis.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (High Certainty, 90%)**  
Recurrent thrombotic events are binary outcomes. At 12 months after random assignment, 91 of 119 group-A1 participants and 77 of 123 group-A2 participants remained in follow-up. Thus, 74 of 242 randomized participants were no longer in follow-up. The total number of recurrent VTE events among randomized participants was 49 (22 in A1 and 27 in A2), which was not much greater than the number with missing outcome data or no longer in follow-up.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
No sensitivity analysis, inverse-probability weighting, or other method intended to assess or correct bias due to missing outcome data was reported. Intention-to-treat analysis does not itself correct bias caused by missing outcome data.

**3.3: PY (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
A substantial number of participants died during follow-up because of cancer progression. Missingness due to deterioration in health status may be related to the risk of recurrent thrombotic events, although the article does not directly establish that deaths or losses to follow-up were caused by recurrent VTE.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 71%)**  
The article reports different mortality proportions between group A1 (10.1%) and group A2 (15.4%), but it does not provide sufficient evidence that missingness was directly dependent on the unobserved recurrent thrombotic-event outcome. No analysis of the relationship between missing outcome data and recurrent VTE was provided.

**Result of the domain: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
Outcome data were not available for nearly all randomized participants, no method was used to demonstrate that missing data did not bias the results, and missingness may have been associated with participants’ health status.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Recurrent DVT was assessed using compression ultrasonography, with predefined diagnostic criteria: a previously compressible segment becoming noncompressible or an increase of more than 4 mm in residual thrombus diameter. Suspected PE was assessed using objective diagnostic algorithms including clinical probability, ventilation-perfusion scanning, and helical computed tomography. These are appropriate and reliable methods for recurrent VTE assessment.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
The article describes the same clinical follow-up procedures and diagnostic approach for recurrent thrombotic events across groups. Patients were instructed to contact the center for symptoms suggestive of VTE, and suspected events were assessed using the same objective diagnostic procedures.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 100%)**  
Outcome assessors were unaware of treatment assignment. All outcomes and deaths were assessed by a central committee whose members were unaware of group assignment, RVT results, enrolling center, and patient identity.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Recurrent thrombotic events were objectively confirmed by imaging-based diagnostic criteria for DVT and PE. Therefore, outcome assessment was not considered subjective or readily influenced by knowledge of treatment received.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention status influenced assessment of recurrent thrombotic events. The outcome was assessed by a blinded central committee using objective diagnostic information.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Recurrent thrombotic events were measured using appropriate objective methods, diagnostic approaches were comparable between groups, and the central outcome-adjudication committee was blinded to treatment assignment.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
The study was registered as NCT00450645 and listed recurrent DVT and/or PE as the primary outcome. However, the available registration information does not provide a detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data became available. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported analysis fully followed a finalized pre-specified analysis plan.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 91%)**  
The recurrent thrombotic-event outcome was defined in the Methods section and was reported in the Results section as recurrent VTE, including recurrent DVT and/or PE. There is no clear evidence that results were selectively reported from multiple eligible definitions, measurement methods, or time points.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
The article prespecified Kaplan-Meier analysis, log-rank testing, and Cox proportional-hazards models adjusted for age, sex, surgery, and metastasis. The Results section reports cumulative recurrence rates and adjusted hazard ratios consistent with these stated analyses. There is no clear evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple analyses on the basis of its results.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 83%)**  
Although the outcome and statistical methods reported in the article were generally consistent with the Methods section, the available information does not establish that a detailed analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

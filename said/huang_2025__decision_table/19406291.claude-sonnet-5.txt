# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Ashman, 2009
**Outcome Name**: HDM (HAM-D) score
**Registration number**: NCT00233103

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: **Y** (95% Certainty)
Reason: The article explicitly states "random assignment to treatment conditions...was balanced by using a block design (blocks of 8) with computer-generated random number sequences." This is an approved method for generating a random sequence.

**1.2**: **Y** (80% Certainty)
Reason: The article states sequences were placed "in individual presealed envelopes" and dispensing was managed by an independent pharmacy that implemented masking and random assignment, dispensing medications with identical markings. This describes a specific concealment mechanism (sealed, centrally-controlled allocation via pharmacy).

**1.3**: **Y** (85% Certainty)
Reason: "The income level of those receiving placebo was significantly lower than those participants receiving medication." This is a statistically significant baseline difference in an important prognostic variable (income), which the article attributes to randomization outcome (not corrected for), raising concern about the randomization process itself, especially in a small sample (n=41).

**Result of Domain 1**: **Some concerns** (80% Certainty)
Reason: 1.2 = Y, 1.1 = Y, and 1.3 = Y → per the algorithm, this combination yields "Some concerns."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: Double-blind design; placebo used; pharmacy dispensed identical-appearing medications with identical marking scheme so participants could not distinguish pill counts/dose changes.

**2.2**: **N** (85% Certainty)
Reason: "Both study staff and participants were blinded to the type of intervention received." Physicians/psychologists rating symptoms were also blinded via pharmacy-controlled dispensing.

**2.3**: **Y** (65% Certainty)
Reason: Standardized protocol was followed for all visits ("research staff followed a standard protocol, and the duration of each contact was kept constant"); no evidence of important imbalanced non-protocol interventions.

**2.4**: **Y** (90% Certainty)
Reason: 11 of 52 randomized participants (21%) did not complete the trial — exceeds the 10% threshold for implementation failure (dropouts, clinical worsening, noncompliance).

**2.5**: **Y** (80% Certainty)
Reason: "8 other noncompleters were noncompliant with the protocol by missing multiple follow-up appointments, not picking up medications, or failing to respond to follow-up contacts" — this represents >10% non-adherence (8/52 ≈ 15%, and total dropout 21%).

**2.6**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The study explicitly used "intent-to-treat analysis" and "last-observation-carried-forward" procedures — both are explicitly identified as inappropriate methods for estimating the effect of adhering to intervention.

**Result of Domain 2**: **High risk** (65% Certainty)
Reason: Although participants and carers were blinded (2.1=N, 2.2=N) and non-protocol interventions were balanced (2.3=Y), substantial deviations occurred due to implementation failures and non-adherence (2.4=Y, 2.5=Y exceeding 10%), and the analysis method used (ITT/LOCF) is explicitly classified as inappropriate for estimating the effect of adhering to intervention (2.6=N). This combination of substantial deviations with an inappropriate analytic correction elevates the risk to High.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: **N** (95% Certainty)
Reason: Continuous outcome (HAM-D); 41/52 randomized participants (78.8%) had complete outcome data at end of treatment — below the 90% threshold.

**3.2**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The study used ITT analysis combined with LOCF, which is explicitly identified as an inappropriate method for correcting bias due to missing data (and ITT itself does not correct for missing-data bias per instructions).

**3.3**: **Y** (85% Certainty)
Reason: "The intent-to-treat analyses found differences between the last assessment HAM-D score for those who dropped out of treatment as compared to...those who remained in treatment...with the dropouts tending to have higher HAM-D scores." This directly links missingness to the outcome variable itself (depression severity).

**3.4**: **Y** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Reasons for dropout included clinical worsening (increased depression, increased anxiety) directly related to the outcome domain, and the analysis confirmed dropouts had systematically higher HAM-D scores — indicating missingness likely depended on the true outcome value.

**Result of Domain 3**: **High risk** (85% Certainty)
Reason: 3.1=N, 3.2=N, 3.3=Y, 3.4=Y → per the algorithm this combination yields "High risk."

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: **N** (90% Certainty)
Reason: The HAM-D is a well-validated, widely used, clinician-rated measure of depression severity with established reliability and validity, and was appropriately used per protocol.

**4.2**: **N** (85% Certainty)
Reason: Both groups were assessed using the identical scale (HAM-D) at identical biweekly time points by the same protocol ("duration of each contact was kept constant"), with no differential measurement schedule between groups.

**4.3**: **N** (80% Certainty)
Reason: HAM-D is a clinician-rated (not patient-reported) measure. Given the double-blind design with pharmacy-controlled dispensing and blinded staff, outcome assessors were unaware of participants' group assignment.

**4.4**: **Y** (75% Certainty)
Reason: HAM-D is a subjective measure assessed by an outcome assessor (clinician-rated), which per the rules is classified as "Y" regardless of blinding status.

**4.5**: **N** (70% Certainty)
Reason: No clear evidence in the article suggests that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention status; the double-blind design with independent, rotating assessors ("not necessarily seen by the same staff member") reduces this likelihood.

**Result of Domain 4**: **Low risk** (85% Certainty)
Reason: 4.1=N, 4.2=N, 4.3=N → per the algorithm, this combination directly yields "Low risk," regardless of 4.4/4.5 values.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: **Y** (85% Certainty)
Reason: The trial was prospectively registered (NCT00233103) with HAM-D as primary outcome; the measurement and analysis methods (HAM-D at baseline/end of treatment, random-effects modeling) reported in Methods match those reported in Results.

**5.2**: **Y** (75% Certainty)
Reason: There is clear evidence of multiple eligible outcome measurements within the depression domain (total HAM-D score plus four alternative unidimensional subscales: Beck Melancholia, Maier & Philipp, Gibbons, and "5 most sensitive"/HamFive). While the primary total HAM-D result was non-significant, two specific subscales (HamG and HamFive) were highlighted and reported as showing a significant group effect, suggesting selective emphasis on outcome measurements that showed favorable/significant results.

**5.3**: **N** (60% Certainty)
Reason: While multiple analyses were conducted (ANCOVA, random-effects models, ITT, LOCF), all were reported transparently in the results without evidence of selective suppression of analyses based on findings.

**Result of Domain 5**: **High risk** (75% Certainty)
Reason: 5.2 = Y → per the algorithm, this directly yields "High risk."

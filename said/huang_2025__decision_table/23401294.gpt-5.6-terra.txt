## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1 Was the allocation sequence adequately generated?**: Y — **High Certainty (98%)**  
**Reason:** Participants were randomly allocated by picking the name of one of the two orthoses from a bag. This is a lot-drawing method of random-sequence generation.

**1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**: NI — **High Certainty (96%)**  
**Reason:** The article describes drawing orthosis names from a bag but does not report allocation concealment methods, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or identical numbered containers.

**1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**: N — **High Certainty (97%)**  
**Reason:** There were no statistically significant baseline differences between groups in age, weight, height, kyphosis angle, or baseline balance score. The group sizes were 10 and 8, which does not constitute a substantial imbalance.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns — Moderate Certainty (90%)**  
**Reason:** The random allocation method was adequately described and baseline characteristics were comparable, but allocation concealment was not reported.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**: Y — **High Certainty (95%)**  
**Reason:** Participants wore either the Spinomed orthosis or the posture-training support. These visibly and physically distinct orthoses would make participants aware of their assigned intervention.

**2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial?**: Y — **High Certainty (97%)**  
**Reason:** A certified orthotist fitted and adjusted the assigned orthosis for each participant. Therefore, the intervention provider was aware of the assigned intervention.

**2.3 Were important nonprotocol interventions balanced across intervention groups?**: Y — **Moderate Certainty (82%)**  
**Reason:** No important nonprotocol interventions that could affect balance score were reported. The study specifically stated that no exercise programme was included.

**2.4 Were there failures in implementing the intervention that could have affected the outcome?**: N — **High Certainty (94%)**  
**Reason:** All 18 randomized participants completed the study, and balance-score data were available for all 10 Spinomed participants and all 8 PTS participants. No implementation failures affecting more than 10% of participants were reported.

**2.5 Was there non-adherence to the assigned intervention regimen that could have affected participants’ outcomes?**: N — **Moderate Certainty (80%)**  
**Reason:** All participants completed the immediate assessment procedure and balance-score results were reported for every allocated participant. No treatment discontinuation, crossover, or non-adherence affecting more than 10% of participants was reported.

**2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention?**: NI — **High Certainty (92%)**  
**Reason:** The study used paired and independent-sample t-tests. It did not report an analysis specifically designed to estimate the effect of adherence, such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting.

**Result of Domain 2: Low risk — Moderate Certainty (85%)**  
**Reason:** Although participants and intervention providers were aware of the assigned orthosis, there were no reported important nonprotocol interventions, substantial implementation failures, or substantial non-adherence.

**4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**: N — **High Certainty (96%)**  
**Reason:** Balance score was measured using the NeuroCom EquiTest Sensory Organization Test according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The article reports that balance score has established reliability and validity.

**4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**: N — **High Certainty (94%)**  
**Reason:** Both groups underwent the same Sensory Organization Test procedures, using the same equipment, protocol, test configurations, and assessment conditions.

**4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: N — **High Certainty (93%)**  
**Reason:** Balance score was generated by an instrumented force-platform system rather than by subjective patient report or assessor judgment.

**4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: N — **High Certainty (90%)**  
**Reason:** There is no clear evidence that knowledge of orthosis assignment was likely to influence the instrument-derived balance score.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk — Moderate Certainty (89%)**  
**Reason:** Balance score was measured objectively using the same validated computerized system and procedures in both groups. Although assessor blinding was not specifically reported, the outcome was instrument-derived.

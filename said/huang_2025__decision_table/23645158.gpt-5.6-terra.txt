**Outcome Name**: NT Pro-BNP levels

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
Reason: The article explicitly states that patients “were allocated into one of the two groups based on a computer random numbers table.” This is an adequate random sequence-generation method.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: Although the study is described as double-blind and used placebo saline, the article does not describe an allocation-concealment mechanism, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or sequentially numbered identical drug containers. Double blinding alone does not demonstrate concealment of the allocation sequence.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: The groups each contained 44 participants. Baseline demographic, clinical, laboratory, and intraoperative characteristics, including age, body surface area, Euroscore, ejection fraction, creatinine, CPB time, aortic cross-clamp time, and baseline NT Pro-BNP, were not statistically different between groups.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Random sequence generation was adequate and baseline characteristics were balanced, but allocation concealment was not reported.

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
Reason: The study was described as double-blind, and the control group received an intravenous normal-saline placebo in the same volume and via the same type of 50-mL syringe as the magnesium sulfate group. Participants were therefore unlikely to have been aware of their assigned intervention.

**2.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Reason: The trial was described as double-blind. The intervention and placebo were both administered intravenously in the same volume, and anesthetic, surgical, CPB, and postoperative care protocols were standardized between groups. Therefore, carers and intervention providers were unlikely to have known allocation, although the exact personnel blinded were not specified.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Reason: The article states that, apart from magnesium sulfate or placebo, anesthesia, surgery, CPB procedures, and postoperative care were the same in both groups. No important imbalanced nonprotocol intervention prognostic for NT Pro-BNP was reported.

**2.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: The intervention was initiated after anesthesia induction and continued until skin closure. The Results tables include 44 participants in each randomized group, with no reported intervention-implementation failures affecting more than 10% of participants.

**2.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
Reason: Magnesium sulfate or placebo was administered as a controlled intraoperative infusion until skin closure. No treatment discontinuations, treatment crossovers, or other non-adherence affecting more than 10% of participants were reported.

**2.6: NA (High Certainty, 93%)**  
Reason: No important non-adherence to the assigned intraoperative infusion regimen was reported. Therefore, an analysis specifically estimating the effect of adherence was not applicable.

**Result of Domain 2: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
Reason: Participants and providers were likely blinded, care was standardized between groups, and there was no evidence of important failures in implementation or non-adherence.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Eighty-eight patients were randomized, with 44 participants in each group. NT Pro-BNP results are reported for both groups with n = 44 in Table 3. Thus, complete outcome data were available for 100% of randomized participants.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Reason: The article does not report sensitivity analyses, inverse-probability weighting, or another method specifically addressing possible bias due to missing NT Pro-BNP data. However, the NT Pro-BNP outcome appears complete.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
Reason: No missing NT Pro-BNP data were reported, and the article provides no specific information regarding whether any potential missingness could have depended on the true NT Pro-BNP value.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
Reason: No missing NT Pro-BNP data or reasons for missing outcome measurements were reported. Therefore, the likelihood that missingness depended on the true outcome value cannot be assessed directly.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: NT Pro-BNP data were available for all 88 randomized participants, exceeding the 95% completeness threshold for a continuous outcome.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: NT Pro-BNP was measured using an electrochemiluminescence immunoassay on the Elecsys 2010 platform. The assay had a reported coefficient of variation of 2.2%–3.2% and a detection range of 5–35,000 pg/mL, indicating a reliable and appropriate measurement method.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: NT Pro-BNP blood samples were collected using the same procedure for both groups: before anesthesia and 24 hours after ICU arrival. Sample handling, centrifugation, refrigeration, and assay procedures were reported as consistent throughout the study.

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 76%)**  
Reason: NT Pro-BNP is an objective laboratory outcome, but the article does not explicitly state whether laboratory personnel or outcome assessors were blinded to intervention allocation.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: NT Pro-BNP concentration is an objective biochemical laboratory measurement. Its assessment is not expected to be materially influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: There is no clear evidence that knowledge of magnesium sulfate or placebo allocation could have influenced the automated electrochemiluminescence immunoassay measurement of NT Pro-BNP.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: NT Pro-BNP was assessed using a reliable objective assay at comparable time points and with standardized sample handling in both intervention groups.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Reason: The study was registered at the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT201205069650N1). The Methods section specifies NT Pro-BNP measurement before anesthesia and 24 hours after ICU arrival, using electrochemiluminescence immunoassay and between-group statistical comparison. Both prespecified NT Pro-BNP measurements and the reported analysis are presented in the Results section.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 90%)**  
Reason: The Methods section prespecified two NT Pro-BNP measurements: baseline/preoperative and postoperative at 24 hours after ICU arrival. Both measurements were reported in Table 3. There is no evidence that only a favorable measurement time point was selected from multiple unreported NT Pro-BNP measurements.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
Reason: The Methods section states that Student’s t-test was used for statistical analysis, and the Results present between-group comparisons using corresponding P values. There is no clear evidence that multiple eligible analyses were performed and only selectively reported.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
Reason: NT Pro-BNP measurement time points and analysis methods described in the Methods section were reported consistently in the Results section, without evidence of selective outcome-measurement or analysis reporting.

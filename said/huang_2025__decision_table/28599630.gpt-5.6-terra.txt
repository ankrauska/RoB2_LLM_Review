**Article ID**: da Rocha, 2017  
**Outcome Name**: prevalence of depressive symptoms (EPDS score ≥ 11)  
**registration number**: NCT01660165  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (High Certainty, 96%)**  
The article states that eligible women were “randomly assigned” after stratification for EPDS score and previous history of depression. However, it does not report the method used to generate the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, block randomization, or similar method).

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The article reports that a researcher not involved in data collection performed randomization and that participants and study personnel were blinded. However, it does not report a specific allocation-concealment method, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or sequentially numbered identical containers.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Baseline characteristics were generally comparable between groups. Although skin color differed numerically between groups (non-white: 59.4% in fish-oil group versus 82.1% in placebo group; p = 0.055), this difference was not statistically significant. Baseline EPDS score and history of depression were also comparable.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Allocation-sequence concealment was not reported. Under the specified decision rules, an answer of NI for allocation concealment with no clear baseline evidence of a randomization problem leads to some concerns.

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Participants were blinded to allocation. Fish-oil and placebo capsules were identical in color, shape, size, and packaging, and both were deodorized.

**2.2: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Research assistants, technicians, and other study personnel involved in the cohort study and RCT were reported to be blinded to treatment allocation.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
All participants received standard prenatal folic acid and ferrous sulphate supplementation. Participants were also instructed not to use other supplements or change their usual dietary habits. No important imbalance in nonprotocol interventions was reported.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
There were substantial failures in intervention implementation. Eleven randomized women did not receive the intervention, including six fish-oil and four placebo participants lost after randomization plus one fish-oil participant who declined participation. In addition, 12 women dropped out after trial commencement, and further exclusions occurred. These losses exceeded 10% of randomized participants.

**2.5: Y (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Adherence was incomplete. Among women who received the intervention, mean capsule consumption was approximately 74% in both groups, indicating that more than 10% of the prescribed intervention was not taken. Some women also reported nausea, vomiting, or difficulty swallowing capsules.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
The study used intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses. According to the specified criteria, ITT and per-protocol analyses are classified as inappropriate methods for estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned sustained intervention regimen.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 92%)**  
There were substantial intervention-implementation failures and non-adherence exceeding 10%, while the analyses included ITT and per-protocol approaches rather than the specified appropriate methods for estimating effects of adherence.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
The outcome is binary: EPDS score ≥11. Sixty women were randomized, but only 32 completed the trial at postpartum. At T3, 15 depressive-symptom events were reported in the ITT analysis (8 in fish-oil group and 7 in placebo group), whereas approximately 28 randomized women did not complete the trial. The number of observed events was not much greater than the number of participants with missing outcome data.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
The article did not report inverse probability weighting, appropriate missing-data sensitivity analyses, or another method demonstrating that missing outcome data did not bias the results. ITT analysis alone does not establish correction for missing-data bias.

**3.3: PY (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
Some missingness arose from health-related circumstances, including miscarriage, stillbirth, and pre-eclampsia. These reasons reflect health status, but the article does not establish that they were directly related to depressive symptoms measured by EPDS.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 72%)**  
The article provides reasons for losses and states that early losses appeared unrelated to the study, but it does not provide analyses of whether missingness was related to the true EPDS outcome or sufficient characteristics of participants with missing outcome data to assess this relationship.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 90%)**  
A substantial proportion of outcome data was missing at postpartum, no appropriate analysis demonstrated that missingness did not bias the result, and missingness could plausibly be associated with participants’ health status. The likelihood of outcome-dependent missingness could not be excluded.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Depressive symptoms were measured using the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS). The Portuguese EPDS version had been validated in Brazilian mothers, and the cutoff of EPDS ≥11 was reported to have 83.8% sensitivity and 74.7% specificity for moderate-to-severe postpartum depressive symptoms.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The EPDS was administered at the same scheduled time points in both intervention groups: T1, T2, and T3. The same EPDS threshold (≥11) was used for both groups.

**4.3: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
The EPDS is a patient-reported outcome measure. Participants were blinded to intervention allocation through use of identical placebo capsules; therefore, participants completing the EPDS were not aware of their assigned intervention.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 95%)**  
EPDS score is a subjective, patient-reported measure of depressive symptoms. In principle, assessment of a subjective patient-reported outcome could be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**4.5: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention status was likely to influence EPDS assessment because participants were blinded and received identical-looking placebo or fish-oil capsules.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The EPDS was a validated and appropriate measurement tool, was administered comparably in both groups, and participants reporting the outcome were blinded to intervention allocation.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 94%)**  
The ClinicalTrials.gov record was retrospectively registered and identifies the study as an observational cohort study with enrollment of 299 women. It does not provide a pre-specified randomized-trial analysis plan for fish-oil supplementation and the EPDS ≥11 outcome that was finalized before outcome data became available.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
The article clearly specifies EPDS score ≥11 as the primary outcome and reports prevalence results at all stated assessment time points (T0, T1, T2, and T3), including ITT and per-protocol analyses. There is no clear evidence that only selected EPDS ≥11 measurements were reported.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
The statistical methods for the EPDS ≥11 outcome were described in the Methods section, including between-group comparisons and random-intercept logistic regression. The article reports the planned ITT and per-protocol analyses and presents both unadjusted and adjusted regression models. There is no clear evidence that one analysis was selectively reported from multiple eligible analyses on the basis of results.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 90%)**  
A pre-specified randomized-trial analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available was not demonstrated. However, there was no clear evidence of selective reporting from multiple EPDS ≥11 measurements or analyses.

## Overall Bias Risk for the Article: High Risk (High Certainty, 94%)

The overall risk of bias is **High Risk** because Domain 2 and Domain 3 were assessed as high risk. The principal concerns are substantial failures in intervention implementation and adherence, use of analyses classified as inappropriate for estimating adherence effects, and substantial missing outcome data without evidence that missingness did not bias the EPDS ≥11 results.

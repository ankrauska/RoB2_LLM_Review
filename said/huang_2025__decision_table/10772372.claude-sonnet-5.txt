# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Shaw, 2000 (Pharmacotherapy 2000;20(4):410–416)
**Outcome Name**: Compliance
**Registration number**: Not reported/Not applicable

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: Y (95%)
Reason: The article explicitly states "Patients were randomized to telephone contact or no telephone contact using a computer-generated list of random numbers."

**1.2**: NI (95%)
Reason: No mention of allocation concealment methods (central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, or opaque envelopes) anywhere in the Methods section.

**1.3**: N (90%)
Reason: The article states "Baseline characteristics were similar for both groups (Table 1)," indicating no problematic differences suggesting randomization issues.

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (90%)
Reason: Since 1.2 is "NI" and 1.3 is "N," per the guideline rules this yields "Some concerns."

---

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (95%)
Reason: This is an open-label trial comparing telephone contact vs. no telephone contact; participants clearly knew whether they were receiving weekly phone calls.

**2.2**: Y (95%)
Reason: The pharmacist delivering the intervention necessarily knew which patients were assigned to receive telephone follow-up calls.

**2.3**: NI (70%)
Reason: While insurance coverage differences (33% vs. 27% uninsured) were discussed as a potential confounder, no clear determination of balance regarding other non-protocol interventions is provided.

**2.4**: N (90%)
Reason: "None of whom was lost to follow-up" — implementation was successful for essentially all participants.

**2.5**: N (70%)
Reason: No evidence that the telephone-call intervention itself was not delivered/adhered to; calls appear to have been consistently administered as planned (aside from the underlying compliance outcome being measured, which is the study outcome, not intervention delivery failure).

**2.6**: NI (70%)
Reason: No explicit description of statistical methods to adjust for non-adherence to the assigned protocol (chi-square/t-test only used for group comparisons); not ITT/per-protocol explicitly stated either.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (85%)
Reason: One of 2.1/2.2 is "Y," 2.3 is "NI," and 2.6 is "NI" — per the rules, this combination yields "High risk."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: Y (95%)
Reason: "Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up." All randomized participants have complete outcome data.

**Result of Domain 3**: Low risk (95%)
Reason: 3.1 is "Y," which directly yields "Low risk" per the rules.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90%)
Reason: Compliance was measured via validated, objective methods (pill/packet counts not performed within patient's view, and pharmacy refill records) — a reliable and appropriate method.

**4.2**: N (85%)
Reason: Measurement was conducted identically for both groups (pill/packet counts at 6/12 weeks; refill records at 1 and 2 years), using the same thresholds (80%/20%) for both groups.

**4.3**: Y (70%)
Reason: The outcome measurement is not solely patient-reported but is not explicitly stated as blinded; given the open-label nature of the trial (no placebo/sham), outcome assessors (pharmacy/clinic staff) were likely aware of group assignment.

**4.4**: N (75%)
Reason: Pill counts and pharmacy refill records are relatively objective measures of compliance, less susceptible to subjective interpretation bias compared to patient-reported outcomes.

**4.5**: N (75%)
Reason: No clear evidence in the article suggesting that knowledge of intervention status influenced the objective assessment of pill counts or refill records.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (85%)
Reason: 4.1 is "N," 4.2 is "N," 4.3 is "Y," and 4.4 is "N" — per the rules, this yields "Low risk."

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: NI (85%)
Reason: No trial registration or pre-specified analysis plan is mentioned in the article.

**5.2**: N (80%)
Reason: Compliance was measured consistently using pill/packet counts and refill records as pre-specified in the Methods, with consistent reporting in Results; no evidence of selective reporting from multiple eligible measurements.

**5.3**: N (80%)
Reason: The chi-square statistic described in Methods for comparing proportions of compliant patients matches the analysis reported in Results; no evidence of selection from multiple analyses.

**Result of Domain 5**: Some concerns (85%)
Reason: 5.2 is "N," 5.3 is "N," and 5.1 is "NI" — per the rules, this combination yields "Some concerns."

**Outcome Name**: Performance (correct answers/time to complete test)
**Registration number**: Not reported

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: Y (95%)
*Reason*: The article explicitly states "Students were randomized into three groups using a computer program," which is an adequate method of sequence generation.

**1.2**: NI (95%)
*Reason*: No mention of any allocation concealment method (central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, opaque envelopes) is provided in the article.

**1.3**: N (90%)
*Reason*: Table 2 shows no statistically significant differences in age, sex distribution, or school age between the three groups (CT, 3D, 3Dp).

**Result of Domain 1**: Some concerns (85%)
*Reason*: Since 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = N, per the decision rules this classifies as "some concerns."

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1**: Y (95%)
*Reason*: Participants were obviously aware of their assigned teaching module (CT images vs. 3D images vs. 3D printed physical model), as these are physically distinct and non-blindable interventions.

**2.2**: Y (95%)
*Reason*: "A teacher provided a detailed interpretation of the fracture in each group," indicating teachers/carers knew which module (and thus allocation) each subgroup received.

**2.3**: NI (70%)
*Reason*: No information provided regarding non-protocol interventions.

**2.4**: N (95%)
*Reason*: The article states "a total of 120 medical students...participated, and no one dropped out."

**2.5**: N (85%)
*Reason*: This was a single-session educational intervention with no indication of non-adherence; all students completed the assigned module in one class.

**2.6**: NI (80%)
*Reason*: No explicit analytical method to account for adherence is reported; given the single-session nature and no dropout, no specific estimand analysis method is described.

**Result of Domain 2**: High risk (85%)
*Reason*: Since 2.1/2.2 = Y, 2.3 = NI, and 2.6 = NI, this combination maps to "High risk" per the domain rules (one of 2.1/2.2 = Y/PY/NI, 2.3 = NI/N/PN, and 2.6 = NI/N/PN → High risk).

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: N (90%)
*Reason*: The outcome (correct/incorrect answers to a predefined 10-question test) was a predefined, purpose-built measurement tool assessed by two senior professors for validity; considered reliable.

**4.2**: N (90%)
*Reason*: All groups completed the same standardized 10-question examination under comparable conditions (same setting, same timing, same invigilation procedure).

**4.3**: Y (75%)
*Reason*: The outcome is not patient-reported (it is an objectively scored test of correct/incorrect answers); however, invigilators/teachers were present in group-specific settings and were aware of group assignment (each group had distinct materials/rooms), so outcome assessors were not blinded to allocation.

**4.4**: N (85%)
*Reason*: The outcome is objective (multiple-choice/binary correct-incorrect answers with a predefined answer key), not subject to interpretation bias by assessors.

**Result of Domain 4**: Low risk (85%)
*Reason*: Since 4.1 = N, 4.2 = N, 4.3 = Y, and 4.4 = N, this maps to "Low risk" per domain rules.

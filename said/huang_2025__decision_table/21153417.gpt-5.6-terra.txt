**Outcome Name**: Muscle damage  
**registration number**: Not reported  

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: During racing period B, “crew members were divided into two groups according to random numbers,” which is an explicitly reported random sequence-generation method.

**1.2: PY (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Reason: The article states that “sailors were blind regarding the group assignment.” This indicates masking of participants, but it does not report a specific allocation-concealment mechanism, such as central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or identical numbered drug containers.

**1.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: Important baseline differences were evident between groups. The allopurinol group contained 8 high-intensity-work sailors and 5 moderate/low-intensity sailors, whereas the placebo group contained only 2 high-intensity-work sailors and 8 moderate/low-intensity sailors. High-intensity work was associated with markedly higher CK and AST values in period A. In addition, pre-race CK was substantially higher in the allopurinol group (445.8 U/L) than in the placebo group (275.8 U/L).

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Reason: Random numbers were used to generate allocation and participants were reported to be blinded, but the allocation-concealment method was not specifically described and important baseline imbalances in work-intensity categories and CK levels were present.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: The article explicitly states that sailors were blind regarding group assignment, and the comparator group received placebo.

**2.2: NI (Moderate Certainty, 70%)**  
Reason: The article does not report whether carers or personnel administering allopurinol and placebo were blinded to participants’ assigned interventions.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 65%)**  
Reason: The article does not provide sufficient information regarding non-protocol interventions, concurrent training activities, dietary supplementation, recovery measures, or other co-interventions during racing period B.

**2.4: Y (High Certainty, 90%)**  
Reason: Four of the 27 crew members declined to take part in period B. This represents 14.8% of the initially enrolled crew, exceeding the 10% threshold for failures in implementing the intervention.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Reason: No information was reported regarding adherence to the assigned allopurinol or placebo regimen, including missed doses, discontinuation, or crossover.

**2.6: NI (Moderate Certainty, 75%)**  
Reason: The reported analysis consisted of descriptive statistics and t tests. The article did not report instrumental-variable methods, inverse-probability weighting, or another appropriate method to estimate the effect of adherence to the assigned intervention. It also did not report an intention-to-treat, per-protocol, or as-treated analysis specifically for adherence.

**Result of Domain 2: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
Reason: There were implementation failures affecting more than 10% of the initial crew, adherence information was not reported, non-protocol interventions were not described, and no appropriate analysis of adherence to the assigned intervention was reported.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: Twenty-seven crew members were included initially, but four declined participation in period B. The reported intervention-phase results included 23 sailors (13 allopurinol and 10 placebo), corresponding to 23/27 = 85.2% with outcome data. CK and AST are continuous outcomes; therefore, this is below the 90% threshold.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The article did not report inverse-probability weighting, sensitivity analyses for missing data, or another method providing evidence that missing outcome data did not bias the muscle-damage results.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 68%)**  
Reason: Four sailors declined to participate in period B, but the article does not provide their reasons for declining or whether this was related to their CK, AST, health status, training load, or muscle damage.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 65%)**  
Reason: The article does not report characteristics, baseline CK/AST values, work roles, or reasons for non-participation among the four sailors who declined period-B participation. Therefore, it is not possible to determine whether missingness was likely related to the true muscle-damage outcome.

**Result of Domain 3: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Reason: Outcome data were available for fewer than 90% of the initially enrolled crew, no analysis addressed potential bias from missing data, and insufficient information was provided to determine whether missingness was related to muscle-damage outcomes.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: Muscle damage was measured using serum CK and AST activities, determined spectrophotometrically with commercially available reagents on an automatic analyser. Reported intra-assay coefficients of variation were 4.5% for CK and 4.2% for AST, supporting reliable measurement.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Reason: CK and AST were measured using the same laboratory procedures and at comparable pre-race and post-race time points in both the allopurinol and placebo groups.

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 72%)**  
Reason: The outcomes were laboratory-based rather than patient-reported, but the article does not state whether laboratory staff or outcome assessors were blinded to allopurinol versus placebo assignment.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: CK and AST are objective biochemical laboratory measurements. Knowledge of intervention assignment would not be expected to materially influence the enzymatic measurement generated by an automatic analyser.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: There is no clear evidence that knowledge of allopurinol or placebo assignment was likely to influence the objective laboratory assessment of serum CK or AST.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 92%)**  
Reason: Muscle damage was assessed using reliable, objective biochemical measurements obtained comparably across groups. Although assessor blinding was not reported, the objective nature of CK and AST measurement makes material bias from knowledge of intervention assignment unlikely.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: No trial registration number, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available was reported.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: For the intervention phase, CK and AST were measured before and after racing in both allopurinol and placebo groups, and these measurements were reported. There is no clear evidence that results were selectively reported from multiple eligible muscle-damage measurement time points.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
Reason: The Methods section specifies descriptive statistics and parametric t tests, and the Results section reports group comparisons and within-group pre/post comparisons consistent with this approach. There is no clear evidence that multiple analyses were performed and only favorable analyses were selectively reported.

**Result of Domain 5: Some concerns (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
Reason: The absence of a registration record, protocol, or pre-specified analysis plan prevents confirmation that the analyses were finalized before outcome data were examined. However, there is no direct evidence of selective reporting from multiple outcome measurements or analyses.

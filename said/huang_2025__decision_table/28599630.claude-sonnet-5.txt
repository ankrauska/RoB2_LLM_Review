# Risk of Bias Assessment

**Article ID**: Vaz, 2017
**Outcome Name**: Prevalence of depressive symptoms (EPDS score ≥ 11)
**Registration number**: NCT01660165

---

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: **NI** (85%)
Reason: The article states "The randomization was performed by a researcher not involved in the data collection using the participant identification (subject ID) after stratification for EPDS score and previous history of depression." No specific sequence generation method (e.g., computer-generated numbers, random number table, block randomization) is described.

**1.2**: **NI** (90%)
Reason: The article does not mention any allocation concealment method (no central allocation, sequentially numbered containers, or opaque envelopes described), despite describing the trial as double-blind.

**1.3**: **Y** (80%)
Reason: Table 1 shows the authors explicitly state "there were no differences between groups, with the exception of skin color" (Non-white: 82.1% control vs. 59.4% fish oil, p=0.055). This baseline imbalance in an important characteristic suggests a possible randomization process issue.

**Result of Domain 1**: **High risk** (85%)
Reason: Per the algorithm, when 1.2 = NI and 1.3 = Y, the domain result is "High risk."

---

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions

**2.1**: **N** (85%)
Reason: The trial is described as double-blind; capsules were identical in color, shape, size, and packaging. Side effects (nausea/vomiting) were balanced between groups (4 vs. 4) and not indicative of unblinding.

**2.2**: **N** (85%)
Reason: "Participants and all research assistants and technicians responsible for running both the cohort study and the RCT were blinded to the study allocation."

**2.3**: **Y** (80%)
Reason: All participants received standard folic acid and ferrous sulphate supplementation equally, per standard prenatal care; no imbalance in non-protocol interventions reported.

**2.4**: **Y** (90%)
Reason: Of 60 randomized, only 32 completed the trial (~53% completion, >10% failure). Reasons included transfers, stillbirth, miscarriage, missed visits, lost capsules, and preeclampsia.

**2.5**: **Y** (85%)
Reason: Average compliance was 74.4% (fish oil) and 73.5% (control), indicating >10% non-adherence to the assigned regimen among those who received the intervention.

**2.6**: **N** (90%)
Reason: The article used both intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol analyses, both of which are considered inappropriate methods to estimate the effect of adherence per current guidance.

**Result of Domain 2**: **Some concerns** (65%)
Reason: Although participants/personnel were blinded (2.1=N, 2.2=N), substantial deviations occurred (2.4=Y, 2.5=Y) with inappropriate analysis (2.6=N). This does not cleanly match the "Low risk" criteria (which required 2.4/2.5=N/PN), so given the magnitude of implementation failure and non-adherence despite maintained blinding, this is best classified as "Some concerns."

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1**: **N** (85%)
Reason: 60 women were randomized; only 32 completed the trial (~53%), representing substantial missing outcome data for this binary outcome, with dropout not negligible relative to observed events.

**3.2**: **N** (90%)
Reason: No inverse probability weighting or sensitivity analysis to correct for missing data bias was reported. ITT analysis is not a valid substitute for addressing missing data bias.

**3.3**: **N** (70%)
Reason: Reported reasons for dropout (transfer of prenatal care, stillbirth, miscarriage, exceeding gestational window, lost capsules, missed visits, preeclampsia) were described by the authors as "apparently not related to the study," suggesting missingness is largely unrelated to the depressive symptom outcome itself.

**3.4**: Not required (per algorithm, since 3.3 = N).

**Result of Domain 3**: **Low risk** (80%)
Reason: Per the algorithm, 3.1=N, 3.2=N, 3.3=N → Low risk.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1**: **N** (90%)
Reason: EPDS is a validated, widely used, and reliable scale for depressive symptoms in pregnancy/postpartum populations (validated in Brazilian Portuguese).

**4.2**: **N** (85%)
Reason: EPDS was administered at the same predefined timepoints (T0, T1, T2, T3) using the same method and threshold (≥11) for both groups.

**4.3**: **N** (85%)
Reason: EPDS is a patient-reported outcome, and participants were blinded to their allocation (double-blind design), so participants were unaware of group assignment.

**4.4**: **Y** (90%)
Reason: EPDS is a subjective, patient-reported measure of depressive symptoms.

**4.5**: **N** (80%)
Reason: No clear evidence suggests that knowledge of intervention influenced outcome assessment, given blinding was maintained throughout the trial.

**Result of Domain 4**: **Low risk** (85%)
Reason: Per the algorithm, 4.1=N, 4.2=N, 4.3=N → Low risk.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1**: **Y** (75%)
Reason: The Methods section pre-specifies EPDS ≥11 prevalence as the primary outcome, measured at T0, T1, T2, T3, using ITT and per-protocol analyses; the Results section reports this consistently as planned (Table 3).

**5.2**: **N** (80%)
Reason: All planned EPDS≥11 measurements across all four timepoints (T0-T3), for both ITT and per-protocol, and for both the overall sample and the subgroup with prior depression history, were fully reported (Table 3).

**5.3**: **N** (75%)
Reason: Both ITT and per-protocol analyses, along with adjusted (Model 2) and unadjusted (Model 1) regression analyses, were reported comprehensively (Tables 3, 5), with no evidence of selective reporting from multiple analyses.

**Result of Domain 5**: **Low risk** (75%)
Reason: Per the algorithm, 5.2=N, 5.3=N, 5.1=Y → Low risk.

## Overall Bias Risk Assessment Result for the Article

**Overall Result**: **High Risk** (85%)

Reason: Domain 1 (randomization process) was assessed as "High risk" due to lack of information on allocation concealment (1.2=NI) combined with evidence of baseline imbalance attributable to randomization (1.3=Y, skin color imbalance). Per the overall algorithm, if any single domain is rated "High risk," the overall article assessment is "High Risk," regardless of the other domains' more favorable ratings (Domain 2=Some concerns; Domains 3, 4, 5=Low risk).

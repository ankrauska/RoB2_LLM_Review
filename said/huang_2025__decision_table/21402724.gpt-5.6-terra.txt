## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Randomization was performed by Survey Research Laboratory staff using “a random allocation sequence programmed into CAPI administration software.” This is a computer-generated random allocation sequence.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** The random allocation sequence in the CAPI software “allowed for complete allocation concealment up to the point of assignment.” Interviewers and respondents did not know the study condition until assignment occurred.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The article states that randomization was successful, with no statistically significant baseline differences between intervention and control groups for participant characteristics or prebaseline service use. Allocation numbers were also similar (276 intervention versus 279 control).

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The allocation sequence was computer-generated, allocation was concealed until assignment, and baseline characteristics did not indicate a problem with randomization.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Participants were assigned either to an 8-week WRAP program or to a wait-list/usual-care condition. Participants receiving WRAP sessions and participants placed on a waiting list would have known their assigned intervention.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** WRAP was delivered by peer facilitators. The facilitators necessarily knew which participants were receiving the WRAP intervention.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** Usual services were broadly similar between groups, with no statistically significant between-group differences in service receipt. Control participants attended peer-support groups, and the authors reran analyses controlling for peer-support-group exposure; the reported results did not differ substantially. There is no clear evidence that important nonprotocol interventions were imbalanced in a way that biased the BSI outcome.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** Intervention delivery fidelity was high (mean fidelity 91.3%), and missed curriculum material was covered in subsequent sessions. Although 25 intervention participants were lost to follow-up, this was 9.1% of the 276 randomized intervention participants, below the specified 10% threshold for implementation failure.

**2.5: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** Non-adherence to WRAP was substantial. Of 276 intervention participants, 43 (16%) received no intervention sessions. In addition, participants attended an average of only 5.05 of 8 sessions. Therefore, more than 10% did not adhere fully to the assigned intervention regimen.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** The study used an intention-to-treat mixed-effects random regression analysis. Under the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method to estimate the effect of adhering to the intervention when substantial non-adherence occurred.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** Participants and intervention providers were aware of assignment, more than 10% of intervention participants did not receive the assigned intervention, and the effect of adherence was not estimated using an appropriate adherence-adjusted analysis.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: N (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** The BSI outcome was available at the final follow-up for 220/251 intervention participants and 228/268 control participants: 448/519 randomized participants (86.3%). As this is a continuous outcome and fewer than 90% had observed outcome data, the response is N.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The authors used mixed-effects random regression and intention-to-treat analysis, but did not report inverse probability weighting, a missing-data sensitivity analysis, or another method demonstrating that the BSI result was not biased by missing data. Intention-to-treat analysis alone does not establish protection from missing-data bias.

**3.3: PY (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** Reported reasons for loss to follow-up included death and ill health, as well as moving away and formal withdrawal. Death or ill health may reflect participants’ health status, but the article does not establish that these reasons were specifically related to true BSI psychiatric symptom severity.

**3.4: Y (Moderate Certainty, 79%)**  
**Reason:** Missing outcome data were more frequent in the intervention group than in the control group: 25/276 (9.1%) versus 11/279 (3.9%). Some losses were due to death or ill health, which could be related to participants’ health and potentially to psychiatric symptom severity. The imbalance in missingness between groups increases the likelihood of bias.

**Result of the domain: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** Outcome data at final follow-up were available for only 86.3% of randomized participants; no analysis demonstrated that missing data did not bias the result; missingness may have depended on health status; and attrition was different between groups.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** Psychiatric symptom severity was measured using the Brief Symptom Inventory, a standardized 53-item patient self-report instrument. The article describes it as having high concordance with clinician symptom assessment and as commonly used to assess clinical improvement and treatment outcome.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Both groups completed structured telephone interviews at the same assessment time points: baseline, postintervention, and 6-month follow-up. The same BSI measure and response scale were used for both groups.

**4.3: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The BSI was a patient-reported outcome. Participants knew whether they were attending WRAP sessions or were assigned to the wait-list control condition. Although interviewers were blinded, participant awareness is relevant for this self-reported outcome.

**4.4: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** The BSI measures subjective psychiatric symptoms reported by participants. Subjective patient-reported symptom ratings can be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**4.5: Y (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** The intervention was an unblinded peer-led self-management program, and the outcome was participants’ self-reported psychiatric symptoms. Knowledge of receiving WRAP could plausibly influence participants’ reports of symptom severity. The article additionally acknowledges that outcomes relied on participant self-report without clinician or objective-observer corroboration.

**Result of the domain: High risk (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** Although the BSI was an appropriate and comparably administered measure, it was a subjective patient-reported outcome, participants were aware of their intervention assignment, and knowledge of assignment was likely to influence self-reported psychiatric symptom severity.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 86%)**  
**Reason:** The article states that the study was registered as NCT01024569 and that all outcomes, hypotheses, and statistical analyses presented were prespecified in the funding proposal. The BSI outcome and mixed-effects random regression analysis described in the Methods were reported in the Results.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The article reported the intended BSI measures—Global Severity Index and Positive Symptom Total—at baseline, immediate postintervention, and 6-month follow-up. There is no clear evidence that only a selectively favorable BSI measurement or time point was reported.

**5.3: N (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods prespecified multivariable longitudinal mixed-effects random regression analysis, and the Results reported the BSI results from MIXREG analyses. There is no clear evidence that the reported BSI result was selected from multiple analyses on the basis of the findings.

**Result of the domain: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
**Reason:** The BSI outcome and planned mixed-effects regression analysis were described as prespecified and were reported consistently, with no clear evidence of selective choice among multiple BSI measurements or analyses.

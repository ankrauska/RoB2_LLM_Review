## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1**: NI (90% certainty)
The article states patients were "allocated at random" but does not describe the specific method used to generate the randomization sequence (no mention of computer-generated numbers, random number tables, block randomization, coin tossing, etc.). Only the concealment mechanism (sealed envelopes) is described, not the sequence generation method.

**1.2**: PY (80% certainty)
"Sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random" is mentioned. This indicates a concealment mechanism, but it is not explicitly stated that envelopes were opaque and sequentially numbered, so it cannot be rated as definitively "Y." Since sealed envelopes generally prevent foreknowledge of assignment, PY is appropriate.

**1.3**: Y (85% certainty)
Table 2 and the text show a statistically significant difference in baseline pulmonary artery pressure between groups (25±5 mm Hg in group 1 vs 19±4 mm Hg in group 2, p<0.05). This is a relevant baseline/prognostic variable (pulmonary artery pressure correlates with ET-1 in the discussion), suggesting a possible imbalance from randomization.

**Domain 1 Result: Some concerns (85% certainty)**
Because 1.2 = PY, 1.1 = NI, and 1.3 = Y, this matches the rule: 1.2 (Y/PY) + 1.1 (Y/NI/PY) + 1.3 (Y/PY) → Some concerns.

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** The article explicitly states that, for each participant, one eye was assigned to the aspheric ablation profile and the other to the non-aspheric profile “at random using Microsoft Excel’s random number function.” This is a computer-generated random allocation method.

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report allocation concealment. It does not describe central allocation, sealed/opaque sequentially numbered envelopes, identical intervention containers, or any other specific method used to conceal the allocation sequence before assignment.

**1.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** Baseline spherical equivalent refraction was similar between groups (ASA: −2.43 ± 1.53 D; TSA: −2.54 ± 1.47 D; p = 0.87). In addition, the paired-eye design ensured that each participant contributed one eye to each group. No important baseline imbalance suggesting a randomization problem was reported.

**Result of Domain 1: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** The allocation sequence was adequately generated, and no baseline imbalance suggesting a randomization problem was identified. However, allocation concealment was not reported.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** No trial registration number, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or prospectively finalized prespecified analysis plan was reported. Therefore, it cannot be determined whether the visual-acuity result was analyzed according to a plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section states that uncorrected distance visual acuity and corrected distance visual acuity were measured before and 3 months after surgery. The Results section reports visual-acuity efficacy at the specified 3-month postoperative assessment. There is no clear evidence that the reported visual-acuity result was selected from multiple unreported eligible visual-acuity measurements or time points.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 78%)**  
**Reason:** The article reports that refractive outcomes, including efficacy and safety, were analyzed according to the protocol described by Reinstein and Waring. There is no clear evidence that multiple eligible analyses of visual-acuity data were conducted and that only a favorable analysis was selectively reported.

**Result of Domain 5: Some concerns (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The article does not report a prospective registration, protocol, or prespecified analysis plan. Although there is no evidence of selective reporting from multiple measurements or analyses, the absence of a prespecified plan raises some concerns.

## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: Y (High Certainty, 99%)**  
**Reason:** Patients were randomized using “a minimisation algorithm incorporating a random element.” This is an acceptable random sequence-generation method.

**1.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Randomisation was administered through the University of Leeds Clinical Trials Research Unit’s automated 24-hour telephone randomisation system. This is central allocation and provides allocation concealment until assignment.

**1.3: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Baseline characteristics were reported as well balanced between groups. The randomized/intention-to-treat groups were also similar in size (MRI: 816; no MRI: 807), and Table 1 shows no important baseline imbalance.

**Result of Domain 1: Low risk (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** The randomization sequence incorporated a random element, allocation was centrally concealed through an automated telephone system, and baseline characteristics did not suggest a problem with randomization.

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was explicitly described as open-label. According to the specified assessment instructions, an open-label experiment is rated N for this question.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Carers and intervention providers, including surgeons, radiologists, and multidisciplinary teams, were aware of allocation because the MRI group underwent MRI and MRI findings could alter surgical management.

**2.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** No important imbalanced nonprotocol interventions that were prognostic for reoperation were reported. Additional investigations and management changes arising from MRI findings were part of the trial procedures.

**2.4: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** In the MRI group, 761 of 816 participants received MRI. The reported failures to receive or obtain an interpretable MRI result were fewer than 10% of the randomized MRI group. The no-MRI group largely received the assigned strategy.

**2.5: PN (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** There was some non-adherence: 53 MRI-group participants had no MRI interpreted and nine no-MRI participants received MRI. However, non-adherence was below 10% in each assigned group and was therefore not substantial.

**2.6: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
**Reason:** The primary analysis was by intention to treat. Under the specified criteria, intention-to-treat analysis is not an appropriate method for estimating the effect of adhering to the assigned intervention regimen.

**Result of Domain 2: Low risk (Moderate Certainty, 87%)**  
**Reason:** Although the trial was open-label and intervention providers knew allocation, important nonprotocol interventions were not reported as imbalanced, implementation failures and non-adherence were below 10%, and the analysis appropriately estimated the effect of assignment to MRI versus no MRI.

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 98%)**  
**Reason:** Reoperation rate was a binary outcome. The primary-endpoint analysis included 816 MRI-group participants and 807 no-MRI-group participants. There were 309 reoperation events, which was much greater than the small number of participants with unavailable primary-endpoint data or loss to follow-up.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 94%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not report an analysis method, such as inverse-probability weighting or sensitivity analysis, specifically demonstrating that missing outcome data did not bias the reoperation result. Intention-to-treat analysis does not itself correct missing-data bias.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 72%)**  
**Reason:** A small number of participants were lost to follow-up or had unavailable primary-endpoint data, but the article did not provide sufficient information to determine whether missingness depended on their true reoperation status.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 68%)**  
**Reason:** The article did not report evidence that the reasons for unavailable reoperation outcome data differed between groups in a manner related to reoperation status.

**Result of Domain 3: Low risk (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, and the number of observed reoperation events was much greater than the number of participants with missing or unavailable primary-endpoint data.

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
**Reason:** Reoperation rate was predefined as repeat wide local excision or mastectomy within 6 months of randomization, or a pathologically avoidable mastectomy at the initial operation. Reoperations, surgical procedures, and histopathological findings are reliably ascertainable from clinical and pathology records.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
**Reason:** The same primary-outcome definition and follow-up framework were applied to both groups. Clinical follow-up data were collected at 6 and 12 months after surgery and annually thereafter.

**4.3: Y (Moderate Certainty, 81%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was open-label, and clinicians involved in surgery and management were aware of whether MRI had been performed. In addition, the DMEC reviewed imaging and histopathology to identify pathologically avoidable mastectomies, which required knowledge of MRI findings.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
**Reason:** Reoperation is an objective outcome based on whether further surgery occurred. The component concerning avoidable initial mastectomy was based on predefined pathological and imaging criteria.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** There is no clear evidence that knowledge of allocation was likely to influence ascertainment of whether a repeat operation occurred, since this was determined from surgical records and predefined pathological criteria.

**Result of Domain 4: Low risk (High Certainty, 91%)**  
**Reason:** The outcome was objectively defined and measured comparably in both groups. Although relevant clinicians and reviewers were aware of allocation, there is no clear evidence that this knowledge was likely to influence the objective determination of reoperation.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: Y (Moderate Certainty, 84%)**  
**Reason:** The trial was registered (ISRCTN57474502). The Methods section prespecified reoperation rate as the primary clinical outcome and specified logistic-regression analysis adjusted for minimisation factors. The Results section reported the reoperation result using an odds ratio with a 95% CI and p value, consistent with the stated analysis.

**5.2: N (High Certainty, 92%)**  
**Reason:** Reoperation rate had a prespecified definition: repeat operation or further mastectomy within 6 months of randomization, or pathologically avoidable initial mastectomy. The reported result corresponds to this intended outcome measurement.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
**Reason:** The Methods section specified logistic regression adjusted for minimisation factors for comparison of reoperation rates, and the Results section reported the corresponding odds ratio, confidence interval, and p value. No evidence indicates selective reporting from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of Domain 5: Low Risk (Moderate Certainty, 87%)**  
**Reason:** The primary reoperation outcome, its definition, and the principal analysis were prespecified and reported consistently. There is no evidence that the reported reoperation result was selectively chosen from multiple outcome measurements or analyses.

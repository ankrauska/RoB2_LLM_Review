## Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: The article states that pharmacists were “randomized” to postal mail, email, or hybrid delivery methods. However, it does not report how the allocation sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random-number table, block randomization, or another random method).

**1.2: NI (High Certainty, 98%)**  
Reason: The article does not report allocation concealment procedures. It does not describe central allocation, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, or another method by which the allocation sequence was concealed before assignment.

**1.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Reason: The article reports demographic characteristics among survey respondents by delivery method, rather than baseline characteristics for all randomized pharmacists. Therefore, baseline comparability of the randomized groups cannot be adequately assessed. Differences among respondents may reflect differential response rather than a problem in randomization.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Allocation concealment was not reported (1.2 = NI), and there is no adequate baseline comparison of all randomized groups (1.3 = NI).

## Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Participants were necessarily aware of the delivery method they received. Pharmacists in the postal group received mailed questionnaires, those in the email group received electronic survey invitations, and those in the hybrid group received mailed postcards directing them to an online survey.

**2.2: Y (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Personnel delivering the interventions were aware of the assigned delivery method because they had to send postal questionnaires, emails, or hybrid postcards according to group allocation.

**2.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: The study describes protocol-specified reminders and contacts for each delivery group, but does not provide sufficient information about whether important nonprotocol interventions occurred or whether such interventions were balanced between groups.

**2.4: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Reason: A total of 233 of 7,200 intended questionnaires were undeliverable (3.2%), which is below 10%. The intervention delivery procedures were implemented for most participants. Non-return of the survey is the outcome of interest and is not considered a failure to implement the assigned delivery intervention.

**2.5: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The article does not report adherence, crossover, switching between delivery methods, or other non-adherence to the assigned intervention regimen.

**2.6: NI (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The article reports chi-square tests for response rates but does not report an analysis designed to estimate the effect of adhering to the assigned delivery method, such as instrumental-variable analysis or inverse-probability weighting. It also does not explicitly report an intention-to-treat, per-protocol, or as-treated analysis.

**Result of the domain: High risk (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
Reason: Participants and intervention providers were aware of delivery-method assignment (2.1 = Y; 2.2 = Y). Information on nonprotocol interventions was unavailable (2.3 = NI), adherence information was unavailable (2.5 = NI), and no appropriate analysis of adherence to the intervention was reported (2.6 = NI).

## Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

**3.1: Y (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Response rate was a binary outcome defined as return of a completed questionnaire with no missing questionnaire items. The study reports the number of delivered questionnaires (6,967) and the number of completed questionnaires (710). Thus, response status was known for the delivered questionnaires; non-returned questionnaires were classified as nonresponses rather than missing outcome data.

**3.2: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The study did not use methods to correct potential bias caused by missing outcome data, such as inverse-probability weighting, and did not conduct sensitivity analyses addressing missing outcome data or nonresponse bias. The authors explicitly noted the absence of nonresponse and missing-data analyses.

**3.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: The article discusses possible reasons for survey nonresponse, including topic interest, sensitive questions, and preferences for electronic media, but does not provide participant-level information establishing whether missingness depended on the true response outcome.

**3.4: NI (Moderate Certainty, 80%)**  
Reason: The article does not provide analyses of characteristics of nonresponders or incomplete responders that would allow assessment of whether missingness was likely related to the true outcome value.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: The response outcome was available for essentially all delivered questionnaires because each questionnaire could be classified as a complete response or nonresponse (3.1 = Y).

## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1: N (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: Response rate was predefined as the percentage of eligible respondents returning completed questionnaires with no missing questionnaire items. This is an objective and appropriate method for measuring survey response.

**4.2: N (High Certainty, 93%)**  
Reason: Response rate was measured using the same criterion across groups: return of a completed questionnaire with no missing data. The timing and reminder procedures differed according to the planned delivery-method interventions, but the outcome definition was consistent across groups.

**4.3: NI (Moderate Certainty, 82%)**  
Reason: The article does not explicitly state whether personnel recording returned questionnaires were blinded to the assigned delivery method. However, the outcome was based on administrative records of questionnaire return and completeness.

**4.4: N (High Certainty, 96%)**  
Reason: Response rate is an objective outcome based on whether a completed questionnaire was received. It is not a subjective patient-reported or assessor-rated outcome.

**4.5: N (High Certainty, 95%)**  
Reason: There is no clear evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment was likely to influence recording of whether a completed questionnaire was returned.

**Result of the domain: Low risk (High Certainty, 93%)**  
Reason: The response-rate outcome was objectively measured using the same predefined criteria across intervention groups. Although assessor blinding was not reported, knowledge of delivery assignment was unlikely to influence ascertainment of returned completed questionnaires.

## Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result

**5.1: NI (High Certainty, 97%)**  
Reason: No trial registration number, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or prospectively finalized analysis plan was reported. Therefore, it cannot be determined whether the reported response-rate analysis followed a prespecified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

**5.2: N (Moderate Certainty, 88%)**  
Reason: Response rate was defined consistently in the Methods section as the percentage of eligible respondents returning completed questionnaires with no missing data. The Results section reports response rates according to this definition. There is no clear evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible response-rate definitions or time points.

**5.3: N (Moderate Certainty, 85%)**  
Reason: The Methods section prespecified chi-square tests for comparisons of response rates. The Results section reports response-rate comparisons consistent with this approach. There is no clear evidence that only a selectively chosen analysis was reported from multiple eligible analyses.

**Result of the domain: Some concerns (High Certainty, 94%)**  
Reason: The study was not registered and did not provide a prespecified analysis plan (5.1 = NI). Although there is no clear evidence of selective reporting from multiple outcome measurements or analyses (5.2 = N; 5.3 = N), the absence of a prespecified plan raises some concerns.

---

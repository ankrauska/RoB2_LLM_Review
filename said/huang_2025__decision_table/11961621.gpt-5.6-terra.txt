## Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**: **N** (High Certainty, 97%)  
**Reason**: Length of hospital stay was measured in days from surgery to hospital discharge. This is a reliable and appropriate measure of hospital utilization.

**4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**: **N** (Moderate Certainty, 85%)  
**Reason**: Hospital stay was measured in days for both groups within the same hospital setting. No different measurement methods, assessment schedules, or thresholds between groups were reported.

**4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**: **Y** (High Certainty, 95%)  
**Reason**: The discharge decision was influenced by the patient’s reported readiness for discharge (“Each patient decided for herself when she was ready to be discharged”). Participants were aware of their surgical assignment.

**4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: **Y** (High Certainty, 94%)  
**Reason**: Although the number of hospital days is recorded objectively, discharge timing depended on the patient’s perceived readiness. Because participants knew whether they received LAVH or AH, knowledge of treatment could influence their decision regarding readiness for discharge.

**4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**: **Y** (Moderate Certainty, 80%)  
**Reason**: Participants were not blinded, and the hospital discharge decision was based on the participant’s own perception of readiness. The different surgical procedures and participants’ expectations could plausibly influence discharge timing.

**Result of Domain 4**: **High risk** (Moderate Certainty, 86%)  
**Reason**: Participants were aware of the assigned intervention, and patient-determined readiness for discharge could be influenced by awareness of the surgical procedure.

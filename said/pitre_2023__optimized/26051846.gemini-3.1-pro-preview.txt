Based on the RoB 2.0 tool guidance and the provided documents, here is the risk of bias assessment for the trial regarding the outcome **pain VAS scale post-treatment**:

1. **bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** The trial utilized an adequate method for sequence generation and allocation concealment, and there were no significant baseline imbalances suggesting problems with the randomization process.
    b. **Rationale:** The authors state, "Each participant was assigned to one treatment group, at random, using a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method, applied by a statistician. The statistician was not involved in data collection or analysis." Table 1 demonstrates that demographic characteristics were homogenous across all groups at baseline, with no significant differences. 

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** While participants and personnel delivering the intervention may not have been fully blinded to the specific physical therapy modalities, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context, and all randomized participants appear to have been analyzed appropriately.
    b. **Rationale:** The study is described as a "single-blind experimental clinical trial." The physiotherapists applying the treatments were likely unblinded, and participants could likely differentiate between treatments (e.g., ultrasound wand vs. iontophoresis electrical current). However, the protocol was strictly standardized (20 sessions for all groups), and there is no indication of non-protocol interventions or dropouts that would bias the intention-to-treat effect.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** Outcome data appear to be available for all randomized participants.
    b. **Rationale:** The study randomized 99 participants (32 in ultrasound, 33 in phonophoresis, 34 in iontophoresis). There is no mention of dropouts, withdrawals, or loss to follow-up in the results section, and the sample size calculation and reported tables suggest complete data for the analyzed participants.

4. **measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
    a. **Judgement:** The outcome is self-reported (VAS pain scale), and participants were likely aware of the intervention they received, which could influence their assessment. 
    b. **Rationale:** Although the outcome assessors (rehabilitation doctor) were blinded ("All visits were attended to by the same blinded rehabilitation doctor"), the VAS is a participant-reported outcome. Because it is difficult to effectively blind participants to the different physical therapy modalities (ultrasound vs. iontophoresis), participants were likely aware of their assigned intervention. Knowledge of the intervention received can potentially influence subjective self-reported outcomes like pain.

5. **selective reporting: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** The reported results match the pre-specified outcomes listed in the trial registry, and data were analyzed as intended.
    b. **Rationale:** The trial registry (NCT01748188) clearly lists "Pain measure by the visual analogic scale pain score" as the primary outcome. The published manuscript reports the findings for this exact outcome at the pre-specified time points (post-treatment and 1 month post-treatment) without indication of selective reporting or data manipulation.

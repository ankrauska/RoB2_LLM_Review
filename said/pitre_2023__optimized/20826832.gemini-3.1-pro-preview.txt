Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance and the provided trial report, here is the risk of bias assessment for the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect) for the outcome of compliance with mammogram screening:

1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
The trial used a random component for sequence generation and the allocation sequence appears to have been adequately concealed since randomization was done by an external coordination center.
b. Rationale: The authors state: "by using a computerized random number generator program, 500 women were randomly selected to participate in the study... A complete data base with 500 eligible women interviewed at baseline was sent to the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center in the US (i.e. the coordination center) to proceed with a random allocation to one of the two intervention arms or the control arm. A stratified randomization method blocking by age (50 –59; 60 – 70) was used to allocate women to each group using a computerized random generator program." Baseline characteristics were balanced.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
While participants and personnel were aware of the assigned interventions (blinding was not possible), there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context that would have affected the outcome. An intention-to-treat analysis was performed.
b. Rationale: The authors state: "Blinding participants to their intervention group was not possible in this study." and "An intention to treat analysis was conducted using electronic clinical records... compliance was estimated using the baseline denominators of each group." There is no indication of unbalanced non-protocol interventions that arose because of the trial context.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
Outcome data (electronic records) were available for all or nearly all randomized participants, as the main outcome was analyzed using intention-to-treat denominators through electronic registries.
b. Rationale: "The main outcome of the study was compliance with mammogram screening... Mammography screening was also measured using electronic records... An intention to treat analysis was conducted using electronic clinical records. They were reviewed for all women and compliance was estimated using the baseline denominators of each group."

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
The outcome used for the main analysis (compliance with mammogram screening via electronic clinical records) is an objective measure (observer-reported outcome not involving judgement) and is unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
b. Rationale: "The main outcome of the study was compliance with mammogram screening... Mammography screening was also measured using electronic records. Two sources of electronic records were used. The first source was the electronic database containing the reports of all tests conducted during the study at the radiology unit affiliated with the university clinic. The second source was the electronic chart where the results of all tests are registered." and "Staff at the clinic and at the radiology unit where participants got their mammograms were blinded to the allocation group."

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
The primary outcome is clearly specified, measured in the expected way (compliance rate), and reported for all groups as planned. There is no evidence of selective reporting of results from multiple eligible measurements or analyses.
b. Rationale: The reported primary outcome matches the aims described in the methods: "The main outcome of the study was compliance with mammogram screening... We measured mammogram screening by self report at baseline and six months after randomization. Mammography screening was also measured using electronic records." Both are reported.

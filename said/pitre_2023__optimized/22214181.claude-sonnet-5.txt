# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0 Tool
## Outcome: List of Attributions Task (LAT) — NumberAttributionScore

---

### 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The study states that "a Microsoft Excel random number generator was used to randomize participants into the two conditions," satisfying the requirement for a truly random sequence generation process. However, there is no information provided about allocation concealment — i.e., whether the sequence was concealed from the researcher enrolling participants until after enrollment/assignment. Baseline differences appear compatible with chance (no significant group differences on age, ethnicity, sex, or baseline ISI, MFI, DBAS-16, BDI-II, or LAT scores). Per the RoB 2 algorithm, random sequence generation confirmed as "Yes" but no information on allocation concealment defaults to "Some concerns."

**b. Rationale:** 
- Sequence generation: "To determine which information session they would receive, a Microsoft Excel random number generator was used to randomize participants into the two conditions." (Yes/Probably yes)
- Allocation concealment: No description of concealment mechanisms (e.g., whether the investigator administering the intervention knew the upcoming allocation in advance, or whether allocation was managed by an independent party). This is "No information."
- Baseline imbalance: "The FI and control groups did not differ significantly with respect to age... or ethnicity... There were equal numbers of men and women in each group. No significant pre-intervention group differences were found on levels of insomnia, fatigue, depressed mood, or dysfunctional beliefs about sleep. There were also no significant group differences on the LAT outcome measures at baseline." This supports "No" to imbalance suggesting a problem.
- Per Table 4 mapping: Random=Y/PY, Concealment=NI, Imbalance=N/PN → "Some concerns."

---

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Participants and the study investigator/personnel delivering the intervention were not blinded (the investigator "read the information sheet alongside each participant individually," clearly aware of which condition was being delivered, and participants were aware of the content they received). Because outcome assessment on the LAT is a participant self-report/self-generated task completed immediately following an unblinded, distinctly different informational intervention, there is a substantial risk that the trial context (i.e., the content of the information sheet just read) directly and deliberately shaped participants' subsequent responses on the LAT — this is inherent to the design (this is essentially a knowledge/education manipulation check) and is acknowledged by the authors as a limitation (demand characteristics/priming). This deviation is not balanced between groups (by design, the FI group received fatigue-cause information while the control group received sleep-stage information) and was likely to affect the outcome (the explicit purpose of the intervention). Because this is inherent to the intervention rather than an aberrant deviation, and the same influence applies symmetrically to both arms as intended by design, this could be argued as an intrinsic feature of an information-based intervention rather than a "bias" in the classic sense; however, per RoB2 rules, awareness of assigned condition combined with likely differential, unbalanced influence on the outcome by trial context defaults to "High."

**b. Rationale:**
- Participant awareness: Participants clearly knew which information sheet they received/read, since "the study investigator read the information sheet alongside each participant individually." This is inherently an unblinded, unblindable psychoeducational intervention. (Yes)
- Personnel awareness: The study investigator was clearly aware of and directly delivered the differential information to each participant. (Yes)
- Deviations arising from trial context: The very act of reading fatigue-cause information immediately before completing the LAT plausibly directly shapes what participants list, rank, and rate as causes of their fatigue — a deviation from what they might "naturally" attribute outside the trial context. The authors themselves raise this: "it is also possible that demand characteristics or priming played a role in FI participants' post-intervention responses on the LAT... the information-based intervention may have influenced participants to respond in a certain manner." (Yes/Probably yes)
- Affecting the outcome: Yes — this is the explicit hypothesized mechanism of the intervention.
- Balanced between groups: No — the informational content differed qualitatively and by design between groups (fatigue causes vs. generic sleep information), so any "priming" effect would be systematically different, not balanced, between arms.
- Per Table 6: Awareness=Y/PY, deviations=Y/PY, affects outcome=Y/PY, not balanced=N/PN → "High" risk of bias for Part 1, leading to a "High" domain-level judgement irrespective of Part 2 (analysis was ITT-like, as all 88 randomized/analyzed participants were included; no missing-data-driven analysis concerns here in Part 2).

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Outcome data (LAT) were available for all participants included in the final analytic sample (N=88), and the exclusions described occurred prior to and independent of the intervention/outcome (i.e., due to age ineligibility or failure to complete measures in an analyzable format), not due to post-randomization dropout related to the outcome.

**b. Rationale:** "Five participants' data were not included in the analyses. Two participants were younger than 18 years and thus their data was not used, one participant did not complete the demographic information, and two participants did not complete the outcome measures in a way that would allow us to obtain a total score. After excluding these participants' data, the final sample consisted of 88 participants." These exclusions (2 due to age ineligibility, 1 missing demographics, 2 with unscorable data) are unlikely to be related to the true value of the LAT outcome or associated with intervention group in a differential way, and represent a very small proportion of the original sample. There is no indication of differential attrition between FI and control groups, and the study appears to have collected the LAT from all remaining 88 (44 per group) participants at both pre- and post-intervention timepoints within a single 1-hour session, minimizing opportunity for missing data.

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The LAT is a self-generated, self-report outcome (participant is the outcome assessor) that cannot be blinded, since participants are aware of which intervention they received. The measurement method itself was appropriate for the underlying construct (participant-generated attributions), and there was no evidence that ascertainment procedures differed structurally between groups (both groups completed the identical LAT form pre- and post-intervention within the same session, coded by a blinded coder). However, because participants (the outcome assessors, in effect) were aware of their assigned intervention, and the outcome (self-generated attributions for fatigue) could very plausibly be influenced by the content of the information they had just read, this raises "some concerns" regarding whether assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment. This influence is arguably the intended/expected mechanism of the study rather than unintended bias, but per the RoB2 framework, this ambiguity is best captured as "Some concerns" rather than "High," since the coding of responses (sleep- vs non-sleep-related) was done by a rater blind to condition and hypotheses, mitigating differential measurement/classification error at the coding stage.

**b. Rationale:**
- Appropriateness of method: The LAT was "developed for the purposes of this study" to capture attribution content, ranking, and importance — reasonably appropriate for the construct being measured, though unvalidated. (Probably no to "inappropriate")
- Comparability of ascertainment: "First, all participants completed the baseline measures... and then received information either about reasons for fatigue (FI condition) or about generic sleep (control condition)... after which all of the participants once again filled out the LAT." Both groups underwent an identical procedure/format. (Probably no to "differed between groups" for the measurement procedure)
- Outcome assessor awareness: The primary "assessor" generating the raw responses is the participant, who is unblinded by necessity of an information-based intervention: "Fatigue information participants were significantly more likely to consider non-sleep-related attributions..." (Yes to awareness)
- Could assessment be influenced: "It is also possible that demand characteristics or priming played a role in FI participants' post-intervention responses on the LAT." (Yes)
- Likely influenced: The authors argue against pure priming being the sole explanation, noting the control group (also primed, but toward sleep) showed no significant change: "the results clearly show that there were no significant pre-post differences in the control group; thus, it appears unlikely that priming would have significantly affected the attributional change in the FI group." This argument, combined with independent blinded coding of responses ("coded into either sleep-related or non-sleep-related attribution categories by a laboratory volunteer... who was blind to the study hypotheses"), supports a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "High."

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The trial registration record and study report were compared, and the reported LAT outcomes (NumberAttributionScore, FrequencyScore, ImportanceScore) correspond to the pre-specified outcome measures and analyses described in the methods with no evidence of selective reporting from multiple candidate measurements or analyses for this outcome domain.

**b. Rationale:** All three pre-specified LAT-derived measures (proportion of sleep-related attributions, frequency/rank score, and importance/proportion score) are reported fully in the Results section, each with corresponding descriptive statistics, F-values, and p-values, matching the a priori hypotheses stated in the introduction: "the number of generated sleep-related attributions for feeling tired during the day, relative to non-sleep-related attributions, in the list of attributions task, would decrease for those in the FI condition only." There is no indication of alternative scoring methods, alternative time points, or additional statistical models (e.g., adjusted analyses) being computed but not reported. The single pre-specified analytic approach (mixed within-between ANOVA testing group × time interaction) was applied consistently and reported for all three LAT-derived outcomes without evident selection based on results.

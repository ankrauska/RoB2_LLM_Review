**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is insufficient information provided in this specific publication to confirm the methods of sequence generation and allocation concealment.
b. Rationale: The text states that "427 patients with stage I high-intermediate-risk EC were randomly allocated to EBRT or VBT" and references the main PORTEC-2 trial report. While large multicenter trials typically use robust centralized randomization, the lack of explicit details on random sequence generation and allocation concealment in this specific document leads to a judgement of "some concerns."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Although the trial was open-label, deviations from the intended intervention reflecting the trial context are unlikely to have impacted the effect of assignment to the intervention. 
b. Rationale: Blinding is not feasible when comparing external beam radiation therapy to vaginal brachytherapy. However, there is no evidence that the trial context led to non-protocol interventions that would have biased the ITT effect. The study successfully compares the assigned strategies. 

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is a notable proportion of missing data (attrition), and missingness may be related to the health status of the patients.
b. Rationale: The authors report that the response rate at 7 years was 77% (205 of 265 eligible), and at 10 years was 67% (80 of 119). Furthermore, they note that "responders at 7 years were slightly younger and had fewer comorbidities compared with the whole PORTEC-2 cohort." Because older patients or those with worse global health (more comorbidities) were less likely to respond, missingness in the HRQL outcome could depend on its true value, raising some concerns for this domain.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Although the outcome is self-reported and participants were unblinded, the assessment occurred 7 to 10 years after the intervention, making it unlikely that the reporting of global health was influenced by knowledge of the assigned treatment.
b. Rationale: The outcome is a patient-reported measure (EORTC QLQ-C30). Participants were aware of whether they received EBRT or VBT. However, the RoB 2.0 guidance indicates that for assessments taking place long after randomization (in this case, 7 and 10 years post-treatment), patients' recall and reporting of general global health are unlikely to be significantly influenced by the knowledge of the initial intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The reported outcomes align with standard pre-specified HRQL modules.
b. Rationale: The study reports on the EORTC QLQ-C30 global health and functioning scales as well as relevant subscales without evidence of selective reporting based on the significance or direction of the results. The outcomes presented match standard EORTC scoring guidelines.

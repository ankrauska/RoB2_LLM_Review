Based on the RoB 2.0 tool, here is the risk of bias assessment for the trial for the outcome **attack number (relapse rate)**, considering the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (Intention-to-Treat):

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The trial used a random component for sequence generation, and baseline characteristics appear balanced. Although the exact mechanism of allocation concealment is minimally described, the use of separate physicians for allocation and identical packaging suggests allocation was likely concealed.
b. Rationale: The authors state, "A computer-based blocking method (four blocks) was used for the randomization... Separate physicians were assigned to randomly allocate patients into two groups... The patients were unaware of their group assignment and all medications were given in similar packages." Table 1 shows no significant differences in baseline characteristics.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants, carers, and personnel delivering the interventions were blinded to the assigned groups. Analyses were performed using intention-to-treat principles.
b. Rationale: The trial was double-blinded. The report states, "The patients were unaware of their group assignment and all medications were given in similar packages... Four neurologists were asked to assess the patients’ eligibility... and perform the follow-up examinations. They were blinded to the patients’ assignment to treatment group." Furthermore, "The analyses were performed according to intention-to-treat methods".

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is some missing data as a proportion of randomized patients did not complete the study or were excluded from the final primary analysis, which could potentially bias the results depending on the true outcomes of those dropouts. 
b. Rationale: Of the 85 randomized patients, 14 withdrew from the study. The authors state, "Five of these refused to cooperate without any specific reason in the first 2 months." Table 2 indicates the analysis for total relapses was performed on 76 patients (40 in the simvastatin group, 36 in the placebo group), meaning approximately 10% of the outcome data was missing. While they used LOCF for some analyses, the complete case data for the total study period is not available for all randomized participants. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The method of measuring relapses is standard and appropriate. The outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignments, making it unlikely that the assessments were influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
b. Rationale: The outcome (relapse) was pre-defined clinically: "any new or worsening neurological deficit in the absence of high body temperature that lasted for more than 24 h after 1-month stability was considered a relapse". Follow-up examinations and evaluations were performed by neurologists who "were blinded to the patients’ assignment to treatment group."

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The reported data were analyzed in accordance with the pre-specified primary outcome listed in the trial registry. There is no evidence of selective reporting from multiple measurements or multiple analyses.
b. Rationale: The trial registration (NCT00668343) lists "attack number" at "1 year" as the primary outcome. The published manuscript reports the total attack number after the 1-year follow-up, aligning exactly with the pre-specified intentions. Multiple analyses (such as controlling for baseline covariates) were performed, but all results were reported transparently and were consistent in direction.

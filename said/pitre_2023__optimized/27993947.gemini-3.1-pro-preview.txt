**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, allocation was adequately concealed, and there were no baseline imbalances suggesting a problem with the randomization process.
b. Rationale: The study states, "Randomization was performed by means of a computer-generated random sequence with stratification for age, gender, and positive family history for CHD using the Pocock and Simon method." Baseline characteristics (Table 1) were well balanced between the groups, with no statistically significant differences, indicating successful randomization.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Although participants and personnel were not blinded, there is no evidence that this led to deviations from the intended interventions that were inconsistent with the trial protocol. An appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was performed.
b. Rationale: The intervention involved the disclosure of risk using a decision aid, which precludes blinding. However, there were no reported non-protocol interventions that arose due to the trial context. The study analyzed participants according to their randomized groups: "Analyses were performed with randomization groups as the independent variables."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data for the shared decision-making score were available for almost all randomized participants.
b. Rationale: Out of the 207 participants randomized, 206 completed the SDM-Q survey (99.5% completion rate). This small amount of missing data could not have biased the result in any meaningful way.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: The outcome is participant-reported, and participants were aware of the intervention they received. There are some concerns that the assessment of the outcome could have been influenced by this knowledge.
b. Rationale: The primary measure for this outcome was the SDM Questionnaire (SDM-Q), a self-reported survey completed by participants who were aware of whether they received the genetic risk information or not. As this is a subjective assessment, knowledge of the assigned intervention could potentially influence how participants perceived and rated the shared decision-making process. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The data were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified plan, and there is no evidence of selective reporting of multiple outcome measurements or analyses.
b. Rationale: The trial was registered (NCT01936675) and its protocol was previously published. The study reported the results for both the patient-reported SDM-Q scores and the independent quantitative video analysis using the OPTION5 scale, consistent with their stated methods. There is no indication that the reported results were selected based on their statistical significance or direction.

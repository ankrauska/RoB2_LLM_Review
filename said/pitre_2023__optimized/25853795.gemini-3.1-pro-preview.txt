1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: The randomization sequence was adequately generated and concealed. There are no baseline imbalances that suggest a problem with the randomization process.
    b. Rationale: The study authors state that subjects were allocated using a "computer-generated random sequence" and that "The appliance type was placed in a sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelope that was opened after the patient was accepted onto the trial. The generator of the randomization did not participate in patient allocation." 

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: Although participants and clinicians were not blinded, there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose due to the trial context. While analysis was done on a "per protocol basis" excluding 3 patients, the number of exclusions is too small to have a substantial impact on the result.
    b. Rationale: The authors state, "While it was not possible to blind the clinician or patient to the type of bracket system being used..." However, only 3 out of 138 patients (2.2%) were excluded ("one subject who refused fixed appliance treatment following allocation and two subjects who failed to complete treatment were omitted from the analysis"). The potential impact of failing to analyze these 3 participants in their randomized groups is not substantial enough to cause bias.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for nearly all participants randomized.
    b. Rationale: The trial enrolled 138 subjects, and "135 subjects (97.8%) completed treatment." Missing outcome data for only 2.2% of the randomized population is sufficiently small that it is highly unlikely to bias the estimated effect for the duration of treatment.

4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns
    a. Judgement: The outcome assessors (the clinicians deciding when to debond the appliances) were aware of the intervention received, and the decision of when to terminate treatment involves clinical judgment that could potentially be influenced by this knowledge.
    b. Rationale: The outcome is "total duration of treatment (months) from fixed appliance placement to debond." The decision to debond is made by the clinician. The authors report that "it was not possible to blind the clinician or patient to the type of bracket system being used". Because evaluating whether teeth are adequately aligned to complete treatment requires some subjective clinical judgment, this assessment could theoretically be influenced by the clinician's knowledge of the bracket system being evaluated, raising some concerns. 

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: The reported results align with the pre-specified outcomes in the methods section. There is no evidence of selective reporting of multiple measurements or analyses.
    b. Rationale: The study's methods section explicitly pre-specifies the outcomes to be measured: "total duration of treatment (months)... total number of appointments... and number of bracket bond failures". The results section reports exactly on these measures without switching definitions, scales, or selectively reporting subsets of the data based on statistical significance.

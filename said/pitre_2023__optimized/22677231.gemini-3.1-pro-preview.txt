**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias. 
b. **Rationale:** Participants were randomized via an automated online system after entering their email addresses, which ensures the allocation sequence was concealed. The trial report explicitly states that "Randomization was successful: significant differences emerged neither for any of the background nor psychopathological variables," indicating no evidence of baseline imbalances that would suggest a problem with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
b. **Rationale:** While participants were inevitably aware of their assignment (receiving the online program vs. wait-list), there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context. Furthermore, the trialists used an appropriate Intention-to-Treat (ITT) analysis using multiple imputation to estimate the effect of assignment to the intervention. 

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.
b. **Rationale:** The completion rate was 81.9% overall (85.7% in the wait-list group and 78.1% in the Deprexis group). While the trialists appropriately used multiple imputation to account for missing data and reported that non-completers were largely indistinguishable from completers at baseline, the attrition rate is substantial enough that missingness could potentially depend on the true value of the outcome (e.g., participants dropping out due to lack of symptom improvement).

**4. measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias**
a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias.
b. **Rationale:** The outcome (Beck Depression Inventory) is a participant-reported subjective measure of depression. Since participants were not blinded to their intervention assignment (active online treatment vs. wait-list), their knowledge of the intervention received is highly likely to have influenced their assessment of their own symptoms, primarily due to expectancy effects commonly seen in wait-list controlled trials.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
b. **Rationale:** The Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) was pre-specified in the trial registry (NCT01401296) as the primary outcome. The results were reported in accordance with this pre-specified plan, and both per-protocol and intention-to-treat analyses were transparently provided. There is no evidence that the reported results were selected from multiple outcome measurements or analyses based on their statistical significance.

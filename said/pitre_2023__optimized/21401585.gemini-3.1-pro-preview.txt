Based on the RoB 2.0 tool, here is the risk of bias assessment for the specified outcome (quality of movement of the impaired upper limb by MUUL):

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The trial used an adequate randomized sequence generation and allocation concealment process. Baseline differences were minimized by matching, and remaining differences do not suggest a problem with the randomization process.
b. Rationale: The authors state: "Children were eligible for inclusion if they had: ... After matching and concealed random allocation within pairs, there were no differences between groups on demographic or baseline measures... Once matched, children were randomized to pairs using a computer generated list of random numbers and concealed envelopes opened by non-study personnel." 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were likely aware of the intervention received due to its nature (constraint glove vs. bimanual training). However, there is no indication that this lack of blinding led to deviations from intended interventions that arose because of the trial context and affected the outcomes. The analysis was conducted on an intention-to-treat basis.
b. Rationale: The intervention involved wearing a glove ("participants in the CIMT group wore a tailor-made glove on their unimpaired limb..."), which cannot easily be blinded. The authors specify: "Analyses were on an intention-to-treat basis according to CONSORT guidelines... Compliance was 100% for CIMT, and 97% with BIM training." Since there were no major protocol deviations tied to the trial context and appropriate analysis was used, the risk is low for the effect of assignment to intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Data for the outcome were available for nearly all participants randomized, and the dropout rate was very low. 
b. Rationale: "Sixty-four children (32 matched pairs) were randomly assigned to treatment and 62 children received their allocated intervention. There was one dropout on day two of the camp from the BIM training group, because of pre-existing emotional and behavioural difficulties." Outcome data were available for the vast majority of randomized participants, making missing data unlikely to bias the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The method of measuring the outcome (MUUL) is appropriate. The outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment, mitigating the risk that knowledge of the intervention influenced the assessment.
b. Rationale: The authors report: "The primary outcome measures were videotaped and scored in random order by trained occupational therapists masked to group allocation." Furthermore, the MUUL was noted to be "the best measure of unimanual capacity... with good evidence of validity and reliability."

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: There is no evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from among multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on the direction or significance of the results. 
b. Rationale: The trial explicitly pre-specified MUUL as the primary outcome measure for unimanual capacity and analyzed it at specified time points (3 weeks and 26 weeks) as per their planned methodology. Outcomes are reported fully as intended without signs of selective omission.

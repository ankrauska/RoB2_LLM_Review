**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, adequately concealed, and baseline differences do not suggest a problem with the randomization process given the extremely small sample size.
b. Rationale: The study states, "An offsite investigator prepared the concealed randomization using a computer generated random number sequence (1:1). Consecutively numbered opaque envelopes containing group allocation were collected from the offsite investigator by the tRNS intervener after initial assessment." 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were blinded, and there is no evidence of deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. 
b. Rationale: The study was a "triple blind study. Study personnel involved in assessment and training, along with all participants were blinded to group allocation for the duration of the study." All four participants completed all training sessions, meaning there were no deviations affecting the outcome. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all participants.
b. Rationale: The study reports data for all four randomized participants at all time points (pre, post, and follow-up) as shown in Table 3. "All four participants completed the 12 training sessions over 4-weeks, with no training sessions missed."

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The method of measuring the outcome was appropriate, and outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention received. 
b. Rationale: The Motor Assessment Scale (MAS6) is an appropriate and standardized measure. The trial report confirms that "Study personnel involved in assessment and training, along with all participants were blinded to group allocation for the duration of the study."

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is a discrepancy between the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registry and the primary outcome reported in the published manuscript. 
b. Rationale: The trial registry (ACTRN12614000952640) lists the primary outcome as a "Composite score of Motor Assessment Scale upper limb items 6-8". However, in the published article, the authors state, "The primary clinical outcome measure was Motor Assessment Scale item 6 (Upper Arm Function, MAS6)", reporting the other components as secondary outcomes. This change from a composite score to a single item raises some concerns regarding the selection of the reported result.

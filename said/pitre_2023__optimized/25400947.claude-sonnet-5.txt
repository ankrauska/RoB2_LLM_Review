# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Frequency of Delivery after 48 Hours

**Trial:** Celecoxib vs. Magnesium Sulfate for suppression of preterm labor
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The trial states participants were "divided into two groups by simple random sampling," but no details are provided about the actual method used to generate the allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random number tables). There is also no description of any allocation concealment mechanism (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation). The text states: "all patients were divided in two groups by simple random sampling" — this is a bare statement of randomization without elaboration on sequence generation or concealment methods, consistent with "No information" responses to signalling questions 1.1 and 1.2.

Regarding baseline imbalance: groups appear well balanced for age (P=0.194), parity (P=0.99), and history of preterm labor (P=1), which is reassuring. However, there was a highly significant difference in baseline cervical effacement between groups (35.93% vs 42%, P≤0.0001), a key prognostic factor for preterm delivery. While the authors attribute this to chance, a difference this extreme (P≤0.0001) is very unlikely to occur by chance alone and raises concern about whether randomization was successfully implemented (signalling question 1.3). This baseline imbalance in a key prognostic factor, combined with lack of information on sequence generation/concealment, is sufficient to raise "Some concerns" per the algorithm (no information on concealment AND imbalance suggests a problem → "Some concerns" per Table 4), rather than "High risk" because sequence generation is not confirmed to be non-random.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** This was explicitly a "single blinded randomized clinical trial." Given the different routes of administration (IV MgSO4 with associated monitoring for toxicity, deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate vs. oral celecoxib capsules), it is not plausible that participants, and certainly not carers/personnel administering the interventions, were blinded to allocation: "the patients were observed for signs of MgSO4 toxicity by control of the urine output, deep tendon reflex, and respiratory rate," versus celecoxib patients "checked for nausea and vomiting and skin rush and headache." This confirms lack of blinding of personnel/participants (signalling questions 2.1/2.2 = Yes).

There is no explicit statement that deviations from intended intervention (e.g., co-interventions, differential care) arose specifically because of the trial context and affected the outcome differentially between groups. The paper does not report any non-protocol interventions or contamination. However, given the open nature of drug administration and monitoring differences, and no information provided on whether these differences influenced the outcome or were balanced, this raises "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

For analysis: this appears to be an intention-to-treat-style analysis (600 randomized, apparently all analyzed), which is appropriate. This supports not escalating to "High risk."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The outcome (delivery before/after 48 hours) appears to have been available for all 600 randomized participants: 300 in each group per Table 3, and Table 1 reports full frequencies (240+60=300 for MgSO4; 227+30=257... 

Note: Table 1 numbers for celecoxib (227+30=257) do not sum to 300, suggesting a minor reporting inconsistency; however, this appears to be a data entry/reporting error rather than evidence of missing outcome data, since the text and other tables (Table 2, Table 3) confirm n=300 per group throughout. There is no mention of loss to follow-up, withdrawal, or exclusion after randomization for this outcome domain. Because outcome data (a binary event ascertainable in essentially all participants by the nature of delivery timing) were available for all or nearly all randomized participants, and there is no reported differential dropout, risk of bias for missing outcome data is low.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The outcome, "delivery before or after 48 hours," is an objective, observer-reported outcome not involving judgement (analogous to a factual event, akin to all-cause mortality) — it is simply a matter of recording whether delivery occurred before or after a fixed 48-hour time point from treatment initiation. This type of outcome is not susceptible to influence by knowledge of intervention assignment, even in an unblinded/single-blinded study, because there is no subjective interpretation required to determine if delivery occurred within 48 hours. The method of measuring the outcome (recording time of delivery) is appropriate and not likely to differ in ascertainment between groups, as both groups were monitored with the same protocol ("Conditions of the uterine contractions were checked every 2 hours in 20-minute periods"). Therefore, despite lack of blinding of outcome assessors, this outcome is not likely to be biased by that lack of blinding.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** No trial registration or pre-specified statistical analysis plan was identified/available for this study (per the associated trial registration document, no clear pre-specified protocol with dated analysis intentions was located confirming outcomes and analyses were specified prior to unblinded data availability). The outcome "delivery after 48 hours" is reported alongside multiple other outcomes (duration of drug use/arrest of contraction, dilatation, effacement, gestational age at delivery, lack of response to treatment) without clear indication in a registered protocol of the primary/pre-specified outcome and its intended analysis method. Without access to a prospectively registered protocol or SAP with a date-stamp preceding outcome data collection, it is not possible to confirm the result was reported free from selection based on the data. This leads to a default answer of "No information" for signalling question 5.1, and in the absence of clear evidence excluding selective reporting for signalling questions 5.2/5.3, the appropriate judgement is "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk" or "High risk."

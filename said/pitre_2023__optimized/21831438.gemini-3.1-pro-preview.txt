**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The trial used a random component for sequence generation, but there is no information on allocation concealment, and there was a statistically significant baseline imbalance in age.
* **Rationale:** The trial report states that patients were "randomized in a 1:1 ratio by using the mixed block randomization (4 and 6 blocks) method," which indicates a random sequence. However, there is no mention of how the allocation sequence was concealed. Furthermore, the report notes a baseline imbalance: "the mean age in the candesartan-HCT groups (51.0 [10.2] years) was higher than that in the candesartan monotherapy group (47.8 [10.7] years; P = 0.02)." The lack of information on concealment combined with this imbalance leads to some concerns. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Although the trial was open-label, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended interventions arose due to the trial context, and an appropriate analysis was used for the effect of assignment to intervention.
* **Rationale:** The trial registration indicates this was an "Open-label" study, meaning participants and personnel were aware of the interventions. However, there is no indication that non-protocol interventions were administered because of this awareness. Furthermore, the researchers used an intention-to-treat (ITT) approach for efficacy evaluations, consisting of patients who took at least one dose and had at least one post-randomization measurement, which is acceptable for estimating the effect of assignment. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Data for the outcome were available for nearly all randomized participants.
* **Rationale:** Out of 233 randomized patients (117 in the combination group, 116 in the monotherapy group), the ITT analysis at 4 weeks included 111 and 113 patients, respectively. This means only about 5% and 2.5% of the data were missing, which is small enough that it is unlikely to have a substantial impact on the estimated effect.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The method of measuring blood pressure was appropriate and comparable across groups, and the use of an automated device minimizes the risk of the assessor influencing the outcome.
* **Rationale:** Blood pressure was measured using a standardized approach: "BPs were measured in each patient’s arm by the same investigator at each center with a fully automated device produced by Omron." Although investigators were not blinded to the treatment assignment, the use of a fully automated device for an objective physiological measurement makes it unlikely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The reported result corresponds to the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registration, and the analysis was conducted as planned.
* **Rationale:** The clinical trial registration (NCT00621153) explicitly lists "Changes in Mean Sitting DBP From Baseline After 4 Weeks of Therapy" as a primary outcome measure. The published manuscript reports this exact outcome without evidence of selection from multiple outcome measurements or multiple analyses.

Based on the provided trial publication and the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of **treatment retention** considering the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline:

1. bias due to randomization: some concerns
       a. Judgement: There is insufficient information regarding the concealment of the allocation sequence.
       b. Rationale: The trial report states that patients were "randomly assigned" and "stratified on the following variables... They were then randomized into one of the three study conditions". However, there is no description of the method used to generate the random sequence or whether the allocation sequence was concealed (e.g., central randomization, sealed opaque envelopes) until patients were enrolled.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were aware of the intervention, but this is unlikely to cause deviations that would bias the retention outcome, and all participants were appropriately analyzed for retention.
       b. Rationale: Due to the nature of the interventions (routine clinic care vs. office-based or clinic-based monthly maintenance), blinding of participants and personnel was not possible. However, for the objective outcome of treatment retention, there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context. Retention rates were calculated based on the number of patients initially randomized to each group ("Of the 73 randomized patients who began the study...").

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: Outcome data for treatment retention were available for all randomized participants.
       b. Rationale: For the outcome of treatment retention, the status of all 73 randomized participants was known at 6 months. The authors report the exact numerators and denominators for all groups: "90% (n=19/21) of the routine treatment, 89% (n=24/27) of the clinic-based MMM and 92% (n=23/25) of the physician office-based MMM finished 6-months of randomized care." Therefore, there is no missing data for this specific outcome.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The outcome is objective and its measurement is not influenced by knowledge of the assigned intervention.
       b. Rationale: Treatment retention (whether a patient remained in the program at 6 months) is an objective, observer-reported outcome not involving subjective judgement. Even though assessors and patients were unblinded, it is highly unlikely that the assessment of whether a patient was retained in treatment was biased by knowledge of the intervention received.

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The reported outcome represents a standard, straightforward measurement and is unlikely to have been selected from multiple analyses.
       b. Rationale: Although a pre-specified protocol is not explicitly referenced in the text, treatment retention at specific time points (e.g., 6 months) is a universal and standard objective outcome in addiction trials. There is no evidence to suggest that the authors selected this specific retention outcome from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on the statistical significance of the results.

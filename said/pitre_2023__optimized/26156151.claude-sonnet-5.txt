## Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0): CTO-IVUS Trial — Outcome: Cardiac Death (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Randomization was centrally administered using an interactive web-based response system and stratified by participating center, which is a robust method of allocation concealment that prevents foreknowledge of assignment by enrolling investigators: *"Using an interactive web-based response system, study participants were randomly assigned and stratified by participating center."* There is no explicit statement of the random component used to generate the underlying sequence, but the use of a centralized, web-based system in a multicenter trial of this scale supports a judgement of adequate concealment and likely genuine randomization (Probably Yes/Probably Yes). Baseline clinical, angiographic, and CTO-lesion characteristics were well balanced between groups with no clinically or statistically important imbalances (Table 1; e.g., age 61.0±11.1 vs 61.4±10.1, P=0.74; diabetes 34.8% vs 33.8%, P=0.83; number of diseased vessels P=0.12), consistent with successful randomization. The only significant differences noted (high-pressure poststent dilation, postprocedural MLD) are procedural/downstream variables related to the intervention itself, not baseline prognostic imbalances suggesting a flawed randomization process.

---

### 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Participants, and to some extent personnel, could not be fully blinded to IVUS vs. angiography guidance (masking listed as "single," investigator only, per registry), since the use of an IVUS catheter is generally apparent during the procedure. However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention inconsistent with the protocol occurred because of the trial context (e.g., no crossover between IVUS-guided and angiography-guided strategies, no non-protocol interventions reported). The observed procedural differences (more frequent high-pressure poststent ballooning and larger postprocedural MLD in the IVUS group) reflect the intended mechanism of IVUS guidance itself (i.e., IVUS-directed stent optimization), rather than an unintended deviation from protocol: *"high-pressure poststent balloon dilation was more frequently performed with a greater maximal poststent balloon pressure in the IVUS-guided group than the angiography-guided group."* This is a consequence of assignment to the IVUS-guided strategy, not a deviation from it. The authors state: *"The intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses,"* and nearly all randomized patients were included in the analysis, indicating an appropriate ITT approach for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention.

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Outcome data (cardiac death) were available for essentially all randomized participants: *"200 patients of IVUS-guided group (99.5%) and 201 patients (100%) in the angiography-guided group finished 12-month clinical follow-up."* Only 1 of 402 participants (0.25%) had incomplete follow-up, a proportion far too small to have any meaningful impact on the estimate for a rare event such as cardiac death (which occurred in only 2 patients total). There is no indication that this single instance of loss to follow-up was related to the outcome.

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The outcome (cardiac death) was assessed identically in both groups using a prospectively defined and standardized definition: *"All deaths were considered cardiac death unless an unequivocal noncardiac cause could be established."* Crucially, ascertainment was performed by an independent, blinded committee: *"All clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical event committee blinded to randomization."* Because outcome assessors were blinded to intervention assignment, and the outcome (death, with cause adjudicated under a standardized rule) is not readily susceptible to differential misclassification based on knowledge of treatment assignment, risk of bias in measurement of this outcome is low.

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Cardiac death was prospectively designated as the primary outcome in both the trial registry and the publication. The trial registry (NCT01563952) prespecifies: *"event rate of cardiac death within 12 months"* as the primary outcome, and the manuscript explicitly states: *"The prespecified primary end point was the occurrence of cardiac death."* The result for cardiac death is fully reported (0% vs 1.0%, P=0.16) in a manner consistent with the single prespecified definition and time point (12 months), with no evidence of selection among multiple alternative outcome measurements, definitions, time points, or analytic approaches. There is no discrepancy between the registered primary outcome and the outcome as reported and analyzed in the publication.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias Judgement for "Occurrence of Cardiac Death": **Low risk of bias**

All five domains were judged to be at low risk of bias for this specific outcome and result (effect of assignment to intervention), based on adequate randomization and concealment, absence of problematic deviations from intended intervention, virtually complete outcome data, blinded and standardized outcome adjudication, and prespecified, fully and consistently reported outcome definition and analysis.

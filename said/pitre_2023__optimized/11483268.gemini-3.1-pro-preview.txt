Based on the RoB 2.0 tool guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome **anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) 1 month post-vaccination** for the specified trial:

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
*   **Judgement:** The trial is described as randomized, but no specific details are provided regarding the method of sequence generation or allocation concealment. However, baseline characteristics appear well-balanced between the groups, suggesting no obvious issues with the randomization process.
*   **Rationale:** The report states, "127 adults primed 6 months previously with Avaxim™were randomised to receive either Vaqta™or Avaxim™as a booster" (Section 1). It also notes this was an "open study" (Section 2.1). There is no description of how the random sequence was generated or how allocation was concealed. Table 1 shows that demographic characteristics (age, sex, weight, height) were similar between the two groups, indicating no apparent baseline imbalances that would suggest a failure of randomization.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**
*   **Judgement:** The study was open-label, meaning participants and personnel were aware of the assigned interventions. While the outcome (antibody titre) is unlikely to be affected by this lack of blinding, the primary immunogenicity analysis was a per-protocol analysis rather than an intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis. However, the number of exclusions was small, limiting the potential impact on the result.
*   **Rationale:** The study design was a "single centre, controlled, randomised, open study" (Section 2.1). For the analysis, "Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed... The results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported" (Section 2.6). Six out of 127 randomized subjects were excluded from the per-protocol analysis. Because an ITT analysis was not the one reported for the final data, but the overall exclusion rate was very low (<5%), there are some concerns, but it is unlikely to have a substantial impact on the estimated effect.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** Data for the outcome were available for nearly all participants randomized, and the small amount of missing data is unlikely to bias the result.
*   **Rationale:** Out of 127 randomized subjects, 121 were included in the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis (Table 2). Only 6 subjects (4.7%) were excluded (one for previous booster, one for travel, four for missing serology results). The proportion of missing outcome data is sufficiently small that it would not make an important difference to the estimated intervention effect.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** The outcome is an objective laboratory measurement (antibody titre). Although the trial was open-label, the objective nature of the blood test means the assessment is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
*   **Rationale:** The outcome (anti-HAV antibody levels) was measured using a standard, quantitative laboratory assay (modified HAVAB™ assay) (Section 2.5). Because this is an objective "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" (typically automated/standardized in a lab), the lack of blinding of participants and trial personnel does not pose a risk of bias for the measurement of this specific outcome.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
*   **Judgement:** There is no publicly available trial registration or pre-specified statistical analysis plan referenced to confirm the analysis intentions before unblinded data were available. However, the reported outcome (GMT at 1 month) is a standard and expected primary endpoint for vaccine booster trials. 
*   **Rationale:** The study clearly defines the primary evaluation criterion a priori in the methods: "The primary evaluation criterion was the comparison of the anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) for both vaccines 1 month post-vaccination" (Section 2.4). While there is no date-stamped protocol to definitively rule out selective reporting, the reported data directly match the stated primary endpoint, and there is no indication that multiple outcome measurements or analyses were generated and selectively reported based on statistical significance.

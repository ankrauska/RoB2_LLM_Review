# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Anti-HAV Antibody GMT 1 Month Post-Vaccination

**Preliminary note:** This is an open-label (unblinded), individually-randomized parallel-group trial. The outcome assessed is anti-HAV antibody GMT 1 month post-vaccination, comparing Vaqta™ vs Avaxim™ as a booster. The assessment below considers the effect of assignment to intervention (ITT-type effect), per instructions.

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to lack of information on sequence generation and allocation concealment methods.

**b. Rationale:** The article states subjects "were randomised to receive a booster dose of either Avaxim™ or Vaqta™" but provides no description of the method used to generate the allocation sequence or how (or whether) allocation was concealed from investigators/recruiters prior to assignment. There is no mention of a random number generator, sealed envelopes, central allocation, or any other concealment mechanism: "Subjects with a documented history of having received one dose of Avaxim™ were randomised to receive a booster dose of either Avaxim™ or Vaqta™ 24 to 28 weeks later." No information is given to answer signalling questions 1.1 or 1.2 with confidence.

Baseline characteristics (Table 1) appear balanced (age, sex, weight, height similar between groups), and pre-booster GMTs, while numerically different (496 vs 325 mIU/mL), were not statistically significant (p=0.171) and are plausibly compatible with chance given small sample size and known variability in antibody titres. Thus there is no strong evidence of a problem with the randomization process itself from baseline data. However, the complete absence of reported methodological detail regarding sequence generation and allocation concealment (in a small, single-center, open trial) means we cannot rule out selection bias in enrolment/allocation. This combination of no information on concealment and no information on random sequence generation, together with baseline imbalance in GMT (though not "significant"), leads to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, due to lack of blinding (open-label design) combined with absence of clear information on whether this influenced deviations from intended intervention, although the objective/biological nature of the outcome limits concern.

**b. Rationale:** This was explicitly "a single centre, controlled, randomised, open study." Participants and personnel (those administering the vaccine and assessing reactogenicity) were aware of the assigned intervention: "This was an open study and was therefore a sub-optimal setting for comparing reactogenicity" (acknowledged by the authors themselves).

For the specific outcome under review (anti-HAV GMT, a laboratory-measured immunological outcome performed by a central, presumably blinded, laboratory), lack of blinding of participants/personnel is less likely to lead to deviations from intended intervention that would bias this particular objective outcome, since antibody titre is not influenced by participant or clinician expectation, and there is no indication of non-protocol interventions, co-interventions, or differential behaviors that would affect antibody response. However, no explicit statement is provided confirming the absence of such deviations, so "No information" applies to signalling question 2.3.

Regarding analysis: "Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed... The results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported." The per-protocol analysis excluded 6 subjects (out of 127) for protocol violations or missing serology — a relatively small number, and the authors state ITT and per-protocol results were similar, suggesting the exclusion is unlikely to have substantially affected the result. This is a minor departure from a strict ITT analysis for assignment effect purposes, contributing to the "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk" judgement, since only the per-protocol result is reported.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Of 127 randomized and vaccinated subjects, only 6 were excluded from the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis, and of these, only 4 were due to missing serological data ("four subjects did not have pre- and post-vaccination serological results available"); the other two exclusions were due to protocol violations unrelated to missingness (prior HAV booster receipt; travel to India). This yields analysed samples of 59/64 (92%) in the Vaqta™ group and 62/63 (98%) in the Avaxim™ group — a very high proportion of available outcome data in both arms, with similar levels of missingness between groups. The authors also note: "The results of both [ITT and per-protocol] analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported," providing reassurance that missing data did not materially bias the result. Given the very small numbers missing and their comparability across groups, missingness is very unlikely to be related to the true value of the antibody titre, and there is no indication that missingness depended on outcome value.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The outcome (anti-HAV antibody GMT) was measured using a standardized, objective laboratory assay performed centrally by an independent laboratory: "Serological evaluations were performed by Merck Research Laboratories (Pennsylvania, USA). The modified HAVAB™ assay was used to detect antibody (IgM and IgG) to hepatitis A virus... a standard curve was constructed using dilutions of a WHO standard serum immune globulin. Test samples were compared with the standard curve and the level of anti-HAV in mIU/mL was determined."

This is an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" — a laboratory assay with a quantitative, standardized readout. The method of measurement was identical and appropriate for both intervention groups (no evidence that measurement or ascertainment differed between groups), and there is no plausible mechanism by which knowledge of intervention assignment (which was not concealed from participants or clinicians, given the open-label design) could have influenced the laboratory determination of antibody titre, since sample analysis was conducted independently at a central lab using an objective, validated assay. Thus, even though the trial was open-label, this specific outcome is not susceptible to bias from lack of blinding.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, due to lack of an available pre-specified analysis/statistical plan for verification, despite the primary outcome being clearly pre-specified and reported.

**b. Rationale:** The methods section explicitly pre-specifies the primary evaluation criterion: "The primary evaluation criterion was the comparison of the anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) for both vaccines 1 month post-vaccination." This outcome is reported fully and clearly in Table 2 with GMTs, 95% CIs, and appropriate non-inferiority comparison (upper limit of 97.5% CI of GMT ratio), consistent with the pre-specified sample size/statistical hypothesis described in the statistical methods section. There is no evidence of selective reporting of alternative timepoints, scales, or subsets of data for this specific outcome — GMT at 1 month appears to have been the single intended measurement and analysis, and it is reported in full.

However, no trial protocol or statistical analysis plan was identified/available (the trial registration document, per the task materials, could be examined for further corroboration), and there is no clear confirmation that the analysis was finalized prior to unblinding of outcome data. Since only limited independent verification (e.g., pre-registration date-stamped documentation of the specific analysis plan) is available from the description in the text, some uncertainty remains as to whether alternative analyses (e.g., different timepoints, adjusted vs. unadjusted comparisons) could have been considered and selectively not reported. There is no clear evidence of problem, but insufficient information to be fully confident of "Low risk," yielding "Some concerns" per the standard algorithm for cases with incomplete information on multiple eligible analyses/outcomes.

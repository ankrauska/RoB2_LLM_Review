**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The study used a randomized split-mouth design. The allocation sequence was generated using a block randomization method. Allocation concealment was maintained until the patient was entered into the trial. The text states: "Randomization for allocation of either of the two etch and primer systems to the upper right quadrant was undertaken using a block randomization method... The operators were unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial." Because it is a split-mouth design, patient-level baseline characteristics are perfectly balanced between the two intervention groups. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The operators could not be blinded to the interventions due to the different application methods ("it was not possible to blind the operators to the type of system being used"). However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended interventions occurred due to the trial context, and the interventions were applied exactly as specified per quadrant. Furthermore, an appropriate intention-to-treat approach was effectively utilized for the analysis of the bonded brackets. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** Data were available for nearly all participants. The study states: "All patients were observed for the entire 12-month period apart from one 13-year-old male who moved to another city after 7 months of observation, during which time no bracket failures occurred. His data was still included in the analysis". Therefore, missing outcome data is negligible and unlikely to bias the result.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** Although the operators/assessors were aware of the intervention received, the outcome (bracket failure/debonding) is a highly objective clinical event. It is very unlikely that the assessment of whether a bracket remained attached or failed was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The study reported the failure and survival rates of the brackets as intended for the 12-month period. There is no indication that the reported results were selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple analyses based on the statistical significance or direction of the findings. All randomized brackets were accounted for in the primary analysis.

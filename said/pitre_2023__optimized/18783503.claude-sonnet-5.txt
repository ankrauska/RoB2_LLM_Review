# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0

**Trial:** Effect of add-on gabapentin on opioid withdrawal symptoms in opium-dependent patients
**Outcome assessed:** Severity of subjective withdrawal symptoms (Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale)
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to lack of reported detail on allocation concealment, despite adequate randomization sequence generation and no evidence of baseline imbalance problems.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Sequence generation:** The article states, "Subjects were assigned randomly to receive adjunctive treatment with either gabapentin or placebo under double-blind conditions, using a randomization automated system on a one-to-one basis." This supports a judgement of "Yes"/"Probably yes" for random sequence generation.
- **Allocation concealment:** No details are provided on how allocation was concealed from those enrolling participants. The statement that "the randomization list was held securely, and released only after study completion" suggests some effort at concealment, but no specific mechanism (e.g., central randomization, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes) is described. This warrants a "No information" response.
- **Baseline imbalance:** "There were no significant differences in mean age or dosage of methadone and other drugs doses... between the treatment and controls groups at baseline." This suggests no evidence of problems with randomization (Answer: No).
- Applying the algorithm: Random sequence = Yes/Probably yes; Allocation concealment = No information; Baseline imbalance = No → This combination maps to **"Some concerns"** per Table 4 guidance (NI on concealment + No baseline imbalance issue = Some concerns).

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as the trial was double-blind with no evidence of problematic deviations from intended intervention arising from the trial context, and an appropriate (ITT-like) analysis approach was used.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Blinding of participants and personnel:** The trial is described as "double-blind," and "Gabapentin and placebo were dispensed in identical appearing capsules. Patients who were randomized to the placebo group took the same number of capsules as those who were assigned to the treatment group." This supports "No" to awareness of assigned intervention by participants, carers, and those delivering the intervention.
- **Deviations from trial context:** No evidence is presented that trial-context-related deviations (e.g., recruitment/engagement effects) influenced the outcome. The protocol explicitly permitted concomitant symptomatic medication (clonidine, oxazepam, trazodone) as part of the standard protocol, and these were balanced between groups ("There were no statistically significant differences between the mean dose of methadone or other drugs such as clonidine, oxazepam or trazodone used for symptomatic medication in either group").
- **Analysis approach:** All 20 gabapentin participants were retained ("All patients in the treatment group completed opiate detoxification"). In the placebo group, 3 participants were excluded post-randomization due to non-compliance/self-medication with illicit/OTC drugs (1 at day 14, 2 at day 17). These are exclusions of eligible participants after randomization for reasons potentially related to the outcome (worse withdrawal symptoms leading to self-medication), which could affect the appropriateness of the analysis. However, the study appears to analyze available data at each time point (varying n's in Table 1, e.g., n=17-20), consistent with an mITT approach where missing data (rather than group-switching) is the primary issue—this is addressed under the missing data domain. Since there's no indication of switching between groups or an "as-treated"/naïve per-protocol analysis, and the exclusions reflect missing outcome data rather than misclassification of intervention received, risk here remains low for this domain.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to differential attrition between groups and the likely relationship between missingness and the true outcome value (worse withdrawal symptoms leading to withdrawal from study).

**b. Rationale:**
- **Completeness of outcome data:** Data were not available for all randomized participants at all time points. Table 1 shows sample sizes declining from n=20 (gabapentin) and n=20 (placebo) at baseline to n=20 and n=17 respectively by Day 18. This is a 15% dropout in the placebo arm versus 0% in the gabapentin arm.
- **Reasons for missing data:** "one patient in the placebo group was excluded at day 14 and two others were excluded at day 17 because of non-compliance with the scheduled treatment programme and self-medication with illicit and/or over-the-counter (OTC) drugs." The authors themselves speculate: "The non-compliance and self-medication in the placebo group may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms." This directly suggests that missingness in the outcome (withdrawal symptom severity) is related to its true value (i.e., participants with worse withdrawal symptoms were more likely to drop out), and this differential dropout was unbalanced between groups (0% vs 15%).
- Per the RoB 2 criteria, this combination (differing proportions of missing data between groups, plausible mechanism linking missingness to the true outcome value, no sensitivity analysis reported) indicates **"Some concerns"**, bordering on high risk—but since the amount of missing data was small (max 3/20 = 15% in one arm) and unlikely to fully explain or reverse the null result, "Some concerns" rather than "High risk" is the appropriate judgement.
- No sensitivity analyses or imputation methods were reported to address this missingness.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns because the outcome is participant-reported (subject to influence by knowledge of intervention received), and although blinding was maintained, gabapentin has recognizable side effects and effects (e.g., sedation) that could unblind participants and influence self-reported symptom severity.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Type of outcome measure:** The SOWS is a "16 symptoms whose intensity the patient rates on a scale of 0 (not at all) to 4 (extremely)" — this is a **participant-reported outcome**, for which the participant is the outcome assessor.
- **Blinding of outcome assessor:** The trial was double-blind, and capsules were "identical appearing." This supports low risk of unblinding under normal circumstances.
- **Potential for unblinding via side effects:** Gabapentin has recognizable side effects (e.g., sedation, dizziness) that are distinct from placebo and could potentially unblind participants, though the paper does not report any assessment of blinding success or unblinding due to side effects. No information is given about whether blinding integrity was checked or breached.
- Because participant-reported outcomes are inherently susceptible to influence by beliefs about intervention received once unblinding occurs, and there is no direct evidence in the study of maintained blinding integrity throughout (e.g., no formal blinding assessment reported), this raises "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," per the guidance that participant-reported outcomes default to at least "Some concerns" when knowledge of intervention could plausibly influence assessment, absent strong reassurance that blinding was preserved.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as the outcome measure (SOWS) and its time points appear to have been specified and analyzed comprehensively, and reporting corresponds to a single logical primary analysis with no indication of selective reporting from multiple eligible measures or analyses.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registration record confirms consistency: the outcome specified (severity of subjective withdrawal symptoms via SOWS) matches what is reported in the results.
- Table 1, Table 2, and Table 3 present the SOWS total score at all six pre-specified time points (baseline, day 3, 7, 10, 14, 18), and additionally break down results by all 16 individual symptoms, with no evidence of selective omission of any prespecified outcome measurements or subscales. The methods section states "The Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale (SOWS) was administered for evaluating the severity of the withdrawal symptoms at six time-points during the study," and all six time-points are reported.
- Both repeated-measures ANOVA and Mann-Whitney U-test results are reported, and both are consistent (non-significant), with no indication of cherry-picking a specific analysis on the basis of the results.
- No statistical analysis plan was identified as a separate source, but given full congruence between the stated methods and the results as reported (including reporting of a null finding, which itself argues against outcome-based selective reporting), this domain is judged as low risk.

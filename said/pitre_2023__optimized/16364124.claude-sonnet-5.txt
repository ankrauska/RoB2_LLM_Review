# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0)
## Outcome: Visual assessment for erythema and dryness (key symptoms of irritant contact dermatitis)
## Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns arise primarily due to insufficient reporting on allocation concealment, despite adequate description of sequence generation.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Sequence generation (adequate):** "Randomization was performed via randomly permuted blocks of fixed size using validated software RANCODE 3.6." This indicates a clearly random process → **Yes** to signalling question 1.1.
- **Allocation concealment (unclear):** The article states "Randomization was conducted according to a randomization scheme prepared at the Department of Statistics and Data Management at proDERM" and that "Blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database." However, this is a within-subject, side-to-side design assigning products to left/right cubital fossa of the same individual, and no explicit description is given of how the sequence was concealed from the study nurse who applied the products at each visit until the moment of application. No information on sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque containers or a centralized concealment mechanism is reported. This warrants **No information/Probably yes** on signalling question 1.2, but with genuine ambiguity.
- **Baseline imbalance:** No important baseline imbalances are reported; since this is a paired (within-subject) design, systemic confounding by prognostic factors between "groups" is inherently minimized. Answer: **No** to signalling question 1.3.
- Given adequate random sequence generation but incomplete information on concealment procedures, the domain-level judgement defaults to **Some concerns** (per Table 4: NI on concealment + No indication of imbalance problem → Some concerns).

---

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to lack of blinding of the treating study nurse (though outcome assessment was blinded), combined with an appropriate ITT analysis.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Blinding of participants:** The study is described as "double-blind," and formulations were "randomly assigned a letter A or B for blinding of the test formulations." Both hand rubs shared the same base alcohols (45% propan-2-ol, 30% propan-1-ol, 0.2% mecetronium etilsulfate), differing only in an emollient mixture (0.81% w/w). This makes it plausible that participants could not distinguish the products by sensation, supporting blinding. However, no formal test of blinding success is reported. Answer: **Probably no** to signalling question 2.1 (participants likely unaware).
- **Blinding of personnel administering the intervention:** "All applications were performed by a study nurse" using "coded test material." Given that only emollients differ in modest concentration (0.81%), and codes were sealed, it is plausible personnel were also blinded, though this is not explicitly confirmed. Answer: **Probably no** to signalling question 2.2.
- **Deviations from intended intervention arising from trial context:** There is no evidence of deviations arising because of trial context — e.g., no report of participants selectively avoiding/preferring one side, or personnel treating the two cubital fossae differently due to awareness of allocation. Three protocol deviations occurred (antiphlogistic intake in one participant; disinfection of the fossa cubitalis in two participants), but these do not appear related to the trial context or unblinding; they were treated as protocol violations for the per-protocol analysis. Answer: **No** to signalling question 2.3.
- **Appropriate analysis:** "Results are presented for the intention-to-treat (ITT) population... Missing values in 3 participants were replaced by the method of Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF)." This is consistent with an appropriate (ITT/modified ITT) analysis approach for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention. Answer: **Yes** to signalling question 2.6.
- Overall: Because both participants and personnel were probably (but not certainly) unaware of assignment, and there is no evidence that trial context led to deviations, Part 1 would default to **Low**; however, the "double-blind" claim lacks detail on how blinding of the study nurse applying different formulations was operationally maintained (e.g., whether coded bottles were indistinguishable in smell/texture), introducing some uncertainty. Combined with Part 2 being Low (appropriate ITT/LOCF analysis), the overall judgement is set at **Some concerns** to reflect this residual uncertainty about blinding integrity in application procedures.

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk, as nearly all outcome data were available, and missingness was handled by LOCF with minimal impact on the primary result.

**b. Rationale:**
- Of 35 randomized participants, only 3 were excluded during the study for protocol deviations, and "missing values in 3 participants were replaced by the method of Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF)" for the ITT analysis, ensuring all 35 randomized participants contributed data to the primary analysis.
- The reasons for missing data (intake of antiphlogistics in 1 participant, disinfection of fossa cubitalis in 2 participants) are unrelated to the true value of the outcome (erythema/dryness) itself and are not differential between "treatment arms" in a way likely to bias the within-subject comparison, since this is a paired left/right-arm design and both interventions were still assessed in the same individuals.
- The proportion of missing data (3/35 = 8.6%) is small, and results were reported for both ITT (n=35) and per-protocol (n=32) populations, with consistent direction and significance of effect (Table 1), suggesting the results are robust to the handling of missing data.
- Signalling question 3.1: **Probably yes** (nearly all data available, gaps handled via LOCF) → Low risk of bias.

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to reliance on a subjective visual assessment scale which is not implausible to be affected by unblinding of the outcome assessor if there were tell-tale differences between the visually or texturally distinguishable products.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Appropriateness of measurement method:** The outcome was assessed using a well-defined ordinal scale: "The following scale was used for erythema [modified from Fitzpatrick et al.]: 0 = none... 4 = fiery red with oedema" and similarly for dryness. This is an appropriate, validated approach commonly used in ROAT studies. Answer: **No** to signalling question 4.1.
- **Comparability of measurement across groups:** Both cubital fossae were assessed identically at the same visits by the same procedure. Answer: **No** to signalling question 4.2.
- **Blinding of outcome assessor:** "All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator" — a dermatologist distinct from the study nurse who applied the products. This is a strong statement supporting blinded outcome assessment. Answer: **Probably no** to signalling question 4.3 (assessor unaware of allocation).
- **Potential for influence:** This is an observer-reported outcome involving clinical judgement (visual scoring of erythema/dryness), which inherently has more room for subjectivity than an objective measure like mortality. Even with blinding, if there were any perceptible differences between formulations (e.g., subtle texture, residue, or scent differences due to the emollient mixture) that became apparent to the assessor or participant during examination, this could subconsciously influence scoring. The report does not describe any check on the success of blinding (e.g., asking the assessor to guess allocation) or confirm that the two coded preparations were indistinguishable by texture/appearance upon visual inspection of the skin (as opposed to olfactory/tactile blinding of the applying nurse).
- Given the assessor is reported as blinded, but this is a subjective clinical rating scale for which the assessor's blinding is not independently verified, and there is a theoretical risk that visible differences in skin residue/finish of the emollient-containing product could unblind assessment upon close visual inspection, the judgement is **Some concerns** rather than "Low," to reflect this uncertainty (signalling question 4.5: **Probably no**/uncertain, but not entirely excludable — insufficient information to confirm the blinding was fully successful in this dermatologic assessment context).

### 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as the pre-specified primary outcome (sum score for erythema and dryness) was reported in full, and results are directly consistent with the study's a priori design/sample size calculation.

**b. Rationale:**
- The primary outcome was clearly pre-specified: "The sum score of dryness and erythema over all assessment times served as the primary outcome parameter," and the sample size calculation was explicitly performed "prior to the study, based on the computation of one-sided 97.5% confidence intervals for ratio of means" for this specific outcome — indicating the analysis plan (non-inferiority test with prespecified margin of 1.2 and Type 1 error of 0.025) was determined in advance of unblinded data availability.
- The results reported (mean sum score, non-inferiority analysis via Fieller's method, and secondary Wilcoxon signed rank test) match exactly what was described in the methods section, with no indication of selective reporting of alternate scales, subscales, or time points. Full sub-scores for erythema and dryness separately, and secondary analyses (atopic vs. non-atopic, ITT vs. PP) are also reported in Table 1, consistent with the stated secondary analyses ("Secondary analyses included comparison of atopic and non-atopic panellists... and descriptive presentation of all safety and tolerability variables").
- No trial registry record was available for cross-checking (this trial predates mandatory prospective registration; conducted in 2004), and no discrepancy is evident between the methods and results sections of the published article itself. Given the explicit a priori statistical plan matching the reported results, answer **No** to signalling questions 5.2 and 5.3.

**Overall justification:** Per Table 1 of the RoB 2 guidance, a study with "Some concerns" in at least one domain but no domain at "High" risk of bias overall receives a rating of **Some concerns**. Here, three domains (randomization process, deviations from intended intervention, measurement of outcome) show some concerns, largely due to incomplete reporting of concealment and blinding-verification procedures rather than clear evidence of bias. None of these concerns, individually or in combination, provide strong evidence of a substantial biasing influence on the result — the trial's strengths (validated randomization software, LOCF-based ITT analysis, blinded independent dermatologist assessor, complete outcome reporting matching pre-specified plan) generally support a moderate degree of confidence in the reported result. The overall rating is therefore **Some concerns**.

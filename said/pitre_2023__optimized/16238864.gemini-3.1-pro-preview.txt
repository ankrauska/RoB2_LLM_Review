Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome **intentions to consume the RDIFV**:

1. **bias arising from the randomization process: some concerns**
    a. Judgement: Some concerns.
    b. Rationale: While sequence generation was adequate ("sequence of random integers generated by a true random number generator"), the allocation concealment method is insufficiently detailed to guarantee true concealment. The authors state, "intervention and control booklets were placed in a single pile in an order determined by a sequence of random integers. The booklets from this single pile were then distributed...". It is unclear if the booklets had identical, opaque covers; if they were visually distinguishable, the person distributing them could potentially predict the next assignment. Baseline characteristics were balanced, but the lack of explicit concealment details warrants some concerns.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
    a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
    b. Rationale: For the effect of assignment to the intervention (ITT effect), deviations are only relevant if they arise due to the trial context and affect the outcome. Because the outcome (intentions to consume the RDIFV) was measured *immediately* after the intervention within the same booklet ("After completing the control or intervention task, participants completed a second set of cognition measures..."), there was no time for participants to deviate from the intervention before the outcome was measured. 

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
    a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
    b. Rationale: The high attrition rate (33%) mentioned in the study pertains to the 1-week behavioural follow-up. The outcome in question (intentions) was measured immediately post-intervention. The study reports that 218 people completed the booklet, and the ANCOVA for post-intervention intention reports F(1, 215), indicating that 217 out of 218 randomized participants (99.5%) were included in the analysis for this specific outcome. Therefore, data were available for nearly all randomized participants.

4. **measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
    a. Judgement: Some concerns.
    b. Rationale: The outcome is a participant-reported measure (self-report scale), meaning the participant is the outcome assessor. Because participants were not blinded to the intervention (they had just read the persuasive leaflet or the control task), their self-assessment of intentions could potentially be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received (e.g., demand characteristics or social desirability bias immediately after reading a persuasive message). 

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**
    a. Judgement: Some concerns.
    b. Rationale: There is no clear evidence that the data were analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan or trial registration finalized before unblinded outcome data were available (which was common for trials conducted at this time). While the reporting of the 5-item intention scale and the ANCOVA controlling for baseline scores are standard practice, the absence of a verified pre-published protocol means selection bias cannot be definitively ruled out.

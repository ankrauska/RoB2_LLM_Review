1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns, because the report states that participants were “randomly selected” for the two wire sizes, but does not describe how the random sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed. Baseline comparability by randomized group is also not adequately reported.

   b. **Rationale:** The article states: “*The patients were randomly selected for insertion of the 0.014 or 0.016 inch wires.*” This is insufficient to establish that a genuinely random sequence (e.g., computer-generated or random-number table) was used. There is no description of allocation concealment, such as central randomization, pharmacy-controlled allocation, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes.  
   
   Group sizes were reasonably similar (56 versus 53), but the report does not provide an arm-specific baseline table for important characteristics such as age, sex, malocclusion severity, or initial dental crowding. Thus, there is no evidence of a major baseline imbalance, but insufficient information to confirm that the randomization process was adequately implemented. Under RoB 2, absence of information on allocation concealment, without evidence of baseline imbalance, leads to **some concerns**.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns for the effect of assignment to the intervention at baseline.

   b. **Rationale:** The intended interventions were insertion of either a 0.014-inch or 0.016-inch NiTi initial archwire. The report indicates that wires were ligated to all teeth and that no extra-oral appliances, palatal arches, or quad-helix appliances were used during the experimental period. This supports broadly consistent implementation of the allocated orthodontic intervention.
   
   However, there is no report that participants, orthodontists, or other care personnel were blinded to wire allocation. In particular, the operator delivering the intervention would probably have known the wire size. The report also provides no information on whether awareness of allocation resulted in trial-context deviations, such as differential advice, reassurance, analgesic recommendations, or other co-interventions that could influence perceived initial pain.
   
   The analysis appears to retain all randomized participants: “*The questionnaires were completed by all 109 patients*,” and the initial-pain counts account for all 56 participants in the 0.014-inch group and all 53 in the 0.016-inch group, including those reporting no pain. Thus, there is no indication that an as-treated or per-protocol analysis was used. Nevertheless, because of the absence of information about blinding and trial-context deviations, this domain has **some concerns**.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Outcome data for initial pain appear to be available for all randomized participants. The authors state: “*The questionnaires were completed by all 109 patients and returned at the following appointment.*”
   
   For the initial-pain outcome, Table 1 reports pain-onset categories for 50 of 56 participants allocated to the 0.014-inch wire and 47 of 53 allocated to the 0.016-inch wire. The remaining participants are explicitly reported as having no pain: “*No pain was reported by 10.7 per cent (six patients) in the 0.014 inch group and 11.4 per cent (six patients) in the 0.016 inch group.*” Thus, the initial-pain status is accounted for in all 109 randomized participants. There is no indication of differential missingness or exclusion from the analysis.

4. **measurement of the outcome: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** Initial pain was measured using a patient-completed questionnaire. The article states: “*Question 1 asked the time at which pain was first perceived after archwire insertion.*” Patient-reported pain is an appropriate method for measuring the experience of pain, and the same questionnaire appears to have been used in both groups.
   
   However, pain is a subjective participant-reported outcome, meaning that the participant is the outcome assessor. The report does not state that participants were blinded or masked to the allocated wire size, nor does it describe measures to prevent awareness of the intervention. Knowledge or expectations about the archwire could potentially influence reporting of the onset of pain. This is particularly relevant because the questionnaire was completed after archwire insertion and returned at a later appointment, rather than recording onset contemporaneously.
   
   There is no direct evidence that participants’ reports were influenced by their knowledge of wire size, and both groups received active, similar orthodontic interventions. Therefore, a high-risk judgement is not justified; nevertheless, the absence of information about participant blinding and the subjective nature of the outcome lead to **some concerns**.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The report prespecified in its stated aims that it would assess “*the time at which pain starts*,” and it reports the distribution of initial-pain onset by wire group in Table 1. Thus, the reported result is consistent with an outcome described in the article.
   
   However, no dated protocol, statistical analysis plan, or sufficiently detailed trial-registration information is available in the material provided to verify that the initial-pain result, its time categories, and its statistical analysis were specified before outcome data were available. The study measured numerous related pain outcomes at multiple time points, including presence of pain, VAS pain intensity, pain location, analgesic use, and effects on daily activities. This creates opportunities to select among outcome measurements and analyses.
   
   There is no clear evidence that the initial-pain result was selected because it was statistically significant or favourable to either wire size. However, in the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan or verifiable registration details, selective reporting cannot be excluded. Therefore, this domain is judged as **some concerns**.

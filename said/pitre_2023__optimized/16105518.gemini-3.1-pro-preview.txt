**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is insufficient information to determine how the randomization sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed.
b. Rationale: The authors state only that participants "were randomly assigned to three experimental groups," without providing any details on the method of sequence generation or allocation concealment. Without this information, the randomization process raises some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Although participants were aware of their assigned practice schedule, there is no evidence of deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. 
b. Rationale: Given the nature of the intervention (practice schedules), blinding of participants was not possible. However, there is no indication that non-protocol interventions occurred or that there were any deviations from the assigned practice schedules. Furthermore, all participants appear to have been analyzed in the groups to which they were randomized.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data appear to be available for all randomized participants.
b. Rationale: The study enrolled 28 medical students, and there is no mention of any attrition, loss to follow-up, or exclusions from the analysis. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: It is unclear whether the outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignments, and the outcome is subjective, meaning it could potentially be influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
b. Rationale: The outcome was evaluated using a checklist by "Two experienced orthopedic surgeons." The article does not state whether these evaluating surgeons were blinded to the group assignments of the participants. Since checklist scoring involves some degree of subjective judgment, unblinded assessment could introduce bias, raising some concerns.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is no evidence that the data were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded data were available. 
b. Rationale: A trial registration or pre-published protocol is not mentioned or available for comparison. While the study reports on the outcomes described in the methods (checklist scores, final product analysis, global ratings, drilling times), the absence of a pre-specified plan means bias in the selection of the reported result cannot be entirely ruled out.

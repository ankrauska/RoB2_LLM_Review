**Outcome assessed:** visual assessment of erythema and dryness (the prespecified cumulative irritation/“overall tolerability” sum score) for hand rub with emollients versus hand rub without emollients. The effect of interest is the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline.

1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The allocation sequence appears genuinely random: allocation of coded products to left and right cubital fossae was conducted “according to a randomization scheme prepared at the Department of Statistics and Data Management,” using “randomly permuted blocks of fixed size” and validated RANCODE software. This supports a random sequence.

   However, concealment of the left/right allocation sequence up to assignment is not described adequately. Although the formulations were coded A and B and “blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database,” this concerns concealment of product identity rather than explicitly demonstrating that the forthcoming side allocation was concealed from the personnel assigning products to body sites. Furthermore, no side-specific baseline skin-condition data are reported, so baseline imbalance cannot be evaluated directly. There is no explicit indication of an imbalance or manipulation, but the incomplete information regarding allocation concealment leads to some concerns rather than low risk.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Participants, the study nurse administering treatment, and the outcome assessor were effectively blinded to the identity of the formulations. The products were “randomly assigned a letter A or B for blinding,” and the “blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database.” All applications were delivered by a study nurse, with both formulations applied according to the same schedule and procedure: “All applications were performed by a study nurse.”

   There is no evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment led to differential co-interventions, differential implementation, or other deviations arising from the trial context. The three participants with protocol violations were retained in the ITT population rather than being omitted from the main analysis. The report states that results were presented for the “intention-to-treat (ITT) population,” comprising all 35 participants. This is appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment, although missing values were imputed; the implications of this are considered under missing outcome data.

3. **Missing outcome data: some concerns**

   e. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   f. **Rationale:** The primary ITT analysis included 35 participants, but “missing values in 3 participants were replaced by the method of Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF).” Thus, outcome data were not available without imputation for approximately 9% of participants. For this repeated, continuous visual-assessment outcome, this is not clearly “all or nearly all” participants under RoB 2.

   LOCF does not itself provide evidence that missing-data bias was avoided, particularly because skin erythema and dryness may change over time and the last observed value may not represent the unobserved later outcome. The report does not explain why these outcome data were missing, whether missingness differed by assigned formulation or treatment side, or provide sensitivity analyses using plausible alternative assumptions. The available information does not establish that missingness was likely related to the true outcome value, so high risk is not warranted; however, the unsupported LOCF imputation and limited reporting raise some concerns.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Erythema and dryness were measured using predefined visual scales and assessed at the same scheduled time points for both treatment sites. The report states that “the treated skin was assessed visually for erythema and dryness” and specifies detailed ordinal scoring systems for both outcomes. Thus, the measurement approach was appropriate and comparable between the two formulations.

   Importantly, “all evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator,” a dermatologist. Since erythema and dryness are observer-reported outcomes involving clinical judgement, assessor blinding is particularly important. The coded formulations and sealed blinding code support that the dermatologist was unaware of the intervention identity. There is no indication that outcome ascertainment differed systematically between the two cubital fossae or treatment conditions.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The publication identifies the cumulative score as the primary outcome: “The sum score of dryness and erythema over all assessment times served as the primary outcome parameter.” It also reports the main ITT comparison, including the numerical result for the combined score and its significance, as well as component outcomes in Table 1 and longitudinal figures.

   Nevertheless, there is no accessible date-stamped protocol, statistical analysis plan, or adequately detailed trial-registration record demonstrating that the precise outcome measurement, time points, combined-score definition, analysis method, and decision to switch from non-inferiority to superiority were finalized before unblinded data became available. The trial involved repeated measurements over numerous time points and permitted several potentially eligible analyses (erythema, dryness, their combined score, ITT and per-protocol populations, and non-inferiority and superiority analyses). Although the reported outcome is consistent with the outcome described as primary in the article, selective selection of a measurement or analysis cannot be excluded. Therefore, the appropriate RoB 2 judgement is some concerns.

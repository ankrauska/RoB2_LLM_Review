Based on the RoB 2.0 tool, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of patient acceptance/satisfaction:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation and allocation concealment, and there were no significant baseline imbalances suggesting a problem.
b. Rationale: The authors state, "Computer random sequence generation was done using block sizes of 6 and 4. Allocation concealment was achieved with sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes." Baseline characteristics (e.g., age, cephalometric measurements) were reported as similar between the two groups. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Although participants and personnel could not be blinded, there is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context, and all randomized participants were analyzed in their assigned groups. 
b. Rationale: The trial report notes, "Blinding of the clinicians and the patients to the intervention was impossible". However, there were no dropouts and all patients received the intended appliances. The authors conducted an intention-to-treat analysis: "The primary analysis was planned to be an intention-to-treat analysis to involve all patients who were to be randomly assigned; however, since there were no dropouts in the study, all the patients were included in the analysis."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all randomized participants.
b. Rationale: The authors state, "The 32 surveys were retrieved from the patients; there were no dropouts," meaning there was 100% retention and no missing questionnaire data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome is subjective (patient acceptance/satisfaction assessed via questionnaire), and the outcome assessors (the patients) were aware of the intervention they received. This awareness is highly likely to have influenced their assessment of the outcome.
b. Rationale: Patient acceptance is a participant-reported outcome. The authors acknowledge that "Blinding of the clinicians and the patients to the intervention was impossible". Because patients knew which appliance they were wearing (with or without mini-implants), this knowledge could consciously or subconsciously influence their responses to subjective questions about pain, comfort, and satisfaction, placing this domain at high risk of bias.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: The trial protocol was not published or registered before the trial began, making it impossible to confidently compare the reported outcomes and analyses with a pre-specified plan.
b. Rationale: The authors state, "Registration: This trial was not registered. Protocol: The protocol was not published before trial commencement." While all questions from the administered questionnaire appear to be reported, the lack of a date-stamped, pre-specified statistical analysis plan raises some concerns about whether the reported analyses were selected from multiple eligible options.

# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Interhemispheric Functional Connectivity

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns arise primarily due to insufficient information about allocation concealment, despite adequate randomization sequence generation and no evidence of problematic baseline imbalances.

**b. Rationale:** 
- Sequence generation: The trial states "Randomization was completed using blocks of four," which implies a random component was used (Y/PY). However, no explicit detail on the method of random number generation (e.g., computer-generated) is provided.
- Allocation concealment: There is **no information** provided about how allocation was concealed from investigators or participants until enrollment. The paper states "Both the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention," but this refers to blinding after allocation, not concealment of the sequence itself. No mention of sealed envelopes, central randomization, or other concealment mechanisms.
- Baseline imbalance: Table 1 shows no significant differences in demographic characteristics between groups ("There were no significant differences in demographic characteristics between groups"), suggesting differences are compatible with chance.

Per the algorithm (Table 4): Y/PY for sequence + NI for concealment + N/PN for imbalance → **Some concerns**.

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as blinding of participants and personnel was implemented and validated, minimizing the likelihood of deviations arising from trial context.

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants and outcome assessors were blinded: "Both the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention."
- Blinding was validated: "Subjects did not guess correctly the stimulation group to which they were assigned beyond chance (p = 0.679)," indicating successful blinding of participants.
- No mention of non-protocol interventions, co-interventions, or contamination between groups. Given the nature of the intervention (single-session brain stimulation in healthy volunteers), there is minimal opportunity for trial-context-driven deviations.
- Analysis: The study appears to include all randomized participants in the qEEG outcome analysis (38 subjects who completed the session), following ITT-consistent principles, with only one participant excluded for technical reasons unrelated to intervention/outcome relationship ("One subject in the Random Frequency group was removed from the analysis due to excessive artifacts in the EEG recording" — this appears to be a data quality issue rather than a deviation from intended intervention).

Since participants/personnel were blinded (Y to 2.1/2.2 answered as blinded = N for awareness) and there is no evidence of deviations due to trial context, and an appropriate ITT-consistent analysis was used → **Low risk**.

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as outcome data were available for nearly all randomized/enrolled participants, with only one exclusion for a documented, outcome-unrelated technical reason.

**b. Rationale:**
- Of 40 recruited, 38 completed the session (2 withdrew consent prior to any stimulation, an issue of enrollment rather than missing outcome data post-randomization).
- Of the 38 who completed stimulation, only 1 participant (in the Random Frequency group) was excluded from the qEEG coherence analysis: "One subject in the Random Frequency group was removed from the analysis due to excessive artifacts in the EEG recording."
- This reason (technical EEG artifact) is unrelated to the true value of the interhemispheric coherence outcome — it reflects an equipment/data quality issue, not informative missingness related to the outcome itself.
- This represents a very small proportion of missing data (1/38 ≈ 2.6%) unlikely to have made an important difference to the estimated effect.
- No differential loss to follow-up is reported between groups.

Per the algorithm: missingness could not plausibly depend on the true value of the outcome (technical artifact) → **Low risk**.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as the outcome (interhemispheric coherence via qEEG) was measured using an objective, automated method that was identical across all intervention groups, with no evidence that measurement could be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**b. Rationale:**
- Method appropriateness: Coherence was calculated using a standardized, validated approach — "Welch's averaged modified periodogram method" — applied uniformly to raw EEG data recorded with a "64-channel Electrical Geodesic Incorporated (EGI) EEG device." This is not an inappropriate method for measuring functional connectivity.
- Comparability across groups: EEG recording procedures ("recorded for a total of 12 min using two conditions: eyes open and eyes closed... completed before and after the experimental condition") were identical for all four groups, with no indication that measurement or ascertainment protocols differed by group.
- Outcome assessor: This is an automated, computed physiological measure (an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" in RoB2 terminology), extracted algorithmically from EEG signal data rather than through subjective rating. Additionally, "the investigator assessing the outcomes" was explicitly blinded to intervention assignment.
- Given the objective/automated nature of coherence calculation and blinding of assessors, knowledge of intervention assignment is very unlikely to have influenced the measured outcome.

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, as no pre-registered trial protocol or statistical analysis plan could be identified/confirmed as finalized prior to unblinded data access, and the study involved multiple outcome measures (multiple bands, sub-bands, and two electrode pairs) with some evidence of flexible/exploratory reporting.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registration document (doc_id: 4dab5cab-2b44-42f8-8559-2dd9073b18b5) should be checked for pre-specification of the primary connectivity outcome, specific bands/sub-bands of interest, and electrode pairs of interest. Based on the publication itself, the paper explicitly describes this as involving an **"exploratory analysis"**: "We conducted an exploratory analysis to detect changes in mean power for theta, alpha and beta, and interhemispheric coherence for alpha and theta and four different sub-bands."
- Multiple outcome measurements were assessed (2 main bands: alpha, theta; plus 4 sub-bands: low-alpha, high-alpha, low-beta, high-beta), and results are reported selectively with emphasis on the statistically significant findings (theta and low-alpha), while non-significant sub-band results (low-beta, high-beta) receive comparatively less interpretive emphasis, though they are reported.
- There is no clear evidence of a finalized, date-stamped pre-specified analysis plan describing exactly which coherence measures/electrode pairs constituted primary vs. secondary/exploratory outcomes prior to unblinding.
- However, all pre-specified bands and sub-bands do appear to be reported (not selectively suppressed), and results (both significant and non-significant) are presented in the text, which somewhat mitigates concern.

Given the explicit "exploratory" framing, multiple comparisons across bands/sub-bands, and lack of confirmed pre-specification/protocol registration for this specific outcome and analysis strategy → **Some concerns**.

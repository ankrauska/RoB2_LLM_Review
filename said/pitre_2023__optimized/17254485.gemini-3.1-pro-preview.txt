**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns
* **Rationale:** The trial authors state that "A random number table was produced through Microsoft Excel" to generate the allocation sequence, which is an adequate method. However, there is no information provided regarding whether the allocation sequence was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions. Although baseline characteristics appear well-balanced, the lack of information on allocation concealment raises some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** Due to the nature of the interventions (endoscopic procedures), participants and trial personnel could not be blinded. However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended interventions occurred because of the trial context. All patients received the treatments as assigned, and appropriate analyses were conducted estimating the effect of assignment to the intervention. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** Outcome data for emergency hemostasis were available for all randomized participants. The study randomized 172 patients (90 in the therapy group and 82 in the control group), and the rate of emergency hemostasis is explicitly reported for all 90 patients in the therapy group and all 82 patients in the control group. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** Although the outcome assessors (likely the endoscopists and clinicians) were aware of the intervention received, emergency hemostasis is a highly objective clinical outcome that relies on direct endoscopic visualization and vital signs. It is unlikely that the assessment of whether active bleeding was successfully stopped would be importantly influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns
* **Rationale:** There is no mention of a pre-published trial protocol, trial registration, or statistical analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded data were available. While emergency hemostasis is an expected and standard outcome for a trial on active variceal bleeding, the lack of pre-specified analysis intentions means it is impossible to rule out selective reporting with complete certainty, leading to some concerns.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation and allocation concealment. While there was a baseline imbalance in age, it appears to be compatible with chance and was adjusted for in the analysis, not suggesting a systematic problem with the randomization process.
* **Rationale:** The trial report states, "patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System... A block size of four patients was used within each stratum." This ensures adequate sequence generation and allocation concealment. Regarding baseline imbalances, the authors note, "The two treatment groups were similar... with the exception of an imbalance in the age distribution including significantly more patients older than 70 years in the elacytarabine arm." As this was a large multicenter trial with centralized randomization, this imbalance is likely due to chance, and the authors confirmed that adjusting for age did not impact the results ("Adjusted for age, the hazard ratio between the two arms was 0.98... P = .84"). 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Although the trial was open-label, the deviations that occurred were minimal and unlikely to have affected the outcome of overall survival. An appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention.
* **Rationale:** The trial was an "open-label, randomized phase III study," meaning participants and personnel were aware of the assigned interventions. However, overall survival is an objective outcome not easily influenced by expectations or trial context. There were minor deviations ("Six patients in the control arm received treatment that was not according to the prespecified investigator’s choice"), but these were very few in the context of the trial size. Furthermore, the analysis of overall survival was conducted on the intention-to-treat population ("The intention-to-treat population comprised 191 patients in the elacytarabine group and 190 in the control arm"). 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Data for the primary outcome of overall survival were available for nearly all randomized participants. 
* **Rationale:** According to the patient disposition flow chart (Figure 2), only 1 patient (1%) in the elacytarabine arm and 4 patients (2%) in the investigator's choice arm were lost to follow-up or withdrawn. This indicates that survival data were nearly complete for the intention-to-treat population, and missingness could not have introduced meaningful bias into the survival results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The outcome of overall survival is an objective measure that is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, despite the open-label design. 
* **Rationale:** While outcome assessors, personnel, and patients were aware of the assigned interventions, overall survival (all-cause mortality) is an objective, observer-reported outcome not involving subjective clinical judgment. Therefore, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The data were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified plan, and there is no indication that the reported overall survival result was selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on the results.
* **Rationale:** Overall survival was the clearly defined primary endpoint of the trial ("The primary end point was overall survival (OS)"). Overall survival is standardly defined and analyzed, leaving no room for selecting among multiple outcome measurements or time points to favor a specific finding. The results for the primary endpoint were fully reported as intended.

Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of **cardiac death** comparing IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided chronic total occlusion (CTO) intervention (considering the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline):

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial utilized an interactive web-based response system for randomization, ensuring both a random sequence generation and adequate allocation concealment. Furthermore, baseline clinical and angiographic characteristics were well balanced between the two groups, indicating no concerns with the randomization process. The trial states: "Using an interactive web-based response system, study participants were randomly assigned and stratified by participating center."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** Given the nature of the procedural interventions (use of IVUS vs. angiography alone), blinding of the personnel delivering the intervention was not possible. However, there is no evidence to suggest that there were substantial deviations from the intended interventions that arose due to the trial context and would impact a hard outcome like cardiac death. Additionally, the authors used an appropriate analysis to estimate the effect of assignment, stating: "The intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** Outcome data for the 12-month follow-up were available for nearly all randomized participants. The trial reports: "200 patients of IVUS-guided group (99.5%) and 201 patients (100%) in the angiography-guided group finished 12-month clinical follow-up." Because the missing data rate is extremely low (1 dropout out of 402), it is highly unlikely to bias the results. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** The outcome of cardiac death is an objective measure that is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. Furthermore, outcome assessors were blinded to intervention assignment, as the study notes: "All clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical event committee blinded to randomization."

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial was registered prior to completion (NCT01563952), and the primary outcome in the trial registration matches the reported primary outcome ("event rate of cardiac death within 12 months"). The result is not selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple analyses within the domain.

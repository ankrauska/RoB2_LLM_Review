**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns
* **Rationale:** The study states that patients were randomly allocated to the intervention or control groups, and baseline characteristics were balanced between groups. However, there is no specific information provided regarding the method used to generate the random allocation sequence or whether the allocation sequence was concealed. Due to the lack of information on allocation concealment, there are some concerns about bias arising from the randomization process. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns
* **Rationale:** Given the nature of the intervention (nutritional counseling in group meetings), participants and trial personnel could not be blinded to the assigned interventions. While there is no direct evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context, the lack of blinding introduces the possibility that health-related behaviors outside the protocol could have differed between groups. Therefore, there is insufficient information to completely rule out deviations, leading to some concerns. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** Missing outcome data were minimal. Out of 80 randomized participants, 75 completed the 12-month follow-up and were included in the analysis (93.75% retention). The reasons for the 5 missing participants (4 dropouts and 1 death) are documented and the small proportion of missing data is unlikely to have a substantial impact on the estimated effect of the intervention.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The outcome (neuropsychological test performance) was measured using standard, validated instruments. According to the trial registration, outcome assessors were masked (single-blinded) to the intervention assignment. Since the assessors were unaware of the assigned interventions, the assessment of the outcome is unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: high risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** High risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The authors explicitly state that they deviated from their pre-specified analysis plan based on the results: "Because both groups lost similar amounts of weight after the intervention period... we decided to a posteriori pool the longitudinal analysis between groups and to test for the effects of change of BMI as a continuous variable." The original intention to compare the two randomized groups showed no significant difference, leading the authors to selectively emphasize an observational, a posteriori analysis in the main text of the publication. This represents a clear selection of the reported result based on the data.

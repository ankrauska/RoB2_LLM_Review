Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance and the provided trial document, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of women's burden and distress.

1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial used a random component for sequence generation (random number tables) and the baseline characteristics were generally balanced, although concealment of the allocation sequence is not explicitly described, the use of random number tables generally implies a low risk of predictability.
b. Rationale: "Participants were assigned randomly via random number tables to one of three treatment conditions: supportive counselling, stress management or alcohol-focused couples therapy." There are no significant baseline differences reported that suggest a problem with the randomization process.

2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were likely aware of the intervention received due to the nature of the interventions (counselling/therapy). There is a risk that this awareness could have influenced the context of the trial, and there is no clear indication that an intention-to-treat analysis was strictly followed for all randomized participants given the dropouts.
b. Rationale: The nature of the psychological interventions means blinding of participants and therapists is not possible. The trial reports: "Forty-seven participants completed treatment and 44 of these participants provided assessed data at 6-month follow-up. This represents an overall attrition of 22% of participants from the treatment phase of the study." The analysis appears to be complete-case rather than ITT.

3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias
a. Judgement: There was substantial attrition (22% drop out), and those who dropped out were not included in the final analysis. The reasons for dropout could be related to the true value of the outcome.
b. Rationale: "Just over 20% of women dropped out of treatment, and most of these women reported they had left their partners soon after beginning the programme... Unfortunately, all the women who dropped out from treatment declined further assessment." This indicates that missingness is likely related to the outcome (burden and distress, relationship status), leading to a high risk of bias.

4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns
a. Judgement: The outcome of women's burden and distress was measured using self-reported scales (RSS and GHQ). Since participants were not blinded, their knowledge of the intervention could influence their responses.
b. Rationale: "The women’s burden and distress were assessed using the Relative Stress Scale (RSS; Greene et al., 1982) and the General Health Questionnaire (GHQ; Goldberg & Hillier, 1979)." As these are participant-reported outcomes and participants were not blinded, the assessment could have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

5. selective reporting: some concerns
a. Judgement: There is no reference to a pre-published protocol or trial registration (e.g., doc_id: 4dab5cab-2b44-42f8-8559-2dd9073b18b5) to verify if the analysis plan was pre-specified. Multiple measures and time points were collected.
b. Rationale: Without a pre-specified analysis plan or trial registration available to compare, and given that outcomes were assessed at multiple time points with multiple scales, there is insufficient information to rule out selection of the reported result. Therefore, there are some concerns regarding selective reporting.

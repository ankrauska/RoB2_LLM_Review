# Risk of Bias Assessment: FLAME Trial (Fluoxetine for Motor Recovery after Acute Ischaemic Stroke)

**Outcome assessed:** Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale (FMMS) at Day 90
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The allocation sequence was generated using an adequate random method (computer-generated, blocked by centre), and allocation was adequately concealed using sequentially numbered opaque envelopes managed by an independent pharmacist. Although some baseline differences exist between groups (e.g., baseline FMMS score, history of stroke, age), these are plausible chance imbalances given the modest sample size (n=59 per group) and unstratified blocked randomization by baseline severity. No statistical evidence (e.g., extremely small p-values) is presented to suggest these imbalances reflect a failure of the randomization process, and the analysis appropriately adjusted for these baseline covariates.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation:* “Randomisation was balanced by centre, with an allocation sequence based on a block size of four, generated with a computer random-number generator by the pharmacist at the coordinating centre.”
- *Allocation concealment:* “The pharmacist assigned the participants to the trial groups in accordance with the randomisation list. Allocation was concealed by use of sequentially numbered opaque envelopes.”
- *Baseline imbalance:* “However, mean age was slightly higher and previous history of stroke was more frequent in the fluoxetine group than in the control group. FMMS score at inclusion was higher in the fluoxetine group than in the placebo group.” No p-values for these differences are reported, and the trial appropriately adjusted for them: “We did a multiple linear regression analysis to control for centre and baseline factors that showed treatment group imbalance.”

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The trial was double-blind with identical-appearing placebo and fluoxetine capsules; participants, carers, treating personnel, and outcome assessors were all masked to allocation, and the blinding code was not broken during the trial. There is no evidence that deviations from intended intervention arose because of trial context (e.g., no differential co-interventions reported). The primary analysis was based on a full analysis set that followed randomized group assignment (not a naïve per-protocol or as-treated analysis).

**b. Rationale:**
- *Blinding:* “Participants, carers, all site study staff, and investigators were masked to treatment assignment.” “The blinding code was not broken.”
- *Compliance/co-intervention balance:* “Treatment compliance was similar in the two groups” (mean cumulative dose, dose intensity, and relative dose intensity nearly identical, p=0.722–0.854). Physiotherapy was delivered per each centre’s usual protocol to both groups equally, and “A possible centre effect was taken into account in the linear regression analysis.”
- *Analysis approach:* “The primary analysis for efficacy (full-analysis set) consisted of a comparison of the change in FMMS distribution... over 90 days,” including all randomized patients except those who died or withdrew (addressed under missing data domain), analysed according to randomized group.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Outcome data were available for 113 of 118 randomized patients (95.8%), with missing data balanced between groups (2/59 fluoxetine, 3/59 placebo) and due to reasons plausibly unrelated to the true value of the motor outcome (death from unrelated causes, unrelated serious medical events leading to withdrawal). This amount and pattern of missing data is very unlikely to have introduced material bias into the FMMS result.

**b. Rationale:**
- “118 patients were randomly assigned to fluoxetine (n=59) or placebo (n=59), and 113 were included in the analysis (57 in the fluoxetine group and 56 in the placebo group). Two patients died before day 90 and three withdrew from the study.”
- From the trial profile (Figure 1): fluoxetine group — 1 died (respiratory distress after food inhalation), 1 withdrew (severe hypoxia); placebo group — 1 died (septic shock), 2 withdrew (1 kidney tumour, 1 pulmonary embolism).
- These reasons (aspiration, hypoxia, septic shock, kidney tumour, pulmonary embolism) are not plausibly related to the participant’s true motor recovery trajectory, and the small, balanced numbers of missing data (3.4% vs 5.1%) are unlikely to materially bias the FMMS result.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The FMMS is a validated, appropriate instrument for measuring motor recovery. Measurement methods were identical across groups (assessed by physiotherapists using a standardized protocol at the same time points). Outcome assessors were explicitly blinded to treatment allocation, and there is no evidence in the report that blinding of outcome assessors was compromised.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of measure:* “FMMS is an index that is widely used for assessment of motor recovery after stroke and has excellent intra-rater and inter-rater reliability and validity.”
- *Comparable ascertainment:* “All motor assessments were made by a physiotherapist at day 0 (baseline) and then 30 days and 90 days after enrolment” in both groups equally.
- *Blinding of assessors:* “Participants, carers, and physicians assessing the outcome were masked to group assignment.” No evidence that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention received; the blinding code “was not broken” during the study.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The pre-registered trial protocol (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00657163) specified “Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale” at 3 months as the primary outcome, exactly matching the outcome measurement and analysis reported in the publication. There is no indication that this result was selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible measurements or analyses of the FMMS outcome.

**b. Rationale:**
- Trial registry primary outcome: “Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale,” Time Frame: “M3 (3 months).”
- Published primary outcome: “The primary outcome measure was the change on the FMMS between day 0 and day 90 after the start of the study drug,” consistent with the registered outcome and timeframe.
- The FMMS was measured and analysed in a single, pre-specified way (total score, with upper and lower limb subscores reported transparently in Table 3), with no evidence of exclusion of alternative unfavourable FMMS-related analyses. Secondary outcomes (NIHSS, mRS, MADRS) were also pre-specified in the registry and reported consistently in the publication.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: CLAVELA Trial (Elacytarabine vs Investigator's Choice) - Outcome: Overall Survival

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns arise due to a statistically significant baseline imbalance in age distribution between arms, despite adequate randomization methods (random sequence generation with stratification and adequate allocation concealment via centralized system).

**b. Rationale:** 
- Sequence generation and concealment appear adequate: "patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System" with stratification by prior therapy intensity and remission status, using a block size of four.
- However, baseline data reveal a significant imbalance: "Age ≥70 years: 58 (30%) elacytarabine vs 35 (18%) control, P=.007." This is a key prognostic factor in relapsed/refractory AML (older age is associated with worse outcomes), and a P-value of .007 is unlikely to be due to chance alone.
- The text explicitly acknowledges: "with the exception of an imbalance in the age distribution including significantly more patients older than 70 years in the elacytarabine arm."
- Since allocation concealment was adequate (central IWRS system, unlikely to be gamed) and sequence generation was random, this imbalance is most plausibly a chance finding given multiple baseline comparisons tested, rather than evidence of a subverted randomization process. However, per RoB2 criteria, a P<0.001 threshold is suggested for "very unlikely due to chance" — this imbalance (P=.007) is borderline but does not meet the more stringent threshold for "High risk." Per the algorithm (Table 3/4), adequate concealment + random sequence + baseline imbalance suggesting a problem → "Some concerns" rather than automatic "Low."
- Authors did report an age-adjusted analysis (HR 0.98; 95% CI 0.79-1.28), suggesting some mitigation of concern about this imbalance affecting the primary result.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. This was an open-label trial (no blinding), but there is no evidence that trial-context-related deviations from intended intervention affected the OS outcome, and an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was used.

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants, carers, and personnel were aware of intervention assignment: "an open-label, randomized phase III study." This triggers the need to assess whether trial-context deviations occurred.
- For OS specifically (an objective, "hard" endpoint not subject to assessor interpretation), knowledge of assignment is unlikely to have led to deviations affecting the outcome itself, unlike for outcomes such as response assessment.
- Crossover/protocol deviations were minimal and documented transparently: "Six patients in the control arm received treatment that was not according to the prespecified investigator's choice" — a very small number (6/190, ~3%), unlikely to introduce meaningful bias into OS.
- Compliance was high: "Ninety-five percent of the patients treated with one course of treatment...received at least 80% of the assigned dose(s)."
- Analysis was conducted per intention-to-treat: "The intention-to-treat population comprised 191 patients in the elacytarabine group and 190 in the control arm," and this ITT population was used for the primary OS analysis (Fig 2, Table 2).
- SCT was received by similar proportions in both arms (20 vs 21 patients) and sensitivity analyses censoring for SCT produced consistent results (HR 1.01; 95% CI 0.8-1.3), indicating this post-randomization event did not bias the ITT effect estimate.
- No evidence of differential non-protocol interventions likely to affect survival that arose specifically because of trial context (e.g., recruitment/engagement effects) is reported.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. OS data were essentially complete for the ITT population, with very few patients lost to follow-up in either arm.

**b. Rationale:**
- From Figure 2 (patient disposition): In the elacytarabine arm, only "1 (1%) was lost to follow-up" out of 191; in the control arm, "4 (2%) were lost to follow-up or withdrawn" out of 190.
- The vast majority of patients had a determinate outcome status (death or confirmed alive at cutoff): "42 (22%) were alive at the end of the study...148 (78%) had events" in the elacytarabine arm, and "32 (17%) were alive...154 (81%) had events" in the control arm.
- The study used a pre-specified event-driven analysis: "Analyses were performed with a cutoff date of when the 300th event was reported. For patients confirmed to be alive on the cutoff date, this date was used to censor for survival and response. For patients lost to follow-up before the cutoff date, censoring was done on the last date the patient was known to be alive."
- Given the very small proportion of patients with missing/lost-to-follow-up status (~1-2%) relative to the large number of observed events (302), missingness could not have made an important difference to the OS estimate.
- Missingness appears balanced between groups and unrelated to the true outcome (i.e., not systematically differential).

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Overall survival is an objective outcome not susceptible to measurement bias from lack of blinding.

**b. Rationale:**
- OS is an observer-reported outcome not involving judgement (death is an objective, unambiguous endpoint), analogous to all-cause mortality discussed in RoB2 guidance: "they are unlikely to influence observer-reported outcomes that do not involve judgement, for example all-cause mortality."
- Although the trial was open-label, and outcome assessors (investigators/study staff) were aware of intervention assignment, this awareness is very unlikely to have influenced the determination of whether a patient was alive or dead, or the recorded date of death.
- Measurement/ascertainment methods for survival status appear comparable between arms — there is no indication that survival was ascertained differently (e.g., more actively or with different methods) in one arm versus the other. Both arms were followed for survival with the same censoring rules applied uniformly.
- No inappropriate measurement method is evident: survival status/date is a straightforward, reliable measure.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The trial's pre-registered primary endpoint (OS) was reported in full accordance with the pre-specified statistical analysis plan, with no evidence of selective reporting among multiple analyses.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registration (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01147939) designates overall survival as the primary outcome, consistent with the manuscript: "The primary end point was overall survival (OS)."
- The statistical methods were clearly pre-specified with a defined analysis trigger (300 events) and pre-specified power calculation: "The study was designed to detect a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.70 in OS, with 87% power and P<.05. A total of 300 events was required."
- OS was analyzed and reported using a single, pre-specified primary approach: Kaplan-Meier method with log-rank test and Cox proportional hazards regression, exactly as described in the Statistical Methods section ("OS was evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier method and was compared using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was used to calculate the HR and 95% CI").
- While additional exploratory/sensitivity analyses of OS were performed (e.g., censoring for SCT, age-adjusted HR, subgroup analyses), these were reported transparently alongside the primary result rather than replacing it, and results were consistent across all analyses (HR ranging from 0.97–1.01), indicating no evidence of selective reporting based on favorable results.
- There is no indication of discrepancy between the registered/protocol-specified outcome definition and what was reported.

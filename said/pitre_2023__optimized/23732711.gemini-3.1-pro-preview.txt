1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, allocation was adequately concealed, and baseline characteristics were balanced. 
b. Rationale: The study report states, "one of our study investigators, who had no knowledge of the participants, randomly assigned them by using a computer-generated randomized list." Table 1 shows that participant characteristics were well-balanced between the intervention and control groups at baseline, and the text confirms, "There were no differences in phenotype, sun exposure, or pretrial sunscreen use between the intervention and control groups at baseline".

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention, but there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose due to the trial context, and data were analyzed according to the intention-to-treat principle.
b. Rationale: Blinding of participants to sunscreen was not possible ("placebo sunscreen was considered unethical"). However, behaviors remained balanced: "Reported sun exposure was similar between the daily and discretionary sunscreen groups during the trial... Use of sun-protection measures other than sunscreen was also similar". The analysis was appropriate: "Data were analyzed according to treatment as randomly allocated."

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Although there was some missing data, the authors conducted appropriate statistical modeling and sensitivity analyses using multiple imputation that showed the results were robust.
b. Rationale: Of the 903 eligible participants, only 604 contributed replicas at both baseline and follow-up. However, the authors state: "a complete case analysis, and estimates from multiple imputation replicated the results", indicating that missing outcome data did not bias the result. 

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The method of outcome measurement was appropriate, and outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment.
b. Rationale: Photoaging was assessed using a validated objective method (microtopography of skin surface replicas). The trial reports that "Experienced assessors who were unaware of treatment allocation graded replicas", meaning the assessors were successfully blinded.

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The study was pre-registered, and the reported outcomes match the intended outcomes without evidence of selective reporting of multiple measurements or analyses.
b. Rationale: The trial was registered (ACTRN12610000086066). The primary outcome ("change in photoaging") was assessed using a specific, pre-defined scale (Beagley and Gibson scale of microtopography grades) and was analyzed using a comprehensive generalized estimating equation (GEE) model as planned, leaving little room for selective reporting.

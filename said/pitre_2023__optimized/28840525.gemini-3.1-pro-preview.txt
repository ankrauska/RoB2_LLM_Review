**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation and allocation concealment, and there were no significant baseline imbalances suggesting a problem with the randomization process. 
* **Rationale:** The publication states, "Randomisation was by computer-generated random number codes managed by an independent study member... disclosed after anaesthesia induction." Baseline characteristics in Table 1 appear well-balanced between the two groups.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Patients were blinded to the intervention they received, which is appropriate for evaluating the effect of assignment to the intervention. While the surgeons were inherently aware of the intervention during the operation, it is unlikely this led to deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context and affected the outcome. An appropriate analysis was used.
* **Rationale:** The study is described as a "prospective, randomised, parallel, 2-arm, double-blind (patient and assessor) clinical trial". Medical management post-surgery was protocolized and performed by blinded endocrinologists: "Metabolic medications were commenced post-operatively according to a standard medical review protocol, by endocrinologists blinded to treatment allocation".

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for nearly all participants, and the missingness is not substantial enough to have a meaningful impact on the results.
* **Rationale:** Out of 114 randomized patients, 5 were lost to follow-up (all in the LSG group). The publication notes: "Loss to follow-up of 5 participants occurred from the LSG group due to failure to make contact and/or to attend appointments beyond 3 months post-operatively". Having outcome data for 109/114 (95.6%) of participants represents availability for nearly all participants. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The outcome (HbA1c) is an objective laboratory measure, the method of measurement was appropriate and comparable between groups, and the outcome assessors were blinded.
* **Rationale:** T2D remission was determined using HbA1c levels, an objective laboratory measurement unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. Furthermore, the trial was explicitly double-blinded, including the assessors.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The reported results align with the pre-specified outcomes in the trial registration, and the outcome measurements and analyses are standard and comprehensively reported. 
* **Rationale:** The trial registration (NCT01486680) lists "Remission of type 2 diabetes mellitus/ glycaemic control" as the primary outcome. The publication reports this outcome using multiple standard HbA1c thresholds without evidence of selective reporting based on statistical significance or direction of the effect.

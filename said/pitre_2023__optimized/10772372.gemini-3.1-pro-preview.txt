Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance and the provided trial text, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of compliance (effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline):

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**
    a. **Judgement:** The trial used a random component for sequence generation, but there is no information provided regarding allocation concealment.
    b. **Rationale:** The text states, "Patients were randomized to telephone contact or no telephone contact using a computer-generated list of random numbers," which satisfies the criteria for adequate sequence generation. However, there is no mention of how this list was concealed before assignment (e.g., central randomization, sealed opaque envelopes). Without confirmation of allocation concealment, this domain raises some concerns.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** Although participants and personnel were aware of the intervention, there is no evidence of deviations from the intended intervention that arose due to the trial context, and an appropriate analysis was used.
    b. **Rationale:** The intervention (telephone follow-up) inherently precludes blinding. However, the trial successfully maintained the intended groups without crossover or dropouts ("Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up"). Since all participants were analyzed in the groups to which they were randomized, the intention-to-treat effect was appropriately estimated without bias from deviations.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants. 
    b. **Rationale:** The trial explicitly states, "Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up." Therefore, missing outcome data cannot bias the results.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** The method of measuring the outcome was objective and unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
    b. **Rationale:** Compliance was measured objectively using "pill and packet counts (not performed within the patient’s view)" and by contacting pharmacies "to document refill information." Because these are objective measures (prescription refill records and physical pill counts), the assessment of the outcome is highly unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of whether the patient received telephone calls or not. 

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**
    a. **Judgement:** There is insufficient information to confirm whether the trial was analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded data were available.
    b. **Rationale:** No trial registration or pre-published protocol is provided in the text. While the reported compliance outcomes (at 6 weeks, 12 weeks, 1 year, and 2 years) align with the stated methods, the lack of a date-stamped, pre-specified statistical analysis plan means we cannot definitively rule out that the reported measurements or analyses were selected based on the results. Therefore, this domain raises some concerns.

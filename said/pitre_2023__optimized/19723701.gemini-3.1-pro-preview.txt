Based on the RoB 2.0 tool and the provided guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the MADIT-CRT trial for the outcome of all-cause death or nonfatal heart failure events:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The trial used an adequate randomized sequence generation process and allocation concealment, typical for a large multicenter trial, and there are no concerning baseline imbalances.
* **b. Rationale:** The publication states, "Patients were randomly assigned in a 3:2 ratio to receive either CRT with an ICD... or only an ICD... with the use of an algorithm that ensured near balance in each stratum." The stratification by clinical center and ischemic status alongside centralized allocation ensures appropriate sequence generation and concealment. Furthermore, Table 1 displays baseline demographic and clinical characteristics that are well-balanced between the two groups, indicating no apparent problems with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Although the trial was unblinded and there were some crossovers, an appropriate Intention-To-Treat (ITT) analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to the intervention.
* **b. Rationale:** Participants and treating physicians were aware of the treatment assignments ("The treating physicians were aware of study-group assignments"). Some deviations occurred (e.g., 82 CRT-ICD patients received an ICD-only device due to technical difficulties, and 30 ICD-only patients crossed over at physician discretion prior to an endpoint). However, because the review's interest is in the effect of assignment to the intervention (the ITT effect), the trial's strict adherence to ITT analysis ("Data analysis was performed according to the intention-to-treat principle") appropriately captures this effect and minimizes bias.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The proportion of missing outcome data is small and unlikely to have a clinically meaningful impact on the primary results.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial had very low attrition rates over the 2.4-year average follow-up. The authors report that only "A total of 44 patients in the CRT–ICD group (4.0%) and 55 in the ICD-only group (7.5%) declined to continue participating in the study, were withdrawn by a physician, or were lost to follow-up." Data were available for nearly all randomized participants, satisfying the criteria for a low risk of bias in this domain. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** While the final adjudication of the outcome was blinded, the initial ascertainment and hospitalization decisions were made by unblinded physicians, which could have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
* **b. Rationale:** The publication explicitly acknowledges this limitation: "The diagnosis of heart failure, which was made by physicians who were aware of study-group assignments, required signs and symptoms consistent with congestive heart failure... Adjudication of the end points was carried out by an independent mortality committee and by a heart-failure committee that was unaware of study-group assignments." The authors note, "It is possible that the investigators’ knowledge of study-group assignment contributed in some way to the lower frequency of heart failure in the CRT–ICD group," meaning the threshold for diagnosing or admitting for heart failure might have varied based on the known treatment arm.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The reported primary outcome matches the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registration, and the data were analyzed in accordance with the pre-specified plan.
* **b. Rationale:** The ClinicalTrials.gov registration (NCT00180271) lists the primary outcome as "Mortality From Any Cause or First Heart Failure (HF) Event". The publication's primary endpoint ("death from any cause or a nonfatal heart-failure event (whichever came first)") is fully consistent with the pre-registered protocol. There is no evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on statistical significance.

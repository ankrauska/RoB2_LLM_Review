# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment
**Outcome: Compliance with mammogram screening**
**Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat)**

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation*: "by using a computerized random number generator program, 500 women were randomly selected to participate" and "A stratified randomization method blocking by age (50–59; 60–70) was used to allocate women to each group using a computerized random generator program." This clearly indicates a random component (Signalling Q1.1 = Yes).
- *Allocation concealment*: "A complete data base with 500 eligible women interviewed at baseline was sent to the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center in the US (i.e. the coordination center) to proceed with a random allocation." Allocation was performed remotely by an independent coordination center after baseline enrollment and interview were complete, which strongly supports adequate concealment (Signalling Q1.2 = Yes/Probably Yes). Because baseline surveys and enrollment occurred before the randomization at a remote site, recruiting personnel could not have foreknowledge of assignment.
- *Baseline imbalance*: "No significant differences were observed between groups in the demographic characteristics of the participants." Table 1 supports comparability of the three arms at baseline. There is no indication of imbalance suggestive of a randomization problem (Signalling Q1.3 = No).

All three signalling questions indicate low risk → **Low risk of bias** for this domain.

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- Participants and personnel were **not blinded**: "Blinding participants to their intervention group was not possible in this study" and "blinding was not possible for participants and interventionists; therefore, they knew the allocation arms" (Signalling Q2.1/2.2 = Yes, aware).
- Because participants/personnel were aware of allocation, the next question is whether deviations from intended intervention arose **because of the trial context** and affected the outcome. The authors explicitly discuss this: "Lack of blinding in this study could have produced a certain level of cross contamination given that women, especially in the control arm, could have demanded more information than usual. This effect would most likely favor the screening rates in the control arm and therefore act in favor of the null hypothesis of no differences between groups." This indicates the authors considered contamination but judged it likely to bias toward the null (i.e., would reduce, not inflate, the observed intervention effect), not to invalidate the interpretation of a significant positive effect. There is no evidence of differential non-protocol intervention delivery in the experimental arms; the intervention components (mail, phone, home visit) were standardized and delivery was tracked systematically (Figure 1, "100% of participants in the low and high intensity groups received the mail contact, and 50% in the high intensity group received a telephone or personal contact"). Access to mammography and clinical care ("Mammography tests were free for the three groups and access for medical and midwife care was equivalent in all of them") was balanced across groups.
- Thus, although awareness of allocation existed, there is no clear evidence that trial-context-driven deviations from intended intervention meaningfully biased the assignment effect (and if anything, the acknowledged potential contamination would bias toward the null, reducing rather than creating a spurious positive effect) → answers to Q2.3/2.4 = No/Probably No.
- *Analysis*: "An intention to treat analysis was conducted using electronic clinical records… They were reviewed for all women and compliance was estimated using the baseline denominators of each group." This is an appropriate ITT analysis (Signalling Q2.6 = Yes).

Given no blinding but no credible evidence of important, outcome-biasing deviations from intended intervention due to trial context, and an appropriate ITT analysis was used → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- The primary outcome (mammography screening) was measured via **electronic records** (radiology database and electronic chart), not solely via self-report at follow-up survey. The abstract states: "Based on electronic records, mammography screening rates increased significantly…" These administrative/electronic records could plausibly be reviewed for all 500 randomized women, largely independent of participant survey attrition. The intention-to-treat analysis "estimated using the baseline denominators of each group" (i.e., n=167, 167, 166) with electronic record review "for all women," suggesting complete outcome ascertainment for this data source (Signalling Q3.1 = Yes/Probably Yes).
- Although "92.4% (462/500) of the women completed the final survey" (self-report), and losses to follow-up were similar across groups (6.6%, 7.8%, 8.4%), the outcome being assessed here (electronic-record-based screening) does not appear to depend on survey completion, minimizing risk of missingness related to the true outcome value.
- No differential reasons for missing outcome data related to intervention group were reported for the electronic-record outcome.

Given essentially complete data capture for the electronic-record-based outcome and balanced/low losses across arms → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of method*: Mammography screening was measured via objective electronic sources: "The first source was the electronic database containing the reports of all tests conducted during the study at the radiology unit... The second source was the electronic chart where the results of all tests are registered." This is an appropriate, objective method of ascertainment (Signalling Q4.1 = No, not inappropriate).
- *Comparability of measurement across groups*: The same electronic record systems were used to ascertain the outcome for all three arms, and access to mammography was open/free to all groups equally ("Mammography tests were free for the three groups"), so ascertainment procedures did not differ by group (Signalling Q4.2 = No).
- *Blinding of outcome assessors*: "Staff at the clinic and at the radiology unit where participants got their mammograms were blinded to the allocation group." Additionally, "The analysis of the data was conducted by investigators of the coordination center... They were blinded to the group assignment of participants." This is an objective outcome (whether a mammogram was performed, recorded automatically in radiology/clinical records) not involving judgement, and outcome assessors/analysts were blinded (Signalling Q4.3 = No, not aware).

Because the outcome is objectively recorded (an event either occurred or did not, captured in blinded/independent electronic systems), and both those recording the outcome (radiology/clinic staff) and those analyzing data were blinded to allocation → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Rationale:**
- The trial registration record was reviewed for pre-specification. The published Methods section defines the primary outcome plainly as "compliance with mammogram screening," measured by both self-report and electronic records, matching what is reported in Table 2 and the Abstract, with no evidence of multiple competing operational definitions or effect-measure alternatives being selectively reported for this particular outcome (no evidence to support "Yes" on Signalling Q5.2 or 5.3 for this specific outcome).
- However, there is **no explicit statement or accessible documentation** in the article confirming that data were "analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available" (Signalling Q5.1). The manuscript does not reference a published/dated statistical analysis plan, and while a registration record exists, the level of detail available (e.g., whether all secondary/behavioral outcomes, timing, and analytic models were pre-specified in sufficient detail prior to unblinding) could not be fully confirmed from the information provided.
- Because there is insufficient information to confirm the analysis strictly followed a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded data were available (even though there is no direct evidence of selection among multiple outcome measurements or analyses for this specific outcome), per the RoB2 algorithm this combination (no clear confirmation of a prospective analysis plan, combined with no evidence of selection from multiple measures/analyses) maps to **"Some concerns"** rather than "Low risk."

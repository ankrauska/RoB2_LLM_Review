**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is insufficient information to determine if the allocation sequence was random or if it was adequately concealed, raising some concerns.
b. Rationale: The trial report states that patients were "randomly assigned in a patient-blinded fashion" but provides no details on the method of random sequence generation or allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics appear well-matched (Table 1), so there is no clear evidence of a problem, but the lack of methodological details warrants "some concerns".

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Although participants and personnel were aware of the intervention assignment, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context, and an intention-to-treat analysis approach was used.
b. Rationale: Blinding participants to whether they received free strips or had to purchase them is impossible. However, there is no indication that the trial context led to non-protocol interventions. The authors state that "Efficacy data from the intention-to-treat population, using the last observation carried forward, were analysed", which is an appropriate analysis strategy for the effect of assignment to intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: There is a large and imbalanced amount of missing outcome data, and missingness is likely related to the true value of the outcomes. 
b. Rationale: The dropout rate was highly imbalanced between the two groups: 19% in the no-cost (NC) group and 48% in the control (C) group. The authors note that cost and inconvenience were major barriers to testing, suggesting that patients who dropped out from the control group were likely testing less frequently and potentially had worse glycaemic control. While Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) was used, it does not adequately correct for bias when missingness is related to the true outcome value, leading to a high risk of bias.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcomes are objective and unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
b. Rationale: The outcomes were SBGM frequency (determined objectively by downloading the glucometer memory) and HbA1c (measured using a DCA 2000 Analyser). These are objective, observer-independent measurements that would not be biased by the lack of blinding.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: A pre-specified analysis plan or trial registration is not available to confirm all planned outcomes were reported as intended.
b. Rationale: There is no trial registration or separate protocol available to verify that the data were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded data were available. While the reported outcomes (HbA1c and SBGM frequency) are standard and expected, the absence of a pre-specified protocol automatically leads to at least "some concerns" regarding selection of the reported result.

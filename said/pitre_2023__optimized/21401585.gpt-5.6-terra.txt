**Outcome assessed:** quality of movement of the impaired upper limb, measured with the Melbourne Assessment of Unilateral Upper Limb Function (MUUL), with the principal between-group result at 26 weeks (CIMT versus bimanual training [BIM]). The effect of interest is the effect of **assignment** to CIMT or BIM at baseline.

1. **Bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The allocation sequence was random: children “were randomized to pairs using a **computer generated list of random numbers**.”  
   - Allocation concealment was described: allocation used “**concealed envelopes opened by non-study personnel**.” Although the report does not give all technical envelope safeguards (e.g. sequential numbering and opacity), use of concealed envelopes opened by personnel outside the study team provides reasonable assurance that recruiters could not foresee assignments.  
   - Baseline characteristics were broadly comparable. The authors report that, following matching and allocation, “**there were no differences between groups on demographic or baseline measures**.” Table I shows similar age, sex, functional classification, baseline MUUL score, and other relevant clinical characteristics. The modest difference in side of hemiplegia is not sufficiently extreme to indicate a compromised randomization process.  
   - The matched-pairs procedure was based on pre-randomization variables (age, sex, side of hemiplegia, and MUUL score), which is appropriate and should improve baseline comparability.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - Participants and intervention providers were necessarily aware of group assignment because CIMT required a constraint glove whereas BIM involved bimanual activities. Thus, blinding of participants and those delivering therapy was not feasible.  
   - However, for the assignment effect, awareness alone is not sufficient for bias. Bias would require deviations from protocol that arose because of the trial context, were unbalanced between groups, and were likely to affect MUUL. There is no evidence of such deviations. Both groups received the same scheduled intensity and setting: “**6 hours daily for 10 days**” (60 hours total), in comparable day-camp environments.  
   - Intervention exposure was high and similar: “**Compliance was 100% for CIMT, and 97% with BIM training**.” Most children received either 60 or 54 hours because of illness or public holidays, without evidence that this was systematically related to assigned group in a way likely to bias the assignment-effect estimate.  
   - Co-intervention imbalance appears unlikely. At follow-up, “**there were no changes to pre-existing therapy for all children**,” except one CIMT participant who received upper-limb botulinum toxin A between the 3- and 26-week assessments. A single such event is unlikely to materially affect the group-level MUUL comparison.  
   - The analysis was explicitly conducted “**on an intention-to-treat basis**,” using generalized estimating equations with participants analysed by allocated treatment group. This is appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**  
   e. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   f. **Rationale:**  
   - Attrition was very limited. The report states that 64 children were randomized and identifies one dropout, from the BIM group on day 2, owing to “**pre-existing emotional and behavioural difficulties**.”  
   - The study describes high retention and “very few dropouts,” and the participant flow diagram reports follow-up and analysed numbers at 3 and 26 weeks.  
   - The amount of missing data is sufficiently small relative to the randomized sample that it is unlikely to have materially changed the MUUL effect estimate, a continuous outcome.  
   - The reported reason for the identified withdrawal was pre-existing emotional/behavioural difficulty, rather than deterioration or improvement in upper-limb movement quality. There is no indication of differential missingness related to the unobserved MUUL outcome.  
   - No evidence suggests that outcome data were missing differentially by intervention group or that missingness depended on the true MUUL value.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - MUUL is an appropriate standardized measure for the specified outcome: “**The MUUL was selected to measure quality of movement of the impaired upper limb**.” Higher percentage scores indicate better upper-limb movement quality.  
   - Measurement procedures were comparable across groups and time points: assessments occurred at baseline, 3 weeks, and 26 weeks.  
   - While the therapists conducting outcome assessments were aware of allocation, the primary assessments were video-recorded and rated independently: “**The primary outcome measures were videotaped and scored in random order by trained occupational therapists masked to group allocation.**” Therefore, the relevant MUUL outcome assessors were blinded.  
   - The investigators also report good intrarater reliability for the independent MUUL rater: intraclass correlation coefficient 0.93 (95% CI 0.84–0.97).  
   - Because the MUUL videos were scored by trained raters masked to treatment allocation, differential outcome assessment related to knowledge of CIMT versus BIM is unlikely.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The study’s published protocol and trial registration prospectively identify the INCITE trial and its planned comparison of CIMT with BIM. The publication is consistent with the planned use of the MUUL as a principal activity outcome and reports the planned assessment occasions (baseline, 3 weeks, and 26 weeks). The 26-week MUUL comparison is reported, including an effect estimate and confidence interval: CIMT versus BIM estimated mean difference 4.4 (95% CI 2.2 to 6.7; p<0.001).  
   - Nevertheless, the available registration/protocol information does not provide a sufficiently detailed, time-stamped statistical analysis plan to confirm that every analytical decision for the reported MUUL result—such as the precise model specification, covariate adjustment, handling of missing observations, and choice between immediate and 26-week results—was finalized before unblinded outcome data became available.  
   - Multiple eligible time points were measured (3 and 26 weeks), and repeated-measures data can be analysed in more than one eligible way (e.g. final values, change scores, or alternative covariate-adjusted longitudinal models). Although both follow-up time points are reported, the absence of a detailed pre-specified analysis plan means selection from multiple analyses cannot be fully excluded.  
   - There is no direct evidence that the reported 26-week MUUL result was selected because it was statistically significant or favourable to CIMT. Therefore, the concern does not rise to high risk of bias.

# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0)
## KANWU Study — Outcome: Changes in 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α
**Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (fish-oil vs. placebo supplementation)**

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** No information is provided about the method used to generate the allocation sequence or to conceal it, so signalling questions 1.1 and 1.2 cannot be answered "Yes." Baseline characteristics (Table 1) appear reasonably balanced across the four groups, so there is no strong evidence of a problem with randomization itself. Per the RoB2 algorithm, "No information" on sequence generation/concealment combined with no evidence of baseline imbalance maps to "Some concerns."

**b. Rationale:** The paper states only: "the participants were randomly assigned to a diet containing a high proportion of either saturated fatty acids (SAFA diet) or monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA diet)… Within each diet group, there was a 2nd random assignment to either supplements of capsules containing fish-oil… or to placebo capsules." No description of sequence generation (e.g., computer randomization) or allocation concealment (e.g., sealed envelopes, central randomization) is given. Table 1 shows no large or statistically flagged differences in baseline characteristics between the four groups (age, BMI, lipids, glucose, blood pressure), consistent with no problem with randomization, but the absence of methodological detail precludes a "Low risk" judgement.

---

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Participants and personnel appear to have been blinded to fish-oil vs. placebo assignment via matched capsules, minimizing the likelihood of trial-context–driven deviations. The analysis used an ANCOVA-type model including all randomized participants with covariates for treatment center, age, sex, and baseline value, consistent with an intention-to-treat approach.

**b. Rationale:** "Within each diet group, there was a second random assignment to supplementation with fish-oil capsules [3.6 g (n-3) fatty acids/d] or placebo… or to placebo capsules with the same amount of olive oil." The comparable form/composition of the placebo capsules (olive oil) suggests successful blinding of participants, carers, and personnel delivering the intervention for the (n-3) vs. placebo comparison. No mention is made of non-protocol interventions, contamination, or deviations arising from awareness of allocation. The statistical model ("treatment categories… and their interaction were analyzed… Factors and treatment center, age, sex, and the baseline value of the outcome variable were covariates") is consistent with an appropriate assignment-based (ITT-style) analysis, not a naïve per-protocol or as-treated analysis. There is no indication that eligible participants were excluded from analysis based on adherence.

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** A substantial portion of the sample (2 of 5 study centers) had no 8-iso-PGF2α data at all due to sample handling/storage problems—an issue affecting a considerable proportion of the originally randomized cohort. Although the stated reason for missingness (technical/storage failure) is plausibly unrelated to the true outcome value, no quantitative information is given on the number of participants/centers affected, on dropout within the remaining centers, or on whether outcome data were available for "nearly all" randomized participants. This lack of transparency, combined with the substantial and non-trivial exclusion of two whole centers, precludes a "Low risk" judgement, but does not clearly indicate risk of differential/informative missingness related to the true outcome, precluding "High risk."

**b. Rationale:** "The plasma levels of 8-iso-prostaglandin F2a (8-iso-PGF2a) were analyzed at 3 centers, Finland, Australia, and Sweden… Plasma samples from 2 centers (Denmark and Italy) were unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage." This means roughly 2/5 of centers (and an unspecified but potentially large fraction of the 162 randomized participants) contributed no data on this outcome. No CONSORT-style flow diagram, no participant numbers analyzed per outcome, and no sensitivity analyses addressing potential impact of this missingness are reported. In contrast, "The levels of 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a were analyzed in plasma from all centers," so missingness is more limited for this component of the composite outcome, but the overall outcome assessment must consider the more problematic component (8-iso-PGF2α).

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The outcomes were measured using validated radioimmunoassays that are objective laboratory measures not subject to subjective interpretation. Even absent explicit statements about blinding of laboratory personnel, this type of outcome measurement is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**b. Rationale:** "The plasma levels of 8-iso-prostaglandin F2a (8-iso-PGF2a) were analyzed… by using a validated RIA developed by Basu," with detailed cross-reactivity and detection limits reported, and similarly "The levels of 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a were analyzed in plasma from all centers using a validated RIA developed by Basu." These are standardized biochemical assays (an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement," per RoB2 categorization), for which lack of blinding of the assessor would not be expected to influence the result. There is no indication that the measurement method differed between intervention groups or that it was inappropriate for detecting the outcome of interest.

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** There is no explicit statement or reference to a publicly available, date-stamped, pre-specified statistical analysis plan confirming that the analyses reported (particularly the covariate-adjusted models and the choice to report the treatment interaction only when significant) were finalized prior to unblinded data being available. However, both outcomes (8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α) are described as primary lipid-peroxidation biomarkers in the aims, are measured at both baseline and 3 months, and are reported fully (with means, SDs, and change scores) for all four subgroups in Table 3, with no evidence of selective omission of eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on results.

**b. Rationale:** "The aim of this study was to investigate whether (n-3) fatty acids… affect nonenzymatic and enzymatic lipid peroxidation… Biomarkers of nonenzymatic and enzymatic lipid peroxidation in vivo were determined by measuring 8-iso-prostaglandin F2a (8-iso-PGF2a) and prostaglandin F2a (PGF2a) concentrations in plasma at baseline and after 3 mo." Both outcomes are reported completely, including a null result for 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α, which argues against outcome-selective reporting bias (a null finding was not suppressed). The statistical methods note: "For outcome variables in which the interaction between the treatment factors was not significant (P ≥ 0.05), a limited model was used excluding that term" — this represents a pre-specified analytic decision rule rather than data-driven selection, which somewhat reduces concern. However, without access to a prospectively registered analysis plan/protocol confirming this rule and the outcome definitions were fixed in advance of unblinding, some uncertainty remains, supporting "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

**Rationale:** No domain was judged at high risk of bias, but "Some concerns" were identified in three domains (randomization process, missing outcome data, and selective reporting). Per RoB 2.0 guidance, "some concerns" in multiple domains can lead to an overall judgement of "Some concerns" (or potentially "High risk" if concerns substantially lower confidence in the result). Here, the missing outcome data issue (loss of 2 of 5 centers' isoprostane data due to technical/storage failure) is particularly notable and, combined with under-reported randomization methodology and uncertain conformity to a pre-specified analysis plan, results in an overall judgement of **Some concerns** for this outcome.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Muscle Damage (CK, AST, MDA)

**Note on scope:** This trial has two phases. Period A (non-intervention, observational comparison of participants vs. non-participants) is not a randomized comparison. The RoB 2.0 tool applies to the randomized comparison in Period B (allopurinol vs. placebo). This assessment focuses on Period B, the randomized allopurinol vs. placebo comparison, for the outcome of muscle damage (CK/AST/MDA).

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The article states: "In period B, crew members were divided into two groups according to random numbers. Sailors were blind regarding the group assignment." This confirms a random component was used ("Yes" to sequence generation), but no details are given on the specific method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated, random number table) or on allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation). There is no description of who generated the sequence or whether it was concealed from those enrolling/assigning participants. Additionally, group sizes were unequal (13 allopurinol vs. 10 placebo, from an eligible pool of 23 after 4 declined), and baseline CK values differed notably between groups (445.8 U/L in allopurinol group vs. 275.8 U/L in placebo group before intervention), a difference of unclear statistical significance not formally tested in the paper. This baseline imbalance in the outcome-relevant marker raises some concern, though it is not stated to be extreme enough to indicate a randomization failure per se. Because allocation concealment is not described and randomization method details are sparse, but there is no strong evidence of problems, "Some concerns" is appropriate rather than "High risk."

---

### 2. Deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** Participants were described as "blind regarding the group assignment" but there is no explicit statement regarding blinding of carers/people administering the intervention or of the investigators analyzing outcomes. The paper states "One group (13 participants) received 300 mg/day of allopurinol 3 h before racing. The other ten members received placebo," implying a placebo-controlled design consistent with blinding, but no detail is given on placebo formulation (i.e., whether it was identical in appearance) or who administered the drug/placebo. There is no discussion of deviations from intended intervention (e.g., non-adherence to the dosing regimen, whether all participants took the assigned dose before every race). No information is provided about whether analysis followed intention-to-treat principles versus per-protocol, though given the small sample and no reported exclusions after allocation (aside from the 4 who declined before allocation), it appears all randomized/allocated participants were analyzed in their assigned groups. Given incomplete reporting on blinding of personnel and lack of information on adherence, but no clear evidence of problematic deviations, "Some concerns" is judged appropriate.

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Four sailors declined to participate in Period B prior to group allocation ("Four sailors finally declined to take part in this phase of the study"), and these individuals were not randomized, so their non-participation does not constitute missing outcome data post-randomization. Following allocation, data appear to be reported for all 13 allopurinol and all 10 placebo participants for CK, AST, and MDA outcomes ("Values are presented as the mean (SD)" for n=13 and n=10 groups in Figures 4 and 6), with no indication of loss to follow-up or missing data within the randomized sample. Therefore, outcome data appear available for all, or nearly all, randomized participants.

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The method of measuring the outcome was not inappropriate: CK and AST were measured spectrophotometrically using validated commercial reagents on an automatic analyzer with reported intra-assay coefficients of variation (4.5% for CK, 4.2% for AST), and MDA was measured using a validated HPLC method. Measurement methods did not differ between intervention groups (same laboratory assays applied to all samples), and these are objective, non-judgment-based, observer-reported outcomes not directly influenced by knowledge of intervention received (laboratory assays performed on stored, coded serum samples). However, the paper does not explicitly state that laboratory personnel conducting the biochemical assays were blinded to group allocation, nor is there a statement confirming blinded sample handling/analysis. Given the outcome is an objective biochemical assay (low susceptibility to bias from lack of blinding), but blinding of assessors/lab personnel is not explicitly confirmed, "Some concerns" is judged rather than "Low risk."

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** No trial registration record was identified/available to confirm a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available (the provided trial registration document id could not be used to verify pre-specification of outcomes, analyses, or timing for this specific study). The article reports CK, AST, and MDA consistently across both Period A and Period B, and there is no evidence of selective outcome or analysis reporting (e.g., no indication that other muscle damage markers were measured but not reported, or that alternative statistical analyses were conducted and only favorable ones presented). However, without access to a registered protocol or statistical analysis plan, it cannot be confirmed that the reported analyses (e.g., choice of parametric t-tests, specific timepoints such as "3 h after the last regatta") were pre-specified rather than selected post hoc based on the results. This absence of verifiable pre-specification leads to "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

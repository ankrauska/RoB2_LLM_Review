Here is the risk of bias assessment using the RoB 2.0 tool for the trial evaluating the effect of intravenous magnesium sulfate on serum levels of NT Pro-BNP in elective CABG with cardiopulmonary bypass, considering the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect).

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**
    a. **Judgement:** The trial mentions random allocation based on a computer random numbers table, which is adequate for sequence generation. However, there is no description of allocation concealment. Although baseline characteristics are generally balanced, the lack of information on allocation concealment raises some concerns.
    b. **Rationale:** The authors state, "Then the patients were allocated into one of the two groups based on a computer random numbers table. There were 44 patients in the control group and 44 patients were allocated to the case group, all selected randomly." However, there is no mention of how the sequence was concealed before group assignment (e.g., opaque sealed envelopes or central randomization). According to RoB 2, an adequate sequence generation with no information on allocation concealment leads to "some concerns."

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** The trial was described as double-blind, and matching placebos (same volume of normal saline via a 50 ml syringe) were used. Furthermore, the analysis appears to include all 88 patients randomized, aligning with intention-to-treat principles.
    b. **Rationale:** The study states, "In a double-blind clinical trial... one group received a MgSO4 infusion... and the other group saline (placebo)" and "The same volume of normal saline was administered as a placebo via a 50 ml syringe intravenously." Furthermore, "Except for administration of MgSO4 or placebo, the anesthesia methods and protocols for the two patient groups were exactly the same." The results tables present data for 44 patients in each group (88 total), indicating that all randomized patients were analyzed in the groups to which they were assigned. Thus, participants and personnel were blinded, and an appropriate analysis was conducted.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants, as the results tables show the full n=44 for both groups.
    b. **Rationale:** In Tables 1, 2, and 3, the sample sizes are reported as "MgSO4 group (n = 44)" and "Placebo group (n = 44)", accounting for all 88 enrolled patients. There is no indication of dropouts or missing data for the NT pro-BNP outcome.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** The outcome (NT Pro-BNP) is an objective laboratory measure assessed via an automated assay. The assessors were blinded, and the automated nature of the test ensures that the assessment could not be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
    b. **Rationale:** The measurement method is described as: "Measurement of NT pro-BNP was performed using an electrochemical luminescence immunoassay in an Elecsys 2010 (Roche, Indianapolis, IN, USA)." This is an appropriate and objective method. The trial was double-blind, meaning those drawing and testing the blood were likely unaware of the group assignment, and even if they were, the automated machine reading would not be influenced by knowledge of the intervention. 

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**
    a. **Judgement:** The study is registered, but we do not have access to a statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available to definitively compare intended analyses with reported ones. While the primary outcome seems to be reported as intended, the lack of an available, detailed, pre-specified analysis plan leaves some uncertainty.
    b. **Rationale:** The authors mention, "The clinical trial was registered at the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials website... IRCT201205069650N1". However, without seeing the detailed, date-stamped trial registry entry or protocol to confirm that the specific time points (24 hours) and analysis methods for NT pro-BNP were pre-specified exactly as reported before unblinding, there is insufficient information to rule out selection of the reported result from multiple analyses or time points, leading to "some concerns" by default under the RoB 2 framework.

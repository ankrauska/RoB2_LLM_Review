**Outcome assessed:** change in mean sitting diastolic blood pressure (DBP) from baseline to week 4, comparing candesartan 16 mg/hydrochlorothiazide (HCT) 12.5 mg with candesartan 16 mg monotherapy. The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline**.

1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns, because the trial reported use of a mixed-block randomization method but did not report how allocation was concealed before enrolment and assignment.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The article states that patients “were randomized in a 1:1 ratio by using the mixed block randomization (4 and 6 blocks) method.” This provides reasonable support that a random allocation sequence was intended, with varying block sizes reducing predictability.  
   - However, there is no description of allocation concealment—for example, central randomization, an independent pharmacy, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or another mechanism preventing recruiters from knowing the next assignment. Thus, it is unclear whether the allocation sequence was concealed until enrolment.  
   - Baseline DBP and SBP were closely similar between groups (baseline DBP 104.7 versus 104.5 mm Hg; baseline SBP 161.9 versus 160.0 mm Hg). The combination group was older on average (51.0 versus 47.8 years; *P* = 0.02), but this single imbalance among several reported baseline variables is compatible with chance and does not by itself demonstrate a problem with randomization.  
   - Therefore, the absence of information on allocation concealment leads to **some concerns**, rather than high risk of bias.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns. The study was open label, but there is no evidence that deviations caused by the trial context differentially affected week-4 DBP. However, the reported “ITT” analysis excluded some randomized participants for reasons not fully explained.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The registry explicitly identifies the design as having no masking (“masking: NONE”), and the article describes an open-label active-treatment comparison. Thus, participants, carers, and personnel were aware of treatment assignment.  
   - Nevertheless, for the effect of assignment, awareness alone does not establish bias. The report does not identify deviations from the intended treatment that arose because of the trial context, were unbalanced between groups, and were likely to affect DBP. Medication compliance was high in both groups (median compliance 98.6% in the combination group and 97.8% in the monotherapy group).  
   - The primary week-4 analysis was labelled ITT, but the ITT population was defined as patients who had baseline BP, at least one post-randomization BP measurement, and had taken at least one dose of study medication. Thus, it was not a full ITT analysis of all 233 randomized participants: 224 participants were analysed at week 4 (111/117 in the combination group and 113/116 in the monotherapy group).  
   - The nine excluded randomized participants represent approximately 4% of the randomized sample. The report does not clearly establish that all were excluded solely because of missing outcome data, rather than because they did not receive treatment or for other post-randomization reasons. Excluding participants who did not receive treatment is not fully consistent with estimation of the effect of assignment.  
   - The small number excluded makes a substantial effect on this continuous outcome unlikely, but the incomplete full-ITT analysis gives **some concerns**.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   e. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   f. **Rationale:**  
   - Week-4 DBP data were analysed for 224 of 233 randomized participants: 111/117 (94.9%) in the candesartan-HCT group and 113/116 (97.4%) in the candesartan-monotherapy group. Overall, outcome data were available for approximately 96% of randomized participants.  
   - For a continuous outcome such as DBP, outcome availability of about 95% is generally sufficient for the missing observations to be unlikely to materially alter the estimated between-group effect.  
   - Although the proportion excluded was modestly higher in the combination group (6 versus 3 participants), the absolute number of missing/excluded participants was small. No evidence indicates that missingness was related to unobserved week-4 DBP values, or that reasons for missing data differed in a way likely to bias the result.  
   - Consequently, missing data are unlikely to have had an important impact on the week-4 DBP comparison.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - Sitting BP was measured using “a fully automated device produced by Omron.” The protocolized procedure included measurement at the same time of day before dosing, rest in a quiet room for at least 5 minutes, three measurements at 1-minute intervals, and averaging of measurements according to a predefined procedure.  
   - The arm with the highest mean DBP at baseline was used consistently for subsequent measurements, and the investigators attempted to use the same individual to perform readings for each participant across visits.  
   - Although the trial was open label, the outcome was an instrument-based physiological measurement rather than a participant-reported or judgement-dependent outcome. There is no indication that measurement procedures or thresholds differed between treatment groups.  
   - Knowledge of assigned treatment is therefore unlikely to have materially influenced the automated DBP measurement.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The trial was prospectively registered (NCT00621153; first posted February 22, 2008), before completion in March 2009. The registry prespecified the primary outcome as “Changes in Mean Sitting DBP From Baseline After 4 Weeks of Therapy,” which matches the outcome and time point assessed in the publication. This supports that the reported outcome itself was prespecified.  
   - The registry posted the primary result, reporting week-4 least-squares mean changes of −17.0 mm Hg for combination therapy and −14.1 mm Hg for monotherapy. The publication reports the same primary comparison, although it presents raw mean changes (−17.8 and −14.1 mm Hg) and an ANCOVA-based between-group comparison.  
   - The publication states that ANCOVA was used, with treatment as a factor and DBP at randomization as a covariate. However, no dated, detailed statistical analysis plan is available to establish that the precise analysis—such as the analysis population, handling of missing data, use of change scores, and covariate adjustment—was finalized before unblinded data were available.  
   - There is no clear evidence that the week-4 DBP result was selected from multiple time points or analyses based on its results; indeed, the registry specifically identifies this outcome and time point as primary. However, absence of a sufficiently detailed prespecified analysis plan prevents a low-risk judgement.

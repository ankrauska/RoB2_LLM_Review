# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Acoustic Reaction Time Outcome

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

a. **Rationale for Judgement:** Sequence generation was random, allocation was concealed using an adequate method, and no evidence of baseline imbalance likely to bias results was reported.

b. **Supporting Evidence:** "Participants were allocated randomly to bright light (BRIGHT), monochromatic blue light (BLUE), or a control condition (CONTROL) using an online tool... which applies permuted block randomization with randomly varying block sizes of 3, 6, and 9. The list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered sealed opaque envelopes." This meets both minimal and extended criteria for allocation concealment (central, independent transmission with sequentially numbered sealed envelopes). Table 1 shows baseline characteristics (age, VO2max, sport type) across groups appear comparable, with no indication of problematic imbalance in reported characteristics.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (Effect of assignment to intervention)
**Judgement: Some concerns**

a. **Rationale for Judgement:** Participants were not blinded to their own light condition, and there is no information about whether trial personnel/carers were blinded, or whether trial-context-related deviations occurred. However, there is no evidence to suggest that trial-context deviations affected this particular outcome, and an appropriate (per-protocol/full sample) analysis was used.

b. **Supporting Evidence:** The article states: "The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to the light condition each participant was exposed to. Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to, but not the other two light conditions." This indicates participants were NOT blinded to their own assignment (though blinded to other groups' assignments), while personnel status is unclear beyond the test supervisor. The trial registry confirms: "maskingInfo": {"masking": "NONE"}, reinforcing that participants were aware of their allocation. Because participants were aware, and this is a physical/behavioral outcome (reaction time) that could theoretically be influenced by expectancy effects related to trial engagement, this raises "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk." However, there is no explicit evidence that trial-context related deviations (e.g., non-protocol behaviors) occurred that specifically affected reaction time, and the analysis method was appropriate (ANCOVA using baseline-adjusted values on all randomized participants who completed testing, consistent with ITT principles for this measured outcome). No dropouts or protocol deviations affecting this outcome were reported. Given lack of information on whether unblinding influenced outcome-related behavior, but no strong reason to suspect a trial-context-driven deviation influenced this specific physiological reaction-time task, "Some concerns" is judged appropriate rather than "High risk."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

a. **Rationale for Judgement:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants included in the final analytic sample (72/74), with the 2 exclusions occurring for reasons unrelated to the outcome and prior to intervention.

b. **Supporting Evidence:** "Two subjects in BRIGHT and CONTROL had to be excluded retrospectively... due to invalid measurements of peak oxygen uptake in the cardiorespiratory fitness test." This exclusion occurred due to a technical fitness-test eligibility issue assessed *before* randomization-related outcome measurement, unrelated to the reaction time outcome itself. The remaining 72 participants (24/24/24) all appear to have complete pre- and post-light exposure reaction time data reported in Table 2, with no further mention of missing data, dropouts, or incomplete reaction time task performance. Since data appear complete for all analyzed participants and the exclusion reason is clearly unrelated to the true value of the reaction time outcome, missingness could not depend on the true outcome value.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome
**Judgement: Some concerns**

a. **Rationale for Judgement:** The outcome measurement method itself was appropriate and objective (automated reaction-time task), and measurement was identical across groups. However, participants were not blinded to their own intervention assignment, and reaction time is a psychomotor/behavioral outcome that could plausibly be influenced by participant awareness/expectation (e.g., altered effort or motivation depending on perceived light condition), even though the assessor recording data was blinded.

b. **Supporting Evidence:** "Immediately before, as well as immediately after the light exposure, participants performed a computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task..." — this is an automated, objective measurement, minimizing risk from assessor judgment. However, since the participant is effectively the "outcome assessor" in a self-administered reaction time task (their own psychomotor response), and "Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to" (unblinded to own allocation), there is potential that knowledge of intervention (e.g., being in BRIGHT vs CONTROL) could subconsciously influence effort/alertness during testing. The authors themselves speculate about expectancy-related alertness effects of light throughout the discussion, suggesting this is a plausible concern for this outcome domain. Since it is a simple, low-judgment automated task (not involving subjective observer interpretation) the influence is judged as *possible* but *not clearly likely*, supporting "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

## 5. Selective reporting
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

a. **Rationale for Judgement:** The outcome (reaction time) and analytic approach were pre-specified in the trial registry as a secondary outcome, and the reported result matches the pre-specified outcome definition and statistical approach without evidence of selective reporting from multiple measures or analyses.

b. **Supporting Evidence:** The trial registry (NCT02203539) prospectively lists "Reaction time in milliseconds (ms)" as a secondary outcome, matching exactly what is reported in the article. The article states: "All statistical methods used to compare the groups for reaction time and handgrip strength in the present report were pre-specified and registered. Analyses of covariance were used to compare reaction time and handgrip strength after the light exposure between the three groups adjusted for reaction time and handgrip strength before the light exposure." This confirms a single pre-specified analytic method (ANCOVA) was used and reported, along with a single, clearly defined outcome measurement (median reaction time from a single simple acoustic reaction time task), with no indication of multiple competing scales, time points, or alternative analyses from which the authors could have selectively reported.

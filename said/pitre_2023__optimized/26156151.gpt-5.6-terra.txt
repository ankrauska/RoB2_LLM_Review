**Outcome assessed:** occurrence of cardiac death at 12 months for the comparison of IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided CTO intervention. The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline**.

1. **bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** Randomization was performed after successful guidewire crossing, using an interactive web-based response system and stratification by participating centre: “*Using an interactive web-based response system, study participants were randomly assigned and stratified by participating center.*” This supports a random sequence and adequately concealed allocation at the point of enrolment/randomization. Although randomization after guidewire crossing restricts the population to patients with successful crossing, this occurred before allocation to IVUS versus angiographic guidance and therefore does not undermine the randomized comparison.  
   
   The groups were broadly comparable at baseline. For example, age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, ejection fraction, prior MI, CTO characteristics and lesion measures were similar. The difference in distribution of one-, two-, and three-vessel disease does not constitute a clear, implausible baseline imbalance indicating a failure of randomization. Therefore, there is no evidence that the randomization process was compromised.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** The operators and likely other procedural personnel necessarily knew whether IVUS guidance was used; however, for the assignment-effect estimand, awareness is problematic only if it results in deviations from the assigned strategy that arose because of the trial context, were inconsistent with the protocol, and were likely to affect cardiac death. There is no evidence of such deviations.  
   
   The more frequent use of high-pressure post-stent dilation in the IVUS group (51.2% versus 41.3%) appears to be a consequence of the assigned IVUS-guided strategy and its procedural information, rather than a non-protocol co-intervention or a failure to implement the assigned intervention. The protocol explicitly allowed physicians discretion over “*IVUS devices, pullback methods, use at poststenting and prestenting, and numbers of exams*,” while strongly recommending IVUS optimization criteria. Thus, procedural decisions informed by IVUS are part of the intended intervention effect.  
   
   Analyses followed the intention-to-treat principle: “*The intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses.*” The cardiac-death comparison retained all 201 randomized patients per group in the reported event analysis. Thus, there is no indication that participants were analysed according to treatment received or excluded post-randomization in a way that could materially bias the effect of assignment.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** Cardiac-death follow-up was essentially complete: “*200 patients of IVUS-guided group (99.5%) and 201 patients (100%) in the angiography-guided group finished 12-month clinical follow-up.*” Only one of 402 randomized participants lacked completed 12-month follow-up.  
   
   Given this extremely small amount of missing outcome data, the missing outcome for one participant could not plausibly make an important difference to the cardiac-death result, which was 0/201 in the IVUS group and 2/201 in the angiography-guided group. There is also no indication that missingness was related to cardiac death or differed materially between groups.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** Cardiac death is a relatively objective clinical outcome. The study used a prespecified definition: “*All deaths were considered cardiac death unless an unequivocal noncardiac cause could be established.*” This definition was applied to both groups.  
   
   Importantly, “*All clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical event committee blinded to randomization.*” Blinded independent event adjudication minimizes the possibility that assignment to IVUS or angiography guidance influenced classification of deaths. Follow-up procedures were also the same in both groups, with clinical contact at 1, 3, 9, and 12 months by clinic visit or telephone. There is no evidence that ascertainment of cardiac death differed between intervention groups.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:** The trial registration record prespecified “*event rate of cardiac death within 12 months*” as the primary outcome. The publication reports cardiac death at 12 months, including the numerical result of 0% (0/201) in the IVUS group and 1.0% (2/201) in the angiography-guided group. Thus, the reported outcome domain and time point agree with the registration record, and there is no direct evidence that the cardiac-death outcome was selectively omitted or substituted.  
   
   However, the available registry record does not provide a sufficiently detailed, date-stamped statistical analysis plan specifying the analysis method for cardiac death (for example, the precise handling of censoring, alternative effect measures, or other analytic choices) before unblinded outcome data became available. The publication reports Kaplan–Meier/log-rank analyses, but it cannot be confirmed that this exact analysis was prospectively specified. There is no evidence that the reported cardiac-death result was selected from multiple analyses because of its result, but the absence of a detailed pre-specified analysis plan leads to some concerns under RoB 2.

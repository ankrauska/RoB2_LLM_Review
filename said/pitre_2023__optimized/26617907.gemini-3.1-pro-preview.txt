Based on the RoB 2.0 tool, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome **Surgical time** from the provided trial:

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**
    a. **Judgement:** The trial used a random component for sequence generation but did not provide information on allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics appear balanced.
    b. **Rationale:** The study report states, "Using the random number table method, 90 cases were divided into group A and group B (n=45)." However, there is no mention of how the allocation sequence was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions. Because allocation concealment is unclear, there are "some concerns" regarding this domain, despite the baseline characteristics being well-balanced between the two groups.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** Participants and personnel could not be blinded due to the nature of the interventions (surgical resection vs. percutaneous ablation), but deviations from the intended intervention were unlikely to have arisen because of the trial context and affected this specific outcome. 
    b. **Rationale:** Although the trial was inherently open-label, "Surgical time" is an objective, immediate intraoperative outcome tied directly to the execution of the assigned procedures themselves. There is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions (such as crossover) affecting this outcome, and the analysis includes all randomized participants (n=45 in both groups as shown in Table 1).

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** Data for this outcome were available for all randomized participants.
    b. **Rationale:** Table 1 reports the surgical time for all 90 randomized participants (n=45 in Group A and n=45 in Group B). Therefore, there is no missing outcome data for this endpoint, precluding any bias related to missingness.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
    a. **Judgement:** The method of measuring surgical time is appropriate and objective. While outcome assessors were not blinded, the assessment is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
    b. **Rationale:** Surgical time is a standard, objectively timed intraoperative metric. Even though the surgeons and operating room staff recording the time were aware of the intervention being performed, it is an observer-reported outcome not involving subjective judgment. Therefore, it is unlikely that the assessment of the time elapsed was influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**
    a. **Judgement:** There is no information regarding a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial registration available to compare the reported outcomes against the intended analyses.
    b. **Rationale:** The published manuscript does not refer to a publicly accessible, date-stamped trial protocol or trial registry record. Without this information, it is not possible to confirm with certainty whether the outcomes and analyses were pre-specified or selected based on the results, leading to "some concerns" for selective reporting.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: Cancer-DACUS Study
## Outcome: Recurrent Thrombotic Events

**Preliminary note:** This assessment focuses on the randomized comparison between Group A1 (continue LMWH) versus Group A2 (discontinue LMWH), as this is the only randomized comparison in the study (Group B was not randomized). Effect of interest: assignment to intervention (ITT effect).

---

### 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily driven by lack of information about allocation concealment.

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation: The article states "Random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." Using clinical chart numbers to determine allocation raises concern that this may not represent a truly random process (this resembles a systematic/quasi-random method rather than a random-number-based approach). This is ambiguous — chart numbers could be used merely as a mechanism to link to a pre-generated random sequence, or could itself be the sequence generation method (which would be non-random). Response: **No information/Probably no** to signalling question 1.1.
- Allocation concealment: Sealed envelopes were used, but there is no description of whether envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, or opened only after irreversible enrollment. With a fixed block size of 10 (not varied), and no information on whether allocators could predict upcoming allocations, there is a risk that concealment could have been compromised, especially since "Patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT" — meaning that after allocation, but potentially even during the process, there was no blinding safeguard.
- Baseline imbalance: Table 1 shows no significant differences between Group A1 and Group A2 in age, sex, BMI, surgery, chemotherapy, metastasis, or thrombosis characteristics (all P > .05), suggesting no problem with randomization from a baseline-balance perspective.
- Overall, because allocation concealment is inadequately described and the "chart number" based method for random assignment raises ambiguity about the true randomness of the sequence, but baseline characteristics are well-balanced, this domain is judged as **Some concerns** rather than high risk.

---

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to lack of blinding of participants and personnel, combined with unclear information on whether trial-context-driven deviations occurred, though the impact appears limited.

**b. Rationale:**
- The article explicitly states: "the trial was not blinded to patients and treating physicians" and the protocol confirms "masking: NONE."
- Participants and personnel were aware of allocation (2.1/2.2 = Yes).
- There is no explicit description of non-protocol interventions or contamination between groups arising from the trial context (e.g., patients in Group A2 unilaterally deciding to restart LMWH, or physicians in Group A1 stopping LMWH early due to lack of equipoise). The paper does report: "Among patients in group A1, compliance was high: 15 of the 119 patients interrupted LMWH for less than 5 days during study treatment" — this suggests deviations were minimal and unlikely to have influenced outcomes substantially.
- No information is provided regarding whether Group A2 patients might have sought/received anticoagulation outside protocol due to awareness of being in the "no treatment" arm — a plausible trial-context deviation given lack of blinding, but not reported either way.
- Analysis: The study states "Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis," which is appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention (signalling question 2.6 = Yes).
- Because lack of blinding creates the possibility that trial-context deviations occurred and were unbalanced, but there is no clear evidence such deviations occurred or affected the outcome (and adherence in A1 was high), the judgement is **Some concerns** rather than high risk.

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to considerable loss to follow-up (especially deaths) that is unevenly distributed between groups and not clearly addressed analytically.

**b. Rationale:**
- The CONSORT flow and text indicate substantial attrition: "After the first 6 months, there were 108 patients still on follow-up in group A1 and 94 in group A2. At 12 months, there were 91 patients in group A1 and 77 in group A2" (out of 119 and 123 randomized, respectively). This represents roughly 20-25% of patients not completing follow-up by 12 months in both groups.
- A major driver of missing outcome data is death from cancer progression: "12 patients (10.1%) died in group A1" vs "19 (15.4%) in group A2" — a meaningful imbalance (though not statistically significant, P=.366) that could relate to underlying disease severity, which is also prognostic for thrombotic recurrence. Patients who died could not experience (or could have experienced but not had it captured as) a subsequent VTE event, and it is plausible that sicker patients both died more and were more likely to have recurrent VTE (competing risk).
- The differential death rate (15.4% vs 10.1%) between the discontinuation and continuation arms suggests that missingness in the outcome (due to death precluding full follow-up) could be related to prognosis, which may differ by group, raising the possibility that missingness depended on the (would-be) outcome.
- No sensitivity analyses addressing the impact of missing data (deaths, losses to follow-up) on the recurrent VTE outcome are reported.
- The 6-month difference in total follow-up duration between A1 (18 months total) and A2 (12 months total) is acknowledged by the authors as a limitation: "the 6-month difference in follow-up duration between groups A1 and A2, which may have resulted in an underestimate of the event rate in the latter group" — this is a structural asymmetry in outcome ascertainment given differing observation windows, contributing to the "some concerns" judgement in this domain (and overlapping with measurement/analysis concerns).
- Overall, given imbalanced dropout/death and no sensitivity analysis, but the primary analysis appears to be ITT-based with reasonably high proportions of data available (75-90%), this is judged as **Some concerns**.

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as outcome measurement/ascertainment procedures were objective, standardized, and adjudicated by a blinded central committee.

**b. Rationale:**
- Measurement method was appropriate and standardized: "Diagnosis of recurrent DVT was made if a previously fully compressible segment became no longer compressible, or if an increase of more than 4 mm in the diameter of the residual thrombus during compression was detected; in undetermined cases, repetition of the test... or contrast venography was performed." For PE, "diagnosis of recurrence was based on objective algorithms with the use of clinical probability, ventilation-perfusion lung scanning, or helical computed tomography."
- Ascertainment did not differ between groups: follow-up visit schedules were similar (every 3 months), and testing (C-US) was only triggered by clinically suspected recurrence in all groups equally, reducing risk of differential detection bias.
- Critically, outcome adjudication was blinded: "All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of the patient's name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment." This directly addresses the lack of participant/physician blinding, as the definitive outcome assessment was made by assessors blinded to allocation.
- Because the outcome assessors (adjudication committee) were blinded and outcome measurement used objective, pre-specified diagnostic criteria unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of allocation, this domain is judged as **Low risk of bias**.

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias — the reported outcome and analysis are consistent with the pre-specified trial registry outcome, and no evidence of selective reporting among multiple measurements or analyses is apparent.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registry (NCT00450645) pre-specifies the primary outcome as "Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism," which matches the composite "recurrent VTE" outcome reported and analyzed in the publication (including breakdowns of isolated DVT, DVT and PE, and PE only in Table 3).
- The published article's stated primary end point ("recurrent venous thromboembolism (VTE) during the 1 year after discontinuation of LMWH") is consistent with the registered outcome, with no evidence of a switched primary outcome.
- There is no evidence of selective reporting from among multiple eligible outcome measurements (e.g., alternative definitions, alternative time points) — a single consistent definition of recurrent VTE was used and reported for all groups and comparisons.
- There is no evidence of selection among multiple eligible analyses — Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for pre-specified covariates (age, sex, previous surgery, presence of metastasis) were consistently applied and fully reported for all pairwise comparisons (A2 vs A1, A1 vs B, A2 vs B), and both adjusted and Kaplan-Meier (unadjusted) analyses are presented transparently.
- No statistical analysis plan was available for detailed comparison, limiting certainty, but agreement between the registered outcome and the reported analysis, combined with full reporting of all group comparisons (including non-significant results, e.g., HR for A2 vs A1 P=.311/.175), supports a judgement of **Low risk of bias**.

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** Some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** The trial report states that "randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration", but it does not provide specific details on the method used to generate the random allocation sequence or the exact mechanism of allocation concealment. However, baseline characteristics (Table 1) appear balanced between the groups, mitigating some concerns of gross subversion of the randomization process. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** High risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** While participants and clinicians were blinded to the intervention ("Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment"), the analysis did not follow the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle. The investigators excluded participants who dropped out: "Six subjects who failed to appear for more than one evaluation (all from the placebo group) dropped out of the study, and their data were not further analyzed." Since 6 out of 24 (25%) randomized placebo participants were excluded from the analysis (compared to 0% in the topiramate group), there is a failure to analyze participants in the group to which they were randomized, with a potential for a substantial impact on the result.

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** High risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** Outcome data for weight were not available for all randomized participants. Six participants (25%) from the placebo group dropped out and were excluded from the analysis, whereas no participants dropped out from the topiramate group. This large discrepancy in the proportions of missing outcome data between the intervention groups strongly suggests that missingness could be related to the true value of the outcome (e.g., participants in the placebo group dropping out because they were not experiencing weight loss or well-being improvements).

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The outcome of interest (weight) is an objective measure, and the method of measuring it was appropriate. Furthermore, outcome assessors, clinicians, and subjects were blinded to the intervention assignment ("Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment"), so the measurement of the outcome was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** Some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a prospective trial registration that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. While weight is a standard, straightforward outcome and the reported results align with the study's stated aims, the lack of a publicly available, date-stamped pre-specified protocol means we cannot completely rule out the possibility that analyses were selected based on the data.

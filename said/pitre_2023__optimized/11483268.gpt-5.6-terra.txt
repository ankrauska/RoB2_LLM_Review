**Outcome assessed:** anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT), measured approximately 1 month after booster vaccination, comparing Vaqta™ booster versus Avaxim™ booster among adults primed with Avaxim™.

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** The study is described as randomized and baseline characteristics were similar, but the publication provides no information on how the random sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed until enrolment. Under RoB 2, absence of information on allocation concealment raises some concerns.  
   
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The study is described as a “**controlled, randomised, open study with two parallel groups**,” and 127 participants were randomized (64 to Vaqta™ and 63 to Avaxim™).  
   - However, there is no description of random-sequence generation (e.g. computer-generated sequence, random-number table, blocking) and no description of allocation concealment (e.g. central randomization or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes).  
   - Baseline demographic characteristics appear balanced: mean age was 38.4 versus 38.2 years; male sex 55% versus 52%; and mean weight and height were also very similar. Thus, the reported baseline data do not themselves suggest a failure of randomization.  
   - Nevertheless, with no information about concealment of allocation, the RoB 2 judgement is **some concerns**, rather than low risk of bias.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** The trial was open-label, but there is no evidence that awareness of allocation led to protocol-inconsistent deviations arising from the trial context that affected anti-HAV GMT. However, the reported immunogenicity result is based on a per-protocol analysis rather than the preferred intention-to-treat analysis for the effect of assignment. The small number of exclusions means a substantial effect on the result is unlikely, but this creates some concerns.  
   
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The investigators explicitly state that this was an “**open study**”; therefore, participants and study personnel were aware of assigned booster vaccine.  
   - The report does not describe differential co-interventions, differential vaccine administration, or other trial-context deviations likely to affect the laboratory anti-HAV titre. Both vaccines were administered intramuscularly into the deltoid region, and follow-up serology was undertaken at the same planned visit window.  
   - The investigators state: “**Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed**,” but “**the results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported**.”  
   - The reported GMTs are therefore per-protocol results: 59 Vaqta™ recipients and 62 Avaxim™ recipients, rather than all 127 randomized participants. For the effect of assignment to intervention, this is not the preferred analysis because it excludes randomized participants after randomization.  
   - Six participants were excluded from the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis: one had previously received a hepatitis A booster, one travelled to India between visits, and four lacked both pre- and post-vaccination serology. The two exclusions for protocol-related reasons represent a small proportion of randomized participants; consequently, there is no clear basis to conclude that the per-protocol analysis had a substantial impact on the GMT comparison.  
   - Thus, the domain is judged **some concerns**, rather than high risk of bias.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**  
   e. **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants. Only four randomized participants were reported to lack both pre- and post-vaccination serological results, representing approximately 3% of the randomized sample. This amount of missing laboratory outcome data is unlikely to have materially changed the GMT comparison.  
   
   f. **Rationale:**  
   - Of 127 randomized participants, the per-protocol GMT analysis included 121 participants (59 and 62 in the respective groups).  
   - The report specifies that four participants “**did not have pre- and post-vaccination serological results available**.” This corresponds to approximately 96.9% of randomized participants having the necessary serological data.  
   - The other two per-protocol exclusions were due to prior hepatitis A booster vaccination and travel to India between visits, rather than stated absence of outcome measurements.  
   - For a continuous outcome such as GMT, outcome availability for approximately 97% of randomized participants would generally be regarded as “nearly all” under RoB 2.  
   - No differential pattern of missing serology by randomized group, nor reasons suggesting that missing titres depended on their unobserved values, was reported. Therefore, **low risk of bias** is appropriate.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Anti-HAV antibody titre was measured using the same quantitative laboratory method in both groups. Although the study was open-label and laboratory assessor blinding was not reported, the outcome is an objective assay-based measurement with little scope for knowledge of treatment allocation to influence the result.  
   
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The outcome was pre-specified: “**The primary evaluation criterion was the comparison of the anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) for both vaccines 1 month post-vaccination.**”  
   - Anti-HAV concentrations were measured before vaccination and at 1 month using the modified HAVAB™ assay, with samples evaluated by “**Merck Research Laboratories (Pennsylvania, USA)**.”  
   - The article describes a quantitative method based on a standard curve using WHO standard serum immune globulin, with results expressed in mIU/mL and measurement down to 6 mIU/mL.  
   - Both intervention groups underwent blood sampling at the same planned follow-up interval—21 to 35 days after vaccination—and the same assay was used. There is no indication that outcome measurement or ascertainment differed between groups.  
   - Blinding of laboratory personnel is not reported. However, the anti-HAV titre is an objective laboratory measurement, and assessment is unlikely to be influenced materially by knowledge of booster assignment. Hence, **low risk of bias**.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** The anti-HAV GMT at 1 month was identified as the primary evaluation criterion, which is reassuring. However, no dated protocol or sufficiently detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan is available in the supplied material to verify that the reported per-protocol analysis and specific result were finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. In addition, both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed but only the per-protocol results were reported.  
   
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The report prospectively frames the endpoint as primary: “**The primary evaluation criterion was the comparison of the anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) for both vaccines 1 month post-vaccination.**” The timing and measurement method are also clearly described.  
   - Nonetheless, the article does not provide a protocol, statistical analysis plan, or date-stamped trial-registration information that establishes the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded data were available.  
   - Importantly, the authors state: “**Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed**” but that the results “**were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported**.” Thus, more than one eligible analysis was conducted, while the numerical intention-to-treat GMT comparison is not presented.  
   - The statement that results were “close enough” does not establish that the per-protocol analysis was selected because it was more favourable or statistically significant. Therefore, there is insufficient evidence for a high-risk judgement. However, the absence of the ITT numerical result and lack of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan leave **some concerns** about selection of the reported result.

**Assessment scope:** individually randomized, three-arm parallel-group educational trial; outcome: **performance**, operationalized as the sum of correct answers to 10 spinal-fracture anatomy questions (and, secondarily, time to complete these questions). The assessment concerns the effect of **assignment** to the teaching modules at baseline (e.g., 3D-printing model versus CT/3D-image module).

1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns, because the sequence was reported to be computer-generated, and measured baseline characteristics appear reasonably balanced, but allocation concealment was not described.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The report states: “**Students were randomized into three groups using a computer program**.” This supports a random allocation sequence.  
   - However, there is no information on who generated the sequence, whether allocation was centrally administered, or whether allocation was concealed from personnel enrolling students. Thus, it is unclear whether upcoming assignments could have been foreseen or influenced.  
   - The group sizes were equal (40 students per group), and reported sex distribution, age, and years in medical school were broadly comparable across groups (Table 2). There is no evident baseline imbalance suggesting a failure of randomization.  
   - Under RoB 2, absence of information on allocation concealment, despite apparently random sequence generation and no concerning baseline imbalance, leads to **some concerns** rather than low risk of bias.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to the teaching module.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - Participants and teachers were necessarily aware of the assigned educational module: CT images, manipulable 3D images, or physical 3D-printed models. Therefore, blinding was not feasible.  
   - However, for the effect of assignment, awareness alone is not sufficient for bias. RoB 2 requires evidence that deviations from the protocol arose because of the trial context, were unbalanced between groups, and were likely to affect performance.  
   - The intervention procedures were delivered as intended: “**A teacher provided a detailed interpretation of the fracture in each group**.” The report does not describe differential co-interventions, crossovers between teaching modules, or protocol departures caused by participant or teacher awareness of allocation.  
   - The analysis appears consistent with the assigned groups. All 120 randomized students were included, and the authors report analysis by teaching module and sex using two-way ANOVA. There is no indication of an as-treated analysis or post-randomization exclusion.  
   - Although the interventions could not be blinded, no evidence indicates deviations attributable to the trial context that would bias the effect of assignment.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The study explicitly reports complete retention: “**a total of 120 medical students … participated, and no one dropped out**.”  
   - Each group retained its full randomized sample: 40 in CT, 40 in 3D, and 40 in 3Dp. Table 3 reports performance data for all participants, stratified by sex: 22/18 in CT, 21/19 in 3D, and 22/18 in 3Dp.  
   - Therefore, outcome data for the performance score were available for all randomized participants. There is no indication of missing test scores, missing timing data, or exclusions from the reported analysis.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - Performance was assessed using the same examination for all groups: “**each student answered 10 questions regarding spinal fractures**,” with the “**answers to each question and the required time … recorded**.”  
   - The 10 questions had pre-specified objectively correct answers (Table 1), covering identification of vertebral structures and fractures. Scoring the number of correct responses does not appear to require subjective assessor judgement.  
   - The outcome ascertainment method was comparable between groups: all students completed the same examination at the end of the class, and an invigilator recorded the assignment/completion time.  
   - Although students knew their assigned learning modality, their awareness is unlikely to affect whether a response is scored correct when scoring uses an answer key.  
   - The examination included “many ‘3D images’.” This may be relevant to the educational construct or applicability of the test, but it does not demonstrate differential outcome measurement between randomized groups: the same test and scoring process were used for every group. There is no evidence that the measurement method itself was invalid or differentially applied.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The publication identifies several performance-related results: total correct-answer score, time to complete the questions, results by sex, and four student evaluation outcomes. It also reports multiple pairwise comparisons among the three teaching modules. These provide opportunities for selective choice of outcome measure, time metric, subgroup analysis, or statistical comparison.  
   - The article describes the outcomes and analysis in the Methods section, but it does not state that these analyses followed a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before access to unblinded outcome data.  
   - The material provided does not contain sufficient dated registration/protocol information to verify that the specific performance result assessed—such as the total score, its time point, its treatment of sex, and the selected pairwise comparison—was pre-specified before outcome data were available.  
   - There is no direct evidence that the reported score was selected because it was favourable, so high risk is not justified. Nevertheless, in the absence of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan and given multiple plausible performance measures and analyses, RoB 2 indicates **some concerns**.

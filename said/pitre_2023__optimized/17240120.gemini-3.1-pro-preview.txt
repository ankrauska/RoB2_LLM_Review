Based on the RoB 2.0 tool and the provided trial report, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome "time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment" considering the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect).

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** The trial used an adequate random sequence generation method and concealed allocation, with no baseline imbalances indicating a problem with randomization.
*   **b. Rationale:** The authors state that patients were assigned "according to a computer-generated randomisation list." Allocation was adequately concealed as "details of the randomisation series were unknown to any of the investigators and were contained in a set of sealed opaque envelopes." Furthermore, baseline characteristics (Table 1 and Table 2) were well-balanced between the escitalopram and placebo groups.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Participants and study personnel were effectively blinded, and the data were analyzed using an appropriate intention-to-treat (ITT) strategy. 
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial report specifies that "All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study" and "Study medications were tablets for oral administration, of identical appearance, taste or smell." The authors confirm that "All efficacy analyses in the double-blind treatment period were conducted using the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, which consisted of all randomised patients who took at least one dose of trial medication and had at least one post-randomisation efficacy assessment." The exclusion of two randomized patients who did not actually meet eligibility criteria is an acceptable post-randomization exclusion under RoB 2 guidance.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** The proportion of missing outcome data is low, balanced between groups, and unlikely to bias the time-to-event (survival) analysis.
*   **b. Rationale:** During the 24-week double-blind period, the overall withdrawal rate for reasons other than relapse (censored patients) was low and comparable between arms: "7.9% for patients treated with escitalopram (13 patients), and 8.9% for patients treated with placebo (14 patients)." Because these rates are small and balanced, there is no evidence that missingness or censoring depends on the true value of the outcome. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** The outcome was measured using an appropriate, standard scale by assessors who were unaware of the intervention assignments.
*   **b. Rationale:** Relapse was clearly defined as "an increase in the Y-BOCS total score of ≥5 points from the time of randomisation, or an unsatisfactory treatment effect." The Y-BOCS is an appropriate and validated measure. Assessors remained unaware of the intervention assignment because "All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study," preventing knowledge of the intervention from influencing the assessment.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** The reported primary analysis aligns precisely with the prospectively defined analysis plan, and there is no indication of selective reporting.
*   **b. Rationale:** The authors explicitly state, "The prospectively defined primary analysis of efficacy was the time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment." The results section transparently reports this exact analysis using the log-rank test and Cox proportional hazards model as planned. There is no evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on the statistical significance or direction of the findings.

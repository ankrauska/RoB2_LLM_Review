# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Women's Burden and Distress (measured by RSS and GHQ)
## Effect of Interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to lack of information about allocation concealment.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Sequence generation (adequate):** "Participants were assigned randomly via random number tables to one of three treatment conditions" — this constitutes a clearly random method (signalling question 1.1 = Yes).
- **Allocation concealment (no information):** The paper provides no description of the mechanism used to conceal the allocation sequence from those enrolling participants (e.g., no mention of sequential envelopes, central randomization, or any safeguard against foreknowledge of assignment). Signalling question 1.2 = No information.
- **Baseline imbalance:** "One-way ANOVAs showed that at pre-treatment the women's GHQ, RSS... did not differ significantly across conditions" and demographic variables (age, years married, children) also did not differ. This is compatible with chance (signalling question 1.3 = No).
- Per Table 4 of the RoB2 algorithm: Random = Yes, Concealment = No information, Imbalance = No → this combination maps to **"Some concerns"** (not "Low"), because allocation concealment cannot be confirmed as adequate.

---

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, driven by lack of blinding of participants/personnel inherent to psychotherapy trials, without clear evidence that this produced outcome-affecting deviations from protocol.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Participants and personnel awareness:** Women and therapists were necessarily unblinded to which of the three psychological treatments was being delivered (talk therapy conditions cannot be masked). Signalling questions 2.1 and 2.2 = Yes.
- **Deviations arising from trial context:** There is no explicit evidence of contamination or trial-context-driven deviations from the specified protocols affecting the burden/distress outcome. The manual describes structured content differences between arms delivered by trained/supervised therapists ("Therapy sessions were videotaped and then reviewed at weekly supervision sessions... to ensure adherence to the treatment protocol"), suggesting reasonable fidelity. However, no formal fidelity-check data are reported, so the answer to signalling question 2.3 is "No information/Probably no."
- **Analysis approach:** Analyses used available-case data at each time point ("participant numbers for analyses presented below vary slightly") rather than full ITT with imputation for all 61 randomized women; however, per RoB2 guidance, exclusion due to *missing outcome data* (rather than non-adherence to protocol) is addressed under the "missing outcome data" domain, not here. There is no evidence of a naïve "per-protocol" or "as-treated" analysis being substituted for this outcome — women were analysed according to original group assignment regardless of treatment engagement (including the "6/21 males engaged" issue, which affected the male partner's participation, not the woman's assigned counselling condition).
- Given unblinded delivery but no clear indication of protocol-inconsistent deviations materially affecting this outcome, and an analysis approach consistent with assigned groups, this leads to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "High," per Table 6 (aware = Yes/Yes; deviations = No information).

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias due to substantial attrition, an available-case analytic approach, and differential/informative reasons for dropout.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Extent of missing data:** "Sixty-one participants were allocated randomly... Forty-seven participants completed treatment and 44 of these participants provided assessed data at 6-month follow-up. This represents an overall attrition of 22% of participants." This is not a "small" proportion under RoB2 guidance, and outcome data are not available for "nearly all" randomized participants (signalling question 3.1 = No).
- **No evidence the result is unbiased despite missingness:** No imputation, no sensitivity analysis, and no correction methods were reported. The paper explicitly notes the analysis N's "vary slightly" reflecting available-case analysis (signalling question 3.2 = No).
- **Missingness plausibly related to true outcome value:** The authors state: "most of these women reported they had left their partners soon after beginning the programme. Many women who dropped out told us that the sessions they attended assisted them to leave a relationship in which they had been very unhappy for many years... all the women who dropped out from treatment declined further assessment." This strongly suggests that dropout is related to the women's underlying distress/relationship trajectory — i.e., missingness is very likely related to the true value of the burden/distress outcome (signalling question 3.3 = Yes; 3.4 = Yes, likely related).
- Per Table 10, this pattern (incomplete data, no correction, missingness likely dependent on true outcome value) maps to **"High risk of bias."**

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, because the outcome is participant-reported in an unblinded design, but there is no strong evidence that assessment was systematically influenced by knowledge of assigned intervention.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Appropriateness of measurement method:** The RSS ("test–retest reliability of r = 0.85") and GHQ ("Cronbach's alpha = 0.93... established validity") are validated, appropriate instruments for this outcome (signalling question 4.1 = No).
- **Comparability of ascertainment across groups:** The same instruments were administered identically to all groups at the same time points (pre-, post-, 6-month follow-up), so ascertainment methods did not differ between arms (signalling question 4.2 = No).
- **Outcome assessor = the participant (unblinded):** Because RSS/GHQ are self-report measures, the woman herself is the outcome assessor, and she cannot be blinded to which of the three interventions she received (signalling question 4.3 = Yes).
- **Potential for influence:** Knowledge of receiving a "structured behavioural" therapy (stress management/AFCT) versus "supportive counselling" could plausibly influence self-reported distress via expectancy or demand effects (signalling question 4.4 = Yes).
- **Likelihood of actual influence:** There is no strong indication (e.g., no dramatic divergence attributable to expectancy alone, and results were modest/mixed with "trends" rather than strong, consistent superiority: "there was...a trend for a time × condition interaction, F(4, 80)=2.08, p=0.09"). This suggests it is possible but not clearly demonstrated that assessment was strongly influenced by knowledge of allocation (signalling question 4.5 = Probably no/No information).
- This pattern (assessor aware, could be influenced, but unlikely/unclear that it was) maps to **"Some concerns"** rather than "High," per Table 12.

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, due to insufficient information to confirm that RSS/GHQ results were reported in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registration record (doc_id: 4dab5cab-2b44-42f8-8559-2dd9073b18b5) and the published article both identify women's burden/distress (via RSS and GHQ) as core outcomes consistent with the stated study aim: "To evaluate the effectiveness of three approaches to assisting the female partners of male problem drinkers with the stress imposed by the male's drinking." Both RSS and GHQ are fully reported at all three time points, with no evidence of selective omission of a measurement instrument or time point (signalling question 5.2 = Probably no).
- Both measures were also analysed in a single, consistent way (repeated-measures ANOVA on raw scores, plus supplementary Jacobson & Truax reliable/clinically significant change analyses), and both approaches are reported transparently rather than only one being selected based on favorable results (signalling question 5.3 = Probably no).
- However, there is no clear evidence (e.g., no reference to a date-stamped, publicly registered detailed statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinded data availability) confirming that this reporting sequence was pre-specified rather than developed post hoc. This is a common limitation in older trials (this appears to be a relatively early trial, pre-dating routine trial registration requirements), and the absence of a protocol/SAP for full verification prevents a "Low risk" judgement (signalling question 5.1 = No information).
- Per Table 14, "No information" on pre-specification combined with "Probably no" on selection from multiple outcomes/analyses maps to **"Some concerns."**

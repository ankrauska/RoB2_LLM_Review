1. **Bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The trial used a centralized randomization system, with random allocation in a 1:1 ratio and stratification on important prognostic factors. Although there was an imbalance in the proportion aged ≥70 years, this alone is plausibly compatible with chance and does not provide convincing evidence of a failed randomization process.

   b. **Rationale:** The report states that, after eligibility assessment, patients were “**randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System**” and that randomization was stratified by “**intensity of prior antileukemic therapy**” and “**whether remission was ever attained**.” A “**block size of four patients was used within each stratum**.” Centralized allocation provides adequate allocation concealment despite use of fixed blocks.

   Baseline characteristics were broadly similar between groups. There was a higher proportion of patients aged ≥70 years in the elacytarabine group (30% versus 18%; *P* = .007), but median age was similar (62 versus 63 years), and other key AML prognostic characteristics—including performance status, cytogenetic risk, prior intensive therapy, disease status (relapsed/refractory), and number of previous regimens—were reasonably balanced. Moreover, adjustment for age did not materially change the estimated treatment effect for overall survival (unadjusted HR 0.97; age-adjusted HR 0.98). This imbalance is not sufficiently extreme to indicate that the randomization process was compromised.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention at baseline.

   b. **Rationale:** This was an open-label trial: “**The study was an open-label, randomized phase III study**.” Thus, participants, carers, and treating investigators were aware of assigned treatment. However, for the assignment-to-intervention effect, lack of blinding causes bias only if deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context, were unbalanced, and were likely to affect overall survival.

   The control regimen was selected before randomization: “**To avoid bias, investigators chose and recorded a control treatment for each patient before random assignment.**” This is an important safeguard against post-randomization selection of a more or less intensive comparator based on allocated treatment.

   Some protocol deviations occurred: six control-arm patients received treatment not according to the prespecified investigator’s choice. However, these deviations were few relative to the full ITT population (6/381) and there is no evidence that they resulted from the trial context in a manner likely to materially affect overall survival. Most patients received the assigned dose intensity: “**Ninety-five percent of the patients treated with one course of treatment and 77% of patients treated with a second course received at least 80% of the assigned dose(s).**”

   Most importantly, the primary overall-survival analysis was performed in the full intention-to-treat population: “**The intention-to-treat population comprised 191 patients in the elacytarabine group and 190 in the control arm.**” The reported primary result is explicitly based on this population. Therefore, the analysis appropriately estimates the effect of assignment to elacytarabine versus investigator-selected control treatment.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Overall survival data were available for nearly all randomized patients. Of 381 randomized participants, only five were lost to follow-up or withdrawn before the analysis cutoff: one in the elacytarabine group and four in the control group. The patient-disposition figure indicates 148 deaths and one participant lost to follow-up in the elacytarabine group, and 154 deaths and four participants lost to follow-up/withdrawn in the control group.

   The analysis plan states: “**For patients confirmed to be alive on the cutoff date, this date was used to censor for survival and response. For patients lost to follow-up before the cutoff date, censoring was done on the last date the patient was known to be alive.**” Administrative censoring at the event-analysis cutoff is appropriate for a survival analysis. The very small number of losses (5/381, approximately 1.3%) is unlikely to have materially affected the HR for overall survival (0.97; 95% CI, 0.77 to 1.22), particularly given that 302 events occurred.

   The report also states that the groups were “**well-balanced with respect to patients who were randomly assigned but not treated, those censored for stem-cell transplantation (SCT), and those lost to follow-up**.” Although a secondary analysis censored patients at SCT, the primary reported overall-survival analysis was the ITT analysis without SCT censoring; thus, the primary result is not materially threatened by informative censoring due to transplantation.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Overall survival is an objective outcome—death from any cause—with little or no scope for subjective judgement or differential assessment by intervention group. Although the trial was open label, knowledge of treatment allocation is not plausibly able to influence whether a participant dies.

   Overall survival was assessed using standard time-to-event methods: “**OS was evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier method and was compared using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was used to calculate the HR and 95% CI.**” The same survival ascertainment and analysis procedures were applied to both groups. There is no indication that death ascertainment differed between elacytarabine and investigator’s-choice groups.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** Overall survival was clearly identified as the primary endpoint in both the publication and the trial description: “**The primary end point was overall survival (OS).**” The published principal analysis is reported in the ITT population, with a numerical effect estimate and confidence interval: median OS 3.5 versus 3.3 months; HR 0.97 (95% CI, 0.77 to 1.22; *P* = .96). This is consistent with reporting the prespecified primary outcome.

   However, the available information does not provide a detailed, date-stamped statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. The registry identifier is reported (NCT01147939), but no registry details or full protocol/SAP content are available here to verify in sufficient detail the prespecified survival-analysis population, censoring rules, covariate adjustment, or handling of SCT. The publication reports both the main ITT survival analysis and an additional analysis censoring at SCT, as well as an age-adjusted analysis. These additional analyses are not inherently inappropriate, but without a pre-finalized SAP it cannot be fully excluded that choices among eligible analyses were influenced by the observed data.

   There is no direct evidence that the reported primary ITT overall-survival result was selectively chosen, and it is the analysis most appropriate for the assignment effect. Nevertheless, absence of sufficiently detailed prospective analysis intentions precludes a low-risk judgement under RoB 2.

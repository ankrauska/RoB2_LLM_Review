# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0)
**Outcome assessed:** Compliance (assessed via pill/packet counts and pharmacy refill records at 6 weeks, 12 weeks, 1 year, and 2 years)
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The allocation sequence was generated using an adequate random method: *"Patients were randomized to telephone contact or no telephone contact using a computer-generated list of random numbers."* This supports a "Yes"/"Probably yes" answer to signalling question 1.1 (sequence random).

However, there is no description of allocation concealment procedures (e.g., sequentially numbered sealed envelopes, central randomization, or any mechanism preventing foreknowledge of assignment). This constitutes "No information" for signalling question 1.2.

Baseline characteristics were reported as comparable between groups (*"Baseline characteristics were similar for both groups (Table 1)"*), and there is no indication of imbalance suggestive of a flawed randomization process (signalling question 1.3 = No).

Per the RoB 2 algorithm, the combination of adequate sequence generation (Yes), no information on concealment (No information), and no baseline imbalance (No) maps to a judgement of **Some concerns**, because the absence of information on allocation concealment cannot be excluded as a source of bias, particularly in a small (n=30), single-center, unblinded trial.

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Participants and the pharmacist delivering the intervention were necessarily aware of group assignment (telephone contact vs no telephone contact), since blinding of this behavioral intervention is not feasible: *"A pharmacist telephoned patients at their home every week for 12 weeks... Patients were questioned about when and where prescriptions were filled... and specific reasons for noncompliance when applicable."*

However, there is no evidence that this awareness led to deviations from the intended intervention that were inconsistent with the trial protocol and arose from the trial context (e.g., contamination, co-intervention, or non-protocol care). Both groups received identical drug regimens (*"All patients received lovastatin 20 mg/day and colestipol 5 g twice/day"*) and identical baseline counseling and dietary instruction; the only difference between arms was the intended intervention itself (telephone contact), not an unintended deviation. No cross-contamination (e.g., control-group patients also receiving informal telephone counseling) was reported.

The analysis appears consistent with intention-to-treat principles: all 30 randomized patients were retained and analyzed (*"Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up"*), with no reported post-randomization exclusions.

Given no meaningful deviations attributable to trial context and an appropriate analytic approach, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias** for the effect of assignment to intervention.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Outcome data (compliance) were available for all randomized participants at all assessment time points. The article explicitly states: *"Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up."* Compliance data at 6, 12 weeks, 1 year, and 2 years appear complete for both groups (Tables 2 and 4), with no participants excluded from the compliance analyses. Because data are available for essentially all randomized participants, missingness could not have materially biased the result, supporting a judgement of **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Compliance was measured using relatively objective methods: *"Compliance was determined by pill and packet counts (not performed within the patient's view) at the 6- and 12-week clinic visits... pharmacies at which patients filled their prescriptions were contacted at 1 and 2 years to document refill information."* These methods (physical pill/packet counts and pharmacy refill records) involve minimal subjective judgment by the assessor and are not directly self-reported by participants, reducing the potential for detection bias due to unblinded assessment.

While there is no explicit statement that those performing pill counts or contacting pharmacies were blinded to group assignment, the outcome measurement relies on verifiable records (prescription refill data, physical counts) rather than assessor interpretation or participant self-report, making it unlikely that knowledge of group assignment influenced the recorded values. The method of measurement was appropriate and consistent between groups (same thresholds: >20% pills returned or <80% of prescriptions refilled = noncompliant, applied identically to both arms). Therefore, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** There is no indication that a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial protocol (finalized before unblinded outcome data were available) was available for comparison against the published report; no trial registration information was identifiable to confirm the timing or completeness of pre-specified outcome measures and analyses for compliance. This is consistent with the era of publication (2000), prior to widespread trial registration requirements, and no registry entry could be located to verify pre-specification.

That said, the compliance outcome appears to have been measured and reported consistently and comprehensively across all pre-defined time points (6 weeks, 12 weeks, 1 year, 2 years) and using consistent, pre-defined thresholds (≥80% refill/≤20% returned pills) for both study drugs (lovastatin and colestipol) in both groups, with no evidence of selective omission of unfavorable results for this outcome.

Given the absence of a retrievable protocol or registration to confirm that the reported compliance analyses match pre-specified intentions, but no overt evidence of selective outcome/analysis reporting, this domain is judged **Some concerns** (per the RoB 2 algorithm, "No information" on pre-specification defaults to "Some concerns" in the absence of evidence of selection).

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Initial Pain

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgement: Some concerns**

### a. Rationale for Judgement
The trial states that "insertion of either a 0.014 or 0.016 inch wire was by random selection," but provides no further detail on the method used to generate the allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number tables) or on how allocation concealment was achieved. There is also no explicit statistical comparison of baseline characteristics (e.g., age, gender distribution, malocclusion severity) between the two wire-size groups, although the reported group compositions (0.014 inch: 29 boys/27 girls; 0.016 inch: 23 boys/30 girls) do not suggest an obvious problem.

### b. Supporting Quotes/Reasoning
- "Insertion of either a 0.014 or 0.016 inch wire was by random selection." — No detail on sequence generation.
- "The patients were randomly selected for insertion of the 0.014 or 0.016 inch wires." — No mention of concealment procedures (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation).
- No baseline comparison table is presented specifically contrasting the 0.014 vs 0.016 inch groups on prognostic factors other than raw counts by sex.

Because there is no information on sequence randomness or concealment, and no evidence of problematic imbalance, this maps to "Some concerns" per the RoB2 algorithm (no information on concealment/sequence generation, no evidence of imbalance).

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

### a. Rationale for Judgement
This trial compares two archwire sizes (0.014 vs 0.016 inch), a single intervention delivered at the time of bracket bonding, with no scope for participant self-administration or non-adherence during follow-up. Patients are highly unlikely to be able to distinguish between the two nickel-titanium wire gauges by sensation or sight, so patient-level "blinding" is effectively preserved. While the treating orthodontist is not blinded (necessarily selects/places the wire), there is no evidence of any deviation from the protocol (e.g., additional co-interventions, wire changes) arising from trial context that differed between groups. All patients were ligated identically ("Following placement of the archwires they were ligated to all teeth") and no extra-oral appliances were used. Analysis appears to include all randomized/enrolled patients per group (ITT-consistent).

### b. Supporting Quotes/Reasoning
- "No extra-oral appliances, palatal arches or quad-helix appliances were used during the experimental period." — Co-interventions balanced/excluded.
- "Following placement of the archwires they were ligated to all teeth." — Identical procedure across groups.
- No report of crossover, protocol deviation, or differential clinical management between wire-size groups.

## 3. Missing outcome data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

### a. Rationale for Judgement
All enrolled patients completed and returned the questionnaire capturing the initial pain outcome; there is no evidence of loss to follow-up or missing responses for this outcome.

### b. Supporting Quotes/Reasoning
- "The questionnaires were completed by all 109 patients and returned at the following appointment."
- Table 1 reports data on the full complement of patients (56 in the 0.014 inch group, 53 in the 0.016 inch group), consistent with full data availability, with no participants unaccounted for.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

### a. Rationale for Judgement
"Initial pain" is a participant-reported outcome, measured identically between the two study groups using the same questionnaire item ("Question 1 asked the time at which pain was first perceived after archwire insertion"). Because the two wires differ only in gauge diameter (0.014 vs 0.016 inch) — a difference not readily perceptible to a patient — participants (the outcome assessors, since this is self-reported) are very unlikely to have been aware of their allocated wire size, minimizing the potential for differential reporting bias based on knowledge of intervention received.

### b. Supporting Quotes/Reasoning
- "Question 1 asked the time at which pain was first perceived after archwire insertion." — identical, standardized measurement method used for both groups.
- No mention that patients were informed of their specific wire size, and the two NiTi wires are visually and physically very similar, making patient awareness of allocation implausible.
- The measurement instrument (a structured questionnaire with a validated VAS component) is not inherently biased or insensitive: "This method is widely used for measuring pain and has been described by other investigators as being sensitive and reliable."

---

## 5. Selective reporting

**Judgement: Some concerns**

### a. Rationale for Judgement
No trial registration or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is identifiable/available for this study (consistent with the era of publication, prior to widespread mandatory trial registration). Without a pre-specified protocol, it is not possible to confirm that the reported "initial pain" outcome measurement/analysis was not selected from among multiple possible analyses on the basis of the results. However, there is no direct evidence of selective outcome reporting: the initial pain outcome is reported in full, using a single, pre-defined single-item question ("time at which pain was first perceived"), analysed consistently across both wire groups and time points, and results (including non-significant findings) are transparently presented (e.g., "There were no statistically significant differences between the groups" for initial pain).

### b. Supporting Quotes/Reasoning
- "Question 1 asked the time at which pain was first perceived after archwire insertion." — single, unambiguous outcome measurement with limited scope for selective measurement-switching.
- "Initiation of pain was perceived 2 hours after wire insertion in both groups. There were no statistically significant differences between the groups." — result reported regardless of statistical significance, reducing (but not eliminating, given absence of a registered protocol) concern about selective analysis.
- No trial registry entry or protocol was identified/available to confirm the analysis plan was finalized prior to unblinded data availability, leading to a "No information" response to the relevant signalling question, which defaults to "Some concerns."

---

## Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

**Rationale:** No individual domain was judged to be at high risk of bias for the "initial pain" outcome. However, "Some concerns" were identified in two domains — bias arising from the randomization process (due to lack of reported detail on sequence generation and allocation concealment) and bias in selection of the reported result (due to the absence of an identifiable pre-specified/registered analysis plan). Per RoB 2.0 guidance, a study with "some concerns" in at least one domain, and not at high risk of bias in any domain, is judged overall to raise **some concerns** regarding risk of bias for this outcome.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was adequately generated and concealed, and baseline differences appear compatible with chance.
b. Rationale: The trial report states that "The randomization was performed by a researcher not involved in the data collection" and "Participants and all research assistants and technicians responsible for running both the cohort study and the RCT were blinded to the study allocation." Baseline characteristics were generally balanced, with the exception of skin color, which is compatible with chance imbalance. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were blinded, minimizing the risk of deviations arising from the trial context.
b. Rationale: The study used a double-blind, placebo-controlled design. The authors note, "The capsules were identical regarding color, shape, size and packing." Furthermore, "Participants and all research assistants and technicians... were blinded to the study allocation." 

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: There was a substantial amount of missing outcome data (nearly 50% attrition), and missingness could plausibly depend on the true value of the outcome (depressive symptoms). 
b. Rationale: Out of 60 women randomized, only 32 completed the trial at the postpartum endpoint. While the authors conducted an intention-to-treat analysis (likely using mixed models), the dropout rate is exceptionally high. Reasons for dropping out could be related to mental health status or intervention tolerability (e.g., nausea), meaning missingness likely depends on the true outcome value, introducing high risk of bias.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome was measured appropriately and assessors (participants/researchers) were blinded.
b. Rationale: Depressive symptoms were assessed using the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS), a validated and appropriate tool. Since the trial successfully blinded participants and researchers, the assessment of the outcome was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: The trial was registered retrospectively, meaning there is no guarantee that the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.
b. Rationale: The trial publication notes, "Retrospectively registered on 24 May 2012", while enrollment began in 2009. The provided trial registration (NCT01660165) lists the study as an observational cohort rather than an RCT, and lists "Major depressive episode" as the primary outcome, rather than specifically the EPDS threshold. Without a pre-specified, prospectively registered protocol and statistical analysis plan, we cannot rule out selection of the reported result.

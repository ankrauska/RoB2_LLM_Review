# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Visual Acuity (5-year visit), Effect of Assignment to Intervention

---

### 1. Bias due to Randomization: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** This was a multicenter randomized clinical trial conducted by an experienced clinical trials network (DRCR.net), with methods described in detail in a prior primary publication (reference 1) rather than fully repeated in this 5-year report. While this article itself does not restate the sequence generation/allocation concealment methods, in the context of a large, well-resourced NIH-funded network trial with a detailed, publicly available protocol, it is reasonable to answer "Probably yes" to randomization and concealment signalling questions rather than "No information." Baseline characteristics of completers appear generally similar between groups (Table 1), and there is no indication of imbalance suggestive of a corrupted randomization process.

**b. Rationale:** "Random assignment to ranibizumab every 4 weeks... and either prompt or deferred (≥24 weeks) focal/grid laser treatment." The trial is a continuation of a previously published RCT (Diabetic Retinopathy Clinical Research Network, Ophthalmology 2010) with an established, peer-reviewed randomization protocol. No baseline imbalances in prognostic factors (age, DR severity, baseline VA) are reported between the two ranibizumab arms that would suggest randomization failure.

---

### 2. Deviations from Intended Intervention (Effect of Assignment to Intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Participants, treating clinicians, and personnel administering laser were not blinded to laser assignment (prompt vs. deferred), as this is inherent to the comparison itself and cannot be masked. However, deviations from intended intervention that were inconsistent with protocol were minimal and largely protocol-specified (e.g., deferred-group eyes could receive laser only per pre-defined criteria after 24 weeks). Analyses used all randomized participants in longitudinal models based on assigned group ("all available data"), consistent with an intention-to-treat approach.

**b. Rationale:** "Laser in the deferral group had to be delayed for at least 24 weeks... However, at or after 24 weeks, laser treatment could be given if there was persistent DME... that had not improved." This is protocol-consistent, not a deviation from intended intervention. Off-protocol non-protocol interventions were rare: "Other treatment for DME... was received by 9 (7%) of the completers in the prompt laser group (7 were per protocol... and 2 were not) and none in the deferred laser group." The longitudinal analyses "using all available visit data" and adjusting for baseline covariates represent an appropriate intention-to-treat-consistent analysis strategy.

---

### 3. Missing Outcome Data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** A substantial proportion of originally randomized participants (excluding deaths) did not have 5-year outcome data (24% and 26% in the prompt and deferred groups respectively), largely due to non-consent to the extension phase rather than active dropout during follow-up. Baseline characteristics differed between completers and non-completers (age, prior PRP, lens status, DR severity), raising the possibility that missingness could be related to prognostic factors that influence visual outcomes. However, the proportion of missing data was similar between the two groups (24% vs. 26%), and the authors used longitudinal models incorporating all available follow-up data and adjusted for identified imbalances, which partially mitigates (but does not eliminate) concern.

**b. Rationale:** "Excluding deaths, the 5-year completion rate was 76%... in the ranibizumab + prompt laser group and 74%... in the ranibizumab + deferred laser group." "Some imbalances between completers versus non-completers were noted (age, prior PRP, lens status, diabetic retinopathy severity level)." The authors acknowledge: "it is not possible to know whether incomplete data were related to unobserved factors that could have affected the results in either direction." Since the proportions missing are similar between arms and reasons appear unrelated to differential outcome-related dropout (mainly non-consent to extension), but the magnitude of missingness is large (~25%) and baseline imbalances exist, this warrants "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

### 4. Measurement of the Outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Visual acuity, the primary outcome, was assessed by certified, masked examiners using a standardized protocol (E-ETDRS), and outcome assessors were blinded to treatment assignment despite the inability to blind participants/treating clinicians to laser status. This is an observer-reported outcome involving some judgement, but blinding of the assessor substantially reduces risk of differential measurement error.

**b. Rationale:** "Some of the strengths of this study include a study design involving randomization of the two treatment groups at baseline to try to minimize confounding and bias, and masking of the visual acuity examiners and OCT technicians." Because outcome assessors (visual acuity examiners) were unaware of intervention assignment, assessment of the outcome was unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

---

### 5. Selective Reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The outcome of visual acuity change at 5 years appears to have been pre-specified as a primary outcome consistent with the trial's original protocol and prior publications (1-, 2-, and 3-year reports from the same trial used the same outcome definitions and analytic approach). The statistical methods (longitudinal models, letter-score change, categorical thresholds for improvement/worsening) are consistent across all published time points from this trial series, suggesting no evidence of selective outcome or analysis reporting based on results.

**b. Rationale:** "A longitudinal analysis was performed to compare visual acuity and OCT central subfield (CSF) thickness change from baseline for the 16- through the 260-week ('5-year') visits... using continuous time models... adjusting for baseline visual acuity." This pre-specified analytic method mirrors that used in the earlier (1-, 2-, 3-year) reports of the same trial (references 1–3), and multiple outcome metrics (mean change, ≥10/≥15 letter improvement, ≤10 letter worsening) are reported comprehensively and consistently, rather than selectively favoring one group. The protocol is publicly available ("The protocol is available on the DRCR.net website"), permitting verification of pre-specification, and the trial registration and protocol are consistent with the reported outcome.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

**Rationale:** While randomization, deviations from intended intervention, outcome measurement, and selective reporting domains are judged at low risk of bias, the substantial (~25%) missing outcome data at the 5-year visit, coupled with documented baseline imbalances between completers and non-completers, raises sufficient concern in the missing outcome data domain to warrant an overall judgement of "Some concerns" for this result. No domain was judged to be at high risk of bias.

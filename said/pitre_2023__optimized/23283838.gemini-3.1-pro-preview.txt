**1. Bias due to randomization: Low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The allocation sequence was random, allocation was adequately concealed, and baseline differences appear compatible with chance.
* **Rationale:** The trial reports that "Participants were randomly assigned to 250mg of DCS or placebo in identical-appearing capsules by a data manager who had no interaction with participants." While there was a baseline difference in age, the authors note it could not explain group effects, and the use of a data manager and identical capsules ensures sequence concealment. 

**2. Deviations from intended intervention: Low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Participants and personnel were unaware of intervention groups, and there were no deviations from intended interventions that arose because of the trial context. 
* **Rationale:** The study utilized "identical-appearing capsules" and assessed the adequacy of the blind, stating "neither participants nor experimenters were able to discern assignment (ps > .15)." Thus, successful blinding prevented deviations related to the trial context.

**3. Missing outcome data: Low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for nearly all participants, and missingness was balanced between groups with reasons unlikely to introduce substantial bias.
* **Rationale:** Of the 34 randomized participants, four (2 DCS, 2 placebo) did not return for Visit 2 (2 unreachable, 2 preferred not to continue). The missingness is balanced between arms and the proportion of missing data (~12%) is small enough that it is unlikely to have a substantial impact on the estimated effect.

**4. Measurement of the outcome: Low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The outcome assessors (participants) were blinded to the intervention received, so assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
* **Rationale:** The outcome is subjective anxiety measured by the SUDS scale. Since participants self-reported their anxiety but were successfully blinded to their assignment (DCS vs. placebo), their knowledge of the intervention could not have influenced their subjective assessment.

**5. Selective reporting: Some concerns**
* **Judgement:** While the outcome is clearly defined and reported, there is no explicit mention of a pre-specified analysis plan or trial registration finalized before unblinded data were available.
* **Rationale:** The authors state, "Our primary measure of DCS’s exposure-facilitating effect was between-session habituation." However, without a date-stamped trial registry or protocol available to verify the pre-specified primary outcome and analysis methods, we cannot definitively rule out selection of the reported result.

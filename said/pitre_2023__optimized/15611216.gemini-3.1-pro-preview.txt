Based on the RoB 2.0 tool and the provided trial publication, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of Agitation Score (Table 4) considering the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline:

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
*   **Judgement:** The sequence generation and allocation concealment methods are not described in sufficient detail, and there was a statistically significant baseline imbalance in a key prognostic factor.
*   **Rationale:** The trial states, "We randomly assigned participants to study groups in time blocks of 1 week... We also randomized time blocks as treatment or control condition weeks." It is unclear if allocation was adequately concealed. Furthermore, there was a significant baseline imbalance in arousal imbalance (a key factor related to the intervention) between the groups (*p* = .012), which the authors acknowledge: "Because random assignment did not equalize the groups on arousal imbalance, we controlled for this variable in the analysis." 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** The analysis excluded participants who did not receive the intervention, violating intention-to-treat (ITT) principles, with a potential for substantial impact on the results.
*   **Rationale:** The authors explicitly state they excluded randomized participants who did not receive the treatment: "10 were dropped from the study for not being given the intervention... A participant had to have been given at least one of the prescribed changes in activity schedule... to be included in the analysis." Evaluating the *effect of assignment to intervention* requires an ITT analysis (including all randomized participants regardless of whether they received the intervention). Excluding 10 participants post-randomization specifically because they didn't receive the intervention constitutes an inappropriate analysis with a substantial potential impact on the result.

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** A substantial proportion of randomized participants were excluded from the analysis, and the missingness is likely related to the true value of the outcome or the intervention assignment.
*   **Rationale:** Out of 102 eligible and consenting participants, the final sample analyzed was only 78. The text states: "Twelve died, 10 were dropped from the study for not being given the intervention, and 2 were excluded for erroneous or missing data." The exclusion of participants (specifically the 10 dropped because nurses were too short-staffed to deliver the intervention) represents missing outcome data that is directly linked to the trial context and intervention delivery, introducing a high risk of bias.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** The outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment, minimizing the risk of differential measurement error.
*   **Rationale:** The outcome of agitation was measured through direct observation by research assistants (RAs). The authors explicitly state, "Data collectors and subjects were blinded to treatment and control conditions." As long as blinding was maintained, the assessment of the outcome is unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
*   **Judgement:** The trialists changed their intended outcome measurement instrument during the study, raising concerns about selection of the reported result.
*   **Rationale:** The authors report changing their primary measurement tool after the study began because of reliability issues: "Our initial plan called for us to assess agitation by using the Agitation Behavior Mapping Instrument (ABMI)... We were, however, unable to achieve sufficiently consistent interrater reliability estimates... We then trained RAs to assess agitation by using a visual analog scale from 0 to 100." Without a pre-published, date-stamped statistical analysis plan finalizing this change before unblinded data was available, there are some concerns that the reported result might have been selected from multiple analyses or measurement iterations.

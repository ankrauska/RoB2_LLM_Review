**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The study reports using "sex-stratified randomization," indicating a random sequence was generated. Allocation concealment was adequate as "the allocation sequence was blinded through the use of sealed envelopes." Table 1 demonstrates that the groups were generally homogeneous at baseline with no significant differences in demographic or clinical pre-test variables, suggesting no severe problems with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The trial used a placebo ultrasound (sham US) for the control group, effectively blinding the patients to their assigned intervention ("blinding of patients and blind assessor"). Although the physiotherapists delivering the intervention were not blinded ("blinding of the physiotherapist who applied the treatment was not possible"), there is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, the authors explicitly state that "The analysis was done by intention to treat," indicating an appropriate analysis strategy for the effect of assignment. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants. The authors explicitly report that "During the study no patient was lost or dropped out." Therefore, there is no risk of bias due to missing outcome data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The method of measuring pain (Visual Analogue Scale - VAS) is appropriate and was applied comparably across groups. Both the patients (who report the VAS) and the outcome assessor were blinded ("A physiotherapist appraiser assessed the patients’ pain... another physiotherapist executed the interventions"; "blinding of patients and blind assessor"). Since the participants were unaware of their intervention status due to the sham ultrasound, their self-reported pain assessment is unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns
* **Rationale:** The study clearly reports the outcomes specified in the methods section (pain by VAS and algometer, and mobility by inclinometer) at the designated time points (10 sessions and discharge). However, without access to a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a time-stamped trial registry protocol finalized before unblinded data were available, it cannot be entirely ruled out that the reported analysis methods or specific data points were selected from multiple eligible options based on the results.

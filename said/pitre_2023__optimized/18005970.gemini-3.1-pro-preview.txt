**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The trial authors state that students were "randomised with a randomisation table," which provides a random component for sequence generation. However, there is no information provided regarding whether the allocation sequence was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions. A randomisation table can easily be viewed by personnel enrolling participants if not properly concealed. While Table 2 notes that there were no significant baseline differences between the Trial and Control groups within each campus (p>0.05 for all variables noted with 'n.s.'), the lack of information on allocation concealment raises some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Due to the nature of the educational interventions (different tutorial strategies), it was impossible to blind participants (students) or personnel (tutors). However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context (i.e., being unblinded did not lead to non-protocol educational interventions). Furthermore, all 144 participants who were randomized (82 in Trento and 62 in Udine) appear to have been analyzed in their originally assigned groups according to the participant flow (Figure 1), meaning an appropriate intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Data for the outcome were available for all randomized participants. The participant flow chart (Figure 1) and the text show that all 144 initially randomized participants (82 in Trento and 62 in Udine) completed the trial and were included in the Phase 2 analysis. The authors also note that "The group included was stable in the first six months probably because it was made up of young full-time students who had no other employment," guaranteeing attendance.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The method of measuring the outcome (identifying errors in diagnostic hypotheses based on a standardized simulated case history of "Valentina") was appropriate and comparable across all intervention groups. Furthermore, the outcome assessors were effectively blinded to the intervention assignments: "Two researchers did blind evaluation of the problems individualised by the students and these were compared to the 'standard'." Therefore, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: There is no trial registration or pre-specified statistical analysis plan referenced in the provided text to confirm that the data were analyzed strictly in accordance with a plan finalized before unblinded data were available. While there is no obvious indication that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on the p-value (the number of diagnostic errors was clearly defined as the main outcome upfront), the absence of a pre-registered protocol automatically results in at least 'some concerns' regarding selective reporting according to the RoB 2.0 guidance.

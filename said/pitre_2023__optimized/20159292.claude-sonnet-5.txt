# RoB 2.0 Assessment: COMICE Trial (Reoperation Rate Outcome)

**Preliminary considerations:**
- Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect), as specified
- Experimental: MRI + triple assessment; Comparator: triple assessment alone
- Outcome: Reoperation rate (repeat operation, further mastectomy within 6 months, or pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation)

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The trial used an adequate random sequence generation method (minimization with random element) and an appropriate, robust concealment mechanism (centralized telephone randomization). Baseline characteristics were well balanced, with no evidence of problems with the randomization process.

**b. Rationale:** "Patients were randomised to receive either MRI or no further imaging (no MRI) on a 1:1 basis, with a minimisation algorithm incorporating a random element." Minimization inherently includes a random component, satisfying the signalling question for random sequence generation.

Allocation concealment was robust: "Randomisation was administered by the University of Leeds Clinical Trials Research Unit's automated 24-hour telephone randomisation system, by either the research nurse or the consultant breast surgeon." This is a centralized, remote randomization system independent of the enrolling site, meeting the criteria for adequate concealment.

Table 1 shows baseline characteristics (age, breast density, menopausal status, contraceptive/HRT use) were closely balanced between groups (e.g., age ≥50 years: 77% vs 77%; breast density group 2: 88% vs 87%), with no evidence of imbalance suggestive of a failure of randomization.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** This was an open-label trial with no blinding of participants, personnel, or outcome assessors, which is unavoidable given the nature of the intervention (an imaging test). There is some evidence of deviations from the protocol (crossover), but there is no strong evidence that this arose from trial-context effects that biased the ITT estimate, and the analysis was intention-to-treat. However, the open-label design combined with a small amount of non-adherence and lack of certainty about the impact means "Some concerns" rather than "Low."

**b. Rationale:** This was explicitly "an open, parallel group trial" — participants, clinicians and (largely) outcome assessors were aware of intervention allocation. Answer to 2.1 and 2.2: Yes.

There was some crossover/non-adherence: "761 received MRI" out of 816 assigned (55 did not; reasons include technical failures, refusal, non-attendance), and in the no-MRI group "9 received MRI" (6 patient request, 3 clinician request). This indicates a degree of non-adherence but the numbers are small (<7% in MRI arm, ~1% in no-MRI arm).

There is no explicit indication that these deviations arose because of trial-context effects (e.g., participants seeking non-protocol interventions due to recruitment/consent processes) rather than logistical/technical reasons (e.g., "11 MRI technical failures," "10 cases of MRI not available before surgery"), which would not be considered deviations attributable to trial context under RoB2 criteria for the ITT effect.

The analysis was conducted according to ITT principles: "Analysis was by intention to treat," and the flow diagram confirms 816 and 807 patients were analysed in their randomized groups (with those lost to follow-up conservatively classified as not having the primary endpoint). This satisfies the requirement for an appropriate analysis (signalling question 2.6: Yes).

Given lack of blinding is unavoidable and inherent to the comparison being tested (whether add-on MRI changes practice), and there is no clear evidence that trial-context deviations differentially affected the primary surgical outcome, but some uncertainty remains about whether the small amount of crossover/non-adherence could have influenced the objectively-measured reoperation outcome, we judge "Some concerns."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, and the small amount of missing data was similar between groups and not clearly outcome-dependent.

**b. Rationale:** The trial profile (Figure 1) shows that of 816 randomized to MRI, 816 were analysed for the primary endpoint (with 3 lost to follow-up counted as no-event); of 807 randomized to no MRI, 807 were analysed (with 4 lost to follow-up counted as no-event). This represents >99% data completeness in both arms.

The methods pre-specify how missing data were handled: "Those who were lost to follow-up were classified as not having a primary-endpoint event," a conservative and pre-specified approach applied equally to both groups.

The very small numbers of missing data (3/816 and 4/807, plus additional small numbers with "data unavailable at time of analysis") are too few to have made an important difference to the estimated effect, particularly given the observed event rate was ~19% in both groups. There is no differential loss to follow-up between groups, and no indication that missingness depended on the true outcome value.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The outcome (reoperation, defined by objective surgical/pathological criteria) is an observer-reported outcome not substantially subject to judgement-based bias, though outcome assessors were not blinded. Measurement methods were appropriate, standardized, and equally applied across groups (aside from MRI itself contributing information in one arm, which is part of the intervention effect, not a measurement bias).

**b. Rationale:** The outcome was defined objectively: "the proportion of patients undergoing a repeat operation—either further wide local excision or mastectomy within 6 months of random assignment, or a pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation." Whether a reoperation occurred is a factual, recorded clinical/surgical event, not subject to subjective interpretation by an unblinded assessor in the way a symptom-based outcome would be.

Histopathological assessment (informing "pathologically avoidable mastectomy" determinations) was conducted according to standardized, pre-specified criteria: "Histopathological assessment of excised specimens was done at every participating centre in accordance with guidelines in the NHS BSP publication." Additionally, a specific mechanism was in place to reduce assessment bias: "Imaging findings from patients undergoing a mastectomy at initial operation were reviewed every 12 months in conjunction with histopathological findings by the DMEC to identify any false-positive MR findings," using "pre-established criteria" defined by an independent DMEC, and a quality-assurance re-read of MRI scans was performed "by an independent radiologist, who was masked to the original MRI findings."

Although outcome assessors (surgeons deciding on reoperation) were not blinded to intervention allocation (unavoidable in this pragmatic, open-label surgical trial), the decision to reoperate was governed by objective, pre-specified, and audited pathological criteria (clear margins definitions provided in advance by each surgeon) rather than subjective global judgement, minimizing the likelihood that lack of blinding influenced the recorded outcome to a meaningful degree. This corresponds to an "observer-reported outcome not involving much judgement," where assessment is unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention received.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The result for reoperation rate corresponds to the pre-specified primary outcome, analysed according to the pre-specified statistical analysis plan, with no evidence of selective reporting from multiple outcome measurements or analyses.

**b. Rationale:** The trial registration (ISRCTN57474502) and published methods indicate reoperation rate was the pre-specified primary endpoint, consistent with the definition used in the results: "The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients undergoing a repeat operation or further mastectomy within 6 months of random assignment, or a pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation." The statistical analysis was fully pre-specified, including sample size calculation, the statistical test to be used ("logistic regression, adjusting for the minimisation factors"), the handling of missing data (lost to follow-up = no event), and the treatment of patient-choice mastectomies ("Patients undergoing a mastectomy at initial operation because of patient decision alone were regarded as having a reoperation").

The paper reports the primary result in a single, clearly defined manner (OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.75–1.24, p=0.77) without evidence of multiple alternative outcome definitions or analyses being tried and selectively reported for this outcome. Subgroup and exploratory analyses (tumour type, age) are clearly labelled as "prespecified exploratory" or "ad-hoc exploratory," and are reported transparently rather than being presented as the primary result. There is no indication of discrepancy between the trial registry and the published outcome definition/analysis for the primary endpoint.

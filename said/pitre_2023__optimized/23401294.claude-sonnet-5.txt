# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0)
## Trial: "The effects of two spinal orthoses on balance in elderly people with thoracic kyphosis"
## Outcome assessed: **Balance Score**
## Effect of interest for Domain 2: **Effect of assignment to intervention (baseline)**

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation*: The authors state, "The randomization procedure was to pick the name of the orthosis from a bag including the names of both orthoses." This includes a genuine random element (analogous to drawing lots), so signalling question 1.1 is answered **Probably yes**.
- *Allocation concealment*: No information is provided about who performed the drawing, whether it was performed by an independent party, or whether the enrolling investigator could anticipate or influence the draw. This is answered **No information**.
- *Baseline imbalance*: Formal comparisons of age, weight, height, Cobb angle, and baseline balance score showed no statistically significant differences (all p>0.05), suggesting balanced prognostic factors. However, the **unequal final group sizes (n=10 Spinomed vs n=8 PTS)** from an 18-person sample is notable. The authors explicitly attribute this imbalance to "the group allocation method," implying that the randomization procedure (picking names from a bag, presumably without a fixed block structure) did not reliably produce balanced group sizes and may have been susceptible to problems (e.g., non-replacement of the "PTS" pick occurring more often, or lack of a formal restricted randomization scheme). This raises concern about the fidelity of the randomization implementation, even though it does not clearly point to selection bias affecting prognostic balance.
- Given: adequate description of a random element, no information on concealment, and a somewhat unusual/unexplained imbalance in group sizes attributable to the allocation method → overall judgement of **Some concerns**.

---

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants were necessarily aware of whether they were wearing an orthosis or not, as this is unblindable ("with and without orthosis" conditions). Signalling question 2.1 = **Yes**.
- Personnel (the certified orthotist who fitted the device) were also aware. Signalling question 2.2 = **Yes**.
- However, deviations from intended intervention arising *because of trial context* were not evident. The study was explicitly designed as a "single blinded clinical trial," with an outcome (balance score) captured by an automated instrumented system (NeuroCom EquiTest), not directly susceptible to participant behavioral changes driven by trial engagement/recruitment effects. There is no evidence or suggestion of non-protocol co-interventions, crossovers, or trial-context-driven behavioral deviations that would differentially affect the with/without-orthosis balance score results: "All subjects in the two groups were asked to wear the orthoses for 1.5 h to get used to them," a standardized protocol was followed for both groups.
- Signalling question 2.3 (deviations from trial context) = **No** — no evidence such deviations occurred.
- Analysis: All 18 randomized participants who completed the trial were analyzed in their assigned groups, using appropriate paired t-tests to compare with/without orthosis performance, consistent with an ITT-style approach applied to the outcome for each individual (no eligible participants were excluded from analysis or crossed over between groups). Signalling question 2.6 = **Yes**.
- Overall: **Low risk of bias.**

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The article states, "Eighteen participants (16 women and 2 men)... completed the study procedure," and Tables 1–3 confirm that outcome data (balance score) are reported for all 10 Spinomed and all 8 PTS participants — i.e., data are available for 100% of enrolled/analyzed participants with no dropouts noted anywhere in the manuscript.
- There is no mention of any participant failing to complete both the "with" and "without" orthosis assessments, and no CONSORT-style flow indicates losses.
- Signalling question 3.1 = **Yes** (data available for all, or nearly all, randomized participants) → default judgement of **Low risk of bias**, with no further signalling questions required.

---

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The balance score was measured using a validated, automated, computerized instrument (the NeuroCom EquiTest Sensory Organization Test), which is an **objective, observer-reported outcome not involving human judgement** (a force-plate/computer generated score from 0–100 calculated by the system's software), rather than a participant-reported or subjectively judged outcome: "The load cells in the force plates transmit force information to the computer 100 times per second... to calculate the total score."
- Method of measurement was appropriate and identical for both intervention groups and both conditions (with/without orthosis); the same instrument, protocol, and calibration procedures were applied uniformly ("NeuroCom systems include a self-calibration feature"). Signalling question 4.1 = **No**; 4.2 = **No**.
- Because the outcome is captured by an automated computerized system rather than a human assessor exercising judgement, even though participants and testers were not blinded to the orthosis condition, this is unlikely to differentially bias the balance score. Signalling question 4.3 (aware) = **Yes**, but 4.4 (could assessment be influenced by knowledge) = **Probably no**, since the outcome is machine generated, not rated subjectively.
- Overall: **Low risk of bias.**

---

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- No trial registration record or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is cited or referenced in the article itself; only a general Ethics Committee approval ("Letter No. 221") is mentioned, with no protocol registration number provided in the publication.
- Per the task instructions, information from the trial registration should be used if available to inform this domain; however, no explicit linkage between the published outcomes and a prospectively registered protocol is presented in the article, and no information is given confirming that balance score (and its specific analysis: paired t-test comparing with/without orthosis, and between-group comparison of change scores) was pre-specified before unblinded data were available. Signalling question 5.1 = **No information**.
- The balance score outcome, as the authors note, "was considered... as it is a common and recommended criterion to evaluate the risk of falling," suggesting it was a planned primary/main outcome measure (single primary index, 0–100 scale, no evidence of multiple alternative composite scores or multiple time-points being selectively reported for this specific measure). There is no clear evidence of selection from multiple eligible measurements or analyses for the balance score specifically (signalling questions 5.2 and 5.3 = **Probably no**, based on internal consistency of reporting, but this cannot be fully verified without registry/protocol access).
- Given the absence of confirmed pre-specified analysis plan information and lack of certainty regarding whether other balance metrics/timepoints/analyses were tried and not reported, but no overt evidence of result-dependent selective reporting for balance score, the judgement defaults to **Some concerns** rather than High or Low risk.

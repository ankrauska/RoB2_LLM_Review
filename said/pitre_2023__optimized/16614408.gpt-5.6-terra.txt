**Effect assessed:** assignment to fish-oil supplementation (3.6 g/day n-3 fatty acids) versus olive-oil placebo at baseline, irrespective of adherence, within the factorial SAFA/MUFA dietary trial. Outcomes: change from baseline to 3 months in plasma 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α.

### 1. Bias due to randomization: **some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, because the trial is described as randomized, and baseline characteristics do not indicate an evident failure of randomization, but neither the method used to generate the random sequence nor allocation concealment is reported.

**b. Rationale:**  
- The report states that participants “were randomly assigned” to the SAFA or MUFA diet and, within each dietary group, underwent “a 2nd random assignment” to fish-oil or placebo capsules.  
- However, it does not specify whether the allocation sequence was computer-generated or otherwise random, nor does it state how allocation was concealed from staff enrolling participants. Thus, the key RoB 2 signalling questions on random-sequence generation and concealment cannot be answered affirmatively.
- Baseline characteristics appear broadly comparable across the four groups. For example, mean age was 47.0–49.5 years, BMI 26.1–26.9 kg/m², and serum cholesterol 5.37–5.45 mmol/L. The authors state that “clinical characteristics of the participants at baseline did not differ among the groups.” There is no marked imbalance in group sizes (42, 41, 40, and 39) or in reported baseline prognostic variables to suggest a clear failure of randomization.
- Nevertheless, baseline balance cannot compensate for missing information about sequence generation and concealment. Under RoB 2, absent information on allocation concealment leads to **some concerns** when baseline differences do not themselves suggest a problem.

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention: **some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, because an analysis broadly consistent with the effect of assignment appears to have been used, but reporting is insufficient to establish that participants, carers, and personnel were blinded or that deviations caused by the trial context did not occur.

**b. Rationale:**  
- The intervention comparison used fish-oil capsules versus “placebo capsules with the same amount of olive oil.” Use of placebo capsules suggests an attempt to mask assignment, but the report does not explicitly state who was blinded, whether the capsules were indistinguishable, or whether blinding was successful. In particular, fish-oil supplements may have recognizable taste, odour, or gastrointestinal effects.
- The report does not describe post-randomization deviations, crossovers, differential co-interventions, or adherence rates for the capsule intervention. It therefore cannot be confirmed that no deviations from intended intervention arose because of awareness of allocation in the trial context.
- On the other hand, the analytical approach is compatible with estimating an effect of assignment. The authors state that treatment effects were estimated in models including “the presence/absence of (n-3) fatty acids,” background diet, their interaction, treatment centre, age, sex, and baseline outcome value. They report the combined comparison of all participants assigned n-3 fatty acids with all assigned placebo:
  > “all subjects supplemented with (n-3) fatty acids were compared with all subjects given the placebo.”
- There is no indication that participants were analysed according to treatment received, or that eligible participants were excluded because of non-adherence. Thus, there is no clear evidence of an inappropriate as-treated or naïve per-protocol analysis.
- The remaining concern is principally incomplete reporting of blinding and trial-context deviations, rather than evidence that such deviations actually biased the result.

### 3. Missing outcome data: **low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, with an important qualification: 8-iso-PGF2α was unavailable for participants at two centres, but the reported reason for missingness was inappropriate specimen handling/storage, which is not plausibly related to the participants’ true biomarker values or fish-oil assignment. The 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α measurement was conducted using samples from all centres.

**b. Rationale:**  
- For 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α, the article reports:
  > “The levels of 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a were analyzed in plasma from all centers.”
  The four randomized group totals sum to 162, and there is no report of participant dropout or missing biomarker observations. Thus, data appear to have been available for all or nearly all randomized participants for this outcome.
- For 8-iso-PGF2α, the report explicitly identifies the missing samples and the reason:
  > “Plasma samples from 2 centers (Denmark and Italy) were unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage.”
- This is substantial missingness for 8-iso-PGF2α at the study level, but it is attributable to specimen handling/storage at whole centres, rather than to participants’ oxidative-stress level, response to intervention, adverse effects, or withdrawal related to outcome. Randomization occurred within the multicentre trial, and the treatment-effect models included treatment centre as a covariate.
- Under RoB 2, missing data need not imply bias when the documented cause of missingness could not depend on the true outcome value. The missing 8-iso-PGF2α data reduce precision and limit applicability to the centres with analysable samples, but there is no persuasive basis to conclude that they biased the randomized comparison.

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, because both outcomes were objectively measured biochemical markers using validated radioimmunoassays, with the same assay methods applied across randomized groups. Knowledge of intervention assignment is unlikely to have materially influenced these laboratory measurements.

**b. Rationale:**  
- The outcomes are plasma biochemical measurements rather than participant-reported or clinician-judged outcomes:
  > “The plasma levels of 8-iso-prostaglandin F2a … were analyzed … by using a validated RIA.”  
  > “The levels of 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a were analyzed … using a validated RIA.”
- The report provides assay characteristics, including cross-reactivity and detection limits (23 pmol/L for 8-iso-PGF2α and 45 pmol/L for 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α), supporting appropriateness of the measurement methods.
- Blood was collected under standardized conditions:
  > “Blood samples were taken from an antecubital vein after an overnight fast at baseline and after 3 mo.”
- The intervention itself did not create different outcome-assessment schedules or inherently different ascertainment opportunities between fish-oil and placebo groups; measurements were made at baseline and after the same 90-day intervention period.
- Although explicit blinding of laboratory personnel is not reported, these are observer-reported laboratory outcomes with limited scope for assessor judgement. Therefore, knowledge of intervention assignment is unlikely to have influenced the assay result materially.
- The inadequate handling/storage of samples at two centres affects availability of 8-iso-PGF2α data, addressed under missing outcome data, rather than indicating differential measurement bias among samples that were analysed.

### 5. Selective reporting: **some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, because the publication identifies the two biomarkers as main outcome variables and reports both, but the available information does not establish that the reported analyses were specified in a sufficiently detailed analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

**b. Rationale:**  
- The report clearly identifies the outcomes as main outcomes:
  > “The main outcome variables were the changes in 8-iso-prostaglandin F2a and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a.”
- Both pre-specified-looking biomarker outcomes are reported in Table 3, including baseline values, 3-month values, changes, and comparisons of fish oil versus placebo and SAFA versus MUFA diets. The principal reported conclusion is consistent with these displayed results:
  > “The plasma 8-iso-PGF2a concentration was significantly decreased after 3 mo of supplementation with (n-3) fatty acids (P = 0.015), whereas the PGF2a concentration was not affected.”
- However, the report does not provide a dated statistical analysis plan or enough registry/protocol detail to verify that the exact chosen outcome metric (change from baseline), time point (3 months), covariate adjustment, handling of the factorial interaction, and approach to incomplete 8-iso-PGF2α data were finalized before unblinded data were available.
- There were multiple potentially eligible analytic choices, including final value versus change from baseline and models with or without the interaction term. The authors state that when the interaction was not significant, they used “a limited model … excluding that term,” which leaves some analytical flexibility.
- There is no direct evidence that the reported results were selected because they were statistically significant or favourable to fish oil. Therefore, high risk of bias is not justified. However, without verifiable prospective analysis intentions, RoB 2 supports **some concerns**.

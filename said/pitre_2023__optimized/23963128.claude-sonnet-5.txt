# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0)
## Outcome: Team checklist score (effects of supervision on patient care)
## Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (ITT-type effect)

---

## 1. Bias Arising from the Randomization Process

**Judgement: Some concerns**

### Rationale:
- **Sequence generation (1.1):** The article states residents "were randomized to one of three levels of supervision" but provides **no description of the method used to generate the allocation sequence** (e.g., computer randomization, random number table). No information is given regarding whether a random component was used, and no supplementary protocol was cited in the article text for this detail. This warrants "No information."
- **Allocation concealment (1.2):** Similarly, **no information is provided** regarding whether the allocation sequence was concealed from investigators or from residents/fellows prior to assignment. There is no description of central randomization, sealed envelopes, or similar procedures.
- **Baseline imbalance (1.3):** Table 1 shows reasonably similar baseline characteristics across groups (age, gender, training level, subspecialty distribution, prior ICU/simulation experience, and baseline confidence score). While the "Immediately Available" group had a notably lower and more variable baseline confidence score (70% [19%] vs. 75-78% in other groups), no formal statistical comparison is reported, and the differences are plausibly compatible with chance given the small sample size (n=16–19/group). No evidence of a substantial or suspicious imbalance is present.

Because there is no information about sequence generation *and* no information about allocation concealment, but baseline characteristics do not suggest a major problem, this combination (per Table 4 algorithm: NI/NI/NI) defaults to **"Some concerns."**

## 2. Deviations from Intended Intervention (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

### Rationale:
- **Awareness of assignment (2.1/2.2):** Both residents and fellows were necessarily aware of the assigned supervision condition, since the intervention is defined by the physical proximity of the supervisor (e.g., "supervisor in the simulation room with the resident from the beginning of the scenario" vs. "supervisor only available on the phone"). Blinding of this intervention is inherently impossible.
- **Deviations arising from trial context (2.3):** The described variability in resident behavior (e.g., "two residents did not call their supervisor" during immediately available supervision; "all residents called their supervisor" during distant supervision) reflects **within-protocol variation in how each supervision arm was implemented**, rather than deviations inconsistent with the trial protocol. Fellows were explicitly instructed to "balance the care of the patient and the teaching to the resident the same way they would in real life," indicating that behavioral variability was an expected and intended feature of the naturalistic simulation, not a protocol deviation caused by trial context (e.g., no evidence of contamination, switching between arms, or co-intervention imbalance driven by recruitment/engagement effects).
- **Appropriate analysis (2.6):** All 53 randomized residents appear to have been analyzed according to their assigned supervision group (18 direct, 16 immediately available, 19 distant), consistent with an intention-to-treat approach; there is no indication of post-randomization exclusions or "as-treated" analysis for this outcome.

Since awareness of assignment is unavoidable given the nature of the intervention, but no protocol-inconsistent deviations attributable to the trial context were reported, and the analysis included all randomized participants in their assigned groups, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

## 3. Missing Outcome Data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

### Rationale:
- Data for the team checklist score during the supervised scenario appear to be available for **all 53 randomized residents** (18 direct + 16 immediately available + 19 distant = 53), matching the total number of residents recruited.
- There is no mention of any resident dropping out, withdrawing, or having unavailable video/checklist data for this outcome. The article states: "Fifty-three residents completing a critical care rotation... were recruited," and results for the team checklist score are reported by group with matching sample sizes (Table 2).
- No indication that any missingness—had it occurred—would be related to the true value of the outcome.

Because outcome data were available for all randomized participants, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

## 4. Measurement of the Outcome

**Judgement: Some concerns**

### Rationale:
- **Appropriateness of measurement method (4.1):** The team checklist appears to be a reasonable, purpose-built method for capturing completion of clinical tasks ("A checklist task was considered done if performed by any member of the team... reported as a percentage of the total number of items on the checklist"). There is no indication the method was inherently unsuitable, though it was a non-validated tool ("we recognize the limitations associated with the use of a nonvalidated assessment tool, such as our scenario-specific checklists").
- **Comparability of ascertainment across groups (4.2):** Measurement method (checklist scoring) appears consistent across all three supervision arms — no indication that ascertainment methods differed systematically between groups.
- **Blinding of outcome assessors (4.3):** This is the primary source of concern. The team checklist score for the *supervised* scenario is an **observer-reported outcome involving some judgement** (i.e., determining whether specific clinical tasks were "done" by any team member). Critically, the level of supervision (physical presence of the fellow) is a **visually obvious feature of the scenario/video** — it cannot be masked from raters scoring the checklist. There is no description of blinding procedures for this specific outcome (in contrast to the more detailed blinding/calibration process described for the Ottawa GRS raters used for the *unsupervised* scenario).
- **Potential for influence (4.4/4.5):** Because the presence/absence of the supervisor is inherently visible to any assessor reviewing the scenario, and because the checklist requires some judgment about task completion (rather than being a fully objective, unambiguous measure like mortality), there is a plausible—but not clearly demonstrated—risk that awareness of the supervision condition could influence scoring. However, no evidence is presented that scoring was actually influenced by this knowledge (e.g., no differential rater behavior reported), and discrepancies were adjudicated by a third independent assessor for at least some checklists.

Given that outcome assessors could not have been blinded to this intervention (a structural feature of the study), and the checklist involves some judgment, but there is no direct evidence that assessment was actually biased by this knowledge, this domain is judged **Some concerns** rather than High risk.

---

## 5. Selective Reporting

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

### Rationale:
- The team checklist score is explicitly identified as a primary outcome measure tied to the first of the three pre-specified research questions ("Does a closer level of clinical supervision enhance patient care?"), and the *Measurements* section pre-specifies that "we measured a team checklist score, the time to stabilization of abnormal vital signs, and the proportion of achievement of definitive management" to address this question.
- The *Statistical Analyses* section explicitly pre-specifies the analytic approach for this outcome ("we conducted a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) on the team checklist scores of the supervised scenario with the level of clinical supervision... as the independent variable"), and this exact analysis is what is reported in the Results (Table 2, p = 0.0013), with no evidence of alternative checklist definitions, time points, or analytic models being tried and selectively reported.
- The trial registration record does not indicate discrepancies in outcome definition, timing, or analysis method for this specific outcome relative to what is reported in the publication; the checklist score outcome, its measurement (team-level, percentage of items completed), and its statistical test (ANOVA across three supervision arms) are consistent between the described methods and the reported results.
- There is no indication that this result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements (e.g., alternative checklists) or multiple eligible analyses (e.g., alternative statistical models) based on the direction or significance of the findings.

Given consistency between the pre-specified outcome/analysis plan and the reported result, with no evidence of selective outcome or analysis reporting, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

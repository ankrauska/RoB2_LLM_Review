**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The allocation sequence was random, adequately concealed, and there is no indication of baseline imbalances that would suggest a problem with the randomization process.
* **b. Rationale:** The study reports: "Participants were allocated randomly to bright light (BRIGHT), monochromatic blue light (BLUE), or a control condition (CONTROL) using an online tool (available at www.randomization.com) which applies permuted block randomization with randomly varying block sizes of 3, 6, and 9. The list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered sealed opaque envelopes."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Although participants were aware of their assigned intervention, there were no reported deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, an appropriate analysis was used.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial reports that "Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to, but not the other two light conditions," meaning they were not strictly blinded. However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention occurred due to the trial context. The analysis included 72 of 74 randomized participants, excluding only two who had invalid baseline fitness test measurements ("Two subjects in BRIGHT and CONTROL had to be excluded due to invalid measurements of peak oxygen uptake"). This post-randomization exclusion of ineligible participants is considered appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Outcome data were available for all eligible, randomized participants. 
* **b. Rationale:** Of the 74 participants randomized, 2 were excluded due to invalid baseline screening measurements, leaving 72 participants. Data for the reaction time task were available for all 72 analyzed participants, which represents nearly all participants randomized.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The method of measuring the outcome was appropriate and comparable across groups. The outcome assessor was blinded to the intervention.
* **b. Rationale:** Acoustic reaction time was measured objectively using a "computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task." Furthermore, the study states: "The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to the light condition each participant was exposed to." Because the measurement was objective and the assessor was blinded, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The reported results were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified plan, and there is no evidence of selective reporting from multiple outcome measurements or analyses.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial registration (NCT02203539) pre-specified "Reaction time in milliseconds (ms)" as a secondary outcome. The authors explicitly state: "All statistical methods used to compare the groups for reaction time and handgrip strength in the present report were pre-specified and registered." There is no indication that the reported results were selected on the basis of statistical significance or direction of the effect.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Visual Acuity (Figure 4 - Efficacy, Cumulative Snellen Visual Acuity)
## Effect of Interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias Arising from the Randomization Process

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Rationale:** 
- **Sequence generation (1.1 – Yes):** The trial explicitly states that intervention assignment used a random component: "one eye was treated with the MEL80 standard aspheric profile (ASA) and the other eye was treated with the MEL80 non-aspheric profile (TSA), assigned at random using Microsoft Excel's random number function." This satisfies the criterion for a truly random sequence generation process.
- **Allocation concealment (1.2 – No information):** There is no description of how the allocation sequence was concealed from the surgeon or personnel prior to assignment. Given that this is a paired-eye (intra-individual) design in which both eyes of the same patient are treated in the same session by the same surgeon, and given the laser platform requires the surgeon to select and program the ablation profile per eye at the time of surgery, there is a plausible risk that the assignment was not concealed until the point of treatment. No description of a centralized or sealed-envelope allocation process is provided.
- **Baseline imbalance (1.3 – No):** Baseline spherical equivalent refraction was very similar between groups (ASA: −2.43 ± 1.53 D; TSA: −2.54 ± 1.47 D; p = 0.87), and because this is a within-patient paired-eye design, baseline systemic and ocular characteristics (age, disease severity, corneal parameters) are inherently balanced between "groups" since each patient contributes one eye to each arm. This strongly reduces concern about problems with the randomization process producing imbalance.

Because sequence generation was adequately random but there is no information about allocation concealment, and no evidence of baseline imbalance, this maps to "Some concerns" per the RoB2 algorithm (random sequence + no information on concealment + no imbalance → some concerns).

---

## 2. Deviations from the Intended Intervention (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- **Awareness of assigned intervention (2.1/2.2):** Because this is a surgical intervention delivered by the same surgeon in the same operative session, it is very likely that the surgeon (person delivering the intervention) was aware of which ablation profile (ASA vs TSA) was being used in each eye, since this must be programmed into the laser. Patients themselves would likely not know which profile was used in which eye, as this is a technical laser parameter not apparent to the patient.
- **Deviations arising from trial context (2.3/2.4/2.5):** There is no indication in the manuscript of any deviations from intended intervention arising from the trial context (e.g., no crossover, no additional non-protocol interventions, no differential co-interventions). The manuscript states "All patients received a routine preoperative evaluation for LASIK surgery" and both eyes underwent standardized LASIK with the same microkeratome and laser platform, differing only in the ablation profile programmed. There is no evidence that awareness of assignment led to any change in surgical technique, additional treatments, or behavior that would bias the objective visual acuity outcome.
- **Appropriate analysis (2.6):** The efficacy/visual acuity outcome was analyzed and reported for all 30 eyes ("30 eyes of 15 myopic patients"; Fig. 4 states "data for 30 eyes at 3 months postop"), consistent with an intention-to-treat/full analysis population with no evidence of post-randomization exclusions or per-protocol restriction.

Since personnel awareness of the intervention is unlikely to translate into behavior affecting an objective, standardized outcome (Snellen visual acuity measured by masked/standard clinical technique), and an appropriate analysis (all eyes) was used, this domain is judged as low risk of bias.

## 3. Missing Outcome Data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- Outcome data (visual acuity) appear to be available for all randomized eyes: "A prospective randomized paired-eye controlled study of 30 eyes of 15 myopic patients was conducted," and Figure 4 explicitly states data for "30 eyes at 3 months postop" for both ASA and TSA groups, matching the full enrolled sample.
- There is no mention in the manuscript of any patients lost to follow-up, withdrawals, or missing visual acuity assessments at the 3-month endpoint. The efficacy results are reported as "100% of eyes were 20/25 or better" for both groups, indicating complete ascertainment.
- No CONSORT flow diagram is provided, but the consistent reporting of "30 eyes" across all outcome figures (refractive outcomes, aberrometry, visual acuity) supports that data were available for essentially all randomized participants.

Because outcome data appear available for all (or nearly all) randomized eyes, this domain is judged as low risk of bias.

---

## 4. Measurement of the Outcome

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- **Appropriateness of method (4.1 – No):** Visual acuity was measured using standard Snellen chart methodology ("Manifest refraction, uncorrected distance visual acuity and CDVA were measured before and 3 months after surgery"), an appropriate and validated method for this outcome.
- **Comparability of measurement between groups (4.2 – No):** The same measurement protocol and timing (3 months postoperatively) was applied identically to both ASA and TSA eyes for each patient, with no indication of differential measurement procedures between the two treatment arms.
- **Blinding of outcome assessor (4.3):** While the manuscript does not explicitly state whether the examiner assessing visual acuity was masked to which ablation profile was used in each eye, visual acuity assessment via Snellen chart is a highly standardized, objective measurement with very limited scope for subjective judgment by the assessor (akin to an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" per RoB2 guidance). Therefore, even if the assessor were aware of treatment allocation, this is unlikely to have influenced the recorded visual acuity outcome.
- Given the objective and standardized nature of Snellen visual acuity testing, knowledge of intervention assignment is unlikely to have influenced the outcome assessment.

This domain is judged as low risk of bias.

---

## 5. Selective Reporting

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- Visual acuity (efficacy) is reported comprehensively as a full cumulative distribution curve (Figure 4) for both treatment groups, following a pre-specified and standard reporting protocol referenced in the Methods: "Refractive outcomes including accuracy, efficacy, and safety were analyzed according to the protocol described by Reinstein and Waring." This is a widely recognized standardized reporting framework for refractive surgery outcomes (cumulative Snellen acuity, defocus equivalent, safety index, etc.), reducing the likelihood that the visual acuity result was selectively chosen from multiple alternative analyses or measurement definitions.
- There is no evidence of multiple alternative outcome measurements or analyses for visual acuity (e.g., no discrepant results for UDVA/CDVA reported elsewhere, no different visual acuity metrics competing for reporting) — the results are presented in full, using a single, standard, pre-defined method (cumulative percentage of eyes achieving specific Snellen acuity thresholds) for both groups.
- No trial registry record contents were available to cross-check pre-specified outcome definitions for visual acuity in detail; however, given the outcome is a standard core efficacy outcome (a mandated element of refractive surgery reporting standards per Waring/Reinstein protocol) and is fully reported for both groups with no signs of partial or selective presentation, there is no clear indication of selective reporting bias for this specific outcome.

This domain is judged as low risk of bias.

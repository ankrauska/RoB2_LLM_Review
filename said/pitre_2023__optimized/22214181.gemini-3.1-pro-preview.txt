Based on the provided RoB 2.0 guidance and the trial publication, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the List of attributions task (LAT) outcome:

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation, but there is no information about allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics appeared balanced, but the lack of concealment information leads to some concerns. 
* **Rationale:** The authors state, "a Microsoft Excel random number generator was used to randomize participants into the two conditions." However, there is no mention of how the allocation sequence was concealed from those enrolling participants. Baseline variables were balanced: "The FI and control groups did not differ significantly with respect to age... ethnicity... No significant pre-intervention group differences were found on levels of insomnia, fatigue, depressed mood or dysfunctional beliefs about sleep."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The assessment is for the effect of assignment to intervention. Although participants were likely aware of the information sheet they read, there is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context. The exclusions from the analysis were minimal and unlikely to have a substantial impact.
* **Rationale:** Participants received an information-based educational intervention in a 1-hour session, making deviations from the protocol (such as non-adherence over time) inapplicable. Regarding the analysis, 5 participants were excluded (two were underage, one lacked demographics, and two had incomplete outcome measures), leaving 88 participants (44 per group). The exclusion of 5 participants out of 93 is small and does not have the potential for a substantial impact on the result.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for all or nearly all randomized participants. 
* **Rationale:** The study was completed in a single 1-hour session. As noted above, only two participants were excluded for not completing the outcome measures. Data were available for 88 participants (44 in each group), meaning data were available for nearly all participants. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The outcome is participant-reported, and participants were likely aware of the intervention they received (the specific information sheet). This knowledge could potentially influence their responses on the subjective LAT questionnaire. 
* **Rationale:** The LAT is a participant-generated list and ranking (participant-reported outcome). The authors note, "The study investigator read the information sheet alongside each participant individually, after which all of the participants once again filled out the LAT." While participants were not explicitly told their group assignment, they were aware of the content they just read, which could influence their subjective responses on the questionnaire immediately afterward.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** There is no information regarding a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registration to verify if the analyses were conducted exactly as intended before unblinded data were available.
* **Rationale:** The publication does not reference a trial registry or a date-stamped protocol/statistical analysis plan. The authors report three specific computed scores from the LAT (NumberAttributionScore, FrequencyScore, and ImportanceScore). While there is no obvious evidence that these were selected from a larger pool of un-reported analyses on the basis of statistical significance, the absence of a pre-specified plan means bias cannot be fully ruled out.

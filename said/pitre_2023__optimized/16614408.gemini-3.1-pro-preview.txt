Based on the RoB 2.0 tool and the provided article, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of changes in levels of 8-iso-prostaglandin F2a and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a, considering the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline:

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** The trial reports random assignment but provides no details on how the randomization sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed.
* **b. Rationale:** The authors state that participants "were randomly assigned to a diet containing a high proportion of either saturated fatty acids (SAFA diet) or monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA diet)" and that there "was a 2nd random assignment to either supplements of capsules containing fish-oil... or to placebo capsules." However, there is no information regarding the method used to generate the randomization sequence or whether the allocation sequence was concealed until participants were enrolled, which warrants a judgement of "some concerns." Baseline characteristics (Table 1) appear balanced, suggesting no severe problems with randomization.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The intervention of interest (n-3 fatty acids vs. placebo) was blinded using placebo capsules, and there is no indication of deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. 
* **b. Rationale:** The study utilized "placebo capsules with the same amount of olive oil" for the n-3 fatty acid intervention, ensuring that participants and personnel were unaware of the specific capsule assignment. Furthermore, the number of participants randomized matches the number analyzed, indicating that an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis approach was likely maintained for the participants with available data.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Although there is missing outcome data for 8-iso-PGF2a, the missingness was due to sample handling and storage issues, which is not related to the true value of the outcome.
* **b. Rationale:** The text states: "Plasma samples from 2 centers (Denmark and Italy) were unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage." Because this missingness is explicitly due to logistical/center-level errors rather than participant health status or intervention effects, it is highly unlikely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value. For 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a, levels were "analyzed in plasma from all centers." 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The outcomes were measured using validated, objective laboratory assays (radioimmunoassay) by personnel who would not have their judgement influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
* **b. Rationale:** Biomarkers of lipid peroxidation were measured from plasma samples using a "validated RIA." Since these are objective, observer-independent laboratory outcomes, the assessment of the outcome could not have been meaningfully influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The reported outcomes align precisely with the study's stated aims, and there is no evidence that the results were selected based on their direction or statistical significance.
* **b. Rationale:** The stated aim was to "investigate whether supplementation of dietary EPA and DHA... affects both the nonenzymatic and enzymatic lipid peroxidation in humans measured as F2-isoprostanes and a metabolite of prostaglandin F2a in plasma." Both of these pre-specified outcomes were reported thoroughly in the results and tables regardless of their statistical significance (e.g., 8-iso-PGF2a was significant, whereas 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a was not). Multiple conflicting analyses or measurements were not presented.

Based on the RoB 2.0 tool, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome **length of hospital stay**:

1. **bias due to randomization**: low risk of bias
   a. **Judgement**: The randomization process used a random component, and allocation was adequately concealed. Baseline characteristics appear balanced, suggesting no issues with the randomization process.
   b. **Rationale**: The trial report states, "Procedure type was assigned by a computer-generated randomization list, and concealment of randomization was done by telephone inquiry." Baseline data in Table 1 show no notable imbalances between the groups.

2. **deviations from intended intervention**: low risk of bias
   a. **Judgement**: While participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention (blinding is impossible for these surgical procedures), there is no evidence that this lack of blinding led to deviations from the intended intervention (inconsistent with the trial protocol) that arose because of the trial context. 
   b. **Rationale**: Both surgical procedures were carried out as intended. The authors note, "There were no conversions from LAVH to AH," and there is no indication of non-protocol interventions that would substantially bias the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline.

3. **missing outcome data**: low risk of bias
   a. **Judgement**: Outcome data for the length of hospital stay appear to be available for all randomized participants.
   b. **Rationale**: A total of 48 women were randomized (28 LAVH, 20 AH). The results for the hospital stay outcome reflect the complete sample, with no dropouts or missing data reported for this specific metric before hospital discharge. 

4. **measurement of the outcome**: high risk of bias
   a. **Judgement**: The outcome assessors (which in this case heavily involved the patients themselves) were not blinded to the intervention. Because the decision to be discharged was subjective and left to the unblinded patients, it is highly likely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
   b. **Rationale**: The trial explicitly states, "Each patient decided for herself when she was ready to be discharged from hospital." Since patients undergoing abdominal hysterectomy (AH) have a larger incision and expect a longer recovery compared to the minimally invasive LAVH, this unblinded, subjective discharge criteria places the length of hospital stay at a high risk of bias.

5. **selective reporting**: low risk of bias
   a. **Judgement**: The reported outcome was the pre-specified primary outcome of the study, and the analysis aligns with the stated intentions.
   b. **Rationale**: The authors explicitly pre-specified this outcome in their methods: "We defined the primary outcome measure as length of hospital stay. We hoped to reduce the median hospital stay... For this purpose, we calculated that we would need a sample size of 20 cases per group." The results for this outcome are fully reported.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The study used an adequate, automated method for generating the randomization sequence and effectively concealed allocation. There is no evidence of baseline imbalances that would suggest a problem with the randomization process.
b. Rationale: The trial report states that "Subjects were assigned randomly to receive adjunctive treatment with either gabapentin or placebo under double-blind conditions, using a randomization automated system on a one-to-one basis." Regarding allocation concealment, it specifies, "During the study, the randomization list was held securely, and released only after study completion. Gabapentin and placebo were dispensed in identical appearing capsules." Furthermore, the authors report, "There were no significant differences in mean age or dosage of methadone and other drugs doses... between the treatment and controls groups at baseline."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants, carers, and personnel were successfully blinded to the intervention assignments, making it unlikely that any deviations from intended interventions arose due to the trial context. 
b. Rationale: The study was a "double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial." To maintain blinding, "Gabapentin and placebo were dispensed in identical appearing capsules." Because blinding of both participants and trial personnel was maintained, any deviations from the intended intervention are unlikely to be related to the trial context or knowledge of intervention assignment. 

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: There is a notable imbalance in missing outcome data between the two groups, and the reasons provided for dropout indicate that the missingness is likely related to the true value of the outcome (severity of withdrawal symptoms). 
b. Rationale: Outcome data were not available for all participants at the end of the trial. As shown in Table 1, the placebo group had 3 dropouts out of 20 by day 18, while the gabapentin group had none. The authors state that these three placebo patients "were excluded... because of non-compliance with the scheduled treatment programme and self-medication with illicit and/or over-the-counter (OTC) drugs." They further hypothesize that "The non-compliance and self-medication in the placebo group may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms." This strongly suggests that missingness in the outcome was dependent on its true value, introducing a high risk of bias.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome was measured using an appropriate and validated scale. Although it is a participant-reported subjective outcome, the participants were adequately blinded, meaning the assessment was unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the assigned intervention.
b. Rationale: The study measured the outcome using the "Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale (SOWS)", which the authors note "has been shown to be a valid and reliable indicator of the severity of the opiate withdrawal syndrome". Because participants (who are the outcome assessors for a subjective scale) received "identical appearing capsules" in a "double-blind" design, they were unaware of their assigned group.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is no available trial registry or pre-published protocol to verify the pre-specified analysis intentions, raising some concerns about whether the reported analyses were selected from multiple options. However, there is no obvious evidence of selective reporting of multiple scales or time points.
b. Rationale: No trial registration number or reference to a published protocol is provided in the text. Without a date-stamped statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinding, it is impossible to confirm with certainty that the reported outcomes and analyses (such as the specific repeated-measures ANOVA approach) exactly match those originally intended. This lack of information defaults to "some concerns" according to the RoB 2.0 guidance.

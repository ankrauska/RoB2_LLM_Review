# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment: NT Pro-BNP Levels

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The trial states randomization was performed "based on a computer random numbers table," which satisfies the requirement for a random sequence generation process. However, there is no description of allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation), and baseline data show no significant imbalances, which is reassuring but does not compensate for the lack of information on concealment.

**b. Rationale:** The article states: "Then the patients were allocated into one of the two groups based on a computer random numbers table." This supports "Yes" for random sequence generation (signalling question 1.1). However, there is no mention of how the allocation sequence was concealed from the enrolling investigators/anesthesiologists prior to assignment — no information on sealed envelopes, central randomization, or pharmacy-controlled dispensing of the study drug/placebo. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) show no significant differences between groups (all P>0.05), suggesting no problem with randomization is evident from the data. Per the RoB2 algorithm: Random sequence = Yes, Allocation concealment = No information, Baseline imbalance = No → this maps to "Some concerns."

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The study is described as "double-blind," with placebo (saline) administered in identical volumes/syringes as the MgSO4 infusion, suggesting participants, carers, and personnel were likely blinded. However, details of the blinding procedure (e.g., identical appearance, who prepared/administered the solutions, whether the anesthesiologist administering the drug was blinded) are not explicitly described, leaving some uncertainty as to whether blinding was fully effective, especially since magnesium infusion can produce recognizable physiological effects (e.g., hemodynamic changes) that could unblind personnel.

**b. Rationale:** The abstract states: "In a double-blind clinical trial... one group received a MgSO4 infusion (15 mg/kg/h) and the other group saline (placebo)." The methods further state: "The same volume of normal saline was administered as a placebo via a 50 ml syringe intravenously," suggesting an attempt to maintain blinding through identical appearance/volume. This supports a "Probably no" answer to whether participants/personnel were aware of assigned intervention. However, no explicit statement is provided regarding who prepared the syringes (e.g., an independent pharmacist) or whether the anesthesia team delivering/monitoring the infusion was blinded to its contents, and MgSO4 can cause detectable physiological effects (e.g., flushing, hypotension) that could compromise blinding. Because there is some ambiguity about the robustness of blinding (No information on independent preparation of solutions), and no evidence of deviations from intended intervention being reported, but the possibility cannot be excluded, this leads to "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The study appears to have complete NT Pro-BNP data for all 88 randomized participants (44 per group), with no reported dropouts or missing biomarker samples for this outcome.

**b. Rationale:** The results section states: "A total of 88 patients were enrolled in the study," and Table 3 reports laboratory data (including postoperative BNP) for all 44 patients per group with no indication of missing values. The methods describe blood sampling protocol at two fixed timepoints (before anesthesia and 24 h postoperatively) for all patients, and there is no mention in the manuscript of patients being lost to follow-up, withdrawing consent, or having missing NT Pro-BNP measurements. There is a mention that "those patients who received triple inotropic agent therapy for CPB weaning were excluded from the study" and that "the number of patients excluded from the study in each group was registered" — however, no such exclusions or their numbers are reported in the results, suggesting either no such exclusions occurred or they were fully accounted for in the enrolled sample of 88. Given data for all randomized participants appear available for this outcome, this is judged as low risk of bias.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** NT Pro-BNP was measured using a standardized, validated electrochemical luminescence immunoassay, applied identically to both groups (an objective laboratory measure), reducing risk of differential measurement error. However, there is no explicit statement that laboratory personnel/assessors analyzing the NT Pro-BNP samples were blinded to group allocation, though this is unlikely to introduce important bias given the automated, objective nature of the assay.

**b. Rationale:** The article describes the outcome measurement method in detail: "Measurement of NT pro-BNP was performed using an electrochemical luminescence immunoassay in an Elecsys 2010 (Roche, Indianapolis, IN, USA)... This method has a coefficient of variation of 2.2–3.2 %, with a detection range of 5–35,000 pg/ml." This is an appropriate, objective, automated method (an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" per RoB2 classification), unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment, so signalling question 4.1 = "No" (method not inappropriate) and this type of outcome assessor is unlikely to be influenced by allocation knowledge. However, the manuscript does not explicitly state whether laboratory personnel performing the assay were blinded to group assignment, nor is there description of sample labelling procedures that would ensure assay personnel were unaware of allocation. Given the outcome is objective/automated and unlikely to be influenced even in the absence of explicit blinding statements, but there remains no explicit information confirming blinding of assay personnel or sample handling to prevent any potential bias, a judgement of "Some concerns" is assigned, primarily driven by lack of explicit reporting rather than strong evidence of a problem.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** There is no information available confirming that NT Pro-BNP was analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. The trial registry entry does not appear to provide sufficient detail (e.g., specific timepoints, definition of outcome) to confirm that the analysis matches pre-specified intentions, and no protocol or statistical analysis plan is referenced.

**b. Rationale:** The article states the trial was registered ("IRCT201205069650N1") at the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials, and NT pro-BNP was reported as a primary/key outcome, measured at two pre-specified timepoints (baseline and 24 h postoperatively), with results reported for both groups (Table 3) using the pre-specified metric (mean ± SEM) and consistent with the described methods. There is no evidence of selective reporting of a subset of multiple outcome measurements (e.g., multiple timepoints or scales for NT Pro-BNP) — data are reported for the two timepoints described in the methods, with no other timepoints/instruments mentioned as being collected but omitted. Similarly, there is no indication of multiple statistical analyses of which only one was reported. However, without access to a detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan or full trial protocol document (only a registry entry is referenced, and its contents/date-stamping relative to unblinding of outcome data cannot be confirmed from the article), it is not possible to definitively rule out selective reporting. This leads to a judgement of "Some concerns" reflecting the absence of information rather than clear evidence of a problem.

---

# Overall Risk of Bias for NT Pro-BNP Outcome: **High risk of bias**

Per RoB 2.0 guidance (Table 1), because "some concerns" are present in **multiple domains** (randomization process, deviations from intended intervention, measurement of the outcome, and selective reporting), this "substantially lowers confidence in the result," warranting an overall judgement of **High risk of bias**, despite no single domain being individually rated as "High."

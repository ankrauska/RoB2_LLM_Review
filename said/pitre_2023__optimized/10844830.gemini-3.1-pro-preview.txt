**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The trial used sealed envelopes for randomization, but did not specify whether they were opaque or sequentially numbered, which leaves the adequacy of allocation concealment unclear. Furthermore, there were notable baseline imbalances between the groups (e.g., sex distribution and pre-bypass pulmonary artery pressure). 
* **Rationale:** The text states, "Sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random 20 consecutive adults". While small sample sizes (n=20) are prone to chance imbalances, the lack of detail on envelope concealment combined with baseline differences (e.g., "Pre-bypass values of pulmonary artery pressures were significantly greater in group 1 (25±5) than in group 2 (19±4)") raises some concerns about the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** There is a high risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention because an eligible participant's data were deliberately excluded from the statistical analysis based on their measured outcome values. 
* **Rationale:** The trial did not adhere to intention-to-treat (ITT) principles, as it excluded a participant post-randomization due to extreme outcome values. The Figure 1 legend states: "Group 2 had an outlier with high arterial ET-1 values... These data were excluded from the statistical analysis in order to simplify statistical interpretation." Given the very small sample size (10 per group), excluding one participant's data represents 10% of the group and has the potential to substantially impact the results.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for the participants. The exclusion of the outlier participant was an analytical decision rather than a failure to collect or measure the outcome data.
* **Rationale:** The data for the excluded outlier were collected but purposefully omitted from the analysis (addressed under Domain 2). There is no indication of participants being lost to follow-up or having genuinely missing outcome data that would bias the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The measurement of ET-1 concentrations was performed using an objective laboratory test, and the assessment was highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
* **Rationale:** The outcome was measured via a "sandwich-type enzyme immunoassay". Since this is an objective, automated laboratory measurement, the assessors (lab personnel or equipment) would not be influenced by knowledge of whether the patient received high or moderate FIO2, even if they were unblinded.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** There is no pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registration available to confirm the original analysis intentions, raising some concerns. 
* **Rationale:** Without a date-stamped trial registry or protocol, it is difficult to determine if the reported results were selected from multiple eligible analyses or measurements. The authors' decision to exclude an outlier to "simplify statistical interpretation" further highlights the lack of a strict, pre-specified analysis plan, warranting at least some concerns for selective reporting.

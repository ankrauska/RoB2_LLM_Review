1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial used a randomized process (stratified by intent to use medication and order of entry). Baseline characteristics were generally balanced, except for one educational category, which is likely due to chance and not indicative of a systematic issue with the randomization process.
b. Rationale: "Immediately after the 5-visit abstinence trial, HTT participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: percentile, fixed criterion, or random incentives. Random assignment to one of the three groups was accomplished by randomly assigning 2 participants to the percentile group, 2 to the fixed criterion group and 1 to the random incentives group from each group of 5 participants completing intake. Approximately 40 participants were assigned to the percentile and fixed criterion groups, and 20 participants were assigned to the random incentives group. Randomization was stratified by intent to use medication to help them stop smoking, and by order of study entry." Also, "Participants in the three groups were statistically similar on all intake measures... except for the proportion of participants attending Vocational/Technical School/Community College."

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Participants were aware of their assigned interventions, as they were explicitly informed of the contingencies. However, there is no evidence that deviations from intended intervention arose because of the trial context that would likely affect the outcome (effect of assignment to intervention). The analysis used all participants randomized to their respective groups.
b. Rationale: "Immediately after randomization all participants were given a brief description of the incentive contingency that they were randomized into after the 5-visit abstinence trial." The analysis compared groups as randomized.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Missing data (missed visits/samples) are inherently treated as failures to meet the abstinence criterion (or in the random group, as a missed visit that didn't earn an incentive based on attendance). This is standard for such trials.
b. Rationale: "The value of the incentive reset to $5.00 with either a missed visit or delivery of a breath CO sample ≥ 3 ppm."

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The outcome is objective (breath CO level). Thus, although participants and assessors were aware of the intervention, the measurement itself is not likely to be influenced by this knowledge.
b. Rationale: "Vitalograph CO monitors (Vitalograph Inc. Lenexa, KS) were used to take breath CO samples... The peak breath CO reading was taken as the participant’s breath CO level."

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial reports the outcomes (breath CO samples < 3 ppm, time to first sample, number of samples, consecutive samples) consistently with what would be expected for an incentive-based smoking cessation trial, with no indication of selective reporting of results.
b. Rationale: The reported results match the expected outcomes for a trial of this design. Missing registry details do not provide strong evidence of selective reporting.

# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Three-Dimensional Printing Models Study
**Outcome assessed: Performance (sum score of correct answers on 10 anatomical questions)**
**Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention**

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to lack of information regarding allocation concealment, despite adequate sequence generation and no evidence of baseline imbalance.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation (1.1 – Yes):* "Students were randomized into three groups using a computer program." This indicates a random component was used.
- *Allocation concealment (1.2 – No information):* The article provides no description of how the allocation sequence was concealed from those enrolling/assigning students (e.g., no mention of central randomization, sealed envelopes, or similar safeguards). This is a notable omission, especially since the study exam paper required students to enter a "unique randomization code," but the process protecting the sequence before assignment is not described.
- *Baseline imbalance (1.3 – No):* Table 2 shows well-balanced age, school age, and sex distribution across groups (e.g., 22, 21, 22 females in the CT, 3D, and 3Dp groups respectively), with no indication of a problem: "indicating no significant sex-related differences... there was no statistically significant difference."
- Per the RoB2 algorithm, "Yes/Probably yes" or "No information" for sequence generation, combined with "No information" for concealment and no baseline imbalance concerns, maps to **Some concerns**.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from the intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Although blinding of participants and personnel was not possible (and not intended, since the "intervention" being compared is the visible teaching modality itself), there is no evidence that this awareness led to inappropriate deviations from intended intervention arising from the trial context, and an appropriate (full) analysis was used.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Participant/personnel awareness (2.1/2.2 – Yes):* By design, students and teachers were fully aware of which teaching module (CT images, 3D images, or 3D-printed models) was being used: "A teacher provided a detailed interpretation of the fracture in each group." This awareness is intrinsic to the comparison of educational modalities and cannot be blinded.
- *Deviations arising from trial context (2.3 – No):* There is no indication that this awareness caused non-protocol interventions, contamination, or implementation failures inconsistent with the study protocol. Each subgroup received its assigned modality only ("Each subgroup was assigned six computers or 12 models"), and there is no evidence of cross-contamination between groups.
- *Appropriate analysis (2.6 – Yes):* All 120 randomized students were included in the analysis with no exclusions: "a total of 120 medical students (54.2% females) participated, and no one dropped out." This is consistent with an intention-to-treat approach.
- Because there is no evidence of protocol-inconsistent deviations arising from the (unavoidable) awareness of intervention, and an appropriate full-sample analysis was used, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as outcome data were available for all randomized participants.

**b. Rationale:**
- The manuscript explicitly states: "a total of 120 medical students (54.2% females) participated, and no one dropped out."
- Table 2 and Table 3 confirm data are reported for all randomized participants in each group and sex subgroup (e.g., CT: 22 females + 18 males = 40; 3D: 21 + 19 = 40; 3Dp: 22 + 18 = 40), consistent with full retention.
- Since outcome data were available for 100% of randomized participants, missing data could not have biased the result, satisfying the criterion for low risk of bias regardless of further signalling questions.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The outcome (score on 10 objective anatomical questions) was measured identically across groups using a standardized, non-judgmental scoring method, and is unlikely to be influenced by awareness of group assignment.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of the measurement method (4.1 – No):* All students "answered 10 questions regarding spinal fractures... assessed by an examination paper," with each question having a pre-defined correct answer (see Table 1, e.g., "b = C2 (correct)"). This is an appropriate, objective, and standard method for assessing anatomical understanding.
- *Comparability of measurement across groups (4.2 – No):* The identical 10-question examination was administered to all three groups under the same conditions ("By the end of the class, each student answered 10 questions... assessed by an examination paper"), so there was no differential ascertainment.
- *Outcome assessor awareness/potential for influence (4.3/4.4 – No):* This is an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" (akin to an objective right/wrong scoring against an answer key), similar to how all-cause mortality is scored. Even if graders were aware of group allocation (given randomization codes were recorded), scoring against a fixed, objective answer key leaves little room for differential judgement to bias results.
- Given the objective nature of the outcome and identical, standardized measurement procedure across arms, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, due to the absence of verifiable information confirming that the reported outcome/analysis was specified in advance of unblinded data availability.

**b. Rationale:**
- No trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, or clearly accessible trial registry record with pre-specified outcomes and analysis methods is referenced or available in the publication to confirm that the 10-question performance score (and its specific analysis via two-way ANOVA with Tukey's/Sidak's post-hoc tests) was the pre-specified primary analysis rather than one selected from multiple possible scoring/analysis approaches.
- The methods section states only: "The data was analyzed using two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) after testing for homogeneity. Tukey's or Sidak's multiple comparison test was used for post hoc analysis," without reference to a prospectively registered plan.
- Without documentation confirming the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available, and without evidence of selective reporting from multiple candidate outcome measures/analyses (no information either way), the signalling questions default to "No information" for questions 5.1–5.3.
- Per the RoB 2 algorithm, this pattern of "No information" across the domain maps to **Some concerns**, rather than "Low risk of bias" (which would require confirmed pre-specification) or "High risk of bias" (which would require clear evidence of result-dependent selection).

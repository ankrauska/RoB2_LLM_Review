Based on the RoB 2.0 tool, here is the risk of bias assessment for the COMICE trial regarding the outcome of reoperation rate (assessing the effect of assignment to the intervention):

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The trial utilized a robust randomization process. The sequence was generated using a "minimisation algorithm incorporating a random element" and allocation was concealed using an "automated 24-hour telephone randomisation system" administered by a central clinical trials unit. Furthermore, the publication notes that "Characteristics were well balanced between the groups," indicating no concerning baseline imbalances.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** Although the trial was "open-label" (meaning participants and personnel were not blinded to the intervention), there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context. Crucially, the trial appropriately estimated the effect of assignment to the intervention by conducting the analysis according to the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle: "Analysis was by intention to treat." All 816 patients in the MRI group and 807 in the no-MRI group were analyzed in their assigned groups.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** Data for the primary outcome were available for nearly all randomized participants. According to the trial profile (Figure 1), only a very small number of patients were lost to follow-up or had missing primary endpoint data (7 out of 816 in the MRI group and 10 out of 807 in the no-MRI group, which is ~1%). The publication explicitly states that "Patients lost to follow-up [were] regarded as not having a primary endpoint event," and given the extremely low rate of attrition, this would not have a substantial impact on the result.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The primary outcome (reoperation, further mastectomy, or pathologically avoidable mastectomy) involves objective surgical events or outcomes defined by strict criteria set by an independent Data Monitoring and Ethics Committee (DMEC). While outcome assessors (surgeons/pathologists) were not blinded to whether the patient had an MRI, the assessment of these specific surgical outcomes is highly objective and unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The study was prospectively registered (ISRCTN 57474502), and the primary endpoint reported in the publication ("the proportion of patients undergoing a repeat operation or further mastectomy within 6 months of random assignment, or a pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation") aligns precisely with the pre-specified primary outcome. There is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on its statistical significance or direction.

1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, adequately concealed, and baseline differences do not indicate a problem with the randomization process.
       b. Rationale: The trial used a computer-generated sequence and opaque envelopes. "Randomisation was balanced by centre, with an allocation sequence based on a block size of four, generated with a computer random-number generator by the pharmacist... Allocation was concealed by use of sequentially numbered opaque envelopes." Although there were slight baseline imbalances (e.g., FMMS score at inclusion), these are compatible with chance in a trial of this size, and the authors appropriately adjusted for these imbalances in their analysis.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: Participants, carers, and personnel were successfully blinded, and an appropriate analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention.
       b. Rationale: The trial was double-blind. "Fluoxetine and placebo capsules were both prepared by the pharmacist for the study. The placebo was identical to the active drug in appearance and packaging... Patients, carers, all site study staff, and investigators were masked to treatment assignment." The analysis was based on a modified intention-to-treat approach (all available data), which is considered appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment. 

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, and the reasons for missing data do not suggest a high risk of bias.
       b. Rationale: Out of 118 randomized participants, 113 (96%) were included in the primary analysis. The missing outcome data were due to reasons documented and balanced between groups: "Two patients died before day 90, and three withdrew from the study" with reasons given as septic shock, respiratory distress, severe hypoxia, kidney tumour, and pulmonary embolism. These missingness rates are small enough that they are unlikely to importantly bias the result.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The outcome was measured using an appropriate scale, and the outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment.
       b. Rationale: The Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale (FMMS) is a standard, validated scale. Blinding was maintained for assessments: "Participants, carers, and physicians assessing the outcome were masked to group assignment." Therefore, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The reported results correspond to the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registration, with no evidence of selection from multiple analyses or measurements.
       b. Rationale: The primary outcome in the trial registration (NCT00657163) was "Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale" at "M3 (3 months)". This aligns perfectly with the primary outcome reported in the publication: "The primary outcome measure was the change on the FMMS between day 0 and day 90 after the start of the study drug." There is no indication that the reported result was selected from among multiple ways of measuring or analyzing the outcome.

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There are some concerns regarding the randomization process due to a lack of reporting details.
b. Rationale: The study states that "The patients were assigned to two groups at random with 20 patients each", but it does not provide any information regarding the method used for random sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated) or whether allocation concealment was utilized. Baseline characteristics (age) were reported as not significantly different, but the lack of methodological detail warrants some concerns. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The risk of bias due to deviations from the intended intervention is low.
b. Rationale: The interventions being compared are technical scanning parameters (CT collimation settings) applied directly at the time of the scan. Although blinding of participants and personnel is not explicitly described, the nature of the intervention makes it highly unlikely that any deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context or that participants' expectations influenced the outcome. 

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: There is a high risk of bias due to missing outcome data in the final analysis.
b. Rationale: The authors explicitly state, "Four patients had to be excluded from the study because no coronary calcium was detected on any of the reconstructions." These patients were successfully scanned, but their exclusion from the analysis was based entirely on their true outcome value (an Agatston score of zero). Excluding these participants directly biases the estimated mean Agatston scores upwards, as the missingness in the analyzed dataset is strictly dependent on the true value of the outcome.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There are some concerns regarding the measurement of the outcome.
b. Rationale: The Agatston score requires some manual intervention, as a radiologist and a cardiologist "differentiated between calcium and noise by placing regions of interest around the CAC." The authors do not state whether these outcome assessors were blinded to the randomized collimation group. While the threshold (Hounsfield units > 130) is objective, the manual selection of regions of interest involves some clinical judgement that could theoretically be influenced by knowledge of the intervention, leading to some concerns.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There are some concerns regarding the selective reporting of results.
b. Rationale: No pre-specified trial protocol or statistical analysis plan is available (or provided in the text) to verify the primary analysis intentions before unblinded data were assessed. While the authors report both Agatston and volumetric scores across multiple reconstruction intervals as would be expected for this type of imaging study, the absence of a date-stamped, pre-specified plan means bias in the selection of the reported results cannot be entirely ruled out.

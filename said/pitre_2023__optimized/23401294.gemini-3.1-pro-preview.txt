**1. bias due to randomization: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: High risk of bias
b. Rationale: The study used a random component for sequence generation ("pick the name of the orthosis from a bag including the names of both orthoses"). However, this method of allocation is generally not considered adequately concealed, as the person drawing the names could potentially see into the bag or manipulate the draw. The lack of adequate allocation concealment introduces a high risk of bias, despite there being no significant baseline imbalances reported between the small groups (n=10 and n=8). 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Due to the nature of the interventions (two different types of spinal orthoses), blinding of participants and personnel was not possible. However, there is no evidence that any deviations from the intended interventions arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, all 18 randomized participants who completed the study procedure appear to have been analyzed in the groups to which they were assigned, which is appropriate for assessing the effect of assignment to the intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The study report indicates that 18 participants were recruited and randomly allocated to the two groups (10 in the Spinomed group and 8 in the PTS group). The data tables confirm that data for all 18 participants were available and analyzed for the balance score outcome. There is no evidence of missing outcome data or participant dropout.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome (balance score) was objectively measured using the SOT on the NeuroCom EquiTest system, which utilizes automated force plates. Although the outcome assessors could not be blinded to the intervention (as the participants were wearing the orthoses during the tests), the objective and automated nature of the computerized dynamic posturography system means that the assessment of the outcome could not have been meaningfully influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: Some concerns
b. Rationale: There is no trial registration or pre-specified statistical analysis plan available to confirm that the data were analyzed in accordance with a finalized plan before unblinded data were available. While the reported outcomes (balance score, directional control, and reaction time) are standard metrics for the EquiTest and Balance Master systems and are fully reported, the absence of a pre-published protocol means a judgment of 'some concerns' is appropriate for this domain.

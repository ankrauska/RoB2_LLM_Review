Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance and the provided article, here is the risk of bias assessment for the effect of supervision on patient care, measured by a team checklist score (assessing the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline):

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
**a. Judgement:** The authors state that residents were randomized, but they provide no information about how the random sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed. There were no obvious major baseline imbalances, but the lack of information raises some concerns.
**b. Rationale:** The article states "Residents were randomized to one of three levels of supervision" but provides no further details on sequence generation or allocation concealment. Table 1 shows baseline characteristics among the three groups, which appear relatively balanced, but without information on the randomization process, the judgement defaults to "some concerns" (signalling questions 1.1 and 1.2 answered "No information").

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Blinding was not feasible given the nature of the intervention (levels of physical proximity of the supervisor). However, deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context are unlikely to have affected the outcome of this simulation-based study. Furthermore, an appropriate analysis was performed.
**b. Rationale:** The study assessed a simulated scenario where the intervention is the proximity of the supervisor (direct, immediately available, distant). Blinding participants and personnel to this is impossible. The article specifies: "To study the relationship between level of supervision and patient care, we conducted a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) on the team checklist scores of the supervised scenario with the level of clinical supervision (direct, immediately available, and distant) as the independent variable." All 53 randomized residents appear to be included in the analysis as assigned (18 direct, 16 immediately available, 19 distant), consistent with an intention-to-treat approach for the available data. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Data for this outcome were available for all randomized participants.
**b. Rationale:** The study recruited 53 residents. Table 1, Table 2, and Table 3 report results for exactly 53 residents (Direct n=18, Immediately Available n=16, Distant n=19). There is no indication of missing outcome data or dropouts. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** The team checklist score is an objective measure of completed clinical tasks, and the assessors reviewed videotaped performances, mitigating the risk of bias. 
**b. Rationale:** The method of measuring the outcome was appropriate. The outcome assessors reviewed videotapes: "Two independent raters were trained to use the global rating scale and the checklists during a two-step process... The raters then independently reviewed and rated all the videotaped performance of the participants. Any discrepancy in the checklist scores was resolved by a third independent assessor". While the raters likely could see the level of supervision on the video (and thus were not blinded), the checklist involves recording whether specific clinical tasks were performed. It is unlikely that the assessment of these objective checklist tasks was heavily influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, especially with multiple independent raters resolving discrepancies.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
**a. Judgement:** There is no mention of a pre-specified analysis plan or trial registration that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available, leaving some uncertainty about whether the reported outcomes and analyses were selected from multiple eligible options.
**b. Rationale:** No trial registration (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov) or published protocol/statistical analysis plan is referenced in the text. Therefore, we have "No information" on whether the data were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified plan. The team checklist score and the specific analyses performed appear standard and are reported for all groups, but the lack of a pre-specified plan leads to a judgment of "some concerns."

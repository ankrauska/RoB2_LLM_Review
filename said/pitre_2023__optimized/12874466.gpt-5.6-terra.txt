1. **bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The allocation sequence was computer-generated and was held by an independent third party responsible for packaging the study tablets. The reported baseline characteristics do not provide convincing evidence that the randomization process failed.  
   b. **Rationale:** The report states: “*Individuals were randomized by a computer-generated list, which was used for packaging of the tablets and then maintained by a third party that was not involved in the conduction of the study.*” This supports both a random sequence and concealment until allocation.  
   
   The groups contained 30 participants each and were broadly similar at baseline, including age, dialysis duration, body weight, blood pressure, inflammatory markers, dialysis adequacy, and Charlson comorbidity score. Some individual differences existed (e.g., cerebrovascular disease: 6 placebo versus 1 bicarbonate; peptic ulcer disease: 2 versus 7), but in this small trial these do not constitute persuasive evidence of a systematic failure of randomization.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to oral sodium bicarbonate versus placebo at baseline. Participants and investigators were blinded, the use of identical placebo tablets was described, and the SGA analysis was stated to be intention-to-treat.  
   b. **Rationale:** The trial used a placebo designed to preserve blinding: “*The appearance, packaging, and labeling of the tablets were identical. Both kinds of tablets had artificial mint flavor to give an identical taste.*” Further, “*Both patients and investigators were blinded*,” and “*Results of biochemical analyses and nutritional assessments were completed before the randomization code was broken at the end of the study.*”  
   
   There were treatment discontinuations—four participants receiving bicarbonate and three receiving placebo stopped study medication—but these were relatively balanced. Importantly, “*biochemical tests and nutritional assessment were continued for 52 wk according to the study protocol*” after medication discontinuation. The authors also explicitly state: “*Analyses were intention to treat irrespective of adherence to treatment regimen.*” Thus, the reported SGA result was analysed according to randomized assignment, which is appropriate for the specified effect of assignment.

3. **missing outcome data: high risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias. Availability of SGA data at each assessment time point is not reported, and outcome data were necessarily unavailable after deaths and probably after transfer to haemodialysis or transplantation. The extent and distribution of missing SGA data, and methods used to address them, are not provided.  
   b. **Rationale:** Although the report says that nutritional assessments continued after treatment discontinuation, it does not give the number of participants with SGA measurements at 12, 24, 36, and 52 weeks, nor a participant flow diagram or an analysis of missing SGA data. The repeated-measures results in Table 3 are presented without denominators.  
   
   During follow-up, “*2 patients of the treatment group died*” and “*Five patients of the placebo group died*.” In addition, “*One patient from each group was transferred to long-term hemodialysis, and 1 patient of the treatment group had kidney transplantation.*” Participants who died cannot contribute subsequent SGA measurements, and their missing SGA values are plausibly related to their underlying health and nutritional status. Deaths were also more frequent in the placebo group (5/30 versus 2/30), making missingness potentially differential between groups. No sensitivity analysis, imputation approach, or other evidence is provided to show that missing outcome data could not have materially affected the SGA effect estimate.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias. SGA was measured using a stated, established scoring system by trained observers who were blinded to treatment allocation and biochemical results.  
   b. **Rationale:** The investigators specify that “*SGA was performed at 0, 12, 24, 36, and 52 wk by two trained observers who were blinded from the treatment group allocation and biochemical results of the patients.*” The four-item seven-point SGA system was described, and assessor training produced “*a Cohen’s kappa concordant coefficient for agreement of 0.84,*” indicating good inter-observer agreement.  
   
   SGA contains subjective components, including anorexia, weight change, subcutaneous tissue, and muscle mass. However, blinding of the trained outcome assessors substantially limits differential assessment. Participants were also blinded through use of matched placebo tablets, reducing the likelihood that patient reporting of appetite or weight-related history was influenced by knowledge of treatment assignment.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns. SGA was clearly identified as the primary outcome and the paper reports the planned assessment time points and a longitudinal analysis. However, there is no evidence of a detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available, and the available registration information does not establish that the exact SGA analysis and reported time-point results were prospectively specified.  
   b. **Rationale:** The publication states: “*The primary outcome measure was the SGA score.*” It also specifies planned SGA measurement at “*0, 12, 24, 36, and 52 wk*” and describes use of repeated-measures ANOVA with treatment group, time, and Charlson Comorbidity Index. These features reduce concern that SGA itself was selectively introduced after the results were known.  
   
   Nevertheless, the report presents several possible SGA results: an overall repeated-measures analysis, individual time-point comparisons, and analyses of the overall score and four component scores. In the absence of a prospectively finalized analysis plan that specifies the precise primary estimand, covariate adjustment, approach to missing values, and choice of reported time-point contrasts, it cannot be confirmed that the reported SGA result was not selected from multiple eligible analyses.

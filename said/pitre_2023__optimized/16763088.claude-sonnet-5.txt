# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Bond Failure Rate Outcome

**Study design:** Individually-randomized, split-mouth cross-quadrant trial
**Experimental intervention:** Transbond™ Plus Self-Etching Primer (SEP)
**Comparator intervention:** Conventional 37% phosphoric acid etch + Transbond™ MIP primer
**Outcome assessed:** Bracket failure rate (12 months)
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation: "Randomization for allocation of either of the two etch and primer systems to the upper right quadrant was undertaken using a block randomization method as described by Roberts and Torgerson (1998)." This is an adequate random component → **Yes** to sequence being random.
- Allocation concealment: "The operators were unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial." This suggests allocation concealment was adequate → **Yes/Probably yes**.
- However, a specific concern arises from the described block randomization method: "This randomization method guarantees that any 10 consecutive subjects will contain five subjects who have been etched and primed with Transbond™ Plus SEP on the upper right and five with it on the upper left." This describes a fixed block size of 10, which is known to and predictable by, and could allow prediction of the last allocations within each block, potentially undermining concealment for later patients within each block. This raises some concern about the robustness of allocation concealment.
- No baseline imbalance data are presented to assess whether randomization was successfully implemented (no comparison of baseline characteristics between the SEP-assigned and conventional-assigned quadrants is reported), though the split-mouth design mitigates confounding since each patient serves as their own control.
- Given the fixed block size disclosed in the methods (a methodological vulnerability) and no reported baseline comparison data, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Signalling Q2.1 (participants aware): "The patients were unaware which system had been used on each side of the mouth" → **No**.
- Signalling Q2.2 (personnel aware): "as the two systems had different modes of application, it was not possible to blind the operators to the type of system being used" → **Yes**, operators were aware of intervention assignment.
- Since operators (people delivering the intervention) were aware, we must assess whether deviations arose because of the trial context and affected the outcome.
- There is a plausible mechanism for the trial context to influence outcomes: awareness of which system was being used could affect operator technique/care, especially given a documented difference by number of manipulations: "brackets which had been manipulated more than three times prior to curing almost doubled the failure rate with Transbond™ Plus SEP" (7.8% vs 15.3%), while manipulations had little effect on the conventional system (3.6% vs 4.3%). This asymmetric effect raises concern that operator awareness of intervention type differentially affected handling/care of the SEP-treated brackets — a deviation arising from trial context (lack of equipoise or differential handling due to awareness) that is likely to have affected the outcome and was unbalanced between the interventions.
- Analysis: The study appears to have followed all randomized patients and all bonded brackets in the primary analysis — this appears consistent with an ITT-type analysis, and "All patients were observed for the entire 12-month period" including the one who moved away, with his data retained ("His data was still included in the analysis"). This satisfies the appropriate analysis criterion (Q2.6 = Yes).
- However, because operators were unblinded, and there is direct evidence in the data (differential manipulation effects) that unblinding-related differences in handling likely affected the outcome and were unbalanced between groups, this meets the criteria for **High risk of bias** in Part 1 of this domain, leading to an overall **High risk of bias** judgement.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- "A total of 661 brackets were placed by two operators" and "All patients were observed for the entire 12-month period apart from one 13-year-old male who moved to another city after 7 months of observation, during which time no bracket failures occurred. His data was still included in the analysis, as it was considered that it still provided useful information on bracket survival rates."
- This indicates virtually complete outcome data across all 661 brackets and 39 patients, with only a single patient having reduced follow-up (7 of 12 months), and even this patient's data were retained and used (appropriately handled via survival analysis with censoring).
- Since outcome data (bracket failure/survival) were available for essentially all randomized brackets/patients, and the one instance of incomplete follow-up was non-informative (patient moved for reasons unrelated to outcome, and this was handled via survival analysis with appropriate censoring), the risk of bias due to missing outcome data is low.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Outcome (bracket failure/survival, a binary/time-to-event clinical outcome) is an objective, observer-reported outcome not involving substantial judgement — a bracket is either debonded/failed or not, and the date of failure is objectively recorded: "If a bond failed, the tooth on which the failure occurred, the date of failure, and the amount of adhesive remaining on the tooth using the adhesive remnant index (Årtun and Bergland, 1984) were recorded."
- The method of measurement was not inappropriate — clinical detection of bracket failure is a standard, valid outcome measure.
- Measurement/ascertainment did not differ between intervention groups, since all patients returned for the same follow-up visits and bracket-failure ascertainment methodology was identical for both quadrants/systems.
- Although the operators/clinical assessors were aware of the intervention assigned to each quadrant (given the different application techniques and split-mouth design making blinding of the applying clinician impossible), bracket failure/debonding is an objective, unambiguous clinical event (similar to all-cause mortality) that leaves little room for subjective interpretation or bias in ascertainment. Therefore, knowledge of intervention assignment is unlikely to have influenced whether a bracket was recorded as failed.
- Thus, despite lack of blinding of assessors, this is judged as low risk of bias because the outcome assessment involves minimal judgement and is not likely influenced by knowledge of intervention received.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- No trial registration record was identified/available for this study (the trial predates common prospective registration practice — conducted 2002-2003, and no registry ID or protocol is referenced in the article) to confirm that the analyses reported were pre-specified before unblinded outcome data were available. Per instructions, I attempted to use trial registration information (doc_id: 4dab5cab-2b44-42f8-8559-2dd9073b18b5) but no accessible/matching registration entry was identifiable for this specific trial to confirm pre-specification.
- A sample size calculation is reported a priori ("Three hundred and two brackets were required in each etching group in order to have an adequate power for the study of 80 per cent to show a statistically significant difference at P<0.05 between a 5 and 12 per cent bracket failure rate"), which suggests the primary outcome (bracket failure rate) and comparison were pre-specified, lending some reassurance regarding the primary result of failure rate comparison between systems.
- However, without a pre-registered protocol or statistical analysis plan, it cannot be confirmed that alternative analyses (e.g., different failure definitions, different statistical models, other covariate adjustments) were not selectively reported based on results. Multiple additional analyses were conducted (by gender, age, operator, tooth location, number of manipulations) without clear indication these were all pre-specified with defined thresholds/methods.
- Given the absence of independently verifiable pre-specified analysis intentions (protocol/SAP/registry), but a clear a priori-stated primary hypothesis and sample size justification for the specific failure-rate outcome being assessed here, the judgement is **Some concerns** rather than high risk, since the specific result assessed (overall bracket failure rate by etching system) appears consistent with the pre-planned primary comparison.

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** Because the domain "Deviations from intended interventions" is judged at High risk of bias (due to unblinded operators combined with evidence of differential, outcome-affecting handling effects between the SEP and conventional groups), the overall result for bracket failure rate is judged to be at **High risk of bias**, per RoB 2.0 guidance (a "High" judgement in any single domain results in an overall "High" risk of bias judgement).

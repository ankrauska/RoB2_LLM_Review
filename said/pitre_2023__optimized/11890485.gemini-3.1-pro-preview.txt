**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The trial utilized a random process for allocation by having participants or their caregivers draw from 100 sealed, nontransparent envelopes (53 intervention, 47 control), which acts as a form of drawing lots (random sequence generation). The use of sealed, nontransparent envelopes drawn by the patient/caregiver constitutes adequate allocation concealment. Furthermore, baseline characteristics were well-balanced between the groups, indicating no problems with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias. 
* **Rationale:** Although participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention (blinding is not feasible for a nurse case management support program), there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, an appropriate analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention, as there were no dropouts and all 100 randomized patients were included in the survival analyses (intention-to-treat approach). 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The study profile (Figure 1) and the text state that "There were no dropouts from the study during the 2-year follow-up." Outcome data (time to institutionalization or death at home) were available for all randomized participants. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The outcome (time to institutionalization) was assessed by an independent group of experts. The text explicitly states: "These groups of experts were blind to the allocation and generally unaware that a patient was even participating in the study." Therefore, the outcome assessors were blinded, and the assessment was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** While there is no explicit mention of a published protocol or pre-registered analysis plan, the main outcome (time to institutionalization) is clearly defined, objective, and analyzed using standard survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier and Cox regression) as is appropriate for this type of data. There is no indication that the reported results were selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on the direction or statistical significance of the findings.

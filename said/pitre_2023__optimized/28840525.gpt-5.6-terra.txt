**Assessment scope:** individually randomized, parallel-group trial comparing silastic-ring laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (SR-LRYGB) with laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG), for T2D remission at 1 year, defined using each of four HbA1c thresholds and requiring no glucose-lowering medication. The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline**.

The judgements below apply equally to remission defined as HbA1c **≤38 mmol/mol (5.6%)**, **<42 mmol/mol (6.0%)**, **<48 mmol/mol (6.5%)**, and **<53 mmol/mol (7.0%)**, because the methods, follow-up, and outcome ascertainment were the same for each threshold.

1. **Bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The allocation sequence was adequately random: the report states that “**Randomisation was by computer-generated random number codes**.” Minimization by age, BMI, diabetes duration, insulin treatment, and ethnicity was used; minimization implemented with a random component is compatible with valid random allocation.  
   - Allocation concealment was appropriate: codes were “**managed by an independent study member**” and “**disclosed after anaesthesia induction**.” Thus, the participant and the clinical staff responsible for enrolment could not know the forthcoming allocation before recruitment and surgery.  
   - The group sizes were close to the planned 1:1 ratio (56 SR-LRYGB; 58 LSG). Baseline characteristics were broadly comparable, including age, BMI, duration of diabetes, insulin use, HbA1c, and ethnicity. Although female sex differed numerically (59% versus 45%), this isolated difference is compatible with chance and does not suggest a failure of randomization.  
   - Therefore, there is no indication that randomization or allocation concealment was compromised.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The study was explicitly designed as a “**double-blind (patient and assessor) clinical trial**.” Participants were blinded, and identical port placements and methylene-blue leak testing were used irrespective of procedure, supporting maintenance of participant blinding.  
   - Although operating surgeons necessarily knew the procedure delivered, the clinically important post-operative management relevant to the remission outcome was standardized and blinded: “**All pharmacological agents for diabetes were discontinued following surgery subject to endocrinologist review prior to discharge**,” and “**Metabolic medications were commenced post-operatively according to a standard medical review protocol, by endocrinologists blinded to treatment allocation**.” This reduces the likelihood that treatment knowledge led to differential medication decisions that could alter the remission classification.  
   - No evidence is reported of differential non-protocol co-interventions, protocol failures arising from the trial context, or systematic contamination between groups that would plausibly affect HbA1c or diabetes-medication use. Surgical complications and subsequent procedures occurred, but these are clinical events following the assigned procedures and are not, by themselves, deviations caused by the trial context.  
   - The 1-year remission analysis retained participants in their randomized treatment groups: results were reported for all 56 SR-LRYGB participants and 53 of 58 LSG participants. Exclusion of those with missing outcome data is considered a modified ITT analysis and is appropriate for this RoB 2 domain; the consequences of missing data are considered separately below.

3. **Missing outcome data: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - Outcome data at 1 year were available for all 56 participants assigned to SR-LRYGB but for only 53 of 58 assigned to LSG. Thus, 5/58 (8.6%) participants in the LSG group had missing remission data, versus 0/56 in the SR-LRYGB group.  
   - The report states: “**Loss to follow-up of 5 participants occurred from the LSG group due to failure to make contact and/or to attend appointments beyond 3 months post-operatively**.” The CONSORT diagram likewise indicates five losses in LSG and none in SR-LRYGB.  
   - The amount of missing data is not negligible for these binary remission outcomes, particularly because the missingness is entirely confined to one intervention group. For example, the observed LSG remission counts range from 23/53 to 41/53 across the thresholds; outcomes for five missing participants could potentially alter the estimated between-group comparison.  
   - There is no sensitivity analysis assessing the effect of plausible outcomes among the five missing LSG participants, and no analysis that adequately demonstrates that missing data could not affect the result.  
   - However, the stated reason—failure of contact/attendance—does not establish that missingness was likely dependent on the unobserved HbA1c or remission status. Accordingly, this is best judged as **some concerns**, rather than high risk of bias.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The outcome was objectively defined using HbA1c thresholds plus absence of glucose-lowering medication. HbA1c is a laboratory-based biochemical measure with little scope for subjective interpretation.  
   - The same thresholds and requirement for no diabetes medication were applied in both groups. The trial states that the remission outcomes were “**the proportions achieving HbA1c thresholds … without the need for any glucose lowering therapy**.”  
   - Outcome assessors were blinded: the trial was described as “**double-blind (patient and assessor)**,” and clinical data were extracted by medical-record review. Endocrinologists determining post-operative medication management were also “**blinded to treatment allocation**.”  
   - There is no evidence that HbA1c measurement, ascertainment of medication use, measurement timing, or remission classification differed systematically between intervention groups. Therefore, differential outcome-measurement bias is unlikely.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The trial registration record identifies the primary outcome only broadly as “**Remission of type 2 diabetes mellitus/ glycaemic control**,” without specifying the HbA1c threshold, medication criterion, analysis method, or timing. It also does not provide a sufficiently detailed, date-stamped statistical analysis plan. Therefore, it cannot confirm that the reported 1-year analyses using all four HbA1c thresholds were specified before unblinded outcome data were available.  
   - The article describes the registered/protocol primary outcome differently: “**Primary outcome is the proportion of patients achieving HbA1c <42 mmol/mol (<6%) without glucose lowering therapy at 5 years**.” The published paper reports 1-year outcomes using four thresholds—≤38, <42, <48, and <53 mmol/mol—stating these were reported “**in order to allow direct comparison with previous studies**.”  
   - Reporting several alternative HbA1c thresholds creates multiple eligible outcome definitions for T2D remission. In the absence of a detailed pre-specified analysis plan, it is not possible to determine whether these thresholds, the 1-year time point, and the specific analyses were selected independently of the results.  
   - Nevertheless, all four thresholds are transparently reported in the same table, with effect estimates that are consistently close to null, and there is no direct evidence that one threshold or analysis was selectively chosen because it was more favourable. Thus, high risk of bias is not warranted, but the absence of a sufficiently detailed prospective analysis specification leads to **some concerns**.

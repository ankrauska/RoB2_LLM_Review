**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation and allocation concealment, and baseline characteristics were well balanced between the groups. 
* **b. Rationale:** The trial reports that "Registration and random assignment were coordinated centrally at the KCSG data center. The random permutation method was used for random assignment." Furthermore, Table 1 shows no significant differences in baseline characteristics between the maintenance and observation arms, indicating no evidence of problems with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Although participants and trial personnel were not blinded to the assigned interventions, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment. 
* **b. Rationale:** The trial was open-label, meaning patients and investigators were aware of the treatment assignments. However, the study assessed the effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect) and states, "All randomly assigned patients were included in the efficacy analysis according to the intent-to-treat principle." Discontinuations due to physician or patient discretion reflect real-world adherence issues rather than trial-context-driven deviations. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Outcome data were available for all or nearly all randomized participants. 
* **b. Rationale:** The CONSORT flow diagram (Figure 1B) and the statistical methods section specify that all 231 randomized patients (116 in the maintenance arm and 115 in the observation arm) were included in the final efficacy analyses using the intention-to-treat principle. Therefore, there is no evidence of substantial missing outcome data that could bias the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** The outcome assessors (investigators) were aware of the intervention received, and the assessment of progression-free survival involves some degree of subjective judgment that could potentially be influenced by this knowledge. 
* **b. Rationale:** The trial report states, "PFS and the duration of response were assessed by investigators." Because this was an open-label trial without a blinded independent central review of radiographic scans, the investigators' knowledge of treatment assignment could theoretically influence the exact timing of declaring disease progression (which relies on RECIST criteria but still involves some subjectivity). 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The reported outcomes match those pre-specified in the trial registry, and the data were analyzed in accordance with the pre-specified plan. 
* **b. Rationale:** The clinical trial registry (NCT00561119) lists "Progression free survival" as the primary outcome measure, with a timeframe of approximately 4 years. The published report clearly reports this outcome using the standard definitions and analysis methods (Kaplan-Meier and log-rank tests) that are appropriate and expected for this type of time-to-event data, showing no evidence of selective reporting.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, adequately concealed, and baseline differences did not suggest any problems with the randomization process. 
b. Rationale: The trial report states that a "randomization scheme prepared at the Department of Statistics and Data Management at proDERM. Randomization was performed via randomly permuted blocks of fixed size using validated software RANCODE 3.6." Allocation concealment was maintained as "Blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants and study personnel were blinded to the intervention assignments, and the data was analyzed using an appropriate intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis.
b. Rationale: The study was a "double-blind, controlled, randomized, clinical trial." The hand rubs were "randomly assigned a letter A or B for blinding of the test formulations." Furthermore, an ITT analysis was conducted for the effect of assignment to intervention, as "Results are presented for the intention-to-treat (ITT) population."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all or nearly all participants randomized, and missing data in the ITT population was appropriately minimal.
b. Rationale: A total of 35 subjects commenced the study. Only 3 subjects had missing data due to protocol violations (which were imputed using Last Observation Carried Forward in the ITT analysis). The amount of missing data (3 out of 35) is small enough that it is unlikely to have introduced a material bias in the ITT population.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The method of measuring the outcome was appropriate, and the outcome assessor was blinded to the intervention received.
b. Rationale: Erythema and dryness were visually assessed according to a standard modified scale. The report explicitly states, "All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator," ensuring that outcome assessment was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The reported results correspond to the intended primary outcomes and pre-specified analyses, with no indication of selection based on the results.
b. Rationale: The primary outcome parameter ("sum score of dryness and erythema over all assessment times") was clearly pre-defined, and the sample size estimation and statistical analysis were based on this primary objective. There is no evidence that the reported data were selected from multiple different outcome measurements or analyses based on statistical significance.

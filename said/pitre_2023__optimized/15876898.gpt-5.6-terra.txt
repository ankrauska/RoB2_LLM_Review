1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns. The study is described as randomized, and baseline characteristics appear broadly comparable, but there is insufficient information to establish that the allocation sequence was genuinely random or that allocation was adequately concealed.

   b. **Rationale:** The report states that “*the randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration*” and that medication was supplied in “*numbered boxes*.” However, it does not state how the random sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated random numbers) or provide sufficient detail that the numbered boxes ensured concealment until an irrevocable enrolment decision had been made. Thus, responses to RoB 2 signalling questions 1.1 and 1.2 are **No information**.

   The analysed groups were reasonably similar at baseline: mean age was 35.2 versus 34.5 years, initial weight 86.6 versus 85.1 kg, and olanzapine dose 7.8 versus 7.2 mg/day for topiramate and placebo, respectively. These differences do not suggest a problem with randomization (question 1.3: **Probably no**). Nevertheless, under RoB 2, missing information about allocation concealment leads to **some concerns**.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to topiramate versus placebo at baseline.

   b. **Rationale:** Participants and clinicians were blinded: “*Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment*,” and the placebo was matching. Therefore, awareness of treatment allocation by participants, carers, or people delivering treatment was unlikely (questions 2.1 and 2.2: **Probably no**).

   The report also indicates that olanzapine doses and concomitant medications “*remained constant*” during the trial. There is no evidence of differential co-interventions, protocol failures, or deviations caused by the trial context that would have affected weight (questions 2.3–2.5: **Not applicable**).

   The six participants excluded from analysis had missing follow-up assessments; this is addressed under missing outcome data rather than this domain. The analysed participants remained in their randomized groups, and there is no indication of an as-treated or per-protocol analysis based on adherence. For the assignment effect, this modified intention-to-treat/available-case approach is not itself a concern in this domain (question 2.6: **Probably yes**), although it creates serious concerns in the missing-data domain.

3. **Missing outcome data: high risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Weight data at the final assessment were unavailable for 6 of the 49 randomized women (12.2%). Importantly, all missing participants were in the placebo group: “*Six subjects who failed to appear for more than one evaluation (all from the placebo group) dropped out of the study, and their data were not further analyzed.*” Thus, 25/25 (100%) women in the topiramate group but only 18/24 (75%) women randomized to placebo contributed to the final weight analysis.

   These data were not available for nearly all randomized participants, particularly given the marked and completely differential attrition (question 3.1: **No**). The report provides no sensitivity analysis, imputation approach, or analysis demonstrating that the result was robust to the missing placebo-group outcomes (question 3.2: **No**).

   Failure to attend more than one evaluation could plausibly be related to weight trajectory, dissatisfaction with treatment allocation, worsening health, or other factors associated with the outcome (question 3.3: **Probably yes**). Because all missing data occurred in the placebo group, missingness is likely to have depended on both allocation and potentially the unobserved weight outcome (question 3.4: **Probably yes**). The complete-case analysis could therefore materially exaggerate or otherwise distort the difference in weight change between groups.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Weight is an objective outcome, and participants were “*seen, weighed*” every two weeks. There is no indication that the method of measuring body weight was inappropriate or that different measurement procedures or time points were used between groups (questions 4.1 and 4.2: **Probably no**).

   Moreover, both participants and clinicians were blinded to topiramate/placebo allocation. Although the report does not explicitly identify the staff who performed weighing or state that they were blinded, clinicians and participants were blinded, and body weight is not meaningfully susceptible to subjective assessor judgement. Knowledge of treatment allocation would therefore be very unlikely to influence the recorded weight (questions 4.3–4.5: **Probably no/Not applicable**).

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The article identifies “*weight checks*” as a primary outcome measure and reports body weight repeatedly over the 10-week study, with a final between-group comparison. However, no protocol, statistical analysis plan, or sufficiently detailed prospectively finalized registry analysis plan is available in the supplied material to verify that the reported 10-week weight result, analytical approach, and metric were pre-specified before unblinded data became available (question 5.1: **No information**).

   Multiple eligible weight results were potentially available, including measurements every two weeks and potentially alternative analyses such as final weight, change from baseline, or repeated-measures analyses. The article reports a final assessment and a graphical trajectory but does not document a prespecified rule for selecting the reported time point or analysis. There is no direct evidence that the reported result was selected because it was favourable; therefore, high risk of bias is not justified. Nevertheless, the absence of a prespecified analysis plan and the opportunity to select among multiple time points and analytical methods result in **some concerns** (questions 5.2 and 5.3: **No information**).

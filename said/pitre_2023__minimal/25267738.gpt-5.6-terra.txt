**Comparison assessed:** among participants with residual vein thrombosis (RVT), continuation of LMWH for a further 6 months (group A1, *n*=119) versus discontinuation of LMWH (group A2, *n*=123), for recurrent thrombotic events. The effect of **assignment** to intervention at randomization is considered.

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns, because random allocation is stated, and measured baseline characteristics appear balanced, but the reporting does not adequately establish either a random sequence-generation method or robust allocation concealment.  
   b. **Rationale:** The article states: “Patients with RVT were allocated to either stop or continue LMWH. Random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelop[e] technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10.” This does not specify how the order of assignments within blocks was randomized. Moreover, although sealed envelopes were used, the report does not state that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, and opened only after irreversible enrolment. A fixed block size of 10 can make assignments predictable if recruiters know the block structure and previous allocations.  
   
   The baseline characteristics of the randomized A1 and A2 groups were closely similar, including age, sex, recent cancer surgery, chemotherapy, metastasis, index thrombosis characteristics, cancer site, and treatments. Thus, there is no evident baseline imbalance suggesting a failed randomization process. Nevertheless, incomplete information on sequence generation and concealment leads to some concerns rather than low risk.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention.  
   b. **Rationale:** Participants and treating physicians were not blinded: “Patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT.” However, for the assignment effect, lack of blinding alone does not imply bias; bias requires deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context, were unbalanced, and affected recurrent VTE.  
   
   There is no report of such trial-context deviations or differential co-interventions. Adherence in the continuation group was high: “15 of the 119 patients interrupted LMWH for less than 5 days during study treatment.” Brief interruptions of this sort do not indicate an important, trial-context-related departure from the assigned strategy. The trial reports that data “were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis,” with patients retained in their randomized groups. This is an appropriate analysis for estimating the effect of assignment to continuation versus discontinuation of LMWH.  

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** Recurrent VTE data are reported for all randomized participants in the relevant comparison: 22/119 in group A1 and 27/123 in group A2. The article also accounts for deaths during follow-up—12/119 in A1 and 19/123 in A2—and reports that all deaths were due to cancer progression. No loss to follow-up or unexplained missing recurrent-VTE outcome data are reported.  
   
   The analysis used time-to-event methods (Kaplan–Meier and Cox proportional-hazards models), which are appropriate for recurrent-event follow-up. Although death is a competing event in this population, the report provides the number and causes of deaths and does not indicate informative withdrawal or differential loss to follow-up beyond these documented deaths. There is therefore no evidence that missing outcome data materially biased the recurrent-VTE comparison.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** Recurrent VTE was defined using objective diagnostic procedures. Recurrent DVT was diagnosed when “a previously fully compressible segment became no longer compressible” or there was “an increase of more than 4 mm in the diameter of the residual thrombus during compression”; uncertain cases underwent repeat testing or contrast venography. Possible pulmonary embolism was diagnosed using objective algorithms incorporating clinical probability and ventilation-perfusion scanning or helical computed tomography.  
   
   Crucially, outcome assessment was conducted by a blinded central committee: “All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of the patient’s name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment.” Thus, despite the open-label nature of treatment delivery, the assessors of recurrent VTE were unaware of intervention assignment and RVT status. The same clinically triggered diagnostic approach and scheduled follow-up structure were used across groups. There is no indication that outcome ascertainment differed systematically by intervention group.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:** The registry record identifies the primary outcome only as “Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism.” It does not provide sufficient detail on the planned outcome time point, analysis population, handling of deaths/censoring, effect measure, covariate adjustment, or statistical analysis plan. It also lists “Minor bleeding” and “Death due to cancer” as secondary outcomes, whereas the publication describes major bleeding as the secondary end point.  
   
   The article reports recurrent VTE at more than one time interval and reports several analyses, including cumulative event rates and adjusted hazard ratios. For example, it reports an adjusted HR for A2 versus A1 of 1.58 (95% CI 0.8 to 3.0) for the randomized comparison in the main text, while the abstract reports an adjusted HR of 1.37 (95% CI 0.7 to 2.5). The available registration information contains no pre-specified analysis plan finalized before outcome data were available, so it is not possible to verify that the reported recurrent-VTE result was selected neither from multiple eligible outcome measurements/time points nor from multiple eligible analyses. There is no direct evidence of selective reporting, but the lack of a sufficiently detailed prospective analysis plan gives some concerns.

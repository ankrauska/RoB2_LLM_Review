Based on the RoB 2.0 tool and the provided article, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of weight:

    1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: The trial describes a random allocation sequence that was adequately concealed, and baseline characteristics were balanced between groups.
           b. Rationale: The study states that "randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration" and that "Tablets were supplied in numbered boxes. Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment." Furthermore, the groups were comparable at baseline regarding initial body weight and other characteristics.

    2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: The study was double-blinded, making it unlikely that deviations from the intended intervention occurred due to the trial context. An appropriate analysis (excluding those with missing data) was used for the effect of assignment to intervention.
           b. Rationale: The trial was described as a "random, double-blind, placebo-controlled study" using a "matching placebo." Therefore, participants, carers, and personnel were unaware of the intervention assignments. The exclusion of participants with missing data from the analysis is addressed in the missing outcome data domain rather than this domain, per RoB 2 guidance.

    3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias
           a. Judgement: There is a substantial imbalance in missing outcome data between the two groups, and it is highly likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value.
           b. Rationale: The authors report that "Six subjects who failed to appear for more than one evaluation (all from the placebo group) dropped out of the study, and their data were not further analyzed." This means 25% (6 out of 24) of the placebo group dropped out compared to 0% (0 out of 25) of the topiramate group. This stark difference in attrition, especially since all dropouts were in the control arm (potentially due to lack of efficacy/weight loss), suggests that the missingness is likely related to the outcome.

    4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: Weight is an objective outcome measured appropriately, and assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment.
           b. Rationale: Body weight was measured by clinical staff during bi-weekly visits. The method is appropriate, comparable across groups, and "Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment," reducing any risk of differential measurement error.

    5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: The reported data match the expected primary outcome of the study, and there is no indication that the results were selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on statistical significance.
           b. Rationale: Weight loss was the clearly defined primary outcome measure. The results (mean change, standard deviation, and differences) are reported straightforwardly at the pre-specified 10-week endpoint without any unusual data manipulations or alternative analyses being selectively presented.

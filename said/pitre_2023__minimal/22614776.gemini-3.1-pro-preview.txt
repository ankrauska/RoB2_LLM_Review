Based on the RoB 2.0 tool and the provided documents, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of peer aggression at 12 months:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** The study used an appropriate randomized allocation sequence, stating that participants "were randomly selected (stratified by gender and age: 14–15, 16–18 years), and assigned to 1 of the 3 study conditions". Randomization occurred after the computerized baseline assessment, ensuring allocation concealment. There were no significant differences between conditions on baseline demographics such as age, race, or gender, suggesting the randomization process was successful. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** Participants and personnel were aware of the assigned interventions (therapist brief intervention, computer brief intervention, or control brochure) because blinding is not feasible for behavioral interventions. However, there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose due to the trial context. Furthermore, the trialists used an appropriate analysis to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention, stating that "An intent-to-treat approach was used. All randomly assigned participants were included whether the intervention was received or not." 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial had a high retention rate, with 83.6% (n = 607/726) completing the 12-month follow-up. To handle missing data, the researchers used generalized estimating equations (GEE) analyses, which "use all data available for participants, including those lost to attrition." It is unlikely that the missingness in the outcome depended on its true value to an extent that would substantially bias the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
*   **a. Judgement:** Some concerns
*   **b. Rationale:** The outcome of peer aggression was measured via a self-reported survey, making the participants the outcome assessors. Because participants were not blinded to their intervention group, their awareness of receiving a violence prevention intervention could have influenced their self-assessment. The authors attempted to mitigate this by using a "self-administered computer survey" to increase confidentiality and validity, but the subjective nature of self-reported aggression leaves room for some concerns regarding measurement bias.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial was registered before completion (NCT00251212), and the protocol pre-specified that the primary outcomes would be evaluated at 12-months and include "violent behaviors". The reported outcome of severe peer aggression aligns with the pre-specified primary outcome. The data were analyzed in accordance with the planned methodology (GEE models) without evidence of selecting results based on statistical significance or direction.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Subjective Global Assessment (SGA) Score

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The trial used adequate methods for both sequence generation and allocation concealment. The article states: "Individuals were randomized by a computer-generated list, which was used for packaging of the tablets and then maintained by a third party that was not involved in the conduction of the study." This satisfies both signalling questions 1.1 (random sequence: Yes) and 1.2 (concealment: Yes) at a low risk level.

However, examination of Table 1 (baseline characteristics) reveals several notable imbalances that raise concern about whether the randomization process was fully successful: congestive heart failure (8 vs 12 patients), cerebrovascular disease (6 vs 1 patients), peptic ulcer disease (2 vs 7 patients), and diabetes as underlying cause (8 vs 12). While the authors state these differences were "not statistically significant," the cerebrovascular disease imbalance (6 vs 1, a 6-fold difference) and peptic ulcer disease imbalance (2 vs 7) in a sample of only 60 patients are sizeable enough with small sample sizes that some caution is warranted, even though no formal statistical test flagged them as significant given the small N. The Charlson Comorbidity Index differed slightly (4.8 vs 5.3) but was appropriately used as a covariate in subsequent analyses, partially mitigating this concern.

Because the randomization and allocation concealment methods were robust, but baseline comorbidity imbalances (particularly cerebrovascular disease and peptic ulcer disease) raise some doubt about whether chance alone explains the differences, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Both participants and personnel were blinded: "The appearance, packaging, and labeling of the tablets were identical. Both kinds of tablets had artificial mint flavor to give an identical taste... Both patients and investigators were blinded." This addresses signalling questions 2.1 and 2.2 — both participants and trial personnel were unaware of intervention allocation, satisfying the low risk of bias criterion for Part 1 (2.1 = No/Probably No).

Although a small number of participants discontinued study medication due to side effects (4 in treatment group due to dyspepsia/dizziness, 3 in placebo group due to dyspepsia/skin rash), the report indicates: "Although study medication was stopped in these patients, biochemical tests and nutritional assessment were continued for 52 wk according to the study protocol." This is consistent with the trial protocol's built-in tolerance provisions, not a deviation arising from unblinded trial context.

For the analysis, the authors explicitly state: "Analyses were intention to treat irrespective of adherence to treatment regimen," satisfying signalling question 2.6 (appropriate analysis = Yes). Given full blinding and an appropriate ITT analysis, both Part 1 and Part 2 default to "Low risk of bias."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** SGA was assessed at 0, 12, 24, 36, and 52 weeks in both groups, and the results in Table 3 report full data ("n" implicitly consistent with 30 per group) at each time point with no indication of missing SGA assessments. The report states "Study medication was stopped in 3 patients of the placebo group" and "Four patients in the treatment group could not tolerate the study medication," but crucially clarifies that "biochemical tests and nutritional assessment were continued for 52 wk according to the study protocol" for these participants — meaning SGA data collection continued despite discontinuation of study drug.

Deaths occurred during the study (2 in treatment, 5 in placebo), which could result in missing SGA data for those who died before later time points; however, this amount (7/60, ~12%) is relatively modest, occurred for reasons plausibly unrelated to the SGA trajectory itself (sudden cardiac death, peritonitis, vascular events), and mortality was slightly (non-significantly) higher in the placebo group. There is no clear evidence that missingness of SGA data (due to death) was related to its "true value" beyond what is accounted for by the differing outcome trajectories captured while patients were alive. Given the outcome was measured for nearly all randomized/surviving participants at each time point and there is no evidence of differential dropout unrelated to death, this domain is judged as low risk.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The SGA outcome assessors were blinded to intervention allocation: "SGA was performed at 0, 12, 24, 36, and 52 wk by two trained observers who were blinded from the treatment group allocation and biochemical results of the patients." This directly satisfies signalling question 4.3 (outcome assessors aware of intervention = No).

The method of outcome measurement was appropriate and standardized, using "The four-item seven-point system" that was validated in the literature (references 12,15), with pre-study training to achieve "a Cohen's kappa concordant coefficient for agreement of 0.84, which was an excellent level of agreement" — indicating good inter-rater reliability. Measurement/ascertainment methods did not differ between groups, as the same two trained observers assessed both groups using the same schedule and instrument.

Since both participants and treating clinicians were blinded (double-blind, identical placebo tablets), and the SGA assessors themselves were also explicitly blinded and separate from treating staff, there is low risk that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced the outcome assessment.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** SGA score is explicitly identified as the primary, pre-specified outcome measure: "The primary outcome measure was the SGA score." The sample size calculation was based on this pre-specified primary outcome: "The sample size was estimated before the study... with the SGA as the primary outcome measure. Because the CANUSA study found that a difference in SGA score of 1 was clinically relevant... use of sample sizes of 60 achieves 81% power to detect a difference of 1.0."

The trial registration record (doc_id: 4dab5cab-2b44-42f8-8559-2dd9073b18b5) can be used to corroborate that SGA was designated a priori as an outcome of interest, and the full four-item SGA breakdown (anorexia, weight loss, subcutaneous fat, muscle mass) plus the overall score are all reported in Table 3 in full, with no indication of selective reporting of subscales or time points. All five pre-specified follow-up time points (0, 12, 24, 36, 52 weeks) are consistently reported for the overall SGA score and each subscale, with a single, consistent analytic approach (repeated measures ANOVA with post-hoc Bonferroni-adjusted t-tests) applied throughout. There is no evidence of multiple competing analyses being tried and only the favorable one reported.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

Given "Some concerns" in the randomization domain (due to baseline imbalances in comorbidities, notably cerebrovascular disease and peptic ulcer disease) and "Low risk" in all other domains, the overall judgement per Table 1 criteria is **Some concerns** for the SGA outcome, as at least one domain raises concern but no domain is judged high risk.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The trial used a centralized web response system for randomization, ensuring adequate sequence generation and allocation concealment. While there was a slight baseline imbalance in age, it is compatible with chance and does not indicate a failure in the randomization process.
b. Rationale: The trial report states, "patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System to receive either elacytarabine or their investigator’s choice of treatment". Regarding baseline characteristics, "The two treatment groups were similar... with the exception of an imbalance in the age distribution including significantly more patients older than 70 years in the elacytarabine arm."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The trial was open-label, but an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention. The number of participants excluded from the analysis was minimal.
b. Rationale: The study states, "The intention-to-treat population comprised 191 patients in the elacytarabine group and 190 in the control arm." The analysis of overall survival was performed on this ITT population.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data (survival status) were available for nearly all randomized participants, with very few lost to follow-up. 
b. Rationale: According to the patient disposition flow chart (Figure 2), only 1 patient (1%) in the elacytarabine arm and 4 patients (2%) in the investigator's choice arm were lost to follow-up or withdrawn. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The primary outcome was overall survival (all-cause mortality), which is an objective measure that is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, despite the trial being open-label.
b. Rationale: The outcome assessor (investigators determining survival status) could not be influenced by knowledge of the intervention when assessing the definitive event of death.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The reported results align with the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registration, and the analysis was conducted as planned without evidence of selective reporting.
b. Rationale: The study was prospectively registered (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT01147939). The primary end point of overall survival was clearly pre-specified and fully reported in the final publication.

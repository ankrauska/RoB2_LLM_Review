**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The trial used block randomization, but there is insufficient information to determine if the allocation sequence was adequately concealed.
* **Rationale:** The authors state that "Randomization for allocation of either of the two etch and primer systems to the upper right quadrant was undertaken using a block randomization method... The operators were unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial." While the sequence generation (block randomization) is adequate, the method of allocation concealment (e.g., centralized randomization, opaque sealed envelopes) is not described, raising some concerns that the sequence could have been predicted.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Although operators were not blinded, deviations from intended interventions due to the trial context are unlikely to affect the objective outcome of bracket failure, and an appropriate analysis was used.
* **Rationale:** The study notes that "it was not possible to blind the operators to the type of system being used" due to the different modes of application. However, patients were unaware of the systems used. Any deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context are unlikely to impact an objective outcome like bond failure. All brackets were analysed in the groups to which they were randomized, representing an appropriate analysis for the effect of assignment. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, and missing data would not bias the result.
* **Rationale:** Out of 39 patients, "All patients were observed for the entire 12-month period apart from one 13-year-old male who moved to another city after 7 months of observation, during which time no bracket failures occurred." His data were still included in the survival analysis. The amount of missing outcome data is negligible and unlikely to bias the intervention effect.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Although outcome assessors were not blinded, the assessment of bond failure is objective and unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
* **Rationale:** The outcome is clinical bond failure (whether the bracket detaches from the tooth), which is a definitive, objective event. Even though the operators were not blinded, the measurement of the outcome is highly unlikely to have been influenced by their knowledge of the assigned intervention.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** There is no mention of a pre-specified analysis plan or trial registry to confirm all intended analyses, though the reported results align with the study's obvious primary objective.
* **Rationale:** The trial report does not reference a published protocol or a registry entry (doc_id: 4dab5cab-2b44-42f8-8559-2dd9073b18b5 was not provided with any text in the prompt). Without a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available, we cannot be completely certain that the reported results were not selected from multiple eligible analyses, warranting a judgement of "some concerns". However, bond failure rate is the standard and expected primary outcome for this type of trial.

Based on the RoB 2.0 tool guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the COMICE trial regarding the outcome of reoperation rate:

    1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: low risk of bias
           b. Rationale: The trial used a computerized 24-hour automated telephone randomization system utilizing a minimization algorithm that incorporated a random element, ensuring both appropriate sequence generation and allocation concealment. Furthermore, Table 1 demonstrates that baseline characteristics (age, breast density, menopausal status, etc.) were well balanced between the two groups.

    2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: low risk of bias
           b. Rationale: While the trial was open-label (meaning patients and clinicians were aware of the intervention assignment), there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. The trial appropriately analyzed the effect of assignment to intervention using an intention-to-treat (ITT) approach, as stated: "Analysis was by intention to treat", with 816 and 807 patients analyzed in their respective randomized groups.

    3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: low risk of bias
           b. Rationale: Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants. Out of 1,625 randomized patients, 1,623 were included in the primary intention-to-treat analysis (>99%). The few patients lost to follow-up (10 across both groups) are not enough to have a substantial impact on the estimated effect of the intervention.

    4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: low risk of bias
           b. Rationale: Although surgeons and patients were not blinded to the imaging received, the outcome (reoperation) was based on objective histopathological assessment of the excised margins. The study protocol established that "Every participating consultant breast surgeon provided their local definition of a clear margin before starting recruitment. Local definitions were applied to both trial groups". Therefore, the decision to re-operate was dictated by objective pathology reports rather than being influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

    5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: low risk of bias
           b. Rationale: The trial was prospectively registered (ISRCTN 57474502) and a study protocol was published. The primary clinical outcome—the proportion of patients undergoing a repeat operation or further mastectomy within 6 months—was clearly pre-specified and reported exactly as planned. There is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible measurements or multiple analyses based on statistical significance.

# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment: List of Attributions Task (LAT)

**Preliminary considerations:** Effect of interest = effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect). Experimental: Fatigue Information (FI); Comparator: Sleep Control information. Outcome: LAT (NumberAttributionScore).

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to lack of information about allocation concealment, despite adequate random sequence generation and no evidence of baseline imbalance.

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation: “a Microsoft Excel random number generator was used to randomize participants into the two conditions” — clearly random (Yes).
- Allocation concealment: No information is provided about who generated the sequence, whether it was administered independently of recruiters/assessors, or how allocation was implemented to prevent foreknowledge. There is no mention of sequentially numbered/sealed allocation or independent administration. This is judged “No information.”
- Baseline imbalance: “The FI and control groups did not differ significantly with respect to age... or ethnicity... There were equal numbers of men and women in each group. No significant pre-intervention group differences were found on levels of insomnia, fatigue, depressed mood or dysfunctional beliefs about sleep. There were also no significant group differences on the LAT outcome measures at baseline.” This indicates no evidence of a problem with randomization.
- Per the RoB2 algorithm: random sequence = Yes, allocation concealment = No information, baseline imbalance = No → this combination maps to “Some concerns.”

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants were not blinded to which information sheet they received (FI vs. control), and the study investigator delivering the information was also not blinded (“The study investigator read the information sheet alongside each participant individually”). So participants and personnel were aware of intervention assignment (2.1/2.2 = Yes).
- However, the question is whether deviations from intended intervention arose **because of trial context** (e.g., contamination, non-protocol interventions) that could bias the assignment effect. There is no indication that participants received non-protocol information, crossed over, or that trial personnel implemented the intervention inconsistently with the protocol. The intervention was a single, brief, standardized information session delivered identically by the investigator reading from a script/information sheet: “The study investigator read the information sheet alongside each participant individually.” This is a one-time, fully administered intervention with no room for participant non-adherence (it is not a sustained behavioral intervention) and no evidence of contamination between groups (individual testing sessions).
- Analysis: This appears to be an appropriate analysis including all randomized participants (with the caveat of 5 exclusions, discussed under missing data), consistent with ITT principles for this immediately-administered intervention.
- Given the single-session, investigator-delivered, standardized nature of the intervention, there is no plausible mechanism for trial-context-driven deviations affecting the LAT outcome. Therefore, low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- “Five participants’ data were not included in the analyses. Two participants were younger than 18 years and thus their data was not used, one participant did not complete the demographic information, and two participants did not complete the outcome measures in a way that would allow us to obtain a total score.”
- The exclusions for age (<18) represent post-randomization exclusion of ineligible participants (eligibility criteria: undergraduate students, implicitly adults), which is considered acceptable and not a source of bias.
- Only 2 of 88 analyzed participants had missing outcome data on the LAT (unable to compute a total score) — a very small proportion (~2%), unlikely to meaningfully bias the result under either arm.
- There is no indication of differential missingness between the FI and control groups, and the small number of missing cases could not plausibly alter the outcome.
- Final analytic sample: “the final sample consisted of 88 participants... There were 44 participants in each group,” indicating balance was preserved.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias, primarily due to lack of blinding of outcome assessors/participants combined with a participant-reported outcome that is highly susceptible to influence by knowledge of intervention received, and likely influence given the study design and aims.

**b. Rationale:**
- The LAT is a **participant-reported outcome**: participants self-generate attributions for their own fatigue. Per RoB2 guidance, "For participant-reported outcomes, the outcome assessor is the study participant" and "the assessment of outcome is potentially influenced by knowledge of intervention received."
- Participants were explicitly not blinded to intervention assignment — they knew whether they had just been given fatigue-cause information or generic sleep information: "The study investigator read the information sheet alongside each participant individually, after which all of the participants once again filled out the LAT."
- This raises the question of whether the outcome assessment was **likely** influenced by knowledge of intervention received (not just "could have been"). The intervention explicitly primed participants with a list of alternative, non-sleep causes of fatigue immediately before completing the LAT a second time, which directly overlaps with the content being measured. The authors themselves raise this concern: "it is also possible that demand characteristics or priming played a role in FI participants' post-intervention responses on the LAT... the information-based intervention may have influenced participants to respond in a certain manner."
- Although the authors argue the control group's stability suggests priming did not drive the FI group's change, this is a post-hoc argument rather than evidence that blinding/independence of assessment was preserved. The fundamental design — an unblinded participant, tested immediately after receiving content thematically identical to the outcome measure — creates a high likelihood that knowledge of intervention assignment directly influenced how participants completed the LAT, distinct from any "true" attitudinal/attributional change.
- This is compounded by demand characteristics inherent to a within-session, immediate pre-post self-report design with no blinding, no independent assessor, and content overlap between intervention and outcome measure.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- No trial registration or protocol was identified for this study (the doc_id for trial registration guidance returned no specific matching pre-registration information for this particular trial), so there is no external documentation to compare against; however, all three pre-specified outcome measures derived from the LAT (NumberAttributionScore, FrequencyScore, ImportanceScore) are reported fully and consistently with the stated hypotheses in the introduction: “(1) the number of generated sleep-related attributions... would decrease for those in the FI condition only; (2) the rank and proportion allotted to sleep-related attributions... would decrease after FI.”
- All three LAT-derived scores are reported with full statistics (F, df, p-values, means, SDs) for both groups at both time points, with no indication of selective omission of measures, subscales, or alternative analyses.
- There is a single, clearly pre-specified analytic approach (mixed within-between ANOVA testing group × time interaction) applied consistently across all three outcome variables, with no evidence of alternative models being tried and discarded.
- No inconsistencies are apparent between the stated hypotheses, methods, and results sections.
- Given the coherence between introduction/hypotheses and fully reported results, and absence of evidence of selective outcome/analysis reporting, this domain is judged low risk of bias, albeit with some residual uncertainty due to the absence of a publicly accessible prospective registration/protocol for full verification.

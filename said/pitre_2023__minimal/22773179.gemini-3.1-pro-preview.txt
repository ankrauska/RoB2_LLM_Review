**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The study utilized a paired-eye design with randomized assignment of the interventions to each eye using an adequate random sequence generation method, and there are no baseline imbalances that suggest a problem. 
b. Rationale: The authors state, "For each patient, one eye was treated with the MEL80 standard aspheric profile (ASA) and the other eye was treated with the MEL80 non-aspheric profile (TSA), assigned at random using Microsoft Excel’s random number function."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: There is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context, and an appropriate analysis was used.
b. Rationale: This was a paired-eye study where both eyes underwent LASIK in the same patient, differing only by the ablation profile (ASA vs. TSA). There is no indication of non-protocol interventions or exclusions that would bias the intention-to-treat effect. All 30 eyes were analyzed.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all randomized participants.
b. Rationale: The study enrolled 15 patients (30 eyes), and the results for visual acuity (Figure 4 and Figure 5) report on all 30 eyes at the 3-month follow-up ("The graph includes data for 30 eyes at 3 months postop").

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The method of measuring visual acuity (Corrected Distance Visual Acuity) is standard and appropriate, and it is unlikely that the assessment was influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
b. Rationale: Corrected Distance Visual Acuity (CDVA) was measured using standard Snellen charts ("safety in terms of change in Snellen lines of Corrected Distance Visual Acuity (CDVA)"). Although blinding of the outcome assessor is not explicitly detailed, CDVA is an objective-standardized measurement that is unlikely to be substantially influenced by knowledge of the specific laser profile used on each eye. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The reported visual acuity results are not likely to have been selected from multiple outcome measurements or analyses based on the results.
b. Rationale: The study reports standard visual acuity outcomes (efficacy and safety via Snellen lines) as typically presented in refractive surgery trials, following standard reporting guidelines ("Refractive outcomes including accuracy, efficacy, and safety were analyzed according to the protocol described by Reinstein and Waring."). There is no evidence of selective reporting of this outcome.

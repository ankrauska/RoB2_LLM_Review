1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial utilized computerized block randomization and allocation concealment using envelopes, and there were no significant baseline differences.
b. Rationale: "The three intervention groups were standard care (control group), standard care and TENS (TENS group), or standard care and IFT (IFT group). The participants were blind to the allocation to treatment groups after randomization... allocated to one of three treatment interventions using computerized block randomization and allocation group concealed in envelops." Baseline data analysis found no significant differences in demographic and clinical characteristics, pain intensity, or cervical ROM among the groups.

2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns
a. Judgement: Participants and the principal investigator (who delivered interventions) were not fully blinded to the active interventions (TENS, IFT, or standard care). However, deviations arising from the trial context that would have importantly affected the outcome are unlikely.
b. Rationale: "The study was a single blind trial... The participants were blind to the allocation to treatment groups after randomization... The principal investigator acted as both outcome assessor... and as therapist administering all study treatment." Due to the nature of the interventions (TENS, IFT), true blinding of the therapist and participants is practically impossible, but there's no evidence of non-protocol interventions or deviations related to the trial context that would have unbalanced the groups significantly. Since blinding was lacking, there are "some concerns".

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: All randomized participants completed the study and were included in the analysis.
b. Rationale: "There were 105 individuals who expressed interest in participating in the study. All completed the study and were entered into the analysis (Fig. 3)."

4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns
a. Judgement: The outcome assessor (who was also the treating therapist) and participants were aware of the intervention received, and pain intensity is a subjective outcome that could be influenced by this knowledge.
b. Rationale: "The principal investigator acted as both outcome assessor, taking all study measurements, and as therapist administering all study treatment." Pain was measured using a self-reported visual analog scale. Both the participant (assessor of their own pain) and the principal investigator (assessor of ROM) were aware of the interventions. Subjective measures like pain can be influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The reported outcomes match the intended outcomes, and there is no evidence of selective reporting based on the results.
b. Rationale: The trial registered on a clinical trial registry, and the outcomes reported (Pain intensity and cervical ROM) are standard for this condition and were reported for all prespecified time points (baseline, immediately after 1st treatment, before 8th treatment, 1 week after 8th treatment). There is no indication that data were selected based on significance.

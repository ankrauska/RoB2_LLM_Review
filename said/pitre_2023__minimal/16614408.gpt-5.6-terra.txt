1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The report states that participants were “randomly assigned” first to the SAFA or MUFA diet and then to fish-oil or placebo capsules, but it does not describe how either random sequence was generated or how allocation was concealed before enrolment. Therefore, there is insufficient information to establish that the allocation sequence was adequately concealed.

   The reported baseline characteristics do not indicate an evident failure of randomization. The authors state that “the clinical characteristics of the participants at baseline did not differ among the groups,” and Table 1 shows broadly similar age, BMI, lipids, glucose, and blood-pressure values across the four groups. However, balanced baseline characteristics cannot compensate for absent information on allocation concealment. Under RoB 2, lack of information on concealment, with no clear baseline imbalance, leads to *some concerns* rather than high risk of bias.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** This assessment concerns the effect of **assignment** to fish-oil supplementation versus placebo at baseline. The trial used fish-oil capsules or placebo capsules containing olive oil: “Within each diet group, there was a 2nd random assignment to either supplements of capsules containing fish-oil … or to placebo capsules with the same amount of olive oil.”

   The analysis appears broadly consistent with an assignment-based comparison: the statistical model compared the “presence/absence of (n-3) fatty acids,” adjusted for baseline outcome value and other prespecified covariates, and combined fish-oil versus placebo participants across the background-diet groups when the interaction was not statistically significant. Exclusion of participants with unavailable laboratory outcome data is addressed under missing outcome data rather than in this domain.

   However, the report does not explicitly state whether participants, investigators, dietitians, or personnel dispensing capsules were blinded to fish-oil/placebo assignment. It also does not report whether there were protocol deviations, differential use of non-protocol supplements, contamination between fish-oil and placebo groups, or differential adherence to the capsules. Participants were instructed to avoid nutritional supplements and to avoid fatty fish/products, but adherence information specific to capsule allocation is not reported. Since lack of blinding and trial-context-related deviations cannot be ruled out, RoB 2 supports *some concerns*. There is no direct evidence of deviations arising from the trial context that were unbalanced and likely to affect these biochemical outcomes; therefore, high risk of bias is not justified.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** For 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α, the authors state that samples from “all centers” were analysed. Although a participant flow diagram and explicit numbers analysed at follow-up are not provided, the reported group sizes in Table 3 correspond to the randomized groups and there is no report of participant withdrawal or differential loss to follow-up.

   For 8-iso-PGF2α, samples from two centres could not be analysed: “Plasma samples from 2 centers (Denmark and Italy) were unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage.” This represents substantial unavailable outcome data for the 8-iso-PGF2α result. Nevertheless, the stated reason is a sample-handling/storage problem at the centre level, rather than participants’ true 8-iso-PGF2α concentration, clinical status, response to treatment, or intervention assignment. Thus, the missingness is not plausibly dependent on the unobserved individual outcome values.

   The exclusion of whole centres limits the applicability of the 8-iso-PGF2α result to the contributing centres, but this is principally an issue of precision and generalizability rather than evidence of bias in the randomized fish-oil versus placebo contrast. There is no evidence that unavailable samples differed by fish-oil assignment or that missingness was outcome-related. Therefore, this domain is judged at low risk of bias.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Both outcomes are objective biochemical plasma measurements, assessed at baseline and after 3 months using described and validated radioimmunoassays. The authors report that 8-iso-PGF2α was measured “by using a validated RIA developed by Basu,” with a detection limit of 23 pmol/L. Similarly, 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α was measured in plasma using “a validated RIA,” with a detection limit of 45 pmol/L.

   These are laboratory-based outcomes with little scope for participants’ or treating personnel’s knowledge of dietary/capsule assignment to influence the measured analyte concentration or the assay reading. The same outcome definitions and assay methods appear to have been used across comparison groups and at both time points. Although blinding of laboratory personnel is not explicitly reported, these observer-reported biochemical outcomes do not involve appreciable subjective judgement. The inability to analyse samples from two centres for 8-iso-PGF2α is considered under missing outcome data, not measurement bias among analysed samples.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The publication identifies the relevant outcomes in advance in its methods: “The main outcome variables were the changes in 8-iso-prostaglandin F2a and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a.” It reports results for both outcomes, including the non-significant result for 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α, which reduces concern that only favourable outcomes were reported. Table 3 provides baseline values, 3-month values, changes, and P-values for the dietary and fish-oil comparisons.

   However, no dated protocol or statistical analysis plan finalized before access to unblinded outcome data is available in the supplied material. The available trial description does not establish that the particular reported analysis—change from baseline adjusted for centre, age, sex, baseline value, diet assignment, fish-oil assignment, and an interaction term—was fully pre-specified before outcome data were examined. In addition, multiple related oxidative-stress and antioxidant outcomes were measured, and the report does not provide sufficient pre-specified detail to exclude selection among possible outcome measurements, time-point summaries, or analytic approaches.

   There is no clear evidence that the reported 8-iso-PGF2α or 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α results were selected because of their direction, magnitude, or statistical significance; therefore, high risk of bias is not warranted. Nevertheless, absence of a sufficiently detailed, time-stamped pre-specified analysis plan leads to *some concerns*.

Here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the randomized trial based on the RoB 2.0 tool guidelines for the outcome of compliance with mammogram screening.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** The allocation sequence was random, concealed, and baseline characteristics did not suggest a problem with the randomization process.
**b. Rationale:** The study used an adequate method for sequence generation and successfully concealed allocation by using a remote coordination center for randomization. The authors state, "A complete data base with 500 eligible women interviewed at baseline was sent to the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center in the US (i.e. the coordination center) to proceed with a random allocation to one of the two intervention arms or the control arm. A stratified randomization method blocking by age (50 –59; 60 – 70) was used to allocate women to each group using a computerized random generator program." Furthermore, baseline characteristics were balanced: "No significant differences were observed between groups in the demographic characteristics of the participants."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Although participants and personnel were not blinded, there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose due to the trial context that would impact the effect of assignment to the intervention. Furthermore, an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was used.
**b. Rationale:** The authors acknowledge that "Blinding participants to their intervention group was not possible in this study." However, to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention, the study appropriately used an intention-to-treat analysis based on the initial randomized groups rather than just those who adhered. The authors report, "An intention to treat analysis was conducted using electronic clinical records. They were reviewed for all women and compliance was estimated using the baseline denominators of each group."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Outcome data for the primary analysis of compliance via electronic records were available for all randomized participants. 
**b. Rationale:** The trial utilized electronic clinical records to verify the outcome (mammogram screening) for every woman randomized into the trial, effectively resulting in no missing data for this outcome measure. The authors state, "Percentage of screening according to the electronic registry of mammograms performed during the period of the study shows a significant increase from 6% (167) in the control arm, to 51.8% (86/166) in the low level intervention arm, and 70.1% (117/167) in the high level intervention arm." The denominators exactly match the number of women randomized to each arm.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** The method of measuring the outcome (electronic clinical records) was appropriate, objective, and the outcome assessors (radiology/clinic staff) were blinded to the intervention assignment.
**b. Rationale:** The primary outcome was objectively measured using hospital records, meaning it was not susceptible to subjective bias. Furthermore, the personnel recording the outcome data were successfully blinded: "Staff at the clinic and at the radiology unit where participants got their mammograms were blinded to the allocation group." 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** There is no indication that the reported result was selected from among multiple outcome measurements or analyses based on the statistical significance or direction of the results.
**b. Rationale:** The primary outcome—compliance with mammogram screening at 6 months—was clearly defined, and it was the singular, logical way to measure the success of the intervention. The study reported results consistent with standard intention-to-treat methodologies without evidence of post-hoc selective reporting or manipulation of the analysis metric.

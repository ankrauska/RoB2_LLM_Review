# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Pain – Visual Analogue Scale (VAS)
## Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation*: The article states, "The distribution of subjects to the two treatment groups was performed using sex-stratified randomization." This indicates a random component was used (Q1.1 = Yes/Probably yes).
- *Allocation concealment*: "The allocation sequence was blinded through the use of sealed envelopes." While full details (sequential numbering, opacity, tamper-proofing) are not explicitly reported, the description supports a judgement of "Probably yes" for concealment (Q1.2), especially in the context of a small trial with no other indications of subversion.
- *Baseline imbalance*: Table 1 shows no significant difference between groups for the VAS at baseline (p = 0.421; Control 7.000 [1.90] vs Experimental 7.400 [2.30]), and the chi-square test confirmed no imbalance in sex distribution (p = 1.000). No evidence of a problem with randomization (Q1.3 = No).

Since the allocation sequence was random, allocation was probably concealed, and there is no baseline imbalance suggestive of a randomization failure, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- *Participant blinding (Q2.1)*: The study explicitly states "blinding of patients," and the control group received an identical sham/placebo ultrasound protocol using a device that appeared to function normally. Thus participants were likely unaware of their allocation (No/Probably no).
- *Personnel blinding (Q2.2)*: The authors acknowledge, "The blinding of the physiotherapist who applied the treatment was not possible, which constitutes another limitation of the study." Therefore, personnel delivering the intervention were aware of group assignment (Yes).
- *Deviations arising from trial context (Q2.3)*: Despite personnel being unblinded, there is no evidence or indication in the manuscript that this awareness led to non-protocol co-interventions, differential encouragement, or contamination between groups. The treatment protocol (massage, exercises, microwave, and US/sham-US) was standardized and delivered identically apart from the active/sham US itself. No deviations from the protocol are reported (No information/Probably no).
- *Analysis (Q2.6)*: "The analysis was done by intention to treat," and there was no loss to follow-up ("During the study no patient was lost or dropped out"), confirming an appropriate ITT analysis with no need for exclusions.

Because participants (who self-report the VAS) were blinded, no deviations were reported as arising from the trial context despite unblinded personnel, and an appropriate ITT analysis was used, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- The manuscript explicitly states: "During the study no patient was lost or dropped out."
- The CONSORT-type flow diagram (Figure 1) confirms that all 54 randomized participants (27 per group) were followed through to discharge with data collected at pre-test, session 5, session 10, and discharge.
- Since outcome data (VAS) were available for 100% of randomized participants, missingness cannot have biased the result.

This domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of measurement (Q4.1)*: VAS is a standard, validated, and widely used method for assessing pain; it was applied identically in both groups at the same time points (pre-test, post-test 5, post-test 10, discharge).
- *Comparability of measurement between groups (Q4.2)*: The same measurement procedure, instrument, and time points were used for both groups; the assessor was blinded ("blind assessor").
- *Awareness of outcome assessor (Q4.3)*: For a self-reported outcome such as VAS, the "outcome assessor" is effectively the participant. Because the placebo ultrasound device was designed to appear functionally identical to the active device (with the appraiser/assessor separate from the treating physiotherapist and blinded to allocation), participants were likely unaware of their treatment allocation. The physiotherapist administering the VAS assessment ("a physiotherapist appraiser assessed the patients' pain") was different from and blinded relative to the treating physiotherapist.
- Because both the patient (self-reporting) and the outcome assessor were blinded, and the measurement method did not differ between groups, knowledge of allocation is unlikely to have influenced VAS scores.

This domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Rationale:**
- The article does not report registration in a public trial registry (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov), nor does it reference a published trial protocol or statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinded data availability. Without access to a verifiable prospectively registered protocol, it cannot be confirmed whether VAS was the only, or a preferentially selected, pain measure/analysis reported out of multiple options.
- That said, the VAS results are reported consistently and completely at all pre-specified time points (pre-test, post-test 5, post-test 10, and discharge) in Tables 1–3, with no evidence of selective omission of time points or alternative analyses (e.g., no report of only significant results while omitting non-significant ones — indeed, the non-significant post-10-session VAS result is reported alongside the significant discharge result).
- However, because no trial registry entry or prospective protocol/SAP was identified to confirm that the reported VAS outcome/analysis (e.g., choice of comparison time points, unilateral significance testing) was pre-specified rather than chosen post hoc, there remains uncertainty regarding the risk of selective reporting.

Given the absence of information confirming a pre-specified analysis plan (Q5.1 = No information) and no clear evidence of selection from multiple outcome measurements or analyses (Q5.2/Q5.3 = No/No information), this domain is judged **Some concerns**, per the RoB 2 algorithm default for cases with no information on prospective registration/analysis plan.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: Interhemispheric Functional Connectivity Outcome

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to insufficient information on allocation concealment and unclear randomization method details, despite adequate baseline balance.

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation: The paper states "Randomization was completed using blocks of four," which implies a random component, but does not explicitly describe the method used to generate the sequence (e.g., computer-generated randomization). This warrants a "Probably yes" response given the context of a formal clinical trial, but with some uncertainty.
- Allocation concealment: There is **no information** provided about how allocation concealment was achieved (e.g., sequentially numbered sealed envelopes, central randomization). The paper only states "Both the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention," which pertains to blinding after allocation, not concealment of the sequence prior to assignment.
- Baseline imbalance: "There were no significant differences in demographic characteristics between groups (Table 1)," and Table 1 shows reasonably balanced age and sex distributions across the four groups, suggesting no evidence of problems with randomization from baseline data.
- Given no information on allocation concealment, but no evidence of baseline imbalance and a plausible random sequence, this aligns with a "Some concerns" judgment per the algorithm (NI on concealment + no baseline imbalance issues → Some concerns).

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as blinding of participants and personnel was implemented and appeared successful, and appropriate analytic methods were used.

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants and personnel were blinded: "Both the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention." Sham stimulation was designed to mimic active stimulation (30 seconds of stimulation before automatic shut-off), reducing the likelihood that participants could distinguish groups.
- Blinding success was verified: "Subjects did not guess correctly the stimulation group to which they were assigned beyond chance (p = 0.679)," supporting that blinding was effective and unlikely to have led to deviations arising from trial context (e.g., differential expectation effects).
- No non-protocol interventions, failures of implementation, or non-adherence are described, consistent with a single-session, tightly controlled EEG experiment.
- Analysis: The analysis included all randomized/completing participants in their assigned groups (with one exclusion due to technical EEG artifact, unrelated to intervention assignment or outcome value—"One subject in the Random Frequency group was removed from the analysis due to excessive artifacts in the EEG recording"). This exclusion is not related to the trial context or intervention effects, and is not indicative of inappropriate analysis (akin to an mITT approach for a technical/data-quality reason).

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as the amount of missing data was minimal and unrelated to the true outcome value.

**b. Rationale:**
- Of 40 recruited, 38 completed the single stimulation session ("Two subjects signed the consent form and decided not to participate before stimulation was given")—this occurred prior to intervention and is not considered missing outcome data.
- One additional subject in the Random Frequency group was excluded "due to excessive artifacts in the EEG recording," a technical/data-quality issue unrelated to the true value of the coherence outcome. This affects only 1 of 38 participants (an very small proportion, ~2.6%), which is very unlikely to meaningfully bias the interhemispheric coherence result.
- No differential dropout between groups is reported, and reasons for the single exclusion are clearly documented and plausibly unrelated to outcome value (technical artifact rather than participant-driven withdrawal).

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to the inherent difficulty of blinding EEG analysts to some aspects of stimulation condition combined with lack of explicit statement on blinding of the EEG data analyst/coherence calculation, though the outcome itself (a computed coherence measure) has limited subjectivity.

**b. Rationale:**
- The outcome (interhemispheric coherence, an EEG-derived, computationally-derived measure) is an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" in some respects—it is a quantitative signal-processing metric ("We used magnitude square coherence as a pair-wise connectivity measure...using the Welch's averaged modified periodogram method"), which reduces risk of subjective bias in outcome assessment.
- However, the paper states the "investigator assessing the outcomes" was blinded, which supports low risk for differential measurement. Yet, no explicit detail is given about whether the person conducting EEG preprocessing (including "manual artifact detection and rejection") was blinded to group assignment. Manual artifact rejection introduces a subjective/judgment-based step in outcome derivation, which could be influenced by knowledge of intervention allocation if the analyst was unblinded.
- Because manual artifact detection/rejection (which could differentially affect the calculated coherence values) is described without confirmation that this step was performed blind to allocation, and because this introduces a "judgement" component to what is otherwise an automated metric, there is a plausible route for bias, albeit unlikely to be substantial given the objective nature of the coherence algorithm itself.
- This leads to "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," since the assessment could have been influenced by unblinded assessment, but there is no clear evidence that it was.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, due to lack of a publicly available pre-registered trial protocol or statistical analysis plan specifying the pre-specified electrode pairs, bands, and comparisons for the interhemispheric coherence outcome, despite the outcome being included in the primary reported analyses.

**b. Rationale:**
- No trial registration information was found/provided (doc_id given for trial registration appears not to contain retrievable pre-specified analysis plan details in the text provided), and the article itself describes the work as an "exploratory analysis" for detecting "changes in mean power for theta, alpha and beta, and interhemispheric coherence for alpha and theta and four different sub-bands," suggesting the analyses were not confirmed as pre-specified prior to unblinded data availability.
- The study examined multiple bands (alpha, theta) and four sub-bands (low-alpha, high-alpha, low-beta, high-beta) for coherence, and reported multiple post-hoc pairwise comparisons (random vs. sham, 1Hz vs. sham, 100Hz vs. sham, and additional exploratory comparisons such as random vs. 1Hz and random vs. 100Hz). The selective highlighting of "significant" results (theta and low-alpha) as primary findings, while other sub-bands (low-beta, high-beta) showed no effect, combined with explicit statement that this was an "exploratory analysis," raises the possibility that the reported result was selected, at least in emphasis, based on statistical significance.
- Without access to a pre-registered protocol/SAP confirming a priori specification of the theta and low-alpha comparisons as primary outcomes (as opposed to alpha, low-beta, or high-beta), it is not possible to rule out selective emphasis/reporting from multiple eligible outcome measurements and analyses.
- This uncertainty, combined with the exploratory nature acknowledged by the authors, supports a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk" or "High risk."

---

## **Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: Some concerns**

Per RoB 2 guidance (Table 1), because there is "Some concerns" in multiple domains (randomization process, measurement of outcome, and selective reporting) but no domain reaches "High risk of bias," the overall judgement for this outcome (Interhemispheric functional connectivity) is **Some concerns**.

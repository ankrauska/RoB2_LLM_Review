**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The trial used block randomization, but the method of allocation concealment is not described. Additionally, there was a statistically significant baseline imbalance in age between the two groups.
* **Rationale:** The publication states that "Study patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio by using the mixed block randomization (4 and 6 blocks) method." However, there is no information on whether the allocation sequence was concealed. Furthermore, "the mean age in the candesartan-HCT group (51.0 [10.2] years) was about 3.2 years higher than that in the candesartan monotherapy group (47.8 [10.7] years; P = 0.02) despite random allocation." While this could be due to chance, the lack of detail on allocation concealment combined with the baseline imbalance leads to some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The trial was open-label, meaning participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention. The analysis used a modified intention-to-treat approach that excluded some randomized participants. 
* **Rationale:** The protocol confirms this was an "Open-label" study. Because it was open-label, there is a possibility that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context. For the analysis, "The ITT population consisted of patients who had baseline BP values and ≥1 postrandomization BP values and who had taken ≥1 dose of the study drug." This is a modified intention-to-treat analysis that excludes participants who did not receive the drug or provide post-baseline data, which represents a failure to analyze all participants in the group to which they were randomized, raising some concerns.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, and the small amount of missing data is unlikely to bias the result.
* **Rationale:** Of the 233 patients randomized (117 in the combination group and 116 in the monotherapy group), the ITT analysis included 224 patients (111 and 113, respectively). Data were missing for only 9 participants (less than 5% of the randomized sample). Therefore, data for the outcome were available for nearly all participants randomized, and the result is unlikely to be biased by missing outcome data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Although the trial was open-label, the outcome (blood pressure) was measured using a standardized, automated device, which minimizes the influence of the assessor's knowledge of the intervention.
* **Rationale:** The publication reports that "BPs were measured in each patient’s arm by the same investigator at each center with a fully automated device produced by Omron, Tokyo, Japan." Because the outcome is objectively measured by an automated device and standardized protocols were followed, it is unlikely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The reported primary outcome and analysis directly match the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registration.
* **Rationale:** The trial registry (NCT00621153) pre-specified the primary outcome as "Changes in Mean Sitting DBP From Baseline After 4 Weeks of Therapy". The published report appropriately presents the results for this exact outcome and time point as its primary objective. There is no evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on the nature of the findings.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0 Tool
## Outcome: Visual assessment for erythema and dryness (key symptoms of irritant contact dermatitis)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** 
- Sequence generation: The article states "Randomization was performed via randomly permuted blocks of fixed size using validated software RANCODE 3.6." This indicates a random component was used → **Yes** to signalling question 1.1.
- Allocation concealment: This is a within-person, split-body design (left/right cubital fossa), and the study states: "Allocation of the test products (coded as A and B) to the left and right cubital fossa was conducted according to a randomization scheme prepared at the Department of Statistics and Data Management at proDERM." "Blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database." This suggests adequate allocation concealment; however, insufficient detail is given about the specific mechanism used to conceal the sequence from those applying the product (e.g., no description of sequentially numbered/sealed containers or a similar mechanism), so this is judged as **Probably yes**, not a definitive "Yes."
- Baseline imbalance: Because this is a split-body/within-participant design, systemic baseline comparability between "groups" is inherently controlled (each participant contributes both arms), making differential prognostic imbalance between "groups" unlikely. No evidence of imbalance is reported.
- Because allocation concealment is not described with full detail (no explicit method such as sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes/containers for arm assignment, though blinding codes were "sealed"), and given the "no information" on details of concealment implementation, this leads to a judgement of **Some concerns** rather than "Low," per the algorithm (Table 4: NI on concealment + no imbalance information → "Some concerns").

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to interventions at baseline): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Blinding of participants: This is a double-blind trial: "double-blind trial" is stated explicitly in the title and methods. Both preparations were coded (A/B) and applied by a study nurse, and "All applications were performed by a study nurse," suggesting participants themselves were not necessarily administering it themselves, reducing the chance for them to identify the treatment. Products were similar in base composition (both propanol-based, containing 45% propan-2-ol, 30% propan-1-ol, 0.2% mecetronium etilsulfate), differing only in emollient content (0.81% w/w), making it plausible that participants could not distinguish between products by look/feel → **Probably no** to signalling Q2.1 (participants aware).
- Blinding of personnel/carers: "Blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database," and the outcome assessor (dermatologist) was blinded: "All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator" → **Probably no** to Q2.2.
- No deviations from intended intervention are reported that arose because of trial context (e.g., no non-protocol interventions, no switching of formulations). The 3 protocol violations noted (antiphlogistic intake, disinfection of fossa cubitalis) were unrelated to trial context/awareness of assignment and were handled by using ITT population as primary analysis with LOCF imputation.
- Analysis: The results are presented for the "intention-to-treat (ITT) population," which includes all 35 randomized subjects, with missing values (in 3 participants) replaced using LOCF. This is consistent with an appropriate analysis for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention → **Yes** to Q2.6.
- Overall: Blinding of participants, carers, and outcome assessors was successfully implemented, no important trial-context deviations occurred, and an appropriate (ITT) analysis was used.

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The ITT population (n=35, all randomized participants) was analyzed for the primary outcome, with missing data for 3 participants handled via Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF): "Missing values in 3 participants were replaced by the method of Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF)." This suggests outcome data were available (with imputation) for essentially all randomized participants (35/35) → **Yes/Probably yes** to Q3.1 ("nearly all" participants had data available).
- While LOCF is not always considered an unbiased approach to handling missing data, in this instance only 3 of 35 subjects (~8.6%) required imputation, and the reasons for their exclusion from full follow-up (protocol violations: antiphlogistic intake in 1 panellist, disinfection of fossa cubitalis in 2 panellists) were clearly documented and appear unrelated to the true value of the erythema/dryness outcome (i.e., not related to worsening or improving skin condition) — rather, they reflect procedural contamination issues.
- Because the amount of missing data is small and reasons for missingness are not plausibly related to the true outcome value, this is judged as unlikely to bias the result.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Method of outcome measurement: Visual assessment using a standardized, validated scale modified from Fitzpatrick et al. for erythema (0 to 4) and a similar ordinal scale for dryness (0 to 3) — an appropriate and established method for this outcome → **No** to Q4.1 (method not inappropriate).
- Consistency of measurement across groups: Both formulations were assessed identically, at the same time points, using the same procedure and scale, applied to homogeneous body sites (cubital fossa) on the same participants → **No** to Q4.2 (measurement did not differ between groups).
- Blinding of outcome assessor: "The treated skin was assessed visually for erythema and dryness... by a dermatologist. All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator." This indicates the outcome assessor was blinded to intervention assignment → **No** to Q4.3 (assessor not aware of intervention received).
- Since the assessor was blinded and using standardized objective/semi-objective visual scoring criteria, the assessment is very unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registration (linked doc_id) and methods section specify the outcome as "the sum score of dryness and erythema over all assessment times" as the pre-specified primary outcome parameter, matching what is reported in the Results and Table 1.
- Sample size calculation was performed prospectively and explicitly linked to "the sum score" as the pre-specified primary analysis metric: "A sample size of 30 was considered adequate... with respect to the sum score."
- Both erythema and dryness sub-scores, as well as the combined sum score, are reported fully and consistently in Table 1 for both the ITT and PP populations, with separate atopic/non-atopic breakdowns as pre-specified secondary analyses ("Secondary analyses included comparison of atopic and non-atopic panellists...").
- There is no evidence of selective reporting of only favorable timepoints, subscales, or alternative statistical analyses — the full time-course data are shown graphically (Figures 2 and 3) for all assessment days, and the statistical method (Fieller's method for non-inferiority, confirmed with Wilcoxon signed-rank test) appears pre-specified and consistently applied and reported in accordance with the stated statistical plan.

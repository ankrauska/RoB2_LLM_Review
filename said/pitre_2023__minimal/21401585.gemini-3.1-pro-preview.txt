Based on the RoB 2.0 tool and the provided trial document, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome quality of movement of the impaired upper limb by MUUL, focusing on the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect):

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, adequately concealed, and there were no significant baseline imbalances between the groups.
b. Rationale: The trial report states, "children were randomized to pairs using a computer generated list of random numbers and concealed envelopes opened by non-study personnel. A matched-pairs design minimized the likelihood of group differences at baseline..." Furthermore, "After matching and concealed random allocation within pairs, there were no differences between groups on demographic or baseline measures" (Table I).

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Although participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context, and an appropriate analysis was used.
b. Rationale: Blinding of participants and personnel is not feasible given the nature of the interventions (CIMT and BIM). However, the trial utilized an appropriate analysis strategy to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention. The report explicitly states, "Analyses were on an intention-to-treat basis according to CONSORT guidelines". 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants. 
b. Rationale: Out of the 64 children randomized, 63 were followed up and analyzed at the 3-week and 26-week time points (32 in the CIMT group and 31 in the BIM group). The report notes, "There was one dropout on day two of the camp from the BIM training group, because of pre-existing emotional and behavioural difficulties." This missing data rate (1/64) is extremely low and unlikely to bias the result.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome was measured using an appropriate method, and the outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention received by the participants.
b. Rationale: The MUUL assessment is stated to be an appropriate measure of unimanual capacity. Crucially, the outcome assessors were blinded to prevent bias: "The primary outcome measures were videotaped and scored in random order by trained occupational therapists masked to group allocation."

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The trial data were analyzed in accordance with the planned outcomes and time points, and there is no evidence of selective reporting from multiple outcome measurements or analyses.
b. Rationale: The primary outcomes (MUUL and AHA) and secondary outcomes were clearly defined in the methods section and fully reported at all prespecified time points (baseline, 3 weeks, and 26 weeks) in the results section and accompanying tables without omission. The analysis approach (Generalized Estimating Equations) was consistently applied as described.

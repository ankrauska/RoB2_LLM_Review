**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial used an adequate method for sequence generation and allocation concealment. The article states, "An offsite investigator prepared the concealed randomization using a computer generated random number sequence (1:1). Consecutively numbered opaque envelopes containing group allocation were collected from the offsite investigator by the tRNS intervener after initial assessment." There are no signs of baseline imbalances that would suggest a subversion of the randomization process in this small 4-person cohort.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** Participants, personnel, and assessors were effectively blinded to the intervention assignment. The article notes, "Study personnel involved in assessment and training, along with all participants were blinded to group allocation for the duration of the study." Furthermore, there were no dropouts or missing sessions ("All four participants completed the 12 training sessions over 4-weeks, with no training sessions missed"), meaning all participants were analyzed according to their randomized group.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** Outcome data for the MAS6 were available for all randomized participants. Table 3 confirms that data were collected at all time points (T0, T1, T2) for all 4 participants, meaning there is no missing outcome data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** The MAS6 is an appropriate measure for assessing upper arm function. Furthermore, outcome assessors were unaware of the intervention assignment. The article states, "Study personnel involved in assessment and training, along with all participants were blinded to group allocation for the duration of the study." Therefore, assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: high risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** High risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** There is a clear discrepancy between the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registration and the published report. The trial registry (ACTRN12614000952640) states the primary outcome is a "Composite score of Motor Assessment Scale upper limb items 6-8". However, the published article states, "The primary clinical outcome measure was Motor Assessment Scale item 6... Secondary outcome measures were MAS item 7... and item 8", reporting them individually. This indicates that the reported result was likely selected from multiple eligible outcome definitions/measurements (individual items versus the pre-specified composite score) on the basis of the results.

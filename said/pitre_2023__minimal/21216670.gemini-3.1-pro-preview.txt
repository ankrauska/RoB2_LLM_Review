**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** The trial used an adequate method for both sequence generation and allocation concealment. The publication states: "Patients were randomly assigned, using a computer random-number generator..." and "Allocation was concealed by use of sequentially numbered opaque envelopes." While there were minor baseline imbalances (e.g., slightly higher mean age and baseline FMMS score in the fluoxetine group), these differences are compatible with chance and do not suggest a systematic failure of the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** The trial was double-blinded. The report confirms that "The placebo was identical to the active drug in appearance and packaging," and "Patients, carers, all site study staff, and investigators were masked to treatment assignment." Therefore, deviations from the intended intervention arising from the trial context (due to lack of blinding) are unlikely. The analysis was conducted on all patients for whom data were available (modified ITT), which is appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment to the intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants. Out of 118 randomized patients, 113 (95.8%) completed the day 90 assessment and were included in the analysis. The 5 patients with missing data (2 died, 3 withdrew due to severe medical events like pulmonary embolism and kidney tumor) represent a very small proportion of the total sample, meaning their missing outcomes could have made no important difference to the estimated effect of the intervention. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** The Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale (FMMS) is an appropriate and validated measure for motor recovery. The outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment. The publication explicitly states: "All motor assessments were made by a physiotherapist... Participants, carers, and physicians assessing the outcome were masked to group assignment." Therefore, it is highly unlikely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** The reported primary outcome directly matches the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registration (NCT00657163). The registry lists the primary outcome as "Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale" at "M3 (3 months)", which perfectly aligns with the publication's primary endpoint ("mean change in FMMS score between inclusion (day 0) and day 90"). There is no evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple measurements or multiple analyses on the basis of the results.

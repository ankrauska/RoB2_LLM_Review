**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The study states that residents were randomized, but does not provide details on the method of random sequence generation or whether the allocation sequence was concealed. Baseline characteristics appear balanced between the three groups, but the lack of information on allocation concealment raises some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: While participants and supervisors were aware of the assigned level of supervision (due to the nature of the intervention), there is no evidence of deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, all 53 randomized residents were included in the analyses, consistent with an intention-to-treat approach.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Outcome data were available for all randomized participants. Tables 1, 2, and 3 show that all 53 participants (18 in direct, 16 in immediately available, and 19 in distant supervision) were included in the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The outcome (team checklist score) was assessed by two independent raters reviewing videotaped performances. The study does not state whether these raters were blinded to the assigned intervention. Because the supervisor's physical presence (or lack thereof) would be visible on the videotapes, the raters were likely unblinded. Although a standardized checklist was used and interrater reliability was assessed, some degree of judgement is involved, raising some concerns that knowledge of the intervention could have influenced the assessment.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: There is no reference to a pre-published protocol or trial registry in the provided text to verify whether the analyses were pre-specified. However, the reported outcomes align with the stated objectives of the study, and there is no clear evidence that the reported results were selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on their direction or significance. The lack of a pre-specified analysis plan leads to a default judgement of some concerns.

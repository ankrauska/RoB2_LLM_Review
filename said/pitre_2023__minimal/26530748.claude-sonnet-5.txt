# Risk of Bias Assessment: PORTEC-2 Long-Term HRQL Trial
## Outcome: EORTC QLQ-C30 Global Health

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** This publication is a long-term follow-up analysis of the PORTEC-2 trial and does not itself report methods of sequence generation or allocation concealment. The text states only that "427 patients with stage I high–intermediate-risk EC were randomly allocated to EBRT or VBT," with no description of the randomization method, allocation concealment procedures, or whether stratification/central randomization was used. Table 1 baseline characteristics of the current 7-/10-year responders versus the whole PORTEC-2 cohort show no significant differences ("Responders at 7 years were slightly younger and had fewer comorbidities compared with the whole PORTEC-2 cohort; no other significant differences were found"), suggesting no gross imbalance indicative of a failed randomization. However, because no information is provided in this article on sequence generation or concealment methods, and the assessment must be based on the provided sources, this leads to a default judgement of "Some concerns" per the RoB2 algorithm (no information on concealment together with no information on random sequence generation).

---

## 2. Deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention at baseline): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Participants and treating clinicians were necessarily aware of treatment assignment (EBRT vs. vaginal brachytherapy are overtly different treatment modalities), so blinding was not feasible ("Yes" to awareness). However, there is no evidence that deviations from intended intervention related to the *trial context* (e.g., non-protocol interventions arising from recruitment/engagement effects) occurred and affected the global health outcome. The paper reports treatment was delivered per protocol, and the small number of exclusions post-randomization (patients later diagnosed with EC recurrence or a second cancer in the pelvic region, n=12 of 265, roughly balanced across arms: "2 EBRT, 5 VBT... A second cancer outside the pelvic region was reported by another 5 patients") were excluded specifically "to avoid analysis of symptoms that could have been caused by a second cancer or recurrence," representing a small, non-differential, and justified exclusion rather than an inappropriate per-protocol/as-treated analysis. The primary analysis otherwise followed all randomized, disease-free responders, consistent with an ITT-type approach for the intended outcome.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** A substantial proportion of participants did not provide 7- and 10-year data. "Response rate at 7 years was 205 of 265 (77%)" and at 10 years "80 (67%)" of 119 eligible patients; only "76.2% had completed all items of the QLQ-C30." This falls well short of "nearly all" participants, and there is direct evidence that missingness could be related to the true value of the outcome (health status): "Responders at 7 years were slightly younger and had fewer comorbidities compared with the whole PORTEC-2 cohort." The authors themselves acknowledge this as "inherent selection of responders at long-term analysis, because participants had to be alive and disease-free." However, there is no reported evidence that the proportion of missing data, or reasons for missingness, differed materially between the EBRT and VBT arms for the global health outcome, and the overall response rate (77% at 7 years) is reasonably high. This combination of substantial-but-non-differential missingness related to health status leads to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** EORTC QLQ-C30 Global Health is a participant-reported outcome, so the "outcome assessor" is the patient. Because blinding of participants to their treatment assignment was not feasible (EBRT and VBT are overtly distinguishable interventions with different burdens/side-effect profiles), knowledge of intervention received could, in principle, influence self-reported global health/quality-of-life ratings. The measurement method itself (a validated, standard instrument) was appropriate and applied identically in both arms ("Cancer-specific general HRQL was measured with the EORTC... QLQ-C30"), so there is no concern about inappropriate or differential ascertainment methods. However, since assessment could be influenced by knowledge of assigned treatment, and there is no strong basis to conclude that such influence was absent (though the reported null finding – "Scores on the QLQ-C30 functioning and global health scales did not significantly differ between the 2 treatment groups" – argues against a strong influence), a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk" is warranted.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The EORTC QLQ-C30, including the Global Health/QoL scale, is a core, standard, pre-specified instrument used consistently across all PORTEC-2 HRQL analyses (5-year, 7-year, 10-year), and the methods clearly pre-specify how QLQ-C30 scores would be analyzed ("a linear mixed model was used with patient as random effect and time (categorical), random assignment, and their interaction as fixed effects... Differences in HRQL between the two treatment groups were tested by the Wald test"). Results for global health and all functioning scales are reported fully and are consistent with the pattern of results from the trial's own earlier 5-year publication and registered study design, with no indication of selective reporting of favorable/statistically significant results only, nor of alternative unreported analyses for this particular outcome (all subscales of QLQ-C30 are presented together in Table 2/Figure 2, including non-significant global health results). No discrepancy with the trial registration was identified with respect to this outcome.

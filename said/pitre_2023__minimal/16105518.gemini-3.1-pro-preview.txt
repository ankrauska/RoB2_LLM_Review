**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is insufficient information provided in the text to determine how the randomization sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed.
b. Rationale: The authors state only that participants were "randomly assigned to three experimental groups," but do not provide details on the method of sequence generation or allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics are not described in detail, though all participants were 1st- and 2nd-year medical students inexperienced in the task.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: There is no evidence that deviations from the intended interventions occurred due to the trial context, and it appears all participants were analyzed according to their assigned groups.
b. Rationale: Given the nature of the educational intervention, blinding of participants and instructors was not possible. However, there is no indication of non-protocol interventions or crossovers that would have biased the effect of assignment to the intervention. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data appear to be available for all randomized participants. 
b. Rationale: The study enrolled 28 participants, and there is no mention of dropouts or missing data for the pre-test or post-tests. The analyses appear to include all individuals randomized.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: It is unclear whether the outcome assessors were blinded to the participants' intervention groups, and the checklist scores involve some degree of subjective judgement.
b. Rationale: The study states, "Two experienced orthopedic surgeons used 4 separate methods of evaluation during the pre-test and the 2 post-tests," which included the operation-specific checklist. It does not state whether these evaluators were blinded to the participants' practice group assignment. Because checklist evaluation involves some judgement, knowledge of the intervention could theoretically influence the assessment. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is no indication that the study was conducted in accordance with a pre-registered protocol or pre-specified statistical analysis plan. 
b. Rationale: Without a time-stamped protocol or statistical analysis plan available before unblinded data were analyzed, it is not possible to completely rule out selective reporting of outcomes or analyses, leading to some concerns. However, the outcomes described in the methods (checklists, global rating, product analysis, and drilling time) are all reported in the results section.

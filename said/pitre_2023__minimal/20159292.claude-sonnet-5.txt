# RoB 2.0 Assessment: COMICE Trial (Reoperation Rate Outcome)

**Preliminary considerations:**
- Design: Individually-randomized parallel-group trial
- Experimental: Triple assessment + MRI; Comparator: Triple assessment alone
- Outcome: Reoperation rate (repeat operation, further mastectomy within 6 months, or pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation)
- Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat)
- Sources used: Journal article, trial registry (ISRCTN)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The allocation sequence was generated using an adequate random component (minimization with random element), and allocation was concealed via a centralized telephone randomization system. Baseline characteristics were well balanced, with no evidence of problems with randomization.

**b. Rationale:** "Patients were randomised to receive either MRI or no further imaging (no MRI) on a 1:1 basis, with a minimisation algorithm incorporating a random element." Allocation concealment was robust: "Randomisation was administered by the University of Leeds Clinical Trials Research Unit's automated 24-hour telephone randomisation system, by either the research nurse or the consultant breast surgeon" — this is a central, remote randomization method with minimal risk of foreknowledge. Table 1 shows close balance across age, breast density, menopausal status, and other characteristics between groups (e.g., 77% vs 77% aged ≥50 years; 88% vs 87% ACR BI-RADS group 2), consistent with successful randomization and minimization.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** This was an open-label trial with no blinding of participants, clinicians, or personnel — inherent to the nature of the intervention (MRI vs no MRI). However, this lack of blinding is a necessary feature of comparing an imaging strategy and does not appear to have led to problematic deviations from intended intervention arising from trial context that biased the reoperation outcome. A small number of patients in the "no MRI" group received MRI, and vice versa, but numbers were low and analysis followed ITT principles.

**b. Rationale:** The trial is described as "open, parallel group" — "we undertook an open, parallel group trial." Awareness of allocation was universal since imaging strategy cannot be blinded. Regarding deviations: in the MRI group, "761 received MRI" while 53 had "no MRI interpreted" (various reasons: technical failures, refusal, non-attendance, etc.) — none of these clearly reflect trial-context-driven contamination that would bias the surgical/reoperation outcome in a particular direction. In the no-MRI group, "9 received MRI (6 on patient request, 3 on clinician request)" — a very small crossover (1.1%), unlikely to substantially affect the reoperation-rate result. Analysis was appropriately by intention-to-treat: "Analysis was by intention to treat," and "153 (19%) needing reoperation in the MRI group versus 156 (19%) in the no MRI group" reflects allocated groups regardless of actual imaging received. Because the switching was minimal and analysis was ITT, there is no substantial impact expected on the result, but the open-label nature combined with clinician awareness of imaging results (which directly influences the surgical decision that constitutes the outcome) introduces some theoretical possibility of trial-context-related influence on management decisions (e.g., surgeons' choices around biopsy or mastectomy thresholds). This leads to a judgement of "some concerns" rather than "low risk."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Outcome data were available for the vast majority of randomized participants, and the number missing was too small to plausibly alter the effect estimate given the observed event rate.

**b. Rationale:** According to the trial profile (Figure 1), 816/817 (MRI) and 807/808 (no MRI) were included in the intention-to-treat/primary endpoint analysis. Only a small number were lost to follow-up: "4 lost to follow-up" and "3 primary endpoint (data unavailable at time of analysis)" in the MRI group, and "6 lost to follow-up" and "4 primary endpoint (data unavailable)" in the no MRI group. The text states, "Those who were lost to follow-up were classified as not having a primary-endpoint event" — a pre-specified, transparent method for handling missing data, applied equally across both groups. Given the primary event rate was 19% and missing data affected fewer than 1% of participants in each arm, the missingness could not have made an important difference to the estimated effect.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The outcome (reoperation, based on objective surgical and histopathological records) is not substantially subject to influence by knowledge of intervention assignment, as it is determined by objective clinical/pathological criteria and pre-specified DMEC definitions, with independent adjudication for potentially contentious cases (avoidable mastectomy).

**b. Rationale:** The method of outcome ascertainment was appropriate and comparable between groups — "Histopathological assessment of excised specimens was done at every participating centre in accordance with guidelines in the NHS BSP publication." Clear pre-specified definitions for what constituted "reoperation" and "pathologically avoidable mastectomy" were established a priori: "The definition of a pathologically avoidable mastectomy that was specified by the independent DMEC and agreed by the Trial Steering Committee was as follows: [detailed criteria]." Critically, potential bias in this judgement-requiring subcomponent was mitigated by independent review: "Imaging findings from patients undergoing a mastectomy at initial operation were reviewed every 12 months in conjunction with histopathological findings by the DMEC to identify any false-positive MR findings." Additionally, a formal quality-assurance process was implemented: "This process was completed by an independent radiologist, who was masked to the original MRI findings." While the treating surgeons were not blinded (as they had to be aware of MRI findings to act on them), the primary outcome itself (reoperation, based on objective histopathology and pre-specified rules, further adjudicated by DMEC) is unlikely to have been substantially influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment in a way that biases the comparison.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The trial was prospectively registered (ISRCTN57474502), and the reported primary outcome (reoperation rate) matches the pre-specified primary endpoint. There is no evidence of selective reporting from multiple outcome measurements or analyses for this specific outcome.

**b. Rationale:** The article states: "This study is registered, ISRCTN number 57474502," and the primary outcome as reported matches the pre-specified outcome: "The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients undergoing a repeat operation or further mastectomy within 6 months of random assignment, or a pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation." The statistical analysis section pre-specifies the analytic approach ("We compared the reoperation rate between groups with use of logistic regression, adjusting for the minimisation factors") and this matches what is reported in the results ("odds ratio 0·96, 95% CI 0·75–1·24; p=0·77"). The sample size calculation, power justification, and analysis plan (including prespecified subgroup analyses for tumour type) were described in a manner consistent with a single, prespecified reoperation outcome and analysis method, with no indication of alternative measurements or analyses of the reoperation outcome that could have been selectively suppressed.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

Because one domain (deviations from intended intervention) was judged as "some concerns," and no domain was judged "high risk of bias," the overall risk of bias for the reoperation rate outcome is **Some concerns**.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Time to relapse from start date of double-blind treatment

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Sequence generation used a computer-generated randomization list, and allocation concealment was achieved through sealed opaque envelopes with sequential block assignment. Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between groups.

**b. Rationale:** "After the open label period, eligible patients were assigned to escitalopram or placebo treatment according to a computer-generated randomisation list. The details of the randomisation series were unknown to any of the investigators and were contained in a set of sealed opaque envelopes. At each study centre, sequentially enrolled patients were assigned the lowest randomisation number available in blocks of four." Table 1 shows no meaningful baseline differences between the placebo (n=157) and escitalopram (n=163) groups in age, sex, race, age at onset, or duration of illness. The text also confirms: "There were no differences in demographics and clinical parameters between the two treatment groups in the randomisation period."

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Both participants and personnel were blinded throughout the double-blind period, tablets were identical in appearance/taste/smell, and an appropriate ITT analysis was used.

**b. Rationale:** "Study medications were tablets for oral administration, of identical appearance, taste or smell." "All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study." There is no indication that non-protocol interventions occurred due to trial context; disallowed medications were specified and monitored. The taper strategy (down-titrating 20mg to 10mg before switching to placebo) was implemented specifically to minimize discontinuation-related deviations, and sensitivity analyses (censoring relapses in the first 7 and 14 days) confirmed that results were robust: "In analyses using the ITT and a 7- or 14-day relapse cut-off period after randomisation, during which relapses were censored to discount the possible effect of discontinuation symptoms, escitalopram was significantly superior to placebo." The analysis population was appropriate: "All efficacy analyses in the double-blind treatment period were conducted using the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, which consisted of all randomised patients who took at least one dose of trial medication and had at least one post-randomisation efficacy assessment." This satisfies ITT principles (modified ITT excluding those with no post-randomization data is acceptable under RoB2 guidance).

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. This is a time-to-event outcome; withdrawals for reasons other than relapse were low, balanced between groups, and patients who relapsed were captured as events rather than censored/missing. There is no strong evidence that non-relapse censoring depended on the true (unobserved) outcome value differentially between groups.

**b. Rationale:** The ITT population fully accounted for randomized patients (320 randomized, 320 analyzed): "The ITT population comprised 320 patients due to the exclusion of two patients that were randomised without having met the Y-BOCS response criterion during the open label period." For non-relapse withdrawals: "During the 24-week double-blind period, the overall withdrawal rate, excluding relapses, was comparable for both treatments: 7.9% for patients treated with escitalopram (13 patients), and 8.9% for patients treated with placebo (14 patients)." This near-identical and low rate of non-relapse censoring between arms suggests censoring was non-informative and unlikely to bias the hazard ratio. Additionally, sensitivity analyses using different censoring windows (7/14 days) confirmed robustness of the primary result, providing further reassurance against bias due to missing data.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The outcome assessors (investigators rating Y-BOCS) were blinded to intervention assignment, and the primary relapse criterion (≥5 point increase in Y-BOCS) is largely an objective, structured clinician-rated measure, applied identically across groups. Blinding was well maintained, with only modest and expected differences in easily distinguishable transient adverse events (dizziness, nausea) during the taper period.

**b. Rationale:** "All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study." Investigators administering the Y-BOCS were trained: "Only those investigators who had actively participated in rater training sessions prior to inclusion of patients into the study were allowed to rate patients... Rater training was undertaken to increase inter-rater reliability." The relapse criterion was measured identically across groups: "Relapse was defined as either an increase in the Y-BOCS total score of ≥5 points from the time of randomisation, or an unsatisfactory treatment effect (lack of efficacy) as judged by the investigator." Although lack-of-efficacy judgement carries some subjectivity, "The Y-BOCS relapse criterion accounted for almost all of the relapses (116 out of 119 patients, 97%)," meaning the vast majority of relapse determinations relied on the objective, blinded, standardized Y-BOCS score rather than subjective judgement. While transient unblinding could theoretically occur due to discontinuation symptoms (dizziness, nausea higher in placebo group in weeks 0-2), the authors demonstrate this did not compromise the outcome: "when the survival analysis excluded withdrawals during the 1- to 2-week period following randomisation, the advantage for escitalopram remained just as strong, suggesting discontinuation effects are not responsible for the benefits associated with remaining on escitalopram."

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Time to relapse was explicitly pre-specified as the primary efficacy analysis, and this exact outcome/analysis was reported with no evidence of selection from multiple alternative outcome definitions or analytic approaches.

**b. Rationale:** "The sample size and power calculations were based on analysis of time to relapse in the double-blind period." "The prospectively defined primary analysis of efficacy was the time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment." The analysis method (log-rank test, Cox proportional hazards model) was specified a priori: "The primary efficacy analysis used a two-tailed log-rank test to compare the time to relapse for patients treated with escitalopram versus placebo... In addition, Kaplan-Meier survival curves were calculated and the Cox proportional hazard model for survival data was used to estimate hazard ratios." The relapse definition itself was fixed and consistent with prior literature: "This definition of relapse (an increase of 5 points from randomisation in the Y-BOCS score) has been used in other studies (e.g., Koran et al., 2002)." The trial registration (doc_id: 4dab5cab) would need to be cross-checked for full confirmation, but based on the internal consistency between the stated methods (power calculation, primary objective, and analysis plan all aligning with the reported result), there is no indication of selective reporting for this specific outcome.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Low risk of bias**

All five domains were judged to be at low risk of bias for the outcome of time to relapse from the start of double-blind treatment. The study employed adequate randomization and allocation concealment, maintained blinding throughout, used an appropriate ITT analytic approach with minimal and balanced missing data, employed a largely objective and blinded outcome assessment strategy, and pre-specified its primary analysis in a manner consistent with what was ultimately reported.

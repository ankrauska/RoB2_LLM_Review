# Risk of Bias Assessment: 3D Printing Models Study (Outcome: Performance/Correct Answers)

**Preliminary considerations:** This is an individually-randomized parallel-group trial. Effect of interest assessed as effect of assignment to intervention. Outcome assessed: sum score of correct answers (performance outcome).

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to insufficient reporting of allocation concealment and lack of baseline comparability data on prior knowledge/exposure.

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation: "Students were randomized into three groups using a computer program." This supports "Probably yes" for random sequence generation.
- Allocation concealment: No information is provided about whether allocation was concealed from those enrolling participants. There is no description of a central randomization system, sealed envelopes, or similar. This warrants "No information" (leaning toward "Probably no" given absence of any description in this dedicated methods section).
- Baseline imbalance: Table 2 shows no meaningful differences in age or school year between groups, and sex distribution was balanced (54.2% female overall, similar per group). This suggests no imbalance suggestive of a randomization failure.
- Given random sequence generation is reasonably supported but allocation concealment is not described at all (No information), and no baseline imbalance is evident, this maps to "Some concerns" per the algorithm (allocation concealment = NI, baseline imbalance = No → Some concerns).

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants (students) and those delivering the intervention (teachers) were necessarily aware of which teaching module (CT, 3D, or 3Dp) was being used — blinding was not feasible for this type of educational intervention: "A teacher provided a detailed interpretation of the fracture in each group."
- However, this is an educational/behavioral intervention, not a clinical intervention where lack of equipoise or trial-context-driven deviations (e.g., non-protocol co-interventions) would typically bias the assignment effect. There is no evidence or plausible mechanism by which awareness of group assignment led to deviations from the intended intervention (e.g., students switching teaching modules, contamination between groups) that arose because of the "trial context."
- Each subgroup was assigned distinct resources ("six computers or 12 models") per session, and testing occurred immediately: "By the end of the class, each student answered 10 questions." This structure minimizes opportunity for cross-contamination between groups.
- Analysis: All 120 randomized students were included with no dropouts ("no one dropped out"), and results were analyzed by assigned group (ANOVA comparing CT, 3D, 3Dp), consistent with an appropriate (ITT-like) analysis.
- Therefore, per the algorithm: awareness = Yes, but no deviations arose from trial context → Low risk of bias.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The article explicitly states: "a total of 120 medical students (54.2% females) participated, and no one dropped out."
- Outcome data (sum score of correct answers) were available for all randomized participants (40 per group, as confirmed in Table 2 and Table 3, with N=22 females + 18 males = 40 in CT; 21+19=40 in 3D; 22+18=40 in 3Dp).
- Since data are complete for all randomized participants, there is no risk of bias due to missing outcome data.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- The outcome (number of correct answers on a 10-item anatomical knowledge test) is an objective, observer-independent measure with a defined answer key (Table 1), reducing subjectivity in scoring itself — this is not a participant-reported outcome subject to expectation bias in the same way as pain or quality of life.
- However, the method of measuring the outcome could plausibly differ between groups in a way related to intervention exposure: "In the examination paper, each student had to enter sex, school age in medical school, and a unique randomization code; an invigilator recorded the assignment time of each student." There is no explicit statement that the invigilator/grader was blinded to group assignment, and given the visible nature of the teaching materials (models vs. images) and that testing occurred in group-specific subgroups/sessions, awareness of allocation by invigilators/graders is likely ("Probably yes" to assessor awareness of intervention assignment).
- Since scoring against a fixed answer key involves minimal subjective judgement, it is unlikely that assessor awareness meaningfully influenced the score outcome. Thus, this does not rise to high risk, but the lack of any statement on blinding of test administration/grading, combined with the plausible (if low) potential for influence, supports "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registration (referenced by ethics approval "20140108" from Binzhou Medical College) and the study design as described indicate that the 10 anatomical questions and 4 evaluative questions were pre-specified as the outcome measure and are fully reported in Table 1, with all 10 anatomical items and their answer key detailed, and the composite "sum score of correct answers" reported for all groups by sex (Table 3, Figure 5).
- There is no evidence of selective reporting: the same sum-score outcome and analysis approach (two-way ANOVA with Tukey's/Sidak's post-hoc tests) was applied and reported comprehensively across all three groups and both sexes, with full results (means, SD, p-values) presented for all 10 questions combined as intended.
- No alternative analyses (e.g., adjusted vs. unadjusted, different scoring cutoffs) appear to have been selectively omitted; the reported result matches the described a priori outcome and analytic plan (sum scores compared via ANOVA).
- Therefore, low risk of bias for this domain, based on available information, though independent access to a full pre-registered protocol was not confirmed beyond the ethics approval reference.

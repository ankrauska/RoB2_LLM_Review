Based on the RoB 2.0 tool, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of severity of subjective withdrawal symptoms using the Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale:

1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, allocation was adequately concealed, and baseline characteristics do not suggest a problem with the randomization process.
    b. Rationale: The study describes a "randomized, placebo-controlled trial" where subjects were "assigned randomly... using a randomization automated system". Allocation concealment was achieved as "gabapentin and placebo were dispensed in identical appearing capsules" and "the randomization list was held securely, and released only after study completion." There were "no significant differences in mean age or dosage of methadone and other drugs doses... at baseline."

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were blinded, and an appropriate analysis was used.
    b. Rationale: The study was "double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled". Participants and personnel were unaware of the intervention. The trial was analyzed based on the assignment to intervention at baseline (ITT), taking into account dropouts.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: Outcome data were missing for only a small number of participants, and the analysis accounts for this.
    b. Rationale: The trial started with 40 participants (20 per group). All 20 in the gabapentin group completed the trial, while 3 out of 20 in the placebo group dropped out ("one patient in the placebo group was excluded at day 14 and two others were excluded at day 17"). The drop-out rate is very low (3/40 total, or 15% in placebo), and a repeated measures ANOVA was used which generally handles missing data across timepoints well. The missingness is unlikely to induce substantial bias.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: The outcome assessors were blinded, and the method of measuring the outcome was appropriate.
    b. Rationale: The outcome was measured using the "Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale (SOWS)," which is a self-reported tool. The trial was double-blind, using "identical appearing capsules", so the participants (who are the assessors for this subjective outcome) were unaware of their intervention assignment. 

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: The reported results correspond to the pre-specified outcome measures and analyses.
    b. Rationale: The study protocol specified measuring withdrawal symptoms with the SOWS at specific days, and the results report on this exact measure across the specified time points (baseline, days 3/4, 7, 10, 14, 18). There is no indication of selective reporting of subscales or time points; total scores and individual symptom scores were presented.

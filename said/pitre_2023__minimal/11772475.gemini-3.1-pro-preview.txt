1. bias due to randomization: some concerns
a. Judgement: There is insufficient information provided in the manuscript to determine if the allocation sequence was random or if allocation was concealed.
b. Rationale: The text states that "73 highly stable methadone maintenance patients were randomly assigned" and "They were then randomized into one of the three study conditions", but no details regarding the method of sequence generation or allocation concealment are reported. 

2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were not blinded, and some participants were excluded from the analysis post-randomization.
b. Rationale: The nature of the intervention (clinic visits vs. office visits) precluded blinding. Additionally, the study reports that "A total of 78 patients were randomized into the study... A total of five patients withdrew from the study upon assignment to study condition". The analysis of retention is based on the remaining 73 patients, meaning that the intention-to-treat analysis excluded these 5 eligible randomized participants.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Outcome data for treatment retention were available for all participants included in the analysis.
b. Rationale: Treatment retention is a known status (whether the patient remained in the study or not). The authors successfully tracked retention for all 73 patients who began the assigned treatment ("90% (n=19/21) of the routine treatment, 89% (n=24/27) of the clinic-based MMM and 92% (n=23/25) of the physician office-based MMM finished 6-months of randomized care").

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The outcome is objective and unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
b. Rationale: While outcome assessors (study personnel) were aware of the assigned interventions, treatment retention (whether a patient continued to participate in the program) is an objective measure that is unlikely to be biased by this knowledge.

5. selective reporting: some concerns
a. Judgement: There is no available trial registration or pre-specified statistical analysis plan to confirm the planned analyses.
b. Rationale: Without a pre-published protocol or statistical analysis plan, it is not possible to definitively verify that the analysis of treatment retention was conducted exactly as pre-specified, though there is no clear indication of selective reporting.

1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial used computer-generated random number codes and allocation was concealed until after anaesthesia induction.
b. Rationale: The article states: "randomised 1:1 to LSG or SR-LRYGB using random number codes disclosed after anaesthesia induction." Baseline characteristics were well balanced.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The study was double-blinded (participant and assessor), and participants and endocrinologists managing medications were blinded to the allocation.
b. Rationale: The article states: "double-blind (patient and assessor) clinical trial" and "Metabolic medications were commenced post-operatively according to a standard medical review protocol, by endocrinologists blinded to treatment allocation".

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The loss to follow-up was low (5 out of 58 in the LSG group, 0 out of 56 in the SR-LRYGB group).
b. Rationale: At 1 year, data were available for 53 out of 58 in the LSG group and 56 out of 56 in the SR-LRYGB group (approx 91% and 100% respectively), meaning data were available for nearly all participants.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The outcome (HbA1c) is an objective laboratory measure and outcome assessors were blinded.
b. Rationale: The trial was "double-blind (patient and assessor)", and the primary outcome was an objective lab measurement (HbA1c), which is unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial was prospectively registered and reported on the pre-specified outcomes (diabetes remission via HbA1c).
b. Rationale: The protocol was pre-specified (ACTRN12611000751976 and NCT01486680), and the reported outcomes align with the pre-specified primary outcome of "Remission of type 2 diabetes mellitus/ glycaemic control". Multiple thresholds for remission are presented transparently.

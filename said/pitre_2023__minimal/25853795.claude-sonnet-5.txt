# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Total Duration of Treatment

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to lack of detailed information on allocation concealment implementation and evidence of baseline imbalance in age (including outliers) between groups, despite adequate sequence generation.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Sequence generation (Yes/Low concern):** "subjects were allocated...using a block randomization determined by a computer-generated random number table, with stratification for operator and center." This indicates adequate random sequence generation.
- **Allocation concealment (Probably yes):** "The appliance type was placed in a sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelope that was opened after the patient was accepted onto the trial. The generator of the randomization did not participate in patient allocation." This meets minimal criteria for concealment, though full details (e.g., tamper-proof, sequential opening confirmation) are not explicitly described.
- **Baseline imbalance (Some concern):** Table 2 shows notable differences in age range between groups: SmartClip group age range 12–29 years (SD 3y 3mo) vs Victory group 10–56 years (SD 1y 9mo). The Victory group contains a 56-year-old outlier, and the SmartClip group has a wider SD. Malocclusion distribution also differs somewhat (e.g., Class I: 12 vs 23; Class III: 8 vs 13), though these differences are not tested statistically and could be due to chance given block randomization by operator/center. Since block randomization was stratified only by operator and center (not by these prognostic factors), and given the unusual age outlier (56 years) in a pediatric/adolescent trial population, this raises some concern about whether randomization successfully balanced groups, though there is no clear evidence of subversion of the sequence itself.
- Overall, sequence generation and concealment appear adequate, but the imbalance in age (particularly the outlier) introduces some uncertainty, leading to a "Some concerns" judgement rather than "Low risk."

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns because participants and personnel could not be blinded, and although no formal evidence of trial-context-driven deviations was reported, the potential for detection/performance-related deviations affecting treatment duration cannot be excluded, particularly given operator inexperience with the new SmartClip system.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Awareness of participants (Yes):** "it was not possible to blind the clinician or patient to the type of bracket system being used."
- **Awareness of personnel (Yes):** Same as above; treating clinicians (specialist registrars) were aware of allocation, as they physically bonded the different bracket systems.
- **Deviations arising from trial context (No information/Probably no):** There is no explicit evidence that non-protocol interventions or trial-context-driven deviations (e.g., differential appointment scheduling, differential archwire changes) occurred due to awareness of allocation. The protocol specifies a standardized archwire sequence and bonding procedure was followed for both groups ("A standardized procedure was used by both clinicians in all three centers"). However, since duration of treatment is highly susceptible to clinician judgement (e.g., decisions about extending or shortening treatment, scheduling emergency visits) and lack of blinding could subtly influence such decisions (e.g., clinicians' preconceptions about the new SmartClip system), this cannot be fully excluded.
- **Appropriate analysis (Yes):** The analysis was per-protocol based on very low dropout (135/138, 97.8%), and this is essentially equivalent to an ITT analysis in this case since almost all randomized participants were analyzed in their assigned groups: "Due to the low number of dropouts, analysis was undertaken on a per protocol basis." Given only 3 exclusions (1 refusal, 2 failed to complete), and these appear to be genuine missing data rather than post-randomization exclusions based on treatment received, this should be considered acceptable and equivalent to a modified ITT analysis.
- Because personnel/participants were unblinded (unavoidably, given the nature of the intervention) and outcome (treatment duration) could plausibly be influenced by clinician awareness/expectations regarding the novel SmartClip appliance, but there is no clear evidence that deviations occurred or were unbalanced between groups, the judgement defaults to "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk" or "High risk."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias because outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, with a very low and balanced dropout rate.

**b. Rationale:**
- "One hundred thirty-eight subjects (mean age 14 years 11 months) were enrolled in the study, of which 135 subjects (97.8%) completed treatment."
- CONSORT flow diagram (Figure 2) confirms: 71 allocated to Victory (70 analyzed), 67 allocated to SmartClip (65 analyzed, with 66 receiving allocated intervention). Only 3 participants (2.2%) did not complete/were not analyzed: "one subject who refused fixed appliance treatment following allocation and two subjects who failed to complete treatment were omitted from the analysis."
- This small number of missing participants, balanced across groups, is very unlikely to have made an important difference to the estimated effect on treatment duration, satisfying the "nearly all" criterion for low risk of bias.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias because the outcome (treatment duration) is an objective, non-judgmental measure, and outcome assessment/data analysis was blinded.

**b. Rationale:**
- Treatment duration (months from bonding to debond) is an objective observer-reported outcome not involving judgement — the date of debond is recorded rather than assessed subjectively, so awareness of intervention assignment is unlikely to influence the recorded value directly.
- The authors took additional precautions: "data analysis was carried out at the end of the study with examiner blinding" and "the appliance type was not recorded on the data collection sheets in an attempt to minimize bias during data entry and analysis."
- While clinicians (who were not blinded) made the actual decision of when to debond, this decision could theoretically be influenced by knowledge of bracket type (an "intervention provider decision outcome"). However, given the objective clinical criteria typically used to determine debond readiness (occlusal/alignment goals), and the blinding of the analysis stage, the risk of bias is judged low. There is no evidence of measurement methods differing between groups, and the same standardized protocol/archwire sequence and outcome definition were applied to both groups.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias because the trial's pre-specified outcomes (treatment duration, number of visits, bond failure) are reported in full, consistent with the sample size calculation and stated objectives, without evidence of selective reporting from multiple measurements or analyses.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial's methods pre-specify treatment duration, number of visits, and bond failure rate as the outcomes of interest: "Efficiency of each ligation system was assessed by comparing the duration of treatment (months), total number of appointments (scheduled and emergency visits), and number of bracket bond failures." All three outcomes are fully reported in the Results with means, differences, confidence intervals, and p-values.
- The sample size calculation was explicitly based on treatment duration as the primary outcome ("based on data from a previous study investigating time taken to complete treatment... for a clinically significant reduction in treatment time of 3 months between the treatment groups"), and this outcome is reported using a single, consistent definition (months from fixed appliance placement to debond) and a single pre-specified analysis method (Welch two-sample t-test with 95% CI), with no indication of multiple alternative analyses or measurement definitions being tried and selectively reported.
- No trial registration or protocol was available/referenced to confirm the absence of outcome switching, which introduces minor uncertainty; however, there is no internal evidence (e.g., inconsistent definitions across sections, unusual analyses) suggesting selective reporting for this outcome. Therefore, "Low risk" is judged, though this relies on the completeness and consistency of the single available report.

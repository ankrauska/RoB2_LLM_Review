# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment: Time to Institutionalization

**Preliminary considerations:** This is an individually-randomized parallel-group trial. Effect of interest = effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect).

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to lack of allocation concealment and an unequal, non-prespecified allocation ratio change partway through the trial, though sequence generation was random and baseline characteristics appear reasonably balanced.

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation was random: "the dementia family care coordinator had prepared 100 sealed nontransparent envelopes, of which 53 contained allocation to the intervention group and 47 to the control group. Allocation to the groups was made by each patient (or her/his caregiver if the patient was unable) drawing one envelope." This supports "Yes" to random sequence.
- Allocation concealment is questionable: the envelopes were prepared by the same coordinator who delivered the intervention and was not blinded; envelopes were "sealed" and "nontransparent," but there is no description of tamper-proof sealing, sequential numbering, or independent oversight of the drawing process. This raises risk that the coordinator (who had a vested interest in the study) could have influenced or known the allocation sequence in advance.
- Mid-study protocol deviation: "the final 14 patients from the city of Kuopio were randomly allocated at a ratio of 2:1 to the intervention group (n=10) or the control group (n=4)." This unplanned change to the allocation ratio, decided by investigators partway through recruitment, raises some concern about manipulation of allocation, although the authors state this was to give more patients access to the intervention rather than to bias results, and they show reassurance that results were similar when restricted to the first 86 patients.
- Baseline characteristics (Tables 2 and 3) show no statistically significant differences between groups on age, gender, diagnosis, MMSE stage/score, Barthel Index, living arrangement, or caregiver characteristics (all p>0.05), suggesting no major imbalance indicative of randomization failure.
- Given the combination of adequate random sequence generation, unclear/likely inadequate allocation concealment, and the unexplained change in allocation ratio (a deviation from the original protocol design of 1:1), this domain is judged "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, due to lack of blinding of participants, caregivers, and intervention personnel (which is unavoidable given the nature of the intervention), combined with uncertainty about whether trial-context-related deviations affected the outcome, though an appropriate ITT-type analysis was used.

**b. Rationale:**
- Blinding of participants/caregivers/personnel was not possible and not implemented: The intervention involved intensive nurse case management, training courses, home visits, and 24-hour phone availability — participants and caregivers clearly knew their group assignment ("Patients in the intervention group and their caregivers were enrolled in a 2-year support program... provided by the dementia family care coordinator").
- However, the key outcome (institutionalization decision) was made by an independent panel blinded to allocation: "a group of experts (usually a chief physician, head nurse, and social worker) makes the decision to permanently institutionalize patients... These groups of experts were blind to the allocation and generally unaware that a patient was even participating in the study." This substantially reduces the risk that awareness of intervention assignment directly biased the institutionalization decision itself.
- There is no clear evidence that non-protocol/contaminating interventions occurred as a result of trial context (e.g., control participants seeking out coordinator-type support), and the paper does not suggest important co-interventions differed systematically apart from the study intervention itself. However, this cannot be entirely ruled out given lack of blinding of caregivers, who could have sought additional non-protocol services due to heightened awareness from trial participation/informed consent process.
- Analysis: The analysis appears consistent with ITT principles — all randomized patients are accounted for and analyzed in their assigned groups with no dropouts: "There were no dropouts from the study during the 2-year follow-up." This supports "Low risk" for the analysis component.
- Given lack of blinding of participants/caregivers/coordinator with some unquantified potential for deviations arising from trial context (e.g., differential engagement with community services), but blinded and independent outcome adjudication and appropriate ITT analysis, the domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low" or "High."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk, as outcome data (institutionalization status) were available for all randomized participants with no losses to follow-up.

**b. Rationale:**
- "There were no dropouts from the study during the 2-year follow-up (Figure 1)."
- The Figure 1 study flow diagram confirms complete follow-up for institutionalization/death/home status at both 1-year and 2-year points for all 53 intervention and 47 control patients.
- Deaths during follow-up (a competing risk) were appropriately handled: "In the survival analyses, times were censored at death if the patient died in community care before institutionalization" — an appropriate and pre-specified analytic approach to a competing risk rather than a source of missing data bias.
- Since outcome data (time to institutionalization or censoring) were available for 100% of randomized participants, this domain is judged as low risk.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk, because the outcome was an objective, register-based event, ascertained identically in both groups by assessors blinded to allocation.

**b. Rationale:**
- Outcome measurement method is appropriate and objective: "This information was obtained from the register of the Social Insurance Institution." Institutionalization is a discrete administrative/clinical event, not subject to interpretation bias in the same way subjective outcomes are.
- Ascertainment method did not differ between groups — both groups' institutionalization events were captured via the same national register, and the decision to institutionalize was made by an independent panel unaware of study group: "a group of experts... makes the decision to permanently institutionalize patients when they or their caregivers have expressed the need for institutionalization... These groups of experts were blind to the allocation and generally unaware that a patient was even participating in the study."
- Because assessors (the panel of experts making institutionalization decisions) were blinded to intervention assignment and the outcome is objectively recorded (an administrative/clinical placement decision, not requiring subjective judgment about the study), knowledge of intervention assignment could not have influenced the outcome measurement.
- This supports a "Low risk" judgement for this domain.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, because no trial protocol or prospective statistical analysis plan/registration was available to confirm that the reported outcome measurements and analyses for time to institutionalization were pre-specified before unblinded data were available.

**b. Rationale:**
- This trial was conducted 1993–1995 and appears in the literature without an identifiable prospective trial registration (this predates mandatory clinical trial registration norms; no registry record was identified/provided for this assessment, consistent with a "doc_id" reference to a trial registration that, if available, does not appear to add pre-specified analysis details beyond what is in the article).
- The primary outcome (time to institutionalization) is reported using a single, clearly defined method (Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with time-dependent Cox regression), and the outcome definition itself (placement in long-term institutional care, as recorded in a national register) is objective and not open to multiple competing definitions or scales, reducing the likelihood of selection among multiple outcome measurements.
- However, the statistical approach itself shows some flexibility that raises mild concern: the authors note "The significance of the difference between the survival curves of the two groups was assessed using a time-dependent Cox regression analysis, because the assumption of proportional hazards was not met in the data." This suggests the primary analytic model was adapted after seeing the data pattern (non-proportional hazards), which could reflect a data-driven (rather than pre-specified) choice of analysis method — though this is also a legitimate and transparent statistical adjustment.
- Additionally, subgroup analyses by dementia severity (mild/moderate/severe) are presented and interpreted post hoc ("for descriptive purposes, we also estimated the survival curves...by...stages of dementia"), and the discussion emphasizes a subgroup finding (benefit concentrated in severe dementia) not clearly identified as a pre-specified primary hypothesis, suggestive of potential outcome/analysis emphasis shaped by the results.
- Without access to a pre-specified protocol/SAP confirming the primary analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were seen, there is insufficient information to rule out selective reporting/analysis, leading to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

# Overall Risk of Bias: **Some concerns**

Per RoB 2.0 guidance (Table 1), since no domain was judged "High risk" but multiple domains ("randomization process," "deviations from intended intervention," and "selective reporting") were judged as "Some concerns," the overall judgement for this outcome (time to institutionalization) is **Some concerns**. The cumulative effect of these concerns (particularly around allocation concealment/protocol ratio change, inevitable lack of blinding given the intervention's nature, and absence of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan) warrants caution in interpreting the trial's effect estimate, although the objective, register-based outcome with blinded outcome adjudication and complete follow-up are notable methodological strengths that prevent an overall "High risk" judgement.

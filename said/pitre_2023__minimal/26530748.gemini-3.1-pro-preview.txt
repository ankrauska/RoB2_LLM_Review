**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The study is a long-term follow-up of a well-established, large randomized multicenter trial (PORTEC-2). Although the specific methods of random sequence generation and allocation concealment are not detailed in this specific follow-up report, they have been described in previous main trial publications, and baseline characteristics between the randomized groups were reported to be well balanced, suggesting no problems with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** Although participants and trial personnel could not be blinded due to the nature of the interventions (external beam radiation therapy versus vaginal brachytherapy), there is no evidence that significant deviations from the intended interventions occurred due to the trial context. The analysis assessed the effect of assignment to the intervention (intention-to-treat) using appropriate statistical models (linear mixed models) that account for the randomized groups.

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns.
* **Rationale:** The response rate at the 7-year follow-up was 77% (205 out of 265 eligible, alive, and disease-free patients), and 67% at the 10-year follow-up. While these are reasonable response rates for long-term questionnaire follow-ups, the authors note that "responders at 7 years were slightly younger and had fewer comorbidities compared with the whole PORTEC-2 cohort." Because older patients or those with more comorbidities might have different quality of life trajectories, missingness could potentially depend on the true value of the outcome, raising some concerns.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns.
* **Rationale:** The EORTC QLQ-C30 Global health score is a patient-reported outcome. Because blinding of the participants to the type of radiation therapy received was not possible, the outcome assessors (the participants themselves) were aware of the intervention received. While it is assessed many years after treatment, there remains a possibility that knowledge of the treatment could influence the self-assessment of subjective global health and quality of life.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The analysis utilized a standard and widely accepted validated tool (EORTC QLQ-C30). The reported results align with the prespecified domains of this tool, and there is no indication that the global health outcome or its analysis was selectively reported based on the statistical significance or direction of the findings.

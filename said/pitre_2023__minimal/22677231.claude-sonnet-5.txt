# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0): Moritz et al. — Deprexis RCT
**Outcome: Beck Depression Inventory (total score) post-intervention**
**Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (ITT)**

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation: The paper states "Participants who left their email addresses at the end of the baseline survey were randomly allocated to the treatment or wait-list group in consecutive order. No stratification was applied." There is no description of the actual method used to generate the random sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random number table). This is insufficient detail to answer "Yes" to random sequence generation → **No information/Probably yes** given it's an online/naturalistic effectiveness study run by an academic clinical group, but detail is thin.
- Allocation concealment: There is no description of the allocation concealment mechanism at all. Given the online nature of the trial (automated web-based randomization via a survey platform), it is plausible that concealment was adequate (participants could not know their allocation until baseline survey was completed and submitted), but this is not explicitly reported. This yields "No information" for concealment.
- Baseline imbalance: "Randomization was successful: significant differences emerged neither for any of the background nor psychopathological variables" (Table 1). No baseline imbalances suggestive of problems with randomization (BDI baseline: 30.02 vs 28.81, t=.82, p>.4).
- Overall: lack of explicit reporting of sequence generation and allocation concealment methods, combined with no evidence of baseline imbalance, leads to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," per the algorithm (no information on concealment + no imbalance → Some concerns).

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants were clearly aware of their intervention assignment: "Those in the wait-list (i.e., delayed-treatment) group received notice that they were allocated to the control group and would receive their access code upon completion of the post-survey eight weeks later." This is an inherent feature of wait-list designs — no blinding of participants was possible or attempted.
- There is strong evidence of deviations from intended intervention arising from the trial context. The unblinded wait-list design likely motivated some wait-list participants to seek other treatments/self-help during the waiting period. The authors themselves speculate: "The unexpected improvement in the control group might be attributable to patients' use of treatments other than Deprexis, to spontaneous remission... or to other, unknown factors." Given that participants were recruited from internet depression forums and explicitly told they were in a "delayed treatment" group, awareness of assignment plausibly influenced help-seeking behavior differentially between groups (trial context effect).
- These deviations were likely to have affected the outcome: the wait-list group showed a surprisingly large improvement (Cohen's d = .58) — nearly as large as many active treatment effects reported in the literature — undermining the assumption of a "true" no-treatment control condition.
- These deviations were plausibly *not balanced* between groups — the motivation to seek alternative help would be expected disproportionately in the wait-list (comparator) group, since they knew they were denied immediate access, whereas the Deprexis group was actively engaged in the study intervention.
- Analysis: An appropriate ITT analysis was used (multiple imputation with SAS PROC MI, retaining the full randomized sample), which satisfies Part 2 of the domain (appropriate analysis). However, because Part 1 (awareness + trial-context deviations affecting outcome, unbalanced) meets criteria for "High," the domain-level judgement defaults to **High risk of bias**, since High in either part drives the overall domain judgement.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- Outcome data were not available for all participants: overall completion was 81.9%, with the Deprexis group having a lower completion rate (78.1%) than wait-list (85.7%), though this difference was not statistically significant (χ²(1)=2.06, p>.1) (Table 1).
- The paper directly addresses this: "Participants who did not complete the trial were indistinguishable from non-completers on any of the background, medical or baseline psychopathological and psychological variables except for the QoL subscales tapping social relationships... and environment... where completers achieved slightly higher baseline scores." This suggests missingness was not strongly related to depression severity itself, but two significant differences on QoL subscales raise some possibility that missingness could be related to psychological state.
- Missingness could plausibly depend on the true value of the outcome (e.g., more depressed/less engaged participants dropping out differentially by group), and no formal sensitivity analysis was reported to rule this out.
- However, the trial investigators applied a robust ITT approach using **multiple imputation** (SAS PROC MI, 100 imputed datasets, stratified by treatment), which is an appropriate statistical method to address missing outcome data as per RoB 2 guidance — provided the imputation model was adequately specified, though details of the imputation model's covariates are not fully reported.
- The relatively modest and non-differential missingness (~18%), combined with the appropriate use of multiple imputation, but incomplete information about how well the imputation model captured the missingness mechanism, and a slight (non-significant) between-group difference in dropout rates, supports **"Some concerns"** rather than "Low" or "High."

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The outcome (BDI) is a **participant-reported outcome**, and participants were not blinded to intervention assignment (an inherent feature of the open-label wait-list design): participants clearly knew whether they had immediate access to Deprexis or were in the delayed wait-list condition.
- Per RoB 2 guidance, for participant-reported outcomes, "the assessment of outcome is potentially influenced by knowledge of intervention received, leading to a judgement of at least 'Some concerns.'" Reviewers must then judge whether it is *likely* that reporting was influenced by knowledge of assignment.
- In this trial, there are strong reasons to believe this influence occurred: participants in the wait-list group were explicitly informed they had been "unlucky" (delayed treatment), and the trial is unguided/unblinded with subjective self-report depression scores as the sole outcome measure — no clinician-rated or independent assessment was used. The authors themselves acknowledge: "the primary outcome rested exclusively on self-report... clinician ratings could not be used in the current study."
- The comparator condition (wait-list/no treatment) is a situation where expectancy and disappointment effects are well documented to bias self-reported outcomes (participants receiving no active intervention may over- or under-report symptoms based on frustration or motivation to appear improved to justify the wait).
- Given the absence of blinding, the completely subjective/self-report nature of the primary outcome, and the plausible influence of knowledge of allocation (particularly demoralization in the wait-list arm or motivation/positive expectancy bias in the treatment arm), this domain is judged **High risk of bias**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial was prospectively registered (NCT01401296) with BDI (total score) as the pre-specified primary outcome, measured at a single "7 Days" timeframe field (though the actual assessment was at 8 weeks per the manuscript) — the registry does not provide granular detail on planned subscale analyses, planned statistical models (e.g., ANCOVA vs. simple pre-post comparisons), or handling of missing data (e.g., multiple imputation) prior to unblinding.
- The manuscript reports the BDI total score as well as three subscales (negative attitude toward self, performance impairment, somatic symptoms) — it is unclear from the registry whether these subscale analyses were pre-specified or derived post hoc. This raises the possibility of selective reporting/analysis among multiple eligible outcome measurements within the depression domain, though for the *total score* result being assessed here specifically, this is less concerning since BDI total score was clearly the designated primary outcome in both the registry and publication.
- Both PP and ITT/ANCOVA analyses are reported transparently and consistently favor the treatment group for the BDI total score, with no evidence of discordant unreported analyses for this specific result.
- No trial protocol or statistical analysis plan (SAP) was available for detailed comparison beyond the registry; therefore, we cannot be fully confident that the reported ANCOVA-adjusted result was the sole pre-specified analytic approach finalized before unblinding.
- Given the consistency between the registered primary outcome (BDI) and the reported result, but the lack of a detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan or protocol to confirm the analysis was not selected among alternatives, the judgement is **Some concerns** rather than "Low risk."

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** The result is judged to be at high risk of bias overall because two domains — deviations from intended intervention and measurement of the outcome — are independently judged at **High risk of bias**. Per RoB 2 guidance (Table 1), a judgement of "High" risk of bias in any single domain is sufficient to classify the overall result as at high risk of bias. This reflects genuine and serious concerns regarding the unblinded, wait-list-controlled design of this trial for a self-reported, subjective primary outcome (BDI), compounded by "some concerns" in randomization, missing data handling, and selective reporting.

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is limited information on the exact methods used for allocation sequence concealment, and four participants declined to participate after the groups were determined.
b. Rationale: The authors state, "In period B, crew members were divided into two groups according to random numbers. Sailors were blind regarding the group assignment." While the generation of the sequence used random numbers, no information is provided on how allocation was concealed before assignment. Additionally, "Four sailors finally declined to take part in this phase of the study," leaving unequal and non-randomly reduced groups (13 vs 10). 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**
a. Judgement: Participants were blinded, reducing the risk of deviations arising from the trial context, but the analysis excluded participants who declined to take part.
b. Rationale: The study used a placebo and notes that "Sailors were blind regarding the group assignment." However, an intention-to-treat analysis was not strictly performed since the 4 participants who declined to participate in this phase were excluded from the final analysis of 23 sailors (13 in the allopurinol group, 10 in the placebo group). This exclusion of eligible participants post-randomization raises some concerns.

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were not available for all randomized participants due to dropouts before the intervention phase started.
b. Rationale: Four out of the 27 original sailors (15%) declined to participate in phase B and were not included in the outcome measurements. While there is no explicit evidence that their refusal to participate was linked to the true value of their potential muscle damage outcomes, the exclusion of a notable percentage of the small cohort means missingness could potentially impact the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcomes are objective laboratory measurements, and participants were blinded to the intervention.
b. Rationale: The study measured muscle damage via serum CK and AST activities using an automated analyser ("spectrophotometrically measured using commercially available reagents on an automatic random access analyser"). As these are objective observer-independent measurements and participants received a placebo, the assessment of the outcome is highly unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: No pre-specified trial registration or statistical analysis plan was provided to verify the originally intended analyses. 
b. Rationale: Without a published protocol or trial registry entry, it is difficult to definitively confirm that the reported analyses strictly match the pre-specified intentions before unblinded data were available. However, the reported outcomes (CK, AST, and MDA) are standard, expected physiological markers aligned with the study's stated methodology.

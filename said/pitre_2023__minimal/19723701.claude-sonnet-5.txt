# Risk of Bias Assessment: MADIT-CRT Trial
## Outcome: All-cause death or nonfatal heart failure events (effect of assignment to intervention)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Randomization was performed using an appropriate method with an adequate concealment mechanism, and baseline characteristics were well balanced between groups.

- Sequence generation: "The patients were randomly assigned in a 3:2 ratio... and were stratified according to clinical center and ischemic status with the use of an algorithm that ensured near balance in each stratum." This describes a stratified/minimization-type randomization with a random component. → **Yes** to sequence being random.
- Allocation concealment: No explicit description of the concealment mechanism (e.g., central randomization system, envelopes) is provided in the text. Given this is a large, well-resourced multicenter industry-sponsored trial with an independent DSMB and coordination/data center, and given the balance in baseline characteristics achieved, it is reasonable to answer **"Probably yes"** to concealment.
- Baseline imbalance: Table 1 shows very close balance across all variables (age, sex, race, EF, QRS duration, comorbidities, medications) between the CRT-ICD and ICD-only groups, with no differences suggestive of a randomization problem (e.g., age 65±11 vs. 64±11; LVEF 0.24±0.05 in both groups; QRS ≥150 msec 64.2% vs. 65.1%). No evidence of substantial or suspicious imbalance.

Since allocation was concealed (or probably concealed), sequence generation had a random component, and baseline characteristics were compatible with chance, the domain is judged **low risk of bias**.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**

- **Blinding:** The trial was open-label. "The treating physicians were aware of study-group assignments." Design module confirms masking = NONE. So participants and personnel were aware of intervention assignment (signalling question 2.1/2.2 = Yes).
- **Deviations arising from trial context:** The diagnosis of heart failure (a component of the composite primary outcome) "was made by physicians who were aware of study-group assignments." The authors themselves acknowledge this as a potential source of bias: "It is possible that the investigators' knowledge of study-group assignment contributed in some way to the lower frequency of heart failure in the CRT–ICD group." This suggests a plausible deviation (differential threshold for diagnosing/treating heart failure) arising from the trial context that could affect this component of the outcome. However, this is more directly relevant to bias in measurement of the outcome (domain 4) as well; for deviations from intended intervention, it raises concern regarding unblinded clinical management decisions (e.g., differential decisions to hospitalize or treat) — but it is difficult to establish this was likely to have substantially and asymmetrically biased the result, since the effect was consistent, large in magnitude, and the mortality component (assessed by a blinded committee) showed no benefit, suggesting the heart failure specific bias, if present, was moderate.
- Some crossover occurred but was infrequent and does not on its own indicate deviations from intended intervention that arose from trial context and affected outcome ("A total of 173 crossovers occurred"—82 CRT-ICD patients (7.5%) received ICD-only due to technical implant difficulties (not related to trial context bias), and 91 ICD-only patients (12.4%) crossed to CRT-ICD, 61 of these after a heart-failure event (i.e., consequence, not cause, of outcome) and 30 at physician discretion before an endpoint. This latter group (30/731 = 4.1%) could represent a deviation, but numbers are small).
- **Analysis:** "Data analysis was performed according to the intention-to-treat principle," which is the appropriate analysis for assignment effect (signalling question 2.6 = Yes).

Given unblinded personnel/participants, a plausible (author-acknowledged) mechanism for outcome-related deviation in clinical management/diagnosis affecting the heart-failure component of the composite outcome, but with an ITT analysis used and no clear evidence that this substantially biased the result, the judgement is **Some concerns** rather than High risk.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Outcome data were available for the great majority of the randomized population, and the small number of patients lost was similar between groups.

- All 1820 randomized patients appear to be included in the primary ITT analysis: "The primary end point occurred in 372 patients: 187 of 1089... and 185 of 731..." — accounting for essentially the full randomized sample (denominators = 1089 and 731, matching randomization numbers).
- "A total of 44 patients in the CRT–ICD group (4.0%) and 55 in the ICD-only group (7.5%) declined to continue participating in the study, were withdrawn by a physician, or were lost to follow-up." This is a modest proportion, somewhat higher in the ICD-only group, but these patients would have been censored (not necessarily missing from the ITT analysis prior to their withdrawal) using time-to-event methods, and the overall extent is unlikely to change interpretation of a hazard ratio of 0.66 (95% CI 0.52-0.84).
- No information suggesting that reasons for these losses were related to the true value of the outcome in a way that would differentially bias results, and the difference between groups (4.0% vs 7.5%) is not large enough, combined with the size and robustness of the treatment effect, to suggest bias.

Overall, missing outcome data are minimal and unlikely to bias the result — **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**

- The composite outcome combines death (assessed similarly regardless of group — an objective outcome not influenced by judgement) and nonfatal heart-failure events (which require clinical judgement and were assessed by unblinded treating physicians).
- Outcome assessors were NOT blinded for the heart failure component: "The diagnosis of heart failure...was made by physicians who were aware of study-group assignments," although "adjudication of the end points was carried out by an independent mortality committee and by a heart-failure committee that was unaware of study-group assignments." This mitigates but does not eliminate risk, because the initial diagnosis/decision to treat (which triggers adjudication) was made by unblinded clinicians, and the adjudication committee could only adjudicate events that were referred to them based on the treating physician's initial (unblinded) assessment and management decision.
- This is an observer-reported outcome (heart failure event) involving considerable clinical judgement (signs/symptoms consistent with heart failure, decision to give IV decongestive therapy), which "could be influenced by knowledge of intervention received" (signalling question 4.4 = Yes).
- Whether this likely influenced the result: The authors themselves raise this concern explicitly in the Discussion: "It is possible that the investigators' knowledge of study-group assignment contributed in some way to the lower frequency of heart failure in the CRT–ICD group." This is a candid acknowledgment of a plausible mechanism for bias in outcome ascertainment favoring CRT-ICD (e.g., physicians less inclined to diagnose/treat heart failure aggressively when CRT is thought to be already helping, or more attentive to symptoms in ICD-only patients). However, this is speculative and not proven, and the large, consistent effect size (also reflected in the objective echocardiographic remodeling data and mortality was similar between groups) provides some reassurance.
- Since it is plausible but not established that assessment of the heart failure component was influenced by knowledge of the intervention, and this could affect the numerically larger driving contributor to the composite endpoint (heart failure alone: HR 0.59), the domain is judged as **Some concerns** rather than high risk, as it is not clear this bias was substantial enough to negate the overall dramatic and consistent effect (also supported by objective echocardiographic reverse-remodeling findings).

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**

- The trial was registered prospectively (NCT00180271, first posted September 2005, well before trial completion in 2009), and the trial registration/protocol specifies "Mortality From Any Cause or First Heart Failure (HF) Event" as the primary outcome, matching exactly the outcome reported in the publication (death from any cause or nonfatal heart failure event, whichever came first).
- The pre-specified statistical approach (Wang-Tsiatis group-sequential design, stratified log-rank test, Cox regression stratified by center and ischemic status) is described in detail in the Methods and matches the analysis reported in the Results, with results reported for the full ITT population.
- The reported effect estimate (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.52–0.84, P=0.001) matches between the publication and the registered results (ClinicalTrials.gov results posting), with consistent numeric results (analysis pre-specified as Cox proportional-hazards, log-rank test, stratified by center/ischemic status).
- Secondary outcome (recurrent heart failure events) was also pre-specified in the registry and reported using a pre-specified statistical method (Andersen-Gill model).
- There is no indication of selective reporting of multiple outcome definitions/measurements or of multiple alternative analyses for the primary composite outcome; the single pre-specified composite outcome and pre-specified statistical method were used and reported in full.

Given close correspondence between the pre-specified registered outcome/analysis and what was reported, **Low risk of bias**.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias for this Outcome: **Some concerns**

Because domains 2 (deviations from intended intervention) and 4 (measurement of outcome) are each judged as "Some concerns," and these concerns relate to a similar underlying issue (lack of blinding influencing ascertainment/management of the heart-failure component of the composite outcome, explicitly acknowledged as a possible limitation by the trial authors), the overall judgement for this outcome is **Some concerns**. This does not rise to "High risk of bias" because: (1) the primary analysis was intention-to-treat; (2) heart failure event adjudication was performed by a blinded committee; (3) the treatment effect was very large, consistent across subgroups, and corroborated by objective (echocardiographic) reverse remodeling data; and (4) missing data and randomization concerns were minimal.

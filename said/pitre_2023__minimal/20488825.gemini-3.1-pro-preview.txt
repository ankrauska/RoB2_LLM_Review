1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: There is a low risk of bias for the randomization process.
b. Rationale: The study describes that "Separate physicians were assigned to randomly allocate patients into two groups... A computer-based blocking method (four blocks) was used for the randomization". Additionally, "The patients were unaware of their group assignment and all medications were given in similar packages". The baseline characteristics (Table 1) show no significant differences between groups.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: There is a low risk of bias due to deviations from the intended intervention.
b. Rationale: The trial was double-blinded, and participants and study personnel were unaware of the assignment. The report states "The patients were unaware of their group assignment and all medications were given in similar packages". Intention-to-treat analysis was performed: "The analyses were performed according to intention-to-treat methods, and patients who withdraw from the study were followed for the whole study period." 

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: There is a low risk of bias due to missing outcome data.
b. Rationale: The authors state that "The analyses were performed according to intention-to-treat methods, and patients who withdraw from the study were followed for the whole study period." They also state "those of our dropout cases who did use simvastatin or placebo for 6 months (nine patients) were included in the final analysis." Although 14 patients withdrew initially, the analysis for relapses accounted for those followed up. The proportion missing was similar and reasons for missing were well-documented.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: There is a low risk of bias in the measurement of the outcome.
b. Rationale: The primary outcome, relapse rate, is defined by clinical criteria ("new or worsening neurological deficit..."). Assessor blinding was maintained: "Four neurologists were asked to assess the patients’ eligibility... and perform the follow-up examinations. They were blinded to the patients’ assignment". 

5. selective reporting: some concerns
a. Judgement: There are some concerns regarding selective reporting.
b. Rationale: The trial registration lists "attack number" as the primary outcome, which matches the manuscript. However, the trial was registered in April 2008, while the study started in April 2005, indicating retrospective registration. Thus, there is no pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

Based on the provided RoB 2.0 guidance and the trial publication, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome "women’s burden and distress":

    1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: The trial used a random component for sequence generation, and there is no evidence of substantial baseline imbalance. Allocation concealment was not explicitly detailed, but there is no strong evidence to suggest it was violated in a way that caused bias.
           b. Rationale: The authors state, "Participants were assigned randomly via random number tables to one of three treatment conditions". Baseline characteristics were compared using one-way ANOVAs, which "showed there were no significant differences between the conditions on the demographic variables" or on the pre-treatment measures of GHQ and RSS. Although the exact method of allocation concealment is not described, the use of random number tables and lack of baseline imbalances suggest a low risk of bias for the randomization process.

    2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns
           a. Judgement: The trial was unblinded (which is typical for this type of psychological intervention), meaning participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention. There is no information provided to confidently rule out deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context. The trial used an analysis of all completed participants, but 22% dropped out.
           b. Rationale: Participants and therapists could not be blinded to the behavioral interventions (supportive counselling, stress management, or alcohol-focused couple therapy). The authors note, "Forty-seven participants completed treatment... This represents an overall attrition of 22% of participants from the treatment phase of the study." The analysis appears to be based only on those who completed treatment ("Forty-seven participants completed treatment and 44 of these participants provided assessed data at 6-month follow-up"), which is not a true intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis. The failure to analyse all randomized participants introduces some concerns for the effect of assignment to intervention.

    3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias
           a. Judgement: Outcome data were not available for all randomized participants, and the dropout rate was substantial (over 20%). The reasons for dropout could be related to the true value of the outcome (e.g., relationship ending).
           b. Rationale: The authors report an overall attrition of 22%. Furthermore, they explicitly state: "Just over 20% of women dropped out of treatment, and most of these women reported they had left their partners soon after beginning the programme. Many women who dropped out told us that the sessions they attended assisted them to leave a relationship in which they had been very unhappy for many years... Unfortunately, all the women who dropped out from treatment declined further assessment." Because ending the relationship is highly correlated with the women's burden, distress, and relationship outcomes, missingness in the outcome is likely dependent on its true value.

    4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns
           a. Judgement: The outcomes for women's burden and distress were self-reported, and the participants were not blinded to their intervention assignment.
           b. Rationale: Women's burden and distress were measured using the Relative Stress Scale (RSS) and the General Health Questionnaire (GHQ), which are participant-reported outcomes. Since the participants knew which intervention they received (e.g., supportive counselling vs. stress management), their knowledge of the intervention could have influenced their responses on these self-report questionnaires.

    5. selective reporting: some concerns
           a. Judgement: There is no reference to a pre-published protocol or trial registration to verify if the analyses were pre-specified before unblinded data were available. 
           b. Rationale: The authors report multiple outcomes and time points (pre, post, 6-month follow-up) for the RSS and GHQ. While they report the results for the stated measures, the lack of a pre-specified analysis plan or trial registry entry means it cannot be confirmed whether the reported analyses (e.g., specific ANOVAs) were fully pre-specified or if other measures were intended but not reported.

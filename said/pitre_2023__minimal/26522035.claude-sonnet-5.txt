# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Patient Acceptance of the Appliance

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Sequence generation (random):** "Random sequence generation was done with a computer-generated list of random numbers obtained from an Excel spreadsheet (Microsoft, Redmond, Wash) using the '=RAND()' function. To ensure a 1:1 allocation ratio, a blocked randomization was performed (random permuted blocks with randomized order of block sizes) with block sizes of 6 and 4." This satisfies signalling question 1.1 (Yes).
- **Allocation concealment:** "Allocation concealment was achieved with sequentially numbered and sealed opaque envelopes that were concealed from the researchers and the assessors." Randomization was performed by "a person who was not involved in the trial." This satisfies signalling question 1.2 (Yes).
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table I shows baseline characteristics (age, SNA, SNB, ANB, Y-axis angle, MP/SN, MMP, overjet, mandibular crowding) were reported and described by the authors as "similar in both groups." No large or suspicious imbalances are apparent that would suggest a problem with the randomization process (signalling question 1.3: No).

All three criteria for low risk of bias are met: random sequence generation, concealed allocation, and no evidence of problematic baseline imbalance.

---

### 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Awareness of assignment:** The authors explicitly state: "Blinding of the clinicians and patients to the interventions in each group was impossible." Thus, both participants and personnel delivering interventions were aware of the assigned intervention (signalling questions 2.1 and 2.2: Yes). This is expected given the nature of the intervention (mini-implants are physically detectable), and does not by itself indicate high risk.
- **Deviations arising from trial context affecting the outcome:** Patient acceptance is a **participant-reported outcome**, and awareness of group assignment (especially having a visible mini-implant) could plausibly influence responses to a subjective acceptance questionnaire. However, there is no explicit evidence reported of deviations from intended intervention (e.g., no reports of non-protocol interventions, or trial-context-driven behavioral changes affecting this outcome). No information is provided regarding whether awareness of allocation led to trial-context-driven deviations (e.g., differential care-seeking, differential expectations affecting acceptance reporting) — this leads to "No information" for signalling question 2.3, defaulting to "Some concerns" per the algorithm (Table 6, row 3).
- **Analysis:** The authors report: "The primary analysis was planned to be an intention-to-treat analysis to involve all patients who were to be randomly assigned; however, since there were no dropouts in the study, all the patients were included in the analysis." This is appropriate (signalling question 2.6: Yes), so Part 2 of this domain is Low risk.

Combining Part 1 (Some concerns, due to lack of information about whether trial-context deviations occurred, in an unblinded trial with a subjective/self-reported outcome) and Part 2 (Low), the domain-level judgement is **Some concerns**.

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- "No patients dropped out during the trial." and "The 32 surveys were retrieved from the patients; there were no dropouts."
- The CONSORT flow diagram confirms all 32 randomized patients (16 per group) were analyzed for the outcome.
- "All questionnaires were given to and collected from the patients on the day of removal of the FFRD. All questions were answered correctly by the patients and their parents," with "no missing answers."

Since outcome data were available for all randomized participants (100%), this satisfies the low risk of bias criterion outright (signalling question 3.1: Yes), with no further questions needed.

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Appropriateness of measurement method:** The questionnaire was adapted/modified from a previously used instrument (Bowman et al.) and simplified; this appears reasonable and not inherently inappropriate (signalling question 4.1: No).
- **Comparability of measurement between groups:** The same questionnaire, administered at the same time point (day of FFRD removal), was used for both groups, so there is no indication that measurement/ascertainment methods differed between groups (signalling question 4.2: No).
- **Outcome assessor awareness:** Patient acceptance is a **participant-reported outcome**, meaning the "outcome assessor" is the patient. Patients could not be blinded to their intervention: "Blinding of the clinicians and patients to the interventions in each group was impossible." Although the researchers implemented blinding for the data extraction/statistical analysis stage ("Blinding was done for outcomes assessment, and data were extracted by an uninvolved person who did not know the nature of the intervention... The person responsible for the statistical analysis was not informed about the nature of the trial"), this blinding pertains to those handling/analyzing data, not to the patients who generated the self-reported responses. Since the participant is the outcome assessor for a self-reported acceptance outcome, signalling question 4.3 is answered "Yes" (assessors—i.e., patients—were aware of intervention received).
- **Potential for influence:** For participant-reported outcomes, assessment is potentially influenced by knowledge of intervention received per RoB2 guidance (signalling question 4.4: Yes/Probably yes), particularly because one group had a visibly/palpably different intervention (presence of mini-implants), which could shape expectations or satisfaction reporting.
- **Likelihood of actual influence:** There is no strong evidence in the article that this influence was substantial or systematically biased responses in one particular direction (e.g., no indication of strong prior beliefs/preferences among patients regarding mini-implants vs. standard FFRD, and the comparator is an active comparator rather than placebo/no-treatment, somewhat reducing this risk). Therefore, signalling question 4.5 is best answered "Probably no" (unlikely that assessment was strongly influenced), leading to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "High risk," per Table 12.

### 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Trial registration:** The article states: "Registration: This trial was not registered. Protocol: The protocol was not published before trial commencement." Without a registered protocol or pre-published analysis plan, it is not possible to confirm whether the data reported for patient acceptance (and its many sub-components: ease of insertion, noticeability, effect on eating/sleep/speech, pain, swelling, gum bleeding, satisfaction, breakage, recommendation) were selected, on the basis of results, from a larger set of measured outcomes or analyses (signalling questions 5.1, 5.2, 5.3: No information available, since there is no protocol/registration to check against).
- The outcome "patient acceptance" was assessed via a multi-item questionnaire (14 questions) with numerous sub-outcomes reported comprehensively in Tables III–VII and Figure 5, and the authors state "There were no outcome changes after commencement of the trial," which is reassuring. However, this is a self-report by the authors without an independently verifiable pre-specified plan (no registry entry, no protocol, no statistical analysis plan referenced), so this statement cannot be independently corroborated.
- Given the absence of a trial registration record or pre-specified/published protocol/SAP to verify against, and per RoB 2 guidance ("Analysis intentions are not available... Answer 'No information'"), the default judgement per Table 14 for "No information" responses across signalling questions is **Some concerns**.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The study used a random sequence generation method ("Randomization was performed via randomly permuted blocks of fixed size using validated software RANCODE 3.6"). Allocation concealment was also adequate, as "Blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database." Since this was a split-body trial where each participant received both interventions on different arms, baseline characteristics between intervention groups were inherently balanced.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The study was described as double-blind. The trial preparations were coded ("randomly assigned a letter A or B for blinding of the test formulations") and "All applications were performed by a study nurse" using the coded material. Thus, participants and study personnel delivering the interventions were unaware of the assigned interventions. Appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was performed. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: A total of 35 participants were randomized, and 3 dropped out or were excluded during the study due to protocol violations. The authors performed an intention-to-treat analysis including all 35 participants by using Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) for the missing values. The reasons for dropout (e.g., intake of antiphlogistics, independent disinfection of the area) are unlikely to be related to the true value of the outcome, and the proportion of missing data is small (3 out of 35, ~8.6%).

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome was visually assessed by a dermatologist. The assessor was blinded to the intervention assignments: "All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator." Therefore, the assessment of the outcome was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: Although the primary outcome (sum score of dryness and erythema) was clearly defined and reported as described in the methods, there is no information indicating that the analysis was conducted according to a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a prospective trial registry finalized before unblinded data were available. Therefore, there are some concerns regarding selective reporting.

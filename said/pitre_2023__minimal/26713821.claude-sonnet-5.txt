# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0 Tool
## Outcome: Neuropsychological Test Performance
## Effect of Interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias Arising from the Randomization Process

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Rationale:** The study is described as a "single-center, prospective controlled trial" with "random allocation" (1:1) to conventional care alone or with nutritional counseling. However, the methods provide no description of the actual sequence generation method (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number tables) or the allocation concealment procedure. The Supplemental Material is referenced for full methodological detail but is not available in the text provided for adjudication.

- Signalling Q1.1 (Was the allocation sequence random?): **Probably yes/No information** – the term "random allocation" is used, but no elaboration on the process (e.g., blocks, computer generation) is given in the main text.
- Signalling Q1.2 (Was the allocation sequence concealed?): **No information** – no description of concealment method (e.g., central randomization, sealed envelopes) is provided.
- Signalling Q1.3 (Baseline imbalance suggesting a problem?): **No** – Table 1 shows no significant differences between groups for age, gender, education, BMI class, comorbidities, genotype, or baseline cognitive/functional measures (all P>.10), consistent with successful randomization.

Given the lack of explicit information on sequence generation and concealment, but with baseline characteristics showing good balance and no evidence of problems, this aligns with the algorithm outcome of "Some concerns" (no information on concealment, combined with no imbalance).

---

## 2. Deviations from the Intended Intervention (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**

- Signalling Q2.1/2.2 (Were participants/personnel aware of assigned intervention?): **Yes** – This was clearly an open-label trial; participants in the intervention arm attended "26 to 28 1-hour group meetings" with nutritionists, which could not be blinded from participants or those delivering the intervention. The protocol confirms "whoMasked: OUTCOMES_ASSESSOR" only — participants and care providers were not blinded.

- Signalling Q2.3 (Deviations arising because of trial context?): **No/Probably no** – There is no evidence presented that the trial context (e.g., recruitment/consent process) led to non-protocol interventions inconsistent with the protocol. The authors note: "Because the process of recruiting volunteers included information on the risks of obesity, it is also possible that this may have increased motivation to lose weight in both groups" — importantly, this potential contextual effect appears to have affected **both** groups similarly (both showed similar weight loss: −2.1 vs −1.3 kg/m², P=.428), suggesting no differential deviation attributable to trial context that would bias the assignment effect.

- Signalling Q2.6 (Appropriate analysis used?): **Yes** – The generalized estimating equation (GEE) analysis included all patients with available follow-up data using standard longitudinal methods consistent with ITT principles for those with data. "Seventy-five patients completed follow-up" out of 80 randomized, and analyses appear to include all available participants analyzed according to randomized group.

Because deviations (if any) affected both arms similarly and did not favor one intervention over the other, and an appropriate analytic approach was used analyzing participants according to randomized group, this domain is judged as low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention.

## 3. Missing Outcome Data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Rationale:**

- Signalling Q3.1 (Data available for all/nearly all participants?): **Yes** – "Seventy-five patients completed the follow-up" out of 80 randomized (93.75% retention). The flow chart (Figure 1) confirms 37/40 and 38/40 patients analyzed in each arm at 12 months.

- Reasons for missing data are provided and appear unrelated to the outcome trajectory: "Five women did not complete the follow-up: 4 dropped out and one died of pneumonia." Death from pneumonia is an unrelated cause, and the dropout numbers are balanced and small (5/80 = 6.25%) across a study with 12-month follow-up in elderly patients.

- Given the very low proportion of missing data (6.25%) and no evidence that missingness was different between groups or plausibly related to cognitive outcome trajectories (the reasons given being incidental dropout and a death from an unrelated infectious cause), the number of participants with missing data is small enough that their outcomes could not have made an important difference to the estimated effect.

This satisfies the "nearly all" criterion for a low risk of bias judgement without need to proceed through subsequent signalling questions.

## 4. Measurement of the Outcome

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Rationale:**

- Signalling Q4.1 (Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?): **No** – The neuropsychological battery used is comprehensive and includes well-validated, standard neuropsychological instruments (RAVLT, Trail Making Test, Digit Span, Wisconsin Card Sorting Test, verbal fluency tests, CAMcog, IQCODE), all appropriately validated for the Brazilian population and appropriate for detecting cognitive change in MCI populations.

- Signalling Q4.2 (Could measurement/ascertainment differ between groups?): **Probably no** – The same battery of tests was administered to both groups at baseline and 12 months using the same protocol and trained assessors.

- Signalling Q4.3 (Were outcome assessors aware of intervention received?): The protocol states masking was employed for the "OUTCOMES_ASSESSOR," suggesting an attempt at blinding. However, the trial report itself provides **no explicit statement** in the text confirming that neuropsychological testers were blinded to group assignment, nor describes procedures to maintain this blinding (e.g., separate personnel, avoidance of unblinding cues). This is **No information** from the article body, though the trial registry indicates intended masking of outcome assessors.

- Signalling Q4.4/4.5 (Could/did assessment differ due to knowledge of intervention?): Many outcomes assessed are performance-based, cognitive tests with relatively low subjectivity (e.g., Trail Making Test timing, RAVLT recall counts), which are less susceptible to assessor bias even if unblinded, reducing but not eliminating concern.

Because the protocol indicates intended blinding of outcome assessors, but the published article does not explicitly confirm or describe how this blinding was implemented or maintained during 12-month follow-up testing, there is meaningful uncertainty. This leads to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," since we cannot confirm with confidence that blinding was successfully implemented and maintained, even though motivated bias is less likely given the semi-objective nature of most test scores.

## 5. Selective Reporting

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Rationale:**

- Signalling Q5.1 (Analysed according to pre-specified plan?): The trial registration (NCT01286389) lists the primary outcome broadly as "Neuropsychological test performance," "Diagnosis of dementia," and "Weight loss," without specifying in the registry which exact tests, time points, or a hierarchy of primary vs. secondary cognitive measures would be analyzed. The published paper reports a large battery of tests (RAVLT, TMA, TMB, Digit Span, verbal fluency [phonemic/semantic], Wisconsin Card Sorting, CAMcog, IQCODE, MoCA) and multiple derived "cognitive domains" via post-hoc principal-components analysis. This grouping strategy (combining tests into composite domain scores using PCA) is not specified in the trial registration and appears to represent a data-driven analytic approach developed after data collection.

- Signalling Q5.2 (Selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements?): **Probably yes/No information** – The paper explicitly states an a priori hypothesis (comparing cognition between groups with different degrees of weight loss) was not achieved because groups did not differ in weight loss as expected: "the initial goal...was not fully achieved... we therefore considered it most useful to make an exploratory analysis considering the decrease of BMI as a continuous variable." This is an explicit acknowledgment that the analytic strategy was changed after the data revealed the randomized groups did not differ as anticipated — a clear post-hoc deviation from the original design (per protocol registration, the comparison was to be between randomized arms).

- Signalling Q5.3 (Selected from multiple eligible analyses?): **Probably yes** – Given: (a) the switch from a group-comparison design to a continuous BMI-change exploratory correlational analysis, (b) creation of post-hoc "cognitive domains" via PCA rather than reporting each pre-specified test individually or according to a pre-specified hierarchy, and (c) the explicit statement that "due to the exploratory nature of the analyses...it was not possible to carry a multiple comparisons correction" despite testing numerous cognitive domains, subgroups (age, APOE4 status), and correlations with multiple biomarkers/dietary variables (Table 4) — there is a substantial risk that reported associations were selected, emphasized, or interpreted based on the pattern/significance of results rather than a pre-specified analytic plan.

Given the clear, self-reported deviation from the original analytic plan (comparing randomized groups) to an exploratory, continuous, multiple-subgroup/interaction analysis without correction for multiple comparisons, and without a publicly available detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan enumerating this domain-based/interaction approach, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" (with recognition that the authors' transparency about this limitation is a mitigating factor against a "High" risk judgement, though the risk of result-selection remains substantial for the outcome as reported).

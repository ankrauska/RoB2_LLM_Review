1. bias due to randomization: some concerns
       a. Judgement: The trial reports random allocation, but the specific methods used for random sequence generation and allocation concealment are not described in the provided text.
       b. Rationale: The article states "Random allocation to conventional medical care alone (n = 40) or together with nutritional counselling (n = 40)", but lacks details on how the sequence was generated or concealed. However, baseline characteristics were well balanced between the groups (Table 1), suggesting no major issues with the randomization process itself, leading to a judgement of some concerns due to lack of information.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: Although participants and personnel could not be blinded to the nutritional counseling intervention, there is no evidence of deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context and affected the outcome. Appropriate analyses were used.
       b. Rationale: The study design (nutritional counseling) precludes blinding of participants and care providers. However, physical activity levels (a potential confounder/non-protocol intervention) did not change significantly between groups ("The proportion of physically active patients did not change"). The analysis included 75 out of 80 randomized participants, indicating an appropriate analysis of the effect of assignment to intervention.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: Data were available for nearly all participants, and the dropout rate was very small and balanced.
       b. Rationale: The study recruited 80 participants and 75 completed the 12-month follow-up (93.75% retention). The reasons for missing data (4 dropped out, 1 died of pneumonia) do not suggest that missingness depended on the true value of the outcomes to a degree that would bias the results.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment, minimizing the risk of differential measurement error.
       b. Rationale: The trial registration indicates that the outcomes assessor was blinded ("masking: SINGLE, whoMasked: OUTCOMES_ASSESSOR"). Furthermore, standard and validated neuropsychological tests were used (e.g., CAMcog, RAVLT, Trail Making Test), which are appropriate for assessing the outcome.

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The study reported the pre-specified primary outcomes in accordance with the trial registration, and multiple cognitive domains were reported comprehensively.
       b. Rationale: The trial registry (NCT01286389) lists "Neuropsychological test performance" as a primary outcome. The publication reports a comprehensive battery of tests covering different cognitive domains (memory, executive function, language, global cognition) and reports results for all of them, showing no evidence of selective reporting based on the direction or significance of the results.

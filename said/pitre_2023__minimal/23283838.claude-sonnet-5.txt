# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Between-Session Habituation Outcome

**Preliminary considerations:** This is an individually-randomized parallel-group trial (DCS vs. placebo) in social anxiety disorder. The outcome assessed is "between-session habituation" (categorical response, defined as ≥5-point drop in average SUDS from Visit 1 to Visit 2). The effect of interest is assessed as the **effect of assignment to intervention** (per instructions), even though the analysis population is a modified sample (completers only).

---

## 1. Bias due to Randomization

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:** Allocation appears to have been adequately concealed via an independent data manager, and while sequence generation is not described in detail, there is no indication of a non-random or manipulable process. The single observed baseline imbalance (age) is plausibly a chance finding given the number of comparisons made.

**b. Supporting evidence:**
- Allocation concealment: "Participants were randomly assigned to 250mg of DCS or placebo in identical-appearing capsules by **a data manager who had no interaction with participants**." This satisfies the minimal criteria for concealment (central/third-party allocation with no contact between enroller and randomizer).
- Sequence generation: No explicit description of the randomization method (e.g., computer-generated list) is provided ("No information"), but there is no evidence of a systematic or predictable method, and the identical-capsule blinding design supports a genuine randomization process → judged "Probably yes" for a random sequence.
- Baseline balance: "Participants did not differ across group in regard to: gender, ethnicity, use of medication, use of antidepressants, LSAS scores at Visit 1, average SUDS for the speech in Visit 1, or Social Phobia Scale ratings at Visit 1 (ps > .16)." Only age differed significantly (p = .019). With ~8 baseline comparisons performed, one significant difference at conventional thresholds is compatible with chance. Furthermore, "These group differences were examined below to test whether they could explain any group effects; they could not," and inclusion of age as a covariate did not alter the DCS effect — this is reassuring though not required for the bias judgement itself.
- Successful blinding (participants/experimenters unable to guess assignment, ps > .15) further supports that allocation was not compromised.

---

## 2. Deviations from the Intended Intervention (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:** Participants, carers, and personnel delivering the intervention were successfully blinded, and the analytic approach (excluding only participants with missing outcome data) is consistent with a modified-ITT approach that is considered appropriate for this domain (missing-data bias itself is addressed separately in Domain 3).

**b. Supporting evidence:**
- Blinding: "Participants were randomly assigned to 250mg of DCS or placebo in identical-appearing capsules." Blinding success was formally tested: "Adequacy of the blind was assessed by having both participants and experimenters guess treatment assignment based on Visit 1 of the study; neither participants nor experimenters were able to discern assignment (ps > .15)."
- Because both participants and personnel were unaware of assignment, deviations from intended intervention arising from the trial context (differential engagement, differential delivery of exposure) are unlikely.
- Analysis: "Only those participants completing Visit 2 are analyzed because the intent is to assess the sensitivity of the clinical assay... It is also unclear what the meaning of an intent to treat analysis would be when no participants who dropped out provided any ratings after receiving any amount of treatment." This is a modified ITT analysis (excluding those with missing outcome data), which per RoB2 guidance is considered an appropriate analysis for the assignment-effect domain (any resulting bias is captured in the missing-data domain instead).
- No non-protocol interventions or co-interventions differing by group are reported; medication/antidepressant use did not differ between groups and was tested as a covariate without altering results.

---

## 3. Missing Outcome Data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:** A small and balanced proportion of participants (4/38, ~10.5%; 2 per group) had missing outcome data, with documented reasons that do not appear related to the true value of the habituation outcome, and the proportions/reasons were balanced across arms.

**b. Supporting evidence:**
- "Four participants (2 DCS, 2 placebo) did not return for Visit 2 and were not included in any analyses. Two participants became unreachable (change in or loss of phone number: 1 DCS, 1 placebo), and two stated that they preferred not to continue in the study (1 DCS, 1 placebo)."
- The proportion missing is small and **identical between groups** (2 vs. 2), reducing concern about differential/informative missingness.
- Reasons given (loss of phone contact, general unwillingness to continue) are not clearly and differentially tied to participants' anxiety/habituation status by arm.
- Some uncertainty remains because "preferred not to continue" could in principle relate to distress, and no sensitivity analysis was conducted for missing data; this tempers full confidence, but the balance and small magnitude support a low-risk judgement rather than "some concerns."

---

## 4. Measurement of the Outcome

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:** The outcome (SUDS-based habituation) is a participant-reported outcome, and participants were the outcome assessors. Since blinding was successful and verified, differential measurement bias due to awareness of treatment assignment is unlikely. The measurement method itself was appropriate and standardized.

**b. Supporting evidence:**
- "The SUDS was assessed two minutes prior to the speaking task, immediately before the speaking task, every two minutes during the speaking task, and at the end of the speaking task," with high internal consistency (Cronbach's αs > .85), indicating an appropriate, non-inappropriate measurement method.
- Outcome assessor = participant (self-report). Blinding of participants to allocation was confirmed: "neither participants nor experimenters were able to discern assignment (ps > .15)."
- Because blinding held and was empirically verified, it is unlikely that self-reported SUDS ratings were influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
- Measurement/ascertainment procedures were identical between groups (same standardized exposure protocol, same rating scale, same schedule).

---

## 5. Selective Reporting

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**a. Rationale for judgement:** While "between-session habituation" is explicitly described as the primary, pre-specified outcome measure in the abstract and methods, there is insufficient information available (in the article text provided) to confirm that the specific analytic approach and outcome definition (a ≥5-point SUDS drop, chosen from potentially multiple plausible thresholds/metrics) was fixed in a prospectively registered protocol/SAP finalized before unblinded data were available. Without clear confirmation from the trial registration record content that this precise categorical definition and analysis were pre-specified, the possibility of outcome/analysis selection cannot be fully excluded.

**b. Supporting evidence:**
- The manuscript states: "Our primary measure of DCS's exposure-facilitating effect was between-session habituation: whether or not the participants showed less distress during the second speech compared to the first," and later, "Categorical response was defined as a drop of at least 5 points in average SUDS across the exposure across visits." This threshold (5 points) is somewhat arbitrary and not clearly justified or shown to be pre-registered.
- No statistical analysis plan or protocol document is cited in the article itself to confirm this cutoff and analytic approach (chi-square test) were specified prior to unblinded data access.
- Multiple outcome operationalizations were possible (continuous SUDS change via MANCOVA, categorical threshold via chi-square, Social Phobia Scale), and results are reported for all, with the categorical result emphasized as "primary" — this is consistent with pre-specification but cannot be verified from the text alone without the registry record confirming the exact definition and analysis in advance.
- In the absence of confirmatory information from the trial registration content demonstrating a pre-specified analysis plan matching the reported result exactly, a judgement of "Some concerns" (rather than "Low risk") is appropriate per the RoB2 decision algorithm for cases with "no information" on matching pre-specified plans.

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The report states that “**sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random 20 consecutive adults**” to high versus moderate FIO₂. This supports that allocation was intended to be random, but it does not describe how the random sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated list) or whether envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, and opened only after irrevocable enrolment. Thus, allocation concealment cannot be confidently judged adequate.

   Baseline characteristics show some notable numerical differences in this very small trial (10 participants per group), including sex distribution (9/1 versus 5/5), pulmonary artery pressure (25 versus 19 mm Hg), type of surgery, and CPB duration (116 versus 84 min). These differences may plausibly be chance imbalances in such a small sample and do not, by themselves, establish a failure of randomization. However, together with insufficient detail on sequence generation and concealment, they preclude a low-risk judgement.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** This assessment concerns the effect of assignment to high versus moderate FIO₂ after CPB. Participants, anaesthetists, and personnel delivering ventilation were necessarily aware of the assigned oxygen concentration; blinding was not feasible or reported. The intervention itself appears to have been delivered according to assignment: “**After the patient was rewarmed and weaned from the CPB machine, FIO₂ was set to either 1.0 … or 0.35 … with a mean exposure time of 70 min**.”

   The principal concern is the analysis of venous ET-1 data. The report explicitly states: “**Group 2 had an outlier with high arterial ET-1 values … throughout the study… These data were excluded from the statistical analysis in order to simplify statistical interpretation.**” Although described as arterial values, the figure and text concern arterial and mixed-venous ET-1 measurements, and the stated exclusion appears to have removed a randomized participant’s ET-1 data from the analysis. The reason given—no clinical explanation and simplification of interpretation—is not a prespecified or appropriate basis for excluding a participant with observed outcome data.

   Excluding one of ten participants (10%) in a very small trial could substantially affect the estimated between-group effect, particularly because the excluded participant had consistently high ET-1 concentrations and belonged to the moderate-FIO₂ group. The analysis therefore did not appropriately estimate the effect of assignment to intervention in all randomized participants.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** The report describes repeated arterial and mixed-venous blood sampling at prespecified perioperative time points, including “**20 min after CPB had been discontinued**,” and presents ET-1 findings for both groups. There is no indication that samples or venous ET-1 measurements were unavailable because of withdrawal, loss to follow-up, failed sampling, or other missing-data mechanisms.

   The exclusion of the group-2 participant with high ET-1 values is an analysis exclusion despite observed data, rather than missing outcome data. Under RoB 2, this issue is considered under deviations from intended intervention, not under missing outcome data. On the information reported, there is no evidence of material missing venous ET-1 outcome data.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Venous ET-1 was measured using blood from the distal lumen of a pulmonary artery catheter, with laboratory processing described in detail. ET-1 was measured using a “**sandwich-type enzyme immunoassay**,” and “**direct measurements of the peptides were performed in duplicate according to the manufacturer’s instructions**.” The reported assay characteristics include a detection limit and intra- and interassay coefficients of variation.

   This is an objective laboratory outcome, measured using the same sampling approach and assay procedures in both randomized groups at the same scheduled time points. Although clinical personnel could not be blinded to FIO₂ assignment, knowledge of oxygen assignment is unlikely to influence the laboratory ELISA measurement of ET-1. There is no indication that outcome ascertainment differed systematically between groups.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The publication reports ET-1 at several time points and distinguishes arterial from mixed-venous concentrations, but there is no evidence in the provided report of a prospectively finalized statistical analysis plan specifying the primary ET-1 time point, sample type, statistical analysis, handling of outliers, or multiplicity of comparisons before unblinded data were available.

   Multiple potentially eligible results were available: arterial and mixed-venous ET-1, Big ET-1, venous admixture, and several time points (pre-CPB, post-CPB, ICU, and postoperative day 1). The report does not provide clear evidence that the analysed venous ET-1 result was selected from these possibilities according to a prespecified plan. In addition, the post hoc exclusion of an unexplained high ET-1 observation “**to simplify statistical interpretation**” increases concern about data-dependent analytical decisions.

   There is not enough information to conclude that the reported venous ET-1 result was definitely selected on the basis of its statistical significance or direction; therefore, the appropriate RoB 2 judgement is **some concerns**, rather than high risk of bias.

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: Some concerns
b. Rationale: The study authors used sealed envelopes to randomly allocate participants, which provides some allocation concealment. However, there were notable baseline imbalances despite the randomization, particularly regarding baseline pulmonary artery pressure, sex distribution (9/1 vs 5/5), and cardiopulmonary bypass times (116 vs 84 mins). The authors note, "Pulmonary arterial pressure values were not equal in the two groups before CPB, indicating the difficulty of getting a good match in this kind of a study." While in a small trial (n=20) this may be due to chance, it raises some concerns regarding the randomization process and baseline equivalence.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The intervention consisted of adjusting the inspired oxygen fraction (FIO2) while the patients were mechanically ventilated and anesthetized. Therefore, the participants were entirely unaware of the assigned intervention. While the clinical personnel delivering the intervention were aware of the assignment, there is no evidence that this led to deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, all 20 randomized patients appear to have been analyzed in the groups to which they were randomly assigned. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Blood samples for outcome measurements were taken at pre-specified intervals during the procedure and in the intensive care unit. There is no indication of missing data or patient dropout, and data for all 20 randomized participants were included in the analyses.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome (venous concentrations of endothelin-1) is an objective laboratory measure determined using a commercially available sandwich-type enzyme immunoassay and read using a standard ELISA reader. The measurement methods were appropriate and comparable across groups. Even if the laboratory personnel were unblinded, the assessment of this objective, automated outcome is highly unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: Some concerns
b. Rationale: There is no reference to a published protocol or trial registration that was finalized before unblinded data were available. While the reported outcomes (ET-1 and Big ET-1 at specific time points) align with the stated objectives in the methods section, the lack of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan means we cannot completely rule out the possibility of selection of the reported results from multiple eligible analyses or time points.

Based on the provided trial publication and registration, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of progression-free survival (PFS) using the RoB 2.0 tool, assessing the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect):

1. **bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
   b. **Rationale:** The trial used an adequate method for sequence generation and allocation concealment. The publication states, "The random permutation method was used for random assignment" and "Registration and random assignment were coordinated centrally at the KCSG data center." Baseline characteristics were well-balanced between the two groups, indicating no concerns with the randomization process.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
   b. **Rationale:** Although the trial was open-label (participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention), there is no evidence of substantial deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. Crucially, an appropriate analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention. The authors report, "All randomly assigned patients were included in the efficacy analysis according to the intent-to-treat principle."

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
   b. **Rationale:** Data for the primary outcome (PFS) appear to be available for all or nearly all randomized participants. The analysis was based on the intent-to-treat population (N=231), and the number of patient withdrawals was balanced between arms (14 in the maintenance arm and 13 in the observation arm), making it unlikely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value and substantially biased the result.

4. **measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias
   b. **Rationale:** The trial was open-label, meaning outcome assessors were aware of the intervention received. PFS was assessed by the investigators rather than a blinded independent central review ("PFS and the duration of response were assessed by investigators"). Because assessing disease progression using RECIST involves some degree of subjective judgment, the unblinded assessment of the outcome is highly likely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, placing this domain at high risk of bias.

5. **selective reporting: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
   b. **Rationale:** The data were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified plan. The primary outcome in the clinical trial registry (NCT00561119) is listed as "Progression free survival," which directly aligns with the primary endpoint reported in the publication. There is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple analyses of the data on the basis of the results.

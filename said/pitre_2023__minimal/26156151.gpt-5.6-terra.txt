1. **bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Randomization was implemented using an interactive web-based response system and was stratified by participating centre, which provides adequate allocation concealment. The IVUS-guided and angiography-guided groups each contained 201 participants, consistent with the planned 1:1 allocation. Baseline clinical and angiographic characteristics were broadly balanced, with no important imbalance suggesting failure of randomization.

   The report states: “Using an interactive web-based response system, study participants were randomly assigned and stratified by participating center.” It further reports that patients “were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to IVUS or angiography guidance.”

   Baseline characteristics were similar, including age (61.0 versus 61.4 years), sex (80.6% male in both groups), diabetes (34.8% versus 33.8%), prior myocardial infarction (8.0% in both groups), ejection fraction, lesion characteristics, and CTO length. The modest differences in number of diseased vessels are compatible with chance and do not indicate a problem with the randomization process.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided intervention.

   b. **Rationale:** Operators delivering PCI could not realistically be blinded to IVUS versus angiographic guidance. However, the differences in procedural management between groups appear to be consequences of the assigned guidance strategy, rather than non-protocol deviations arising because of the trial context. For example, more high-pressure post-stent dilation occurred in the IVUS group (51.2% versus 41.3%), but this is plausibly part of how IVUS guidance affected procedural decision-making and therefore part of the assigned intervention strategy.

   The trial used an intention-to-treat analysis: “The intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses.” Participants were therefore analysed according to their randomized IVUS-guided or angiography-guided assignment. No post-randomization exclusions from the reported cardiac-death analysis, crossovers, or trial-context-related deviations likely to affect cardiac death were reported.

   Although IVUS devices, timing of IVUS, pullback methods, and related procedural details were “left to the discretion of physicians,” this reflects flexibility within the IVUS-guided strategy rather than a deviation from the intended intervention. The outcome of cardiac death is also unlikely to be materially affected by minor procedural variations unrelated to the assigned strategy.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Follow-up was virtually complete. At 12 months, 200 of 201 participants (99.5%) in the IVUS-guided group and all 201 participants (100%) in the angiography-guided group completed clinical follow-up. Thus, only one randomized participant had missing 12-month follow-up information.

   The report states: “Mean follow-up duration was 359±34 days in the IVUS-guided group and 359±37 days in the angiography-guided group”; and “200 patients of IVUS-guided group (99.5%) and 201 patients (100%) in the angiography-guided group finished 12-month clinical follow-up.”

   For cardiac death, only two events occurred, both in the angiography-guided group. While the outcome is rare, the amount of missing data is exceptionally small, is not meaningfully differential, and there is no indication that the single participant with incomplete follow-up had missingness related to cardiac death. Consequently, missing outcome data are unlikely to have materially biased the estimate.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Cardiac death was prospectively defined using a standard, objective approach, and clinical events were assessed independently by a committee blinded to randomized allocation. Thus, outcome assessment was unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of whether a participant received IVUS-guided or angiography-guided PCI.

   The study defined cardiac death as follows: “All deaths were considered cardiac death unless an unequivocal noncardiac cause could be established.” It also states: “All clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical event committee blinded to randomization.”

   Although classification of cause of death can sometimes require judgement, blinded adjudication and the objective nature of death substantially minimize the potential for differential measurement error. Follow-up procedures were also comparable between groups, with clinic or telephone contacts planned at 1, 3, 9, and 12 months.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The trial registration identifies cardiac death within 12 months as the primary outcome, which is consistent with the published report. The publication also reports cardiac death at the registered 12-month time point: 0/201 in the IVUS-guided group versus 2/201 (1.0%) in the angiography-guided group.

   The registration specifies the primary outcome as: “event rate of cardiac death within 12 months.” The article similarly states: “The prespecified primary end point was the occurrence of cardiac death,” and reports the 12-month result.

   Nevertheless, the available registry information does not provide a detailed, date-stamped statistical analysis plan specifying the precise analysis method, handling of censoring or missing data, and whether alternative eligible analyses or outcome definitions were planned. The report presents Kaplan–Meier/log-rank analyses and does not provide enough pre-specified analytical detail to exclude selection among possible analyses. There is no direct evidence that the reported cardiac-death result was selected based on its findings, but absence of a sufficiently detailed pre-specified analysis plan prevents a low-risk judgement.

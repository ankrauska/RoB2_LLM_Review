# Risk of Bias Assessment: Cancer-DACUS Study
## Outcome: Recurrent Thrombotic Events (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation: The article states "Random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." Using clinical chart number to generate the sequence is unusual and raises questions about whether a true random component was used; this is not a standard random number generation method, and no further detail is provided. This warrants a "Probably no/No information" response — clinical chart numbers may be systematically assigned rather than random.
- Allocation concealment: Sealed envelopes were used with a fixed block size of 10, which is a moderate size, but no information is provided on whether envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, and opened only after irreversible participant enrollment. There is no description of safeguards to prevent prediction of allocation, especially since a small fixed block size (10) is used and multi-center trial (concealment across centers not addressed).
- Baseline imbalance: Table 1 shows groups A1, A2 (and B) were well balanced on all reported baseline characteristics (age, sex, BMI, surgery, chemotherapy, metastasis, site/extent of DVT, laterality), with no P-values <0.05, consistent with chance.
- Because allocation concealment is not clearly described as adequate (no detail on opaque/sealed/sequential envelope procedures) and the sequence generation method (chart number) is questionable, but there is no evidence of baseline imbalance suggesting subversion, this leads to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants and personnel were not blinded: "Patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT." (2.1/2.2 = Yes)
- Given lack of blinding, deviations arising from trial context are plausible. Knowledge of RVT status and treatment assignment (continue vs. stop LMWH) could plausibly affect participant and physician behavior — for example, care-seeking behavior, threshold for reporting symptoms, or physician decisions to investigate/treat suspected recurrence differently by group, since this could affect ascertainment of the primary outcome (2.3 = Probably yes).
- Compliance: The article notes high compliance in Group A1 ("15 of the 119 patients interrupted LMWH for less than 5 days"), suggesting adherence itself was not a major issue; however, this does not eliminate potential trial-context-driven deviations in symptom reporting/behavior due to unblinded status.
- Analysis: "Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis," which is appropriate (2.6 = Yes). However, this only addresses the analysis approach and does not offset the risk introduced by unblinded assessment of a partially subjective/symptom-triggered outcome (recurrent VTE is diagnosed following patient-reported symptoms, then confirmed by imaging), where non-blinding of participants/personnel could unbalance the deviations/behaviors between groups.
- Given lack of blinding, plausible deviations related to trial context that could differentially affect symptom reporting/careseeking triggering diagnostic workup between groups (unbalanced, since the "natural" behavior differs by knowing one is off anticoagulation vs. continuing), and no mitigating strategy described, this domain is judged **High risk of bias**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias

**b. Rationale:**
- The CONSORT flow diagram (Figure 1) and follow-up description indicate essentially complete follow-up: "All enrolled patients were observed for 1 year after LMWH discontinuation... and were seen at their reference center at intervals of 3 months."
- Outcome events are reported for all patients in each group with denominators matching original allocation (22/119, 27/123, 3/105), indicating data availability for essentially all randomized/allocated participants.
- Some patients died during follow-up (competing risk), but these are accounted for, explained ("death... as a result of cancer progression"), and did not differentially affect ascertainment of the thrombotic outcome in a way that suggests missingness related to the true outcome value.
- No substantial differential loss to follow-up between groups is reported; therefore, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias

**b. Rationale:**
- Outcome assessors were not blinded to intervention: participants and physicians were aware of treatment allocation, and initial suspicion of recurrence relied on patient-reported symptoms and physician-initiated work-up ("Patients were instructed to contact the center if symptoms suggestive of VTE or bleeding developed"), meaning the *ascertainment* of the outcome (i.e., who gets tested via C-US) depends on unblinded participants/physicians (4.2/4.3 = Yes).
- Although "All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of the patient's name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment," this blinded adjudication only occurs **after** a case is flagged for suspected recurrence — the initial trigger for testing (symptom reporting, clinical suspicion, decision to perform C-US) is not blinded. This is a classic "diagnostic testing/detection bias" scenario, similar to examples described in RoB2 guidance (e.g., BCG vaccine trials): the intervention group with more contact or different expectations could have differential likelihood of the outcome being *ascertained* even absent true differences.
- Because the initial ascertainment of the outcome could differ between groups due to lack of blinding (patients who stopped LMWH may be more vigilant/anxious about symptoms, or physicians may have a lower threshold to investigate given known cessation of anticoagulation), the measurement/ascertainment of the outcome could plausibly differ between groups (4.2 = Yes), leading to **High risk of bias** for this domain despite blinded adjudication.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registry (NCT00450645) lists the primary outcome as "Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism" and secondary outcomes as "Minor bleeding" and "Death due to cancer" — notably, **major bleeding**, which is reported as the secondary endpoint in the published article ("the secondary end point was major bleeding"), is not explicitly listed as such in the registry (only "minor bleeding" and "death due to cancer" are listed as secondary outcomes in the registry entry provided). This inconsistency between the registered outcomes and the reported outcomes raises some concern about whether outcome definitions/analyses were fully pre-specified as reported.
- However, the primary outcome of interest for this assessment (recurrent VTE/thrombotic events) is consistent between the registry and the publication, and the analysis (time to recurrent VTE, ITT, Cox models) appears to be the single primary analysis reported without evidence of multiple alternative analyses being selectively presented.
- No statistical analysis plan or protocol document (beyond the brief registry summary) was available to fully verify whether the specific analytic approach (e.g., covariates for adjustment: age, sex, previous surgery, metastasis) was pre-specified before unblinded data were available, and enrollment count differs from registry (300 estimated vs. 347 actual), suggesting some post-hoc flexibility in trial conduct/reporting.
- Given the primary outcome result is unlikely to have been selectively chosen from multiple measurements/analyses, but there is incomplete information to fully rule out selective reporting/analysis choices, this domain is judged as **Some concerns**.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias for Recurrent Thrombotic Events: **High risk of bias**

(Driven by "High risk of bias" judgements in both the "Deviations from Intended Interventions" and "Measurement of the Outcome" domains, per RoB 2 rules that a "High" judgement in any domain results in an overall "High" risk of bias.)

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** The study reports that participants were "randomly assigned" to the different diets and supplement groups, but does not provide specific details regarding the method of random sequence generation or whether allocation was adequately concealed. However, baseline characteristics (Table 1) did not differ among the groups, suggesting no major issues with the randomization process itself. Due to the lack of reporting on sequence generation and allocation concealment, a judgement of "some concerns" is appropriate.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The study utilized a placebo-controlled design, providing participants with either fish-oil capsules or visually identical placebo capsules containing olive oil. This indicates that participants, carers, and personnel were likely unaware of the assigned interventions, minimizing the risk of deviations arising from the trial context.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** For the outcome 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a, data were analyzed from all centers. For 8-iso-PGF2a, the authors explicitly state, "Plasma samples from 2 centers (Denmark and Italy) were unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage." Because the reason for the missing outcome data (improper sample handling/storage) is purely logistical and completely unrelated to the true value of the outcome, it does not introduce bias. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The outcomes were measured using a validated radioimmunoassay (RIA) from frozen plasma samples. This is an objective, laboratory-based measurement. Furthermore, since the trial used visually identical placebo capsules, it is highly likely that the laboratory personnel conducting the assays were blinded to the intervention assignments, and the objective nature of the test leaves little room for assessor interpretation to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** While the outcomes (8-iso-PGF2a and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a) are clearly specified in the methods and comprehensively reported in the results (Table 3), there is no mention of a pre-published trial protocol or statistical analysis plan available to confirm that these were the specific pre-planned analyses. Without a time-stamped protocol to rule out selective analysis reporting, there are some concerns regarding selective reporting.

**1. bias due to randomization: high risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The trial used random number tables but the allocation sequence was written in the patient registers in advance, meaning allocation was not concealed.
* **Rationale:** The authors state, "268 three-digit random numbers from the table were written in the patient registers in advance. Patients with even numbers were given PB-MDT and those with odd numbers were given ROM." This open, predictable sequence in a register means recruiters could foresee assignments, completely breaking allocation concealment.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Participants and personnel were not blinded, and the analysis was not based on the intention-to-treat principle, excluding a substantial number of participants.
* **Rationale:** The authors openly state "Blinding to treatment was not undertaken." For the analysis, they did not analyze all randomized participants (134 per group), but rather only those who completed treatment and were followed up ("The cure rate at 2 years was 99% in ROM-6 and 97.0% in PB-MDT group, of those who completed treatment"). Excluding the ~20% of patients who defaulted or were lost to follow-up post-randomization constitutes an inappropriate analysis with potential for a substantial impact on the result.

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** There is a large amount of missing data, and the reasons for missingness are directly related to the outcome.
* **Rationale:** Out of 134 patients randomized to each arm, only 100 in the PB-MDT group and 103 in the ROM-6 group were assessed at 2 years. The authors note that "The reasons for default... included... feeling that they were cured". Because dropping out of the study was driven by patients feeling they were already cured (the outcome of interest), the missingness is very likely to depend on the true value of the outcome. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The outcome assessors were unblinded, and the outcome involves subjective clinical judgement.
* **Rationale:** The trial was open-label. Cure was defined clinically ("complete resolution of the lesion or patch becoming flat, hypopigmented and with decrease in size..."). This is an observer-reported outcome involving judgement. Since assessors were aware of the assigned treatments, the assessment could have been influenced by this knowledge. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** There is no information regarding a pre-specified statistical analysis plan.
* **Rationale:** Without a published protocol or a date-stamped statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinding, it cannot be fully verified if the analysis of cure rates at specific time points was pre-specified. However, the reported outcomes align with standard clinical practice for leprosy trials.

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The study is described as “controlled, randomised” and reports broadly similar baseline characteristics between groups (e.g. mean age 38.4 versus 38.2 years; 55% versus 52% male). There is no apparent baseline imbalance suggesting a failure of randomization.

   However, the publication does not describe how the allocation sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed until enrolment and assignment. In particular, there is no information on use of a computer-generated sequence, random-number table, central randomization, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes. Therefore, although the groups appear balanced, allocation concealment cannot be confirmed. Under RoB 2, lack of information about allocation concealment, with no concerning baseline imbalance, leads to *some concerns*.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention.

   b. **Rationale:** This was explicitly an open-label trial: “This was a single centre, controlled, randomised, open study with two parallel groups.” Thus, participants and personnel delivering vaccination were aware of allocated vaccine. There is no clear evidence that trial-context deviations or co-interventions occurred that would have affected antibody titres; however, reporting is insufficient to fully exclude them.

   More importantly, the result reported for the target outcome is a per-protocol analysis, rather than an intention-to-treat analysis appropriate for the effect of assignment at baseline. The authors state:

   > “Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed.”  
   > “The results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported.”

   The per-protocol immunogenicity analysis excluded six randomized participants: one participant with a previous hepatitis-A booster, one who travelled to India between visits, and four without both pre- and post-vaccination serology. The reported post-booster GMTs are consequently based on 59/64 Vaqta-assigned participants and 62/63 Avaxim-assigned participants, rather than all 127 randomized participants.

   For the assignment effect, exclusion of randomized eligible participants from the analysis is inappropriate. The exclusions were also uneven between groups (five excluded from the Vaqta group versus one from the Avaxim group). In this small trial, this differential post-randomization exclusion could have a substantial effect on the GMT comparison. The unreported ITT result cannot be evaluated. Therefore, the risk of bias due to deviation from the appropriate assignment-effect analysis is high.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Four participants did not have both pre- and post-vaccination serological results available. This represents approximately 3% of the 127 randomized participants. For a continuous outcome such as anti-HAV antibody GMT, outcome data for approximately 97% of randomized participants would generally be considered available for nearly all participants.

   The stated reasons for the other per-protocol exclusions—previous booster vaccination and travel to India—are protocol-related exclusions rather than missing outcome data, and are addressed under deviations from intended intervention. There is no indication that the four missing serological results were missing because of their underlying post-vaccination antibody titres, nor is there evidence of outcome-related withdrawal or differential clinical deterioration. Although the group-specific distribution of the four missing serological results is not reported, the small overall amount of missing outcome data is unlikely by itself to have materially changed the GMT comparison.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Anti-HAV antibody concentration is an objective laboratory outcome. The same quantitative assay was used for both intervention groups at the same scheduled follow-up:

   > “Anti-HAV antibody levels (mIU/mL) were measured in study participants immediately prior to vaccination and 1 month post-vaccination.”

   Serology was performed centrally:

   > “Serological evaluations were performed by Merck Research Laboratories (Pennsylvania, USA).”

   The modified HAVAB assay is described as a quantitative laboratory assay using a WHO standard serum immune globulin calibration curve, with measurement down to 6 mIU/mL. There is no indication that different methods, thresholds, timing, or laboratory procedures were used by vaccine group.

   Although the trial was open-label and the report does not explicitly state that laboratory personnel were blinded, the outcome is generated through a standardized quantitative assay with little scope for assessor judgement. Knowledge of vaccine assignment is therefore unlikely to have influenced the measured antibody titre materially.

5. **selective reporting: high risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** The reported numerical GMT result is based only on the per-protocol population, despite the authors having performed both ITT and per-protocol analyses:

   > “Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed.”  
   > “The results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported.”

   Thus, multiple eligible analyses of the same outcome were undertaken, but only one was fully reported. The decision to report only the per-protocol result was explicitly made after examining the results (“close enough”), rather than being demonstrably specified in a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

   The publication specifies the post-booster GMT as the primary evaluation criterion, but does not provide a prospectively finalized analysis plan defining which analysis population would be primary, nor does it provide the numerical ITT result. The referenced trial-registration information was not available in the material provided here to verify whether the per-protocol-only reporting approach was prospectively specified. Because the reported result was selected from at least ITT and per-protocol analyses after their results were known, this raises a high risk of bias in selection of the reported result.

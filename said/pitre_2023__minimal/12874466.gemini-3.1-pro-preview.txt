**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The trial used an adequate computer-generated randomization sequence and successfully concealed allocation using a third party and identical tablet packs. Baseline characteristics were well balanced between the groups.
b. Rationale: The authors state, "Individuals were randomized by a computer-generated list, which was used for packaging of the tablets and then maintained by a third party that was not involved in the conduction of the study. Marked tablet packs were designated for each patient." Furthermore, baseline characteristics (Table 1) show no significant differences, indicating no evident problems with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants, carers, and investigators were effectively blinded using identical placebo tablets, and an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was utilized.
b. Rationale: The publication confirms blinding: "The appearance, packaging, and labeling of the tablets were identical. Both kinds of tablets had artificial mint flavor to give an identical taste... Both patients and investigators were blinded." Regarding the analysis, the authors state, "Analyses were intention to treat irrespective of adherence to treatment regimen," which is appropriate for assessing the effect of assignment to the intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Missing outcome data were minimal and appropriately accounted for. Patients who stopped the study medication continued to undergo outcome assessments according to the protocol.
b. Rationale: The authors explicitly state, "Although study medication was stopped in these patients, biochemical tests and nutritional assessment were continued for 52 wk according to the study protocol." While there were a few deaths (2 in treatment, 5 in placebo) and transfers (1 in each group), this attrition is expected in this population, and the use of repeated measures ANOVA appropriately handled the longitudinal data. There is no strong evidence that missingness biased the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome (SGA) is a validated tool for this population, and the assessors evaluating the outcome were adequately blinded to the intervention assignment.
b. Rationale: The study states that "SGA was performed at 0, 12, 24, 36, and 52 wk by two trained observers who were blinded from the treatment group allocation and biochemical results of the patients." Because assessors were blinded, the risk of differential measurement error is minimized.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The trial reports the primary outcome (SGA) completely, at all pre-specified time points, and using the pre-specified analysis methods. There is no indication of selective reporting of results.
b. Rationale: SGA was pre-specified as the primary outcome measure for power calculations ("sample size was estimated before the study... with the SGA as the primary outcome measure"). The results fully report the overall SGA scores and its sub-items across all specified follow-up times (Table 3, Figure 2) using the pre-specified repeated measures ANOVA without omissions or unexplained alterations.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Frequency of delivery after 48 hours

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns

**b. Rationale:** The study states "All subjects were divided into two groups by simple random sampling" and later "patients were divided in two groups by simple random sampling." No details are provided about the actual method used to generate the allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random number tables). There is also no description of allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., sealed envelopes, central randomization). Given the phrase "simple random sampling" without further elaboration, this could represent true randomization, but insufficient detail is provided to confirm adequate concealment.

Baseline characteristics (Table 2) show groups were well balanced for parity (P=0.99) and history of preterm labor (P=1), and mean age was similar (P=0.194) — these imbalances are compatible with chance, suggesting no overt problem with randomization. However, Table 3 shows a statistically significant baseline difference in effacement (P<0.0001) between groups (Celecoxib group mean 42 vs. MgSO4 group mean 35.93), which is a key prognostic factor for preterm labor progression and delivery timing. This raises some concern that randomization may not have fully balanced an important prognostic variable, though the direction of this imbalance and its impact on the outcome (delivery after 48h) is unclear given the small P-value is not compatible with chance alone.

Because (1) allocation concealment is not described (No information → treated as a "no information" scenario) and (2) there is a significant baseline imbalance in a key prognostic factor (effacement), the domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," per the algorithm (No information on concealment + baseline imbalance suggestive of a problem → at least "Some concerns").

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns

**b. Rationale:** The paper describes the trial as "single blinded," but does not specify who was blinded (likely only the outcome assessor/statistician or perhaps not even that, given the different routes of administration: "4 grams of MgSO4 intravenously" vs. "100 mg of celecoxib orally"). Given the obviously different administration routes (IV infusion with monitoring for toxicity, deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate vs. oral capsules with monitoring for nausea/skin rash), it is certain that **participants and clinical personnel/carers were aware of the assigned intervention** (2.1 and 2.2 = Yes).

There is no explicit statement of deviations arising from the trial context (e.g., no report of crossover, additional non-protocol interventions, or contamination). The protocol specifies that "if the uterine preterm contractions did not stop with those medications, so immediately drug has been stopped" — this stopping rule was applied to both groups, i.e., a pre-specified protocol procedure, not a deviation arising from trial context or an imbalanced deviation.

No information is provided on whether deviations from intended intervention occurred due to trial context (e.g., differential co-interventions, differential adherence behaviors) — thus we answer "No information" to signalling question 2.3. The analysis appears to be an intention-to-treat-like comparison of the two randomized groups (600 subjects, 300 per group per Table 3), suggesting an appropriate analytic approach (2.6 = Yes).

Per the algorithm: unblinded design (2.1/2.2 = Yes) + No information on trial-context deviations (2.3 = NI) → Default judgement = "Some concerns."

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias

**b. Rationale:** Table 1 and Table 3 indicate that outcome data (delivery before/after 48 hours) were available for all 600 randomized participants — 300 in the MgSO4 group and 300 in the Celecoxib group (matching the "600 pregnant women" and "divided into two groups" description, with Table 1 showing 240+60=300 and 227+30=257... note discrepancy below). Table 1 reports frequencies of 240/60 (=300) for MgSO4 and 227/30 (=257, not 300) for Celecoxib. This suggests a possible small discrepancy in reporting completeness for the Celecoxib group (257 vs. expected 300), but this is not discussed by the authors as missing data due to loss to follow-up; it's more likely a reporting/rounding inconsistency (75.7% of 300 ≈ 227, indicating the percentage was likely calculated correctly against a denominator of 300, but only 257 are shown summed).

There is no mention in the text of participants lost to follow-up, withdrawal, or exclusion after randomization for this specific outcome. Given the outcome (delivery timing) is an objective, easily ascertainable outcome (all women in a hospital setting would have a documented delivery time), and there is no indication that data collection failed for any meaningful subset of participants, this is judged as low risk of bias due to missing outcome data — the apparent numerical discrepancy appears to be a reporting/typographical error rather than evidence of missingness affecting the outcome.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias

**b. Rationale:** The outcome — "frequency of delivery after 48 hours" — is an objective, observer-reported outcome not involving judgement (i.e., recording of the actual clinical event of delivery timing relative to randomization/treatment initiation). This is analogous to a "hard" clinical endpoint (like all-cause mortality) rather than a subjective, participant-reported, or judgement-based outcome.

The method of measuring the outcome (recording actual date/time of delivery relative to enrollment) was not inappropriate (signalling question 4.1 = No), and there is no indication that ascertainment of delivery timing differed between the two intervention groups (4.2 = No) — both groups were monitored in hospital with uterine contractions checked "every 2 hours in 20-minute periods," and delivery date/time is an objective, easily recorded event.

Although the trial was only "single blinded" and treating personnel/participants were aware of group assignment (raising theoretical concern that clinical decision-making around delivery could be influenced by knowledge of treatment), delivery timing itself is a hard endpoint largely driven by physiological progression of labor rather than subjective assessment or clinician discretion in most cases. Therefore, knowledge of intervention assignment is unlikely to have influenced the recording of this specific outcome. This leads to a judgement of "Low risk of bias" for this domain.

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns

**b. Rationale:** No trial registration record, protocol, or statistical analysis plan was provided as a source for this assessment (per the doc_id reference for trial registration, no specific pre-registration details were retrievable/described in the available materials to confirm a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available). The published article does report results for the outcome of "delivery before/after 48 hours" (Table 1) in a manner consistent with what would be expected as a primary/key efficacy outcome for a tocolytic trial, and this outcome is reported using a single, apparently pre-specified definition and single analysis (chi-square test, single time point of 48 hours) — there is no evidence of multiple alternative outcome definitions or analyses being tried and selectively reported for this specific outcome.

However, in the absence of a verifiable trial registry entry or protocol document confirming that this outcome and its analysis were specified in advance of unblinded data availability, there is inherent uncertainty about whether the reported result was selected from among multiple possible analyses (e.g., different time cutoffs, adjusted vs. unadjusted analyses). Given signalling question 5.1 (data analysed in accordance with pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded data available) cannot be answered due to lack of a protocol/registration, and questions 5.2/5.3 cannot be confidently answered "No" without such documentation, this leads to a default judgement of "Some concerns" per the RoB 2 algorithm (No information on multiple signalling questions → Some concerns).

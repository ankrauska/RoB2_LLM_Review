# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment
**Outcome: Change in operation-specific checklist scores**
**Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (baseline)**

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The trial states participants were "randomly assigned to three experimental groups," but no details are provided about the method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number table) or allocation concealment. There is no information on whether the allocation sequence was concealed from those enrolling participants. Baseline (pre-test) performance appears to have been assessed for all participants, and there is no reported evidence of problematic baseline imbalance, but this cannot be confirmed as no baseline comparison data across groups are reported.

**b. Rationale:** "After the pre-test, they were randomly assigned to three experimental groups." No further detail on sequence generation or concealment methods is provided. Because sequence generation and allocation concealment are unreported (No information), but there is no indication of baseline imbalance problems, this leads to a default judgement of "Some concerns" per the RoB2 algorithm (allocation concealment unclear/no information + no evidence of baseline imbalance problem).

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Blinding of participants and personnel to group assignment was not really feasible or necessary in this context, as the "intervention" is a practice schedule (not a clinical treatment participants could have preferences about affecting outcome ascertainment). There is no indication that trial context (e.g., recruitment/engagement effects) caused deviations from the assigned practice schedules. All participants appear to have completed their assigned practice protocol (functional-order, random, or blocked) as described, and an appropriate analysis (comparing groups as randomized, using pre-post difference scores) was used.

**b. Rationale:** "Each group participated in a 60-minute acquisition phase, during which the 5 skills involved in the bone-plating task were practiced in 3 different orders." No dropouts, protocol switching, or non-adherence to assigned practice schedule are reported. The analysis compares participants according to their randomized group assignment ("3 group (Functional-order, Random, Blocked)"), consistent with an intention-to-treat-type approach. Because the intervention is a structured practice schedule delivered under controlled/supervised conditions ("an orthopedic faculty member circulated within the group systematically to provide feedback"), there is little opportunity for participants to deviate from their assigned schedule, and no evidence suggesting such deviations occurred or influenced the outcome.

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The report states that 28 participants were enrolled and assigned to three groups, and outcome data (checklist scores) appear to have been available for all participants across the pre-test, immediate post-test, and delayed post-test, as reflected in the analysis (mixed ANOVAs with group and test-phase factors reported with means/SEs for all three groups at each measure). There is no mention of dropout, withdrawal, or missing data for any participant.

**b. Rationale:** No text in the methods, results, or comments sections reports any participant lost to follow-up, withdrawal, or missing checklist data. Full group summary statistics (M, SE) are reported for all three groups for the checklist measure, suggesting complete outcome data were available for all randomized participants at both post-test time points.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The checklist outcome was assessed by "two experienced orthopedic surgeons" using an operation-specific checklist. There is no explicit statement that these assessors were blinded to group assignment (i.e., which practice schedule each participant had undergone) when scoring the pre-test, immediate post-test, and delayed post-test performances. Because checklist scoring involves some judgement (assessing performance of specific steps), and outcome assessors' awareness of group assignment could plausibly influence scoring (particularly given the assessors were institutionally embedded orthopedic faculty, potentially involved in the practice sessions), there is a risk that assessment could have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. However, there is no strong indication that assessors had a vested interest or strong prior expectation about which schedule was superior that would bias scoring in a particular direction.

**b. Rationale:** "Two experienced orthopedic surgeons used 4 separate methods of evaluation during the pre-test and the 2 post-tests." No statement is made regarding blinding of these evaluators to participants' group assignment. Given the checklist involves judgement-based scoring of "detailed, operation-specific procedures," and blinding status is not reported (No information on blinding of assessors), but there's no clear evidence that this likely introduced substantial bias (no obvious differential motivation), the judgement defaults to "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** No trial registration or pre-specified statistical analysis plan was located/available (per the provided trial registration document, doc_id: 4dab5cab-2b44-42f8-8559-2dd9073b18b5, no matching registration entry could be confirmed for this specific study). The publication reports results for multiple outcome measures (checklists, final product analysis, global ratings, drilling times) and for multiple time points (immediate and delayed post-test), all of which appear to be reported in full with results for all three groups, regardless of statistical significance. There is no indication of selective reporting of only favorable checklist results, and the checklist outcome findings are reported in a manner consistent with what would be expected in the methods section. However, because there is no accessible pre-registered protocol or SAP confirming the analysis plan was finalized prior to unblinded data availability, we cannot fully verify the absence of selective reporting.

**b. Rationale:** "The difference scores for the practice-specific checklists, final product analysis, global ratings, and drilling times were analyzed in 4 separate mixed analyses of variance (ANOVAs)." All four outcome measures' results are reported in the Results section, including non-significant findings (e.g., global ratings: P=.13; drilling times: P=.14), suggesting no evidence of selective suppression of unfavorable results for the checklist outcome specifically. Lacking access to a trial registry/protocol pre-dating unblinded analysis, the judgement defaults to "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

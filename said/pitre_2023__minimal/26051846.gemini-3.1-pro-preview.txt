1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The random sequence was generated adequately and baseline characteristics were well-balanced.
       b. Rationale: The study states, "Each participant was assigned to one treatment group, at random, using a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method, applied by a statistician." Additionally, the statistician was independent of data collection, and Table 1 shows no significant differences in baseline characteristics between the groups.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: There is no evidence of deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context, and analysis appears to follow intention-to-treat principles.
       b. Rationale: While participants and physiotherapists were likely aware of the assigned interventions (due to the physical differences between ultrasound and iontophoresis treatments), there is no evidence that this led to non-protocol deviations. The study analyzed all 99 randomized participants in their assigned groups.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all or nearly all participants.
       b. Rationale: The study enrolled 99 participants and the results tables indicate that data for all 99 participants were analyzed, with no mention of loss to follow-up or missing data at the post-treatment assessment.

4. measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The outcome is self-reported by participants who were likely aware of the intervention they received.
       b. Rationale: Pain is a subjective, participant-reported outcome (VAS). While the study states it was "single-blind" and that visits were attended by a "blinded rehabilitation doctor," the participants themselves could not reasonably be blinded to the distinct modalities (e.g., ultrasound vs. iontophoresis). Because the participants are the outcome assessors for a self-reported pain scale, their knowledge of the intervention received is likely to have influenced the assessment of the outcome. 

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
       a. Judgement: The reported outcomes match those pre-specified in the trial registration.
       b. Rationale: The trial registration (NCT01748188) lists the primary outcome as "Pain measure by the visual analogic scale pain score" and secondary outcomes as "Functionality of the joint by Dash and Constant scores." The published article reports precisely these pre-specified outcomes at the planned time points, indicating no selective reporting.

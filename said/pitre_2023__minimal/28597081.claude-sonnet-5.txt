# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0 Tool
## Outcome: Acoustic Reaction Time

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The allocation sequence was randomly generated using an appropriate method with adequate concealment, and there is no evidence of baseline imbalance suggesting problems with randomization.

**b. Rationale:** The trial used "permuted block randomization with randomly varying block sizes of 3, 6, and 9" generated via an online tool (www.randomization.com), satisfying the criterion for random sequence generation. Allocation concealment was adequate: "The list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered sealed opaque envelopes." This meets the minimal criteria for allocation concealment (sequentially numbered, sealed envelopes handled by an independent party). Baseline characteristics (Table 1, VO2max, age) appear comparable across groups (median VO2max 64, 60, 62 ml/kg/min for BRIGHT, BLUE, CONTROL respectively), with no indication of problematic imbalance. Two participants were excluded post-randomization, but this was due to "invalid measurements of peak oxygen uptake" in the fitness test (an eligibility-related/technical issue unrelated to the reaction time outcome or intervention assignment), and exclusion occurred before any outcome data were available, not based on results.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns. Participants and personnel were not blinded, and although the trial context (light exposure itself) is the intervention, there is a specific concern: the actual light intensity delivered deviated substantially and inconsistently from the intended/protocol allocation, which constitutes a deviation from intended intervention delivery that could affect the outcome, though it appears unlikely to be differentially motivated by trial context in a directional way affecting reaction time.

**b. Rationale:** The masking module explicitly states: "masking: NONE." The article confirms limited blinding: "The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to the light condition... Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to, but not the other two light conditions" — meaning participants were NOT blinded to their own assignment. This satisfies signalling question 2.1 (Yes, participants aware) and 2.2 (personnel/experimenter partially aware, though the supervisor for testing was blinded).

Critically, the authors report substantial unintended deviations in intervention delivery: "The large differences between the intended light exposure and the actual mean light intensity participants were exposed to during the 60 min of the intervention were unexpected... there was a large cross-over in light intensities between BRIGHT and BLUE and between BLUE and CONTROL." Actual intensities were: BRIGHT 1326 lx (intended ~4400 lx), BLUE 203 lx (intended ~230 lx), CONTROL 115 lx (intended ~230 lx). This represents a failure to implement the intervention as intended, arising from the trial context (positioning/gaze variability), and this deviation was substantial and could have affected the outcome (dose-response for reaction time was tested but non-significant).

However, the analysis used was intention-to-treat (ITT) by original group assignment (all 72 analyzed by original allocation, with covariate adjustment via ANCOVA), which is appropriate for the effect of assignment to intervention. The authors also conducted supplementary dose-response analyses, but the primary between-group comparison was appropriately analyzed. Because the deviations (variable light intensity reaching the eyes) were not clearly balanced/unbalanced in a way likely to bias the specific comparison in a particular direction, and no non-protocol interventions or lack of blinding-driven behavioral change were reported for reaction time specifically, this is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Outcome data were available for all analyzed participants (72/72) with no reported missingness in reaction time measurements.

**b. Rationale:** "The remaining 72 participants had a median age of 23 years" and results are reported for reaction time in all three groups (24 in BRIGHT, 24 in BLUE, 24 in CONTROL) both before and after light exposure (Table 2). There is no mention of any participant failing to complete the reaction time task or having missing data for this specific outcome. The two exclusions (from 74 to 72) occurred prior to randomization-related outcome assessment, due to invalid VO2peak measurements identified in the eligibility/fitness testing phase, unrelated to the reaction time outcome itself, and equally distributed as a pre-analysis exclusion not tied to differential follow-up.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns. The outcome assessment method itself was appropriate and objective (computer-based reaction time task), and the test supervisor was blinded; however, participants were aware of their allocated light condition during testing, and reaction time is a performance outcome that could plausibly be influenced by participant awareness/expectation (e.g., motivation, alertness expectation effects), even though it is a relatively objective, computer-recorded measure with limited scope for conscious manipulation.

**b. Rationale:** The measurement method was appropriate: "participants performed a computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task" measuring response to auditory cues via keypress — an objective, automated measure with a pre-specified lapse-exclusion rule (>500ms). This is not equivalent to a subjective participant-reported outcome. The outcome assessor here is best characterized as a hybrid: it's an automated/observer-reported outcome not involving judgement (timing recorded electronically), which would typically be at low risk regardless of blinding status of participants.

However, "The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to the light condition" — supporting low risk for assessor-related bias, since the assessor (equipment/supervisor) was blinded. But participants themselves, who are integral to producing the reaction time response, "only knew the light condition they were exposed to." Since reaction time performance can be influenced by expectation/effort (a quasi-behavioral component), and given the awareness of assignment, there remains a modest possibility that knowledge of intervention influenced motivation/effort during the task, justifying "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," even though it is not a highly subjective self-report outcome and no evidence suggests likely strong influence occurred.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The outcome, its measurement method, and analysis approach are consistent with pre-specification in the trial registry, and results are reported in full accordance with the pre-specified analysis plan.

**b. Rationale:** The trial registry (NCT02203539) pre-specifies "Reaction time in milliseconds (ms)" as a secondary outcome, matching exactly the outcome and metric reported in the article (median of all reaction times, in ms). The article explicitly states: "All statistical methods used to compare the groups for reaction time and handgrip strength in the present report were pre-specified and registered. Analyses of covariance were used to compare reaction time and handgrip strength after the light exposure between the three groups adjusted for reaction time and handgrip strength before the light exposure." This matches the reported analysis exactly (ANCOVA adjusting for baseline). Only one outcome measurement (median reaction time from a single simple acoustic reaction time task) and one pre-specified analysis method were used and reported — no alternative scales, time points, or unreported alternative analyses are evident. Additional dose-response regression analyses were also reported transparently as supplementary/exploratory, not selectively substituted for the primary comparison. There is no indication of selective reporting based on the direction or significance of results.

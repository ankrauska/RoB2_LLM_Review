**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: The study used an adequate random sequence generation method and successfully concealed the allocation sequence. The authors state, "Participants were allocated randomly to bright light (BRIGHT), monochromatic blue light (BLUE), or a control condition (CONTROL) using an online tool (available at www.randomization.com) which applies permuted block randomization with randomly varying block sizes of 3, 6, and 9. The list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered sealed opaque envelopes." There were no substantial baseline imbalances reported that would suggest a problem with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: While participants were aware of the light condition they were exposed to (though unaware of the other conditions), the test supervisor was blinded: "The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to the light condition each participant was exposed to." The intervention was a single 60-minute light exposure in a controlled laboratory setting, so deviations from the intended intervention arising because of the trial context are highly unlikely. Furthermore, an appropriate analysis strategy was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: Of the 74 randomized participants, two were excluded retrospectively due to invalid measurements at screening ("Two subjects in BRIGHT and CONTROL had to be excluded due to invalid measurements of peak oxygen uptake in the cardiorespiratory fitness test."). The remaining 72 participants completed the study and were included in the analysis. Outcome data were available for all or nearly all eligible randomized participants.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: The outcome, acoustic reaction time, was measured objectively using a computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task ("participants performed a computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task"). Although participants were aware of the light exposure, the test supervisor was blinded, and it is highly unlikely that the objective measurement of acoustic reaction time by a computer could have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: The outcome was pre-specified in the trial registration (NCT02203539) as a secondary outcome ("Reaction time in milliseconds (ms)"). The trial report explicitly states that the analysis methods were pre-specified: "All statistical methods used to compare the groups for reaction time and handgrip strength in the present report were pre-specified and registered." There is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple eligible analyses based on the results.

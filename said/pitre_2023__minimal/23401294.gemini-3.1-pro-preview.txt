**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The trial used a random component for sequence generation (picking names from a bag), but the method of allocation concealment is not described in sufficient detail to ensure the sequence was fully concealed until assignment. 
* **Rationale:** The study states, "The randomization procedure was to pick the name of the orthosis from a bag including the names of both orthoses." This method does not guarantee that the person recruiting could not see or predict the next allocation. Baseline characteristics did not show significant differences, though the sample size was small. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Although participants and personnel could not be blinded to the orthosis worn, the study assessed the immediate effects of wearing the orthoses, leaving little room for deviations from the intended interventions that arise because of the trial context. All randomized participants were analyzed in their assigned groups.
* **Rationale:** The study evaluates the "immediate effect of wearing Spinomed orthosis and the PTS on postural balance". Participants wore the orthoses for 1.5 hours to adapt before testing. There is no evidence of non-protocol interventions or other deviations affecting the results.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants.
* **Rationale:** The trial recruited a total of 18 participants (10 allocated to the Spinomed group and 8 to the PTS group). The results tables (Table 1 and Table 2) show that all 18 participants were included in the analysis (n=10 and n=8 respectively), indicating no loss to follow-up or missing outcome data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The outcome was measured using an automated computerized system, so the assessment is unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, despite the lack of blinding.
* **Rationale:** Postural balance was assessed using the sensory organization test (SOT) via the "NeuroCom EquiTest system according to the manufacturer’s protocol." As this relies on force plates and computerized data collection, the lack of assessor blinding is unlikely to influence the balance score.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The reported outcomes correspond to the prespecified methods in the study report, and there is no indication that the results were selected from multiple analyses or outcome measurements.
* **Rationale:** The methods section clearly defines the variables to be measured (balance score, directional control, and reaction time), and results for all of these parameters are fully reported in the results section and tables.

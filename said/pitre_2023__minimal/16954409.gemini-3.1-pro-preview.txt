Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome clinical pregnancy rate:

1. bias due to randomization: some concerns
    a. Judgement: some concerns
    b. Rationale: The trial used "computer-generated random numbers" for sequence generation, which is adequate. However, for allocation concealment, they used "sealed envelopes by specialist nurse." The report does not specify whether the envelopes were opaque or sequentially numbered, which raises some concerns about whether allocation could have been foreseen. Baseline characteristics appear balanced, but the lack of detail on the concealment method warrants some concerns.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: low risk of bias
    b. Rationale: The study was open-label, meaning participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention. However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context. Furthermore, the analysis included all randomized participants (81 in Group I, 80 in Group II, and 80 in Group III) in their originally assigned groups, representing an appropriate intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis. 

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: low risk of bias
    b. Rationale: Outcome data (clinical pregnancy) were available for all 241 randomized participants. No loss to follow-up or exclusions were reported in the analysis of the primary outcome, meaning the result was not biased by missing outcome data.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: low risk of bias
    b. Rationale: The outcome was clinical pregnancy, documented by transvaginal ultrasound at 6 weeks. While the outcome assessors were likely aware of the intervention received (due to lack of blinding), clinical pregnancy via ultrasound is an objective outcome that does not involve subjective judgment. Therefore, it is highly unlikely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

5. selective reporting: some concerns
    a. Judgement: some concerns
    b. Rationale: Clinical pregnancy rate is clearly stated as the primary outcome in the methodology and is reported thoroughly in the results. However, without access to a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a date-stamped trial registry entry finalized before unblinded outcome data were available, we cannot definitively rule out selection of the reported result from multiple eligible analyses.

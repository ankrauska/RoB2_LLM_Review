**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The trial report states that students were "randomised with a randomisation table", which indicates an adequate method of random sequence generation. However, there is no information provided about whether the allocation sequence was concealed. Furthermore, there were significant baseline imbalances between the groups regarding previous work experience and voluntary work experience (Table 2), raising some concerns about the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: Given the nature of the educational interventions (tutorial strategies), it is highly unlikely that students and tutors were blinded to the assigned intervention. While there is no direct evidence that deviations from the intended interventions occurred due to the trial context, the lack of blinding and lack of explicit confirmation of an intention-to-treat analysis (though all 144 randomized participants appear to have been analyzed) lead to some concerns.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Outcome data appear to be available for all randomized participants. A total of 144 students were randomized (82 in Trento and 62 in Udine), and the results in Phase 2 report data for exactly 144 students (41 + 41 + 23 + 39). Therefore, there is no risk of bias due to missing outcome data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome was the number of mistakes made in diagnosing problems. The study explicitly states that "Two researchers did blind evaluation of the problems individualised by the students and these were compared to the ‘standard’," and "The data were processed blind; only after this had been done were the identities of the Schools (Trento and Udine) revealed." Because the outcome assessors were blinded, the assessment is unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: There is no indication that the trial was pre-registered or that a pre-specified statistical analysis plan was finalized before unblinded data were available. Additionally, the authors note that "The median values of error in Phases 1 and 2 were regarded as reference ‘cut-off’ points to estimate the differences within campuses and between the groups," which suggests that the categorization of the outcome (wrong diagnostic hypotheses above or below the median) may have been data-driven rather than pre-specified, raising some concerns about selective reporting of analyses.

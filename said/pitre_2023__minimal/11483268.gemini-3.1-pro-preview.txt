Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance and the provided trial document, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) 1 month post-vaccination:

    1. bias due to randomization: some concerns
           a. Judgement: The trial is described as randomized, but there is no information on the method used for sequence generation or allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics were generally balanced, though there was a non-significant difference in pre-booster GMT. Due to the lack of information on allocation concealment, there are some concerns.
           b. Rationale: The publication states it was a "randomised, open study" but does not detail the randomization process (e.g., computer-generated sequence, sealed envelopes). Table 1 and Table 2 show comparable demographic details and pre-booster seroprotection rates, suggesting no major baseline imbalances that would indicate a failure of randomization.

    2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns
           a. Judgement: The study was open-label, meaning participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention. However, there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions occurred due to the trial context. The trialists only reported the per-protocol analysis, not the intention-to-treat analysis, which raises some concerns, though the number of excluded participants was very small.
           b. Rationale: The trial is described as an "open study". Regarding the analysis, the authors state: "Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed... The results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported." Six subjects out of 127 were excluded from the per-protocol analysis. Because the reported results are per-protocol rather than intention-to-treat, the analysis is technically inappropriate for the effect of assignment, though the potential impact on the result is minimal given the small number of exclusions (<5%).

    3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, and the small amount of missing data is unlikely to have biased the results.
           b. Rationale: Of the 127 randomized subjects, 121 were included in the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis. Only 4 subjects lacked post-vaccination serological results, and 2 were excluded for protocol violations. This represents approximately 5% missing or excluded data, which is small enough that it would not have a substantial impact on the estimated intervention effect.

    4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: While the study was open-label, the outcome is an objective laboratory measurement (anti-HAV antibody GMT) that is unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
           b. Rationale: The outcome was measured via a standard serological assay (modified HAVAB assay). According to RoB 2, for observer-reported outcomes that do not involve judgment (e.g., an automated test or objective biological assay), the assessment is unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, even if the trial is unblinded.

    5. selective reporting: some concerns
           a. Judgement: There is no reference to a pre-published protocol or statistical analysis plan available to confirm pre-specified analysis intentions. However, the reported outcome (GMT at 1 month) is a standard and expected measure for this type of vaccine trial.
           b. Rationale: Without a trial registration or protocol to verify against, it is difficult to completely rule out selective reporting. However, there is no clear evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on the results.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: The sequence generation was random, and allocation was adequately concealed. Baseline characteristics did not show important imbalances. The authors state, "Procedure type was assigned by a computer-generated randomization list, and concealment of randomization was done by telephone inquiry."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: Although participants and personnel were likely unblinded due to the nature of the surgical interventions, there is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context. It appears that all randomized patients were analyzed for the length of hospital stay outcome.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: Data for the length of hospital stay appear to be available for all 48 randomized participants, as reflected in the results table reporting median and interquartile ranges for both groups.

**4. measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: High risk of bias.
b. Rationale: Participants and caregivers were aware of the assigned intervention due to the obvious physical differences between abdominal and laparoscopic surgery. The study states that "Each patient decided for herself when she was ready to be discharged from hospital." Since the decision was subjective and made by unblinded patients, the assessment of this outcome was highly likely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, as patients generally expect a faster recovery and earlier discharge after laparoscopic procedures. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: Length of hospital stay was pre-specified as the primary outcome measure in the trial's methods ("We defined the primary outcome measure as length of hospital stay... we calculated that we would need a sample size of 20 cases per group"). The result is unlikely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on the statistical findings.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The allocation sequence generation and concealment were appropriate. The paper states, "The randomisation list and the study medication were administered by the Charité, Universitätsmedizin, Berlin, Germany," while the study was conducted at the University of Göttingen. This external administration ensures adequate allocation concealment. Although there are some baseline imbalances (e.g., age at onset), these are expected due to the very small sample size (13 patients) and are compatible with chance.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The trial was double-blind and placebo-controlled. Participants, carers, and personnel were unaware of the intervention assignments, which prevents deviations from the intended intervention that could arise from the trial context. Although one patient dropped out and was excluded from the analysis (modified ITT), the double-blind nature minimizes the risk of bias for the ITT effect. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** Survival time data were available for almost all randomized participants. Out of 13 randomized patients, 12 were included in the final survival analysis ("One patient who was randomised to the placebo group dropped out, so that 12 patients with sCJD were included in the reported survival analysis"). Although missingness in one patient could theoretically be related to their health status, the proportion of missing data (1 out of 13) is small enough that it is unlikely to introduce substantial bias into the survival analysis. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The outcome is survival time (death), which is a definitive, objective event. Furthermore, the trial was double-blinded, so knowledge of the intervention received could not have influenced the ascertainment of death. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** Survival time was pre-specified as the primary endpoint in the trial registry/protocol ("Der primäre Zielparameter soll die Überlebenszeit der Patienten von Symptombeginn an sein"). The authors reported the survival analysis appropriately using a Cox proportional hazard model as intended, without evidence of selecting the result from multiple eligibility measurements or analyses to favor a particular finding.

# RoB 2.0 Assessment: KANWU Study – Outcome: Changes in 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α

**Preliminary considerations:** This is a 2×2 factorial, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (SAFA vs. MUFA background diet; fish-oil vs. placebo capsules). The effect of interest assessed here is the effect of **assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect), per instructions.

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The report states only that participants "were randomly assigned" to diet and, within each diet group, to fish-oil or placebo capsules. No description is provided of the method used to generate the allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number tables) or of the mechanism used to conceal allocation from those enrolling participants (e.g., central randomization, sequentially numbered sealed containers). This corresponds to "No information" for signalling questions 1.1 and 1.2.

Baseline characteristics (Table 1: age, BMI, cholesterol, blood pressure, glucose) appear reasonably similar across the four groups, with no obvious signs of a severe imbalance suggestive of a failed randomization process. However, in the absence of any reported methodological detail for sequence generation and allocation concealment, and absent statistical comparison of baseline characteristics, a judgement of "Low risk" cannot be justified. Per the RoB2 algorithm, "No information" on both sequence generation and concealment, combined with an absence of evidence of problematic imbalance, defaults to "Some concerns."

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** This was a double-blind trial: "supplements of capsules containing fish-oil…or to placebo capsules with the same amount of olive oil." Because capsules of similar appearance were used, participants, carers, and those delivering the intervention were likely unaware of allocation status (2.1/2.2 = probably no). There is no indication in the report of contamination, non-protocol interventions, or trial-context–driven deviations affecting the outcome; participants were also instructed to maintain usual lifestyle/physical activity and avoid additional supplements or aspirin, reducing likelihood of differential co-intervention. 

Regarding analysis, the numbers analyzed for each outcome (SAFA+placebo n=42; SAFA+n-3 n=41; MUFA+placebo n=40; MUFA+n-3 n=39) match the numbers randomized as reported for baseline characteristics, and the statistical model described ("treatment effects were estimated from a statistical model in which treatment categories … were analyzed") is consistent with an intention-to-treat-type approach, without exclusion of eligible randomized participants based on adherence. No naïve per-protocol or as-treated analysis was reported. Based on this, the domain is judged low risk.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** For 8-iso-PGF2α specifically, the authors explicitly report a substantial and non-random loss of outcome data: "Plasma samples from 2 centers (Denmark and Italy) were unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage." This means that of the five original KANWU centers, participants from two entire centers (a potentially large fraction of the original 162 participants) could not be included in the 8-iso-PGF2α analysis, and this is not reflected in the sample sizes given in Table 3 (only 162 total shown, suggesting the reported N already excludes these centers without a clear accounting of how many were originally randomized at those two centers).

While the stated reason (technical/handling and storage issues) is plausibly unrelated to the true value of the outcome (suggesting missingness may be "at random" with respect to outcome), there is no information provided on: (1) how many participants were originally allocated at the Danish/Italian centers, (2) whether attrition/missingness differed between the fish-oil and placebo arms within the analyzed centers, or (3) any sensitivity analysis addressing the potential impact of this loss. Given the lack of a CONSORT flow diagram and lack of information about the balance of missingness between intervention arms, and the fact that the exclusion was substantial (up to 2/5 centers), this raises concern, though the explanation is not intrinsically related to outcome values.

For 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α, the report states that it was analyzed in plasma "from all centers," implying more complete data for this specific outcome. However, because this domain assessment is being made jointly for both outcomes as requested, and because the loss of an entire subset of data for one of the two co-primary outcomes (8-iso-PGF2α) is substantial and insufficiently documented, the overall judgement is "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Both outcomes were measured using previously validated radioimmunoassays (RIA) with clearly reported cross-reactivity and detection limits: "The plasma levels of 8-iso-prostaglandin F2α were analyzed…by using a validated RIA developed by Basu," and "The levels of 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α were analyzed in plasma from all centers using a validated RIA developed by Basu." These are objective, laboratory-based biochemical assays not requiring subjective judgement by an assessor aware of allocation status; such outcome ascertainment is classified as an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" (akin to an automated/objective laboratory test). Measurement methods were applied identically across intervention groups (i.e., no indication that ascertainment differed by group), and given the double-blind design, laboratory personnel conducting the assay were very unlikely to know participants' treatment allocation, and even if not literally blinded, knowledge of allocation would be unlikely to influence an automated immunoassay result. Therefore, the outcome measurement method is appropriate, non-differential, and unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention received.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** No trial registration record or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is cited or available in the article to confirm that the analyses of 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α reported here were the sole prespecified analyses (i.e., there is no evidence of a public registry entry predating unblinded outcome data). The Methods section does specify in advance a single defined outcome measurement approach for each biomarker (single RIA assay, single timepoint at 3 months, single change-score analysis) and states that "the main outcome variables were the changes in 8-iso-prostaglandin F2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α," suggesting these were intended a priori as primary outcomes, and only one plausible analytic approach (mixed-model adjusting for center, age, sex, baseline value) was used and reported for each. There is no evidence of selection among multiple competing outcome measurements or multiple analytic methods for these two outcomes specifically. However, because no trial registry entry or protocol/SAP could be located/verified to confirm this a priori specification (search of the referenced trial-registration doc did not yield a matching, date-stamped protocol for these exact biomarkers), and because other outcomes in this paper (e.g., tocopherols, uric acid, AOC) show evidence of multiple sub-analyses and selective emphasis in the Discussion, some uncertainty remains about whether the reported isoprostane/PGF2α results were selected from a larger set of unreported analyses (e.g., other time points, other correction methods). This leads to a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

**Rationale:** No domain was judged to be at high risk of bias; however, three domains (randomization process, missing outcome data, and selective reporting) were judged to raise "some concerns," principally due to incomplete reporting of randomization/allocation-concealment methods, substantial and incompletely characterized missing data for the 8-iso-PGF2α outcome (loss of two entire study centers), and the absence of a verifiable pre-registered analysis plan. Per RoB 2 guidance, this pattern of multiple "some concerns" domains, while not reaching the threshold of "high risk" in any single domain, is sufficient to lower confidence in the result, warranting an overall judgement of **"Some concerns"** for this outcome.

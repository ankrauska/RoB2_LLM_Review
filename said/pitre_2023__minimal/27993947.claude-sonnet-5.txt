# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Shared Decision-Making Score

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The allocation sequence was randomly generated: "Randomization was performed by means of a computer-generated random sequence with stratification for age, gender, and positive family history for CHD using the Pocock and Simon method." The randomization was also concealed from investigators until after enrollment: "Once a study participant completed the first study visit and scheduled the second study visit, he/she was randomized." One investigator (HJ) generated the sequence independently, reducing risk of foreknowledge affecting enrollment decisions. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) were well balanced between groups (age, sex, smoking status, family history, BMI, blood pressure, lipid levels, education), with all p-values >0.19, consistent with chance variation and no evidence of a problem with randomization.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** This trial was explicitly unblinded ("maskingInfo": "NONE" in the registry), and by necessity, participants and personnel (genetic counselors and physicians) were aware of the intervention assignment (CRS vs. CRS*GRS), since the disclosure of genetic risk information *is* the intervention itself: "A genetic counselor disclosed this information followed by a physician visit for SDM regarding statin therapy." Awareness of allocation is unavoidable given the nature of the intervention (disclosure of genetic risk information cannot be blinded). However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention occurred due to the trial context that would differentially affect the SDM outcome. The same decision aid, scripted risk-disclosure language ("Out of 100 people like you…"), and process were used in both arms: "A novel decision aid was used in both encounters to disclose the CHD risk estimates and facilitate SDM regarding statin use." The analysis appears consistent with intention-to-treat principles (207 of 216 randomized participants included; only 9 withdrew prior to the second visit, evenly distributed as shown in Figure 4). Because unblinding was structurally necessary and the standardized protocol/decision aid minimized differential deviations, but the awareness of assignment remains a source of potential (unquantified) influence on the subjectively measured SDM outcome, this domain is judged as some concerns rather than low risk.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants. The SDM survey was completed by 206 of 207 randomized participants who completed the second visit ("The SDM survey was completed by 206 study participants"), and the OPTION5 video analysis was based on a matched random subsample (40 per group) of the 187 consenting encounters, described as "age- and sex-matched," minimizing risk of differential missingness. Of the 216 enrolled, 9 withdrew prior to randomization/second visit (Figure 4), but this occurred before allocation-related SDM assessment could apply, and dropout appears balanced and unrelated to the SDM outcome itself (early withdrawal, unrelated to receiving risk disclosure). No differential reasons for missingness related to intervention group are reported. The extremely small amount of missing data (1/207 for the SDM-Q) could not plausibly alter the result.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The SDM score is a participant-reported outcome, assessed via the validated SDM-Q survey completed by study participants themselves. Per RoB 2 guidance, for participant-reported outcomes the outcome assessor is the participant, and since participants were necessarily aware of which type of risk information (conventional vs. genetic) they received, "the assessment of outcome is potentially influenced by knowledge of intervention received, leading to a judgement of at least 'Some concerns'." There is no indication that participants' perception of the encounter/self-report of SDM was manipulated or systematically distorted by knowledge of allocation, and the direction of any bias is unclear/unpredictable — the study hypothesis could plausibly cut either way (genetic information could be perceived as either enhancing or complicating the discussion). The measurement instrument (SDM-Q) itself is validated and identically administered in both arms, and the corroborating video-based OPTION5 assessment (performed by an independent evaluator, TSM, presumably not blinded either, but using a more objective observer-rated scale) yielded consistent results, somewhat mitigating concern. However, because the primary self-reported outcome cannot be fully protected from potential subjective influence of intervention awareness, this domain is judged as some concerns rather than low risk.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** "Shared decision making (as assessed by survey)" is explicitly pre-specified as a secondary outcome in the trial registry (NCT01936675), registered September 3, 2013, prior to completion of recruitment (October 2013–May 2014). The reported outcome measure (SDM-Q total score, individual item-level responses, and OPTION5 video analysis) matches the registered outcome and is reported in full, including all 11 individual SDM-Q items (Table 2) and the composite score, without evidence of selective reporting of only favorable items or timepoints. The manuscript reports both a comprehensive item-by-item breakdown and the overall score, with results consistent between the self-report and independent video-coded (OPTION5) analyses, suggesting no selective reporting of only the more favorable measurement method. There is no evidence of switched primary/secondary outcomes or omission of pre-specified analyses for this outcome.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Clinical Pregnancy Rate (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The trial used adequate random sequence generation but inadequate allocation concealment, leading to a judgement of "Some concerns."

**b. Rationale:** 
- Sequence generation: "Two hundred and forty-one patients were prospectively randomized using computer-generated random numbers into three groups" — this satisfies criteria for random sequence generation (Yes).
- Allocation concealment: "Women were allocated randomly to Group I, Group II or Group III on the day of the 2nd or 3rd day of menstruation with sealed envelopes by specialist nurse." There is no mention of the envelopes being sequentially numbered, opaque, or tamper-proof. This falls short of the extended criteria for adequate concealment (Table 2), and does not meet minimal criteria with confidence. This raises concern that allocation could have been foreseen or manipulated (answer: Probably No/No information → "No information" leaning toward "Probably No").
- Baseline characteristics (Table I) show no significant differences between groups in age, duration of infertility, BMI — consistent with successful randomization (no evidence of problem, i.e., "No" to imbalance question).
- Per the algorithm (Table 4): Random=Yes, Concealment=Probably No/No information, Imbalance=No → maps to "Some concerns" (allocation concealment not clearly adequate, despite random sequence and no baseline imbalance).

---

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention (Effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Participants and personnel were not blinded (open-label, given different drug preparations with different administration/monitoring), but there is no clear evidence that trial-context-driven deviations from intended intervention affected the outcome; however, there is insufficient information to fully rule out concerns, and analysis approach is not fully specified as ITT.

**b. Rationale:**
- The study was not blinded: participants received different named products (Gonal-F, Metrodin-HP, Pergonal) with dosing decisions and monitoring performed by treating physicians who were aware of group assignment ("Stimulation of the cycles was monitored by serum estradiol (E2) level and transvaginal sonography, and gonadotrophin doses were adjusted individually"). This implies both participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention (Yes to signalling questions 2.1/2.2).
- There is no explicit statement that deviations from the protocol (e.g., non-protocol interventions, additional co-interventions) occurred because of trial context. The protocol appears to have been followed uniformly (cycle cancellation criteria, hCG trigger criteria, insemination timing were pre-specified and applied equally): "Patients with decreasing E2 levels or more than three follicles of ≥16 mm, on the day of HCG, the cycle was cancelled." This suggests deviations, if any, were protocol-driven rather than context-driven.
- However, no explicit statement is provided regarding whether analysis was by intention-to-treat. The flow diagram indicates all randomized patients were analyzed ("Analysed (n=81)" and "Analysed (n=80)" for each arm), suggesting an appropriate ITT-type analysis, but exclusions criteria/dropouts are not described in text in detail, creating some uncertainty.
- Given lack of blinding but no clear evidence of context-driven deviations affecting outcome, and an analysis that appears to include all randomized participants, the answer leans toward "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," primarily due to absence of explicit confirmation of ITT principles and open-label design without described safeguards against differential co-intervention/monitoring bias.

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Outcome data (clinical pregnancy) appear to be available for all or nearly all randomized participants.

**b. Rationale:**
- The CONSORT flow diagram indicates 81 patients were analyzed in Group I and 80 in Group II/III, matching exactly the number allocated ("Randomized (n=241)" → 81, 80, 80), with no indication of loss to follow-up preventing outcome ascertainment for the primary outcome (clinical pregnancy, assessed by serum β-hCG/progesterone at 14 days and confirmed by ultrasound at 6 weeks).
- "Serum samples were obtained 14 days after hCG administration for progesterone and βhCG levels. Clinical pregnancy was documented by TV-US, 6 weeks after the IUI" — indicating pregnancy status was ascertained for all participants who underwent the single insemination cycle.
- No text mentions exclusions from the pregnancy outcome analysis or missing outcome data due to lost follow-up.
- Given data were available for all/nearly all participants, this domain is judged as low risk.

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The outcome (clinical pregnancy) was measured objectively and consistently across all three groups using standard, appropriate methods, and is not the type of outcome likely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

**b. Rationale:**
- Clinical pregnancy was determined by objective measures: "Serum samples were obtained 14 days after hCG administration for progesterone and βhCG levels. Clinical pregnancy was documented by TV-US, 6 weeks after the IUI." This is an objective, observer-reported outcome with minimal judgment involved (biochemical assay plus ultrasound visualization of pregnancy), classified as an "observer-reported outcome not involving [substantial] judgement."
- The method of outcome ascertainment (β-hCG, progesterone, and ultrasound) was applied identically across all three groups, so there is no indication that measurement or ascertainment of the outcome differed between intervention groups.
- Although participants/personnel were not blinded (open-label), because clinical pregnancy is confirmed by an objective ultrasound/biochemical criterion, it is unlikely that lack of blinding influenced the assessment of this specific outcome (unlike a subjective outcome such as patient-reported symptoms).
- Therefore, risk of bias in measurement of the outcome is judged low.

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The clinical pregnancy rate was pre-specified as the primary outcome, and there is no evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple analyses or multiple outcome definitions.

**b. Rationale:**
- The manuscript explicitly states a priori: "The primary outcome was clinical pregnancy rate with duration of stimulation, total gonadotrophin dose, number of dominant follicles, clinical pregnancy rate, multiple pregnancy, miscarriage rate and ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) rate being secondary outcomes." This clearly designates clinical pregnancy rate as the primary outcome before results were known, and this is the outcome reported.
- A power calculation based on clinical pregnancy rate was also pre-specified ("This study was designed to have sufficient power to detect an absolute difference (rFSH) of 15% in the clinical pregnancy rate..."), consistent with pre-planned analysis of this specific outcome using a single definition (TV-US-confirmed clinical pregnancy at 6 weeks) and a single statistical comparison (three-group comparison via chi-square/ANOVA/Fisher's exact test as appropriate).
- There is no trial registration record identified (doc_id provided contains no retrievable prospective registration details distinguishing planned vs. reported analyses), and no evidence of multiple alternative definitions or analyses of clinical pregnancy being available and selectively reported; the outcome was measured and analyzed in a single, consistent manner as described in the Methods.
- No discrepancies were noted between the described primary outcome/analysis plan and the reported result.
- Given the primary outcome was clearly pre-specified and reported in a single manner consistent with methods, this domain is judged as low risk.

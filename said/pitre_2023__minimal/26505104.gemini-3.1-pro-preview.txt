**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The study states that "Participants were randomly allocated to one of four study conditions", but does not provide specific details on the methods used to generate the randomization sequence or whether allocation was concealed until participants were enrolled. Baseline characteristics between groups were not reported in detail to check for imbalances. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The interventions were delivered via a computer program, meaning participants were aware of the learning materials they received, though likely unaware of the other conditions. The authors excluded two participants from the analysis because they spent less than five minutes on the delayed diagnostic test. While this represents a post-randomization exclusion and deviates from a pure intention-to-treat analysis, the number of excluded participants was very small (2 out of 96 who returned for the delayed test), meaning the potential for a substantial impact on the result is low, though it raises some concerns.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Of the 101 enrolled participants, 5 failed to complete the delayed session and 2 were excluded for spending less than five minutes on the delayed test. The overall amount of missing data is small (less than 7%), and it is unlikely that this small number of missing outcomes would introduce significant bias into the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome was measured using a standardized diagnostic test (12 clinical vignettes) delivered via a computer program. The assessment was objective (identifying the correct diagnosis from a list of four), and the automated nature of the testing ensures that the measurement of the outcome was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: No pre-specified trial protocol or registration was available to verify the original analysis intentions. However, the reported outcomes (immediate and delayed performance on memory and diagnostic tests) are consistent with the described methodology and standard practices for this type of educational study. Without a pre-specified protocol, a judgement of some concerns is appropriate.

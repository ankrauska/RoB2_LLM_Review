# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0)
## Outcome: Severity of subjective withdrawal symptoms (Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale)
## Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The trial reports use of an "automated randomization system" allocating participants 1:1, and the randomization list was "held securely, and released only after study completion," which is consistent with adequate allocation concealment. Baseline comparisons of age, methadone dose and use of symptomatic medications showed no significant differences. However, no details are given about how the random sequence itself was generated (e.g., algorithm, block size, stratification), and there is no baseline table presenting demographic/clinical characteristics with numerical comparisons (only a narrative statement that no differences were found). This absence of detail on sequence generation and full baseline data prevents a judgement of low risk.

**b. Rationale:** 
- "Subjects were assigned randomly to receive adjunctive treatment with either gabapentin or placebo under double-blind conditions, using a randomization automated system on a one-to-one basis. During the study, the randomization list was held securely, and released only after study completion."
- "There were no significant differences in mean age or dosage of methadone and other drugs doses such as clonidine, oxazepam or trazodone used for symptomatic treatment between the treatment and controls groups at baseline... (results not shown)."
No further methodological detail on sequence generation (e.g., use of a random number generator, blocking) is provided, and underlying baseline data are not shown, limiting confidence that randomization was implemented without problems → **Some concerns**.

---

### 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Participants, carers, and personnel were blinded ("double-blind…Gabapentin and placebo were dispensed in identical appearing capsules"), which reduces risk that trial context led to differential non-protocol behavior. However, 3 participants in the placebo group (and none in the gabapentin group) were excluded from the study after randomization "because of non-compliance with the scheduled treatment programme and self-medication with illicit and/or over-the-counter (OTC) drugs." The authors explicitly speculate that this non-compliance may have been driven by "the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms" in the placebo arm — i.e., a trial-context-related deviation (differential distress under blinded, but ongoing, withdrawal) that led to post-randomization exclusion from analysis. The data presented in Table 1/Table 3 show declining sample sizes only in the placebo arm (20→17), indicating that the analysis was not a full intention-to-treat analysis retaining all randomized participants; this constitutes an inappropriate (per-protocol-like) analysis for the ITT effect, though the impact of losing 3/20 participants is not obviously huge for the overall repeated-measures result.

**b. Rationale:**
- "Gabapentin and placebo were dispensed in identical appearing capsules. Patients who were randomized to the placebo group took the same number of capsules as those who were assigned to the treatment group."
- "one patient in the placebo group was excluded at day 14 and two others were excluded at day 17 because of non-compliance with the scheduled treatment programme and self-medication with illicit and/or over-the-counter (OTC) drugs. The non-compliance and self-medication in the placebo group may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms."
- Table 1 shows n=20 for gabapentin at all time points but n declines to 19 then 17 for placebo, confirming exclusion of these participants rather than retention with imputed values → **Some concerns**.

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Outcome data were not available for all randomized participants — 3 of 20 placebo participants (15%) had missing SOWS data at later time points, versus 0 missing in the gabapentin group. There is no evidence (e.g., sensitivity analysis, imputation) that the result was robust to this missingness. Critically, the reasons for the missing data (non-compliance and self-medication attributed by the authors to greater symptom severity) suggest that missingness in the outcome plausibly depended on its true value (i.e., participants suffering worse withdrawal were more likely to drop out), and this occurred asymmetrically between groups (only in placebo). This combination — differential missingness rate between arms plus a plausible outcome-dependent mechanism explicitly acknowledged by the authors — indicates a likely relationship between missingness and true outcome values.

**b. Rationale:**
- "one patient in the placebo group was excluded at day 14 and two others were excluded at day 17 because of non-compliance… The non-compliance and self-medication in the placebo group may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms, and demonstrates the possible superiority of gabapentin over placebo…"
- Table 1: placebo group n drops from 20 to 17 by day 18, while gabapentin remains at n=20 throughout; Table 3 shows similarly reduced n (17–18) for placebo across individual symptoms.
- No sensitivity analyses or imputation methods (e.g., LOCF, multiple imputation) are reported to address this missingness → **High risk of bias**.

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The SOWS is a participant-reported outcome (self-rated intensity of 16 symptoms), so the "outcome assessor" is the participant. The double-blind design (identical capsules) should have prevented participants from readily identifying their allocated intervention, and there is no indication that the measurement method or timing of assessment differed between groups. However, because gabapentin can produce distinctive perceptible effects (e.g., sedation) relative to placebo, and no formal test of blinding integrity is reported, there remains a possibility that some participants inferred their treatment allocation, potentially influencing self-reported symptom severity (a subjective, self-rated outcome highly susceptible to expectation effects). This possibility is not excluded by the trial report.

**b. Rationale:**
- "Gabapentin and placebo were dispensed in identical appearing capsules... under double-blind conditions."
- "The Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale (SOWS) was administered for evaluating the severity of the withdrawal symptoms... This scale contains 16 symptoms whose intensity the patient rates on a scale of 0 (not at all) to 4 (extremely)."
- No statement addressing whether blinding was checked or maintained throughout the trial, and given the subjective, self-reported nature of the outcome, unblinding (even partial, due to drug effects) could plausibly influence responses → **Some concerns**.

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The trial reports results for all 16 individual SOWS symptoms (Table 3), the total symptom score (Fig. 1), and the repeated-measures ANOVA across six time-points (Table 2), suggesting reasonably comprehensive reporting of the pre-specified outcome domain and measurement instrument (SOWS). There is no evidence of selective outcome-measurement reporting (e.g., reporting only certain symptoms or time-points) or of switching between multiple candyanalytic approaches based on results. However, the publication does not reference a prospectively registered protocol or statistical analysis plan, and no trial registration details are provided in the article itself to confirm that the reported analyses (repeated-measures ANOVA adjusting for age and methadone dose, Mann-Whitney U-test for total score) were pre-specified before unblinded data were available. Without this information, it cannot be confirmed that the specific analyses reported were selected in a manner free from result-dependent choices.

**b. Rationale:**
- The results section provides full data for all 16 symptoms (Table 3), the composite score across six time points (Table 1, Fig. 1), and the ANCOVA/repeated measures results (Table 2), indicating no obvious selective omission of the SOWS outcome domain.
- No trial registry number, protocol reference, or statistical analysis plan is cited in the manuscript to confirm the analysis (covariate adjustment for age/methadone dose; choice of Mann-Whitney test for total score) was pre-specified prior to unblinding.
- In the absence of a protocol/registration record to verify this, a judgement of "no information" on this signalling question defaults to **Some concerns**.

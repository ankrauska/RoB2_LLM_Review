1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:** The sequence-generation method was random: “one eye was treated with… ASA profile and the other eye… TSA profile, assigned at random using Microsoft Excel’s random number function.” The paired-eye design also ensures one eye per participant in each group. However, the report does not describe allocation concealment—e.g., who generated/accessed the Excel sequence and whether the assignment was concealed until the patient/eye was irrevocably enrolled. Baseline refractive error was similar between groups (mean spherical equivalent −2.43 D for ASA versus −2.54 D for TSA; *p* = 0.87), and all included eyes had preoperative CDVA of 20/20, with no evident baseline imbalance suggesting failure of randomization. Nevertheless, absent information on concealment leads to some concerns.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** This assessment concerns the effect of assignment to ASA versus TSA at baseline. The surgeons/personnel were probably aware of the ablation profile, since the procedures used different laser profiles; blinding of participants is not reported. However, the report provides no indication of deviations caused by the trial context, differential co-interventions, crossovers, or failure to deliver the allocated profile. Each patient had one eye assigned to ASA and the fellow eye assigned to TSA, with a standard 6-mm optical zone and the same LASIK procedure otherwise used for all eyes. Visual-acuity outcomes in Figure 4 are reported for all 30 eyes, consistent with analysis according to allocated treatment. There is no evidence that awareness of the assigned profile led to protocol-inconsistent deviations that would affect visual acuity.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** The study enrolled 15 patients (30 eyes), and the visual-acuity figure explicitly reports postoperative data for “30 eyes at 3 months postop.” The article states that “100% of eyes were 20/25 or better” and presents results for both treatment groups, with no reported exclusions, losses to follow-up, or missing visual-acuity measurements. Thus, outcome data appear available for all randomized eyes, and there is no indication that missing data could have materially affected the visual-acuity result.

4. **measurement of the outcome: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:** Visual acuity was measured before and 3 months after LASIK, and Figure 4 reports cumulative Snellen visual-acuity outcomes. Measurement using a standardized visual-acuity chart is an appropriate method, and there is no indication that the measurement method or timing differed between ASA and TSA eyes. However, the report does not state whether the personnel measuring visual acuity were masked to the eye’s assigned ablation profile. Visual acuity requires participant responses and can be influenced to some degree by encouragement, testing conditions, or assessor expectations. Although there is no specific evidence that outcome assessment was actually influenced, the lack of reported masking leaves some concerns.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:** The article reports postoperative visual acuity graphically in Figure 4, stating that “100% of eyes were 20/25 or better.” However, it does not identify a prospectively finalized statistical analysis plan for visual-acuity outcomes, including the intended visual-acuity metric, time point, analytic approach, or whether all planned visual-acuity analyses would be reported. The available report includes several possible visual and refractive outcomes—uncorrected visual acuity, CDVA, efficacy, safety, accuracy, and stability—and visual-acuity data are primarily presented descriptively. In the absence of sufficiently detailed, prospectively specified analysis intentions demonstrating that the reported Figure 4 result was selected independently of the findings, selective reporting cannot be excluded.

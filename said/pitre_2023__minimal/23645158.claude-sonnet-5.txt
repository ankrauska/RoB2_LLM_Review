# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0 Tool
## Outcome: NT Pro-BNP levels

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The trial describes random allocation using a "computer random numbers table," which satisfies the criterion for a truly random sequence generation process. However, there is no information provided about allocation concealment—no description of sealed envelopes, central randomization, or any other mechanism to prevent foreknowledge of assignment. Additionally, no formal baseline imbalance testing (p-values) between groups is reported for all covariates, though the presented values appear compatible with chance (Table 1).

**b. Rationale:** 
- Sequence generation: "Then the patients were allocated into one of the two groups based on a computer random numbers table." This meets criteria for "Yes" on signalling question 1.1.
- Allocation concealment: No description of concealment method (e.g., sealed opaque envelopes, central allocation service) is provided anywhere in the methods section. This results in "No information" for signalling question 1.2.
- Baseline characteristics (Table 1) show no statistically significant differences between groups (all P > 0.05), suggesting no evidence of problems with randomization (signalling question 1.3 = "No").
- Per the algorithm: No information on concealment + no baseline imbalance suggesting problems = **Some concerns**.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** This was described as a "double-blind" trial; however, no details are provided regarding the specific blinding procedures (e.g., identical appearance of MgSO4 and saline solutions, who prepared solutions, whether the anesthesiologist/surgical team were blinded to allocation). No signalling question responses can be made with full confidence due to lack of detail on blinding procedures. The trial did report an appropriate analysis (all randomized patients appear to be analyzed, i.e., ITT-like approach), but details on exclusions (specifically noting patients on "triple inotropic agent therapy" were excluded) raise some concern about deviations from intended intervention.

**b. Rationale:**
- The study states: "In a double-blind clinical trial..." and "MgSO4 or placebo" were administered via identical 50 ml syringes ("The same volume of normal saline was administered as a placebo via a 50 ml syringe intravenously"), suggesting attempts at blinding participants and personnel. This supports "No" or "Probably no" for signalling questions 2.1 and 2.2 (participants/personnel unaware).
- However, no explicit statement confirms that the syringes were indistinguishable in appearance, that pharmacy staff who prepared them were separate from those administering/assessing outcomes, or that blinding was maintained/verified. This creates uncertainty (leaning toward "Probably yes" they were aware, or at minimum "No information").
- Critically: "Those patients who received triple inotropic agent therapy for CPB weaning were excluded from the study. The number of patients excluded from the study in each group was registered." This indicates **post-randomization exclusion of eligible participants**, which is inconsistent with strict ITT principles. This is a deviation that could bias the effect of assignment to intervention if these exclusions were differential between groups (numbers excluded per group are not clearly reported in the text, though "registered").
- Because clinical need for triple inotropic support may itself be related to the intervention (MgSO4 could plausibly reduce inotrope requirements via its physiologic effects), this exclusion could be non-random with respect to outcome, raising concern about whether this exclusion affected the NT-proBNP comparison.
- Given incomplete reporting on blinding integrity and the potential for non-negligible post-randomization exclusions affecting analysis, the domain is judged as **Some concerns** rather than "Low risk."

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The final analyzed sample includes 44 patients per group (as reported in Tables 1–3), matching the initial randomized allocation described ("44 patients in the control group and 44 patients were allocated to the case group"). However, the text explicitly acknowledges that some patients were excluded due to receiving triple inotropic therapy, and it is unclear whether these excluded patients had already contributed baseline blood draws but not the 24-hour postoperative NT-proBNP measurement, and whether the final n=44 per group reflects the original randomized cohort or a post-exclusion cohort.

**b. Rationale:**
- The article states: "Those patients who received triple inotropic agent therapy for CPB weaning were excluded from the study. The number of patients excluded from the study in each group was registered." This confirms exclusions occurred, but the actual numbers excluded per group and reasons in relation to outcome status are not reported in the text or tables.
- Since Table 1–3 both report n = 44 in each group, it's ambiguous whether 88 total patients were originally randomized and analyzed with zero dropout, or whether exclusions occurred and replacement/additional recruitment brought the totals back to 44/44 (which would itself introduce bias, as sample size calculations pre-specified n=44/group).
- Because NT-proBNP is a physiological marker plausibly related to hemodynamic instability necessitating triple inotropic support, it is plausible that missingness in the outcome (via exclusion) could be related to the true (higher) NT pro-BNP values in the excluded patients, and this reason for missingness may not be balanced between groups if MgSO4 reduces inotrope requirements.
- Given this ambiguity and lack of a CONSORT flow diagram detailing exact numbers randomized, excluded, and analyzed, we cannot confirm "outcome data were available for all, or nearly all, randomized participants," nor can we rule out that missingness depended on the true value of the outcome. This leads to **Some concerns**.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** NT pro-BNP was measured using a standardized, objective laboratory immunoassay method identical for both groups, at pre-specified time points (before anesthesia and 24 hours postoperatively), by laboratory personnel who would have no reason to be aware of or influenced by group assignment when running an automated assay.

**b. Rationale:**
- "Measurement of NT pro-BNP was performed using an electrochemical luminescence immunoassay in an Elecsys 2010 (Roche, Indianapolis, IN, USA)." This is an objective, automated, appropriate laboratory method (signalling question 4.1 = "No," not inappropriate) with a "coefficient of variation of 2.2–3.2%," which indicates good precision/validity.
- "Sampling temperature, centrifugation and refrigeration were kept consistent throughout the study" for both groups—measurement/ascertainment of the outcome did not differ between intervention groups (signalling question 4.2 = "No").
- This is an observer-reported outcome not involving judgement (an automated laboratory assay), and laboratory technicians running the assay would typically be unaware of/unable to be influenced by clinical group assignment (signalling question 4.3 = "No" or "Probably no").
- Because this is an objective biomarker assessed via automated immunoassay rather than a participant-reported or subjectively-judged outcome, risk of bias due to lack of blinding of outcome assessors (if any existed) is minimal.
- This domain is judged as **Low risk of bias**.

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The trial registry entry (IRCT201205069650N1) was checked as instructed; however, without being able to directly compare a pre-specified, date-stamped statistical analysis plan or trial protocol against the final reported outcomes and analyses, it cannot be conclusively determined whether NT pro-BNP was reported using the only measurement/analysis approach originally planned, or whether it was selected from multiple possible time points, analyses, or comparisons.

**b. Rationale:**
- The manuscript only reports two NT pro-BNP time points (pre-CPB and 24-hour postoperative), which appears consistent and pre-specified within the text itself, and this matches what is described in the methods ("we took 10 ml...before anesthesia and 24 h after patient arrival at the ICU").
- However, the study explicitly acknowledges as a limitation: "we just assessed two points of the serum levels of NT pro-BNP; while the trend of the marker or the 'area under curve' (AUC) was not measured," suggesting no evidence of selective omission of additional pre-specified time points—this supports lower concern.
- Nonetheless, without access to a full trial protocol or statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinding to confirm that the analysis method (Student's t-test on 24-hour values) was the pre-specified primary analysis (rather than one of several possible comparisons, e.g., change-from-baseline vs. final value, or alternate statistical tests), there remains uncertainty.
- Because the registry-derived information (doc_id: 4dab5cab...) was intended to be used for this assessment but specific protocol details on pre-specified outcome analysis for NT pro-BNP are not provided/confirmed as matching the published report in the material available, this results in "No information" being applied to relevant signalling questions (5.1–5.3), defaulting to **Some concerns** per the RoB 2 algorithm.

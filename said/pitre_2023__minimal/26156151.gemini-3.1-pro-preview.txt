**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: The trial used an interactive web-based response system for randomization, which ensures both a random sequence generation and adequate allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics were well balanced between the two groups, indicating no problems with the randomization process. As stated in the text, "Using an interactive web-based response system, study participants were randomly assigned and stratified by participating center."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: While the trial operators and participants were likely aware of the assigned intervention (IVUS vs. angiography guidance) due to the nature of the procedure, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention occurred because of the trial context. Furthermore, the trial utilized an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis. The publication states, "The intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants. Follow-up was essentially complete, with very few dropouts. The text notes, "200 patients of IVUS-guided group (99.5%) and 201 patients (100%) in the angiography-guided group finished 12-month clinical follow-up."

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: The outcome being assessed is the occurrence of cardiac death, which is an objective, observer-reported outcome that does not involve subjective judgment. Therefore, even if outcome assessors were aware of the intervention received, the assessment of cardiac death is highly unlikely to have been influenced by this knowledge. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias.
b. Rationale: The trial was prospectively registered (NCT01563952), and the primary outcome specified in the registry was "event rate of cardiac death within 12 months." The published report presents the results for this exact outcome, matching the pre-specified plan. There is no evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple analyses of the data based on the results.

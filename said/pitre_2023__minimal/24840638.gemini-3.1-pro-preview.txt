**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: The method of sequence generation is described as random, but there is no information provided on whether the allocation sequence was concealed. There was a baseline imbalance in education level.
b. Rationale: "Random assignment to one of the three groups was accomplished by randomly assigning 2 participants to the percentile group, 2 to the fixed criterion group and 1 to the random incentives group from each group of 5 participants completing intake." No information is provided about allocation concealment. The authors note, "A higher proportion of percentile incentive participants achieved this level of education, relative to fixed criterion participants."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were aware of the assigned interventions, but there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. All randomized participants appear to have been included in the analyses.
b. Rationale: Given the nature of the behavioral contingency management intervention, blinding was not possible: "Immediately after randomization all participants were given a brief description of the incentive contingency that they were randomized into". There is no indication of post-randomization exclusions or inappropriate analyses.

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is a substantial amount of missing outcome data (missed visits), and it is likely that missingness in the outcome depends on its true value (e.g., participants who smoked may be more likely to miss a visit). 
b. Rationale: Participants were expected to submit a breath CO sample every workday for 60 visits. "The average number of delivered breath CO samples per participant (out of 60) was 42 (SD = 22), 40 (24), and 52 (19) for the percentile, fixed criterion and random incentive groups, respectively." Missing visits are common in smoking cessation trials and are often related to smoking status.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome was measured using an objective device, and the assessment is unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
b. Rationale: "Vitalograph CO monitors (Vitalograph Inc. Lenexa, KS) were used to take breath CO samples." This is an objective measurement not prone to observer bias. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is no information regarding a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registration available to confirm that the reported analyses were strictly pre-planned. 
b. Rationale: The manuscript does not mention a clinical trial registry number or reference a published protocol/statistical analysis plan that was finalized prior to unblinding. Without this information, a judgement of 'some concerns' is appropriate.

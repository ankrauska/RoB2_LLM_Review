**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
- **Judgement:** The trial used an adequate randomized sequence generation process and baseline characteristics were well balanced, indicating no major issues with the randomization process. 
- **Rationale:** The publication states, "The patients were randomly assigned in a 3:2 ratio... and were stratified according to clinical center and ischemic status with the use of an algorithm that ensured near balance in each stratum." Table 1 shows that baseline clinical and demographic characteristics were highly comparable between the ICD-only and CRT-ICD groups, suggesting the randomization process was successful and allocation concealment was likely maintained in this large, multicenter trial.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
- **Judgement:** Although participants and trial personnel were aware of the assigned interventions, an appropriate intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to the interventions.
- **Rationale:** The trial was open-label: "The treating physicians were aware of study-group assignments." While there were some crossovers (e.g., 12.4% of ICD-only patients received a CRT-ICD device, mostly after an event, and 7.5% of CRT-ICD patients received an ICD-only device due to technical difficulties), the trialists utilized an appropriate analysis method for the effect of assignment. The text confirms, "Data analysis was performed according to the intention-to-treat principle."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
- **Judgement:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, and the proportion of missing data was small enough that it is unlikely to have introduced material bias.
- **Rationale:** The trial had very high retention rates. The report notes, "A total of 44 patients in the CRT–ICD group (4.0%) and 55 in the ICD-only group (7.5%) declined to continue participating in the study, were withdrawn by a physician, or were lost to follow-up." These proportions are low and thus missing outcome data could not have substantially biased the observed 34% risk reduction in the primary endpoint. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
- **Judgement:** Although the initial diagnosis of heart-failure events was made by unblinded physicians, all primary endpoint events were adjudicated by an independent committee blinded to the treatment assignment.
- **Rationale:** The publication explicitly describes the safeguards against measurement bias: "Heart-failure events were diagnosed by physicians who were aware of the treatment assignments, but they were adjudicated by a committee that was unaware of assignments... Adjudication of the end points was carried out by an independent mortality committee and by a heart-failure committee that was unaware of study-group assignments, according to prespecified criteria." 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
- **Judgement:** The reported primary outcome matches the pre-specified outcome in the trial registry, and there is no evidence that the reported results were selected from multiple analyses or outcome measurements based on their statistical significance.
- **Rationale:** The clinical trial registration (NCT00180271) lists the primary outcome as "Mortality From Any Cause or First Heart Failure (HF) Event," which aligns exactly with the primary end point reported in the publication: "The primary end point was death from any cause or nonfatal heart-failure events, whichever came first." The analyses follow the pre-specified plan.

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** The study randomized participants into two groups, but it did not provide information on allocation concealment or baseline characteristics beyond age.
* **Rationale:** The authors state, "The patients were assigned to two groups at random with 20 patients each." However, there is no mention of how the random sequence was generated or whether allocation was concealed. Furthermore, baseline characteristics (other than age) are not reported, making it difficult to assess baseline imbalances.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Although personnel (CT technologists) were inevitably aware of the assigned scanning protocol (collimation), this knowledge is unlikely to lead to deviations from the intended intervention that would affect the outcome.
* **Rationale:** The intervention involves setting specific collimation parameters on a CT scanner (64 × 0.6 mm vs. 20 × 1.2 mm). Because it is a single-session imaging protocol, there are no meaningful opportunities for protocol deviations that arise specifically due to the trial context, and participants were likely unaware of the technical parameters of their scan.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Outcome data (CT scans) were available for all randomized participants. 
* **Rationale:** Forty patients were enrolled and scanned. The authors note, "Four patients had to be excluded from the study because no coronary calcium was detected on any of the reconstructions." While they were excluded from the variability analysis because their score was zero, their outcome was successfully measured, meaning there is no genuinely missing outcome data that could bias the comparison. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** The outcome was measured using largely automated software followed by observer verification, making it unlikely that knowledge of the intervention influenced the assessment.
* **Rationale:** The authors describe the process: "CAC was defined as the presence of more than two contiguous pixels with Hounsfield units greater than 130. These lesions were automatically identified and marked in color by the workstation. In a second step, a radiologist... and a cardiologist... differentiated between calcium and noise." While there is no mention of blinding the readers to the collimation group, the initial automated detection and objective nature of the Agatston score make it unlikely that the assessment was meaningfully biased by knowledge of the intervention.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** There is no available trial registration or pre-published protocol to verify the pre-specified analysis plan.
* **Rationale:** The authors report the outcomes (Agatston scores and volumetric scores) comprehensively for the different reconstruction intervals and increments. However, without a pre-specified statistical analysis plan, we cannot completely rule out the possibility that the reported analyses were selected based on the results. Therefore, there are some concerns regarding selective reporting.

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:** The allocation sequence was appropriately generated: patients were “**randomized to telephone contact or no telephone contact using a computer-generated list of random numbers**.” Baseline characteristics were reported as similar between the two groups, and the intended 1:1 allocation was achieved (15 versus 15 participants). However, the report provides no information on allocation concealment—i.e., whether personnel recruiting/enrolling patients could foresee the upcoming assignment. Under RoB 2, a random sequence with no information about concealment leads to *some concerns*.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to telephone follow-up versus no telephone follow-up at baseline.  
   b. **Rationale:** Participants and the pharmacist delivering the calls necessarily knew group assignment. However, the telephone calls were the intended experimental intervention, rather than an unintended co-intervention or deviation. The intervention was described consistently: “**A pharmacist telephoned patients at their home every week for 12 weeks**,” using “**a standard set of questions**.” All participants received the same lipid-lowering regimen, initial counselling, and dietary instruction.  
  
   There is no evidence that unplanned deviations arising specifically from the trial context occurred differentially between groups and affected compliance. The analysis appears to compare the randomized groups: 15 were assigned to telephone contact and 15 to no contact, and “**none of whom was lost to follow-up**.” Thus, despite lack of blinding, there is no indication of a biasing deviation from the assigned intervention strategy or of exclusion/switching of participants in the analysis.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** The authors explicitly state that “**Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up**.” Compliance was assessed at scheduled short-term visits using pill/packet counts and at one and two years using pharmacy refill records. The report presents compliance findings for both randomized groups and provides no indication that outcome data were unavailable for a material number of participants or that missingness differed by group. Therefore, missing compliance data could not plausibly have materially affected the comparison.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.  
   b. **Rationale:** Compliance was measured using relatively objective methods: “**pill and packet counts (not performed within the patient’s view)**” at 6 and 12 weeks, and pharmacy refill records at one and two years. The definition of noncompliance was specified: patients returning more than 20% of medication or failing to fill at least 80% of prescriptions were classified as noncompliant.  
  
   Although participants and study personnel were aware of telephone-contact allocation, the principal long-term outcome assessment used pharmacy refill information, which is not readily subject to subjective assessor judgement. Pill/packet counting was also performed outside the patient’s view. The same measurement methods and thresholds were described for both groups, with no evidence that ascertainment differed according to group assignment. Awareness of allocation is therefore unlikely to have materially biased outcome measurement.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.  
   b. **Rationale:** The publication reports compliance at several time points (6 weeks, 12 weeks, 1 year, and 2 years), for two separate medicines (lovastatin and colestipol), and using two different measurement approaches (pill/packet counts and refill records). These features create multiple potentially eligible outcome measurements and analyses.  
  
   The article does not report a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. In the materials supplied here, no sufficiently detailed registration/protocol information is available to confirm that the reported compliance result—particularly the emphasis on statistically significant 1- and 2-year refill-based findings—was selected according to a pre-specified plan rather than from multiple time points or analytical options. There is no direct evidence of selective reporting, but RoB 2 indicates *some concerns* when prespecified analysis intentions cannot be verified in the presence of multiple plausible outcome measurements/analyses.

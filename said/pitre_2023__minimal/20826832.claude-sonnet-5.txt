# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0)
## Outcome: Compliance with mammogram screening
## Effect of Interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (ITT)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The trial used adequate random sequence generation and concealed allocation. "A random sample of 500 women... were randomly assigned to one of the three intervention groups" using "a computerized random number generator program." Concealment was robust: "A complete data base with 500 eligible women interviewed at baseline was sent to the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center in the US (i.e. the coordination center) to proceed with a random allocation." This central, remote allocation by an independent coordination center after baseline data collection strongly protects against foreknowledge of assignment. A "stratified randomization method blocking by age (50–59; 60–70)" was used, which is a valid approach. Baseline characteristics were balanced: "No significant differences were observed between groups in the demographic characteristics of the participants," and Table 1 shows similar values across groups, consistent with successful randomization. There is no evidence of problematic baseline imbalance.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** Blinding of participants and intervention deliverers was not possible: "Blinding participants to their intervention group was not possible in this study" and "blinding was not possible for participants and interventionists; therefore, they knew the allocation arms." Since participants and personnel were aware of their group, the domain requires assessment of whether trial-context deviations affected the outcome. The authors themselves flag a plausible contamination effect: "Lack of blinding in this study could have produced a certain level of cross contamination given that women, especially in the control arm, could have demanded more information than usual." However, they argue this would bias towards the null (favoring the control arm) rather than exaggerating the intervention effect, and the effect observed was very large (6% vs. 51.8% vs. 70.1%), making it implausible that such contamination fully explains the results. There is no clear evidence of unbalanced non-protocol interventions strongly affecting the outcome, but the potential for contamination in an unblinded design is a genuine, acknowledged concern. The analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention was appropriate: "An intention to treat analysis was conducted using electronic clinical records... compliance was estimated using the baseline denominators of each group," which follows ITT principles (analysis by randomized group regardless of receipt, using full baseline denominators). Given lack of blinding with an acknowledged but likely small/conservative bias, and an appropriate ITT analysis, this domain is best classified as "Some concerns" rather than "Low" or "High."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The outcome (mammogram screening) was measured via **electronic records**, and the primary result reported and analyzed is based on this administrative data source rather than self-report, for essentially all 500 randomized participants: "Percentage of screening according to the electronic registry of mammograms performed during the period of the study shows a significant increase from 6% (167) in the control arm, to 51.8% (86/166) in the low level intervention arm, and 70.1% (117/167) in the high level intervention arm" — these denominators correspond to the full number randomized to each arm (167, 166, 167), indicating complete outcome ascertainment via records for essentially all participants regardless of survey completion. Although "92.4% (462/500) of the women completed the final survey" (self-report), the electronic record-based outcome (which is the outcome assessed here) does not appear to suffer from missingness, since clinic/radiology records could be checked regardless of participant follow-up survey completion. Loss to follow-up for the self-reported outcome was small and similar across groups (6.6%, 7.8%, 8.4%), and reasons for dropout (refusal, inability to contact, one death) do not suggest a relationship between missingness and the true value of mammogram uptake, nor differential patterns likely to bias the electronic-record-based estimate.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The primary outcome data for this assessment come from electronic/administrative records rather than participant self-report, an objective outcome-ascertainment method not subject to participant or assessor subjective judgement. Critically, outcome ascertainment and analysis were blinded to allocation: "Staff at the clinic and at the radiology unit where participants got their mammograms were blinded to the allocation group," and "The analysis of the data was conducted by investigators of the coordination center at the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center. They were blinded to the group assignment of participants." Measurement of whether a mammogram was performed (a binary record of an event) is an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement," and thus is not susceptible to influence by knowledge of intervention status even in the (unlikely) case blinding of radiology staff was imperfect. Measurement method did not differ between groups (same radiology unit/electronic chart system used for all arms).

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Based on the trial registration record, the outcome of mammography screening compliance was pre-specified as a primary outcome, and the result reported in the publication (mammogram completion assessed via electronic records/registry at 6 months, analyzed by intention-to-treat across all three arms: control, low intensity, high intensity) matches the pre-specified outcome domain and analysis approach without evidence of selection among multiple alternative measurements, definitions, time points, or analytic approaches. The paper reports a single, consistent definition of the outcome (mammogram performed, ascertained by electronic record) across all three groups and both timepoints (baseline and 6 months), with results for all randomized participants presented (denominators of 166/167 per group), consistent with full, non-selective reporting of the pre-specified primary outcome.

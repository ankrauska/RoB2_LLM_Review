**1. bias due to randomization: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: High risk of bias
b. Rationale: The sequence generation was based on a deterministic method (clinical chart number) rather than a truly random process, which is an inadequate method of randomization and allows for predictability. The trial reports: "Random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." According to the RoB 2.0 guidance, methods based on patient record numbers are not considered random.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Although the trial was open-label and participants and personnel were aware of the assigned interventions ("the trial was not blinded to patients and treating physicians"), there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, the analysis was appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention, as the authors stated: "Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis".

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Data appear to be available for all or nearly all randomized participants. The primary reason for missing data or drop-out during the follow-up was death due to cancer progression, which was balanced between the intervention groups ("12 patients (10.1%) died in group A1, 19 (15.4%) in group A2"). There is no indication that missingness in the outcome was related to its true value.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The method of measuring the outcome (objective imaging and clinical criteria for recurrent VTE) was appropriate. While the treating physicians and patients were unblinded, the actual outcomes were adjudicated by an independent and blinded committee. The text states: "All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of the patient’s name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment."

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The data were analyzed in accordance with the pre-specified plan. The primary outcome reported in the publication (recurrent venous thromboembolism) matches the primary outcome registered in the trial protocol (NCT00450645: "Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism"). There is no evidence of selection of the reported result from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple eligible analyses.

1. **Bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The allocation sequence was random, allocation was adequately concealed, and the reported baseline data do not show an imbalance clearly incompatible with chance.  
   b. **Rationale:** The investigators report that allocation used “a block randomization determined by a computer-generated random number table, with stratification for operator and center.” Allocation was concealed using “sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelope[s]” opened only after trial acceptance; additionally, “the generator of the randomization did not participate in patient allocation.” These procedures meet RoB 2 criteria for random sequence generation and allocation concealment.  

   The randomized group sizes (67 SmartClip and 71 Victory) are reasonably consistent with the intended 1:1 allocation. Although there were some numerical differences in baseline malocclusion categories and mean age, there is no indication of extreme imbalance in an important prognostic variable or of a pattern suggesting compromised randomization.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns. The trial was necessarily unblinded to participants and treating clinicians, but there is no reported evidence that trial-context deviations from protocol occurred and affected treatment duration. However, the reported analysis was per protocol rather than an analysis retaining all randomized participants in their allocated groups.  
   b. **Rationale:** Participants and clinicians could not be blinded: “it was not possible to blind the clinician or patient to the type of bracket system being used.” This makes deviations due to the trial context possible in principle. However, both operators followed a standardized bonding procedure and manufacturer-recommended archwire sequences “as far as possible”; no evidence is reported of differential co-interventions, systematic departures from the assigned appliance strategy, or differential management that would plausibly affect total treatment duration.

   Nevertheless, the authors explicitly state: “Due to the low number of dropouts, analysis was undertaken on a per protocol basis.” Three of 138 randomized participants were omitted: one refused fixed-appliance treatment after allocation and two did not complete treatment. For the effect of assignment at baseline, a per-protocol analysis is not the preferred approach because it excludes randomized participants. The small number excluded (3/138; 2.2%) makes a substantial effect on this continuous outcome less likely, but the analysis does not fully estimate the assignment-to-intervention effect. Therefore, some concerns are warranted rather than high risk.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Outcome data on total treatment duration were available for nearly all randomized participants.  
   b. **Rationale:** Of 138 randomized participants, 135 (97.8%) completed treatment and were analysed: 65/67 in the SmartClip group and 70/71 in the Victory group. Thus, outcome data were unavailable for only three participants overall. For a continuous outcome such as duration of treatment, data availability above approximately 95% will generally be sufficient under RoB 2 unless the missing outcomes could plausibly change the result materially.

   Missingness was also balanced in absolute terms: one participant in each group did not complete treatment; the remaining excluded participant in the SmartClip group refused treatment following allocation. Although refusal or non-completion could be related to treatment experience, the very small amount of missing outcome data is unlikely to have made an important difference to the estimated between-group difference in treatment duration.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**  
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Total treatment duration was defined objectively, measurement methods appear comparable between groups, and the outcome is unlikely to be materially influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.  
   b. **Rationale:** The outcome was pre-defined as “total duration of treatment (months) from fixed appliance placement to debond.” This is based on treatment dates rather than subjective participant or clinician judgement. The same definition and measurement period were used for both groups.

   Although clinicians and participants were aware of bracket allocation, knowledge of bracket type is unlikely to alter the recorded dates of appliance placement and debond in a way that materially biases the duration measure. Further, the report states that “data analysis was carried out at the end of the study with examiner blinding” and that appliance type was omitted from data-collection sheets to minimize bias during data entry and analysis. There is no indication that outcome ascertainment differed systematically between intervention groups.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**  
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns. Treatment duration was clearly identified as an outcome and a numerical result was reported, but there is insufficient information to confirm that the analysis of total treatment duration followed a fully pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available.  
   b. **Rationale:** The publication prospectively identifies total treatment duration as an outcome: “total duration of treatment (months) from fixed appliance placement to debond.” It reports the group means, between-group difference, 95% confidence interval, and P value: 25.12 months for SmartClip versus 25.80 months for Victory; difference 0.68 months (95% CI −1.4 to 2.7; P=.51). Thus, there is no direct indication that the outcome itself was selectively omitted or incompletely reported.

   However, the available report does not provide a dated statistical analysis plan, nor does it establish that the choice of per-protocol analysis, use of Welch two-sample *t*-tests, or handling of the three excluded participants was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. The stated trial registration information is not available in sufficient detail here to verify the pre-specified measurement and analysis of total treatment duration. Because more than one eligible analysis could have been undertaken (for example, all-randomized versus per-protocol analysis, or adjusted versus unadjusted analyses), RoB 2 indicates **some concerns** rather than low risk.

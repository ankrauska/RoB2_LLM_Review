Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of total duration of treatment (treatment time):

1. **bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** The allocation sequence was generated randomly and adequately concealed. The authors state, "Subjects were allocated to either the study (SmartClip) group or control (Victory) group using a block randomization determined by a computer-generated random number table... The appliance type was placed in a sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelope that was opened after the patient was accepted onto the trial. The generator of the randomization did not participate in patient allocation." Baseline characteristics appear reasonably balanced between the groups, indicating no obvious problems with the randomization process.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** Participants and clinicians were not blinded to the intervention because "it was not possible to blind the clinician or patient to the type of bracket system being used." However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context. While the authors state "analysis was undertaken on a per protocol basis" excluding the 3 patients who dropped out, the potential impact of excluding only 2.2% of participants (3 out of 138) is not substantial.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** Data for this outcome were available for nearly all participants randomized. Out of 138 subjects randomized, 135 (97.8%) completed the study and were included in the final analysis. Only 3 participants were lost (one refused treatment and two failed to complete), which is a small enough proportion that their missing data could not have a substantial impact on the estimated effect.

4. **measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.
   b. **Rationale:** The outcome is the total duration of treatment, which ends when the clinician decides the orthodontic treatment is complete (debonding). Since it was "not possible to blind either clinician or subject to the bracket type," the unblinded clinician's judgement of when to finish the treatment could potentially be influenced by their knowledge of which bracket system the patient received. While the authors note that "the appliance type was not recorded on the data collection sheets in an attempt to minimize bias," the clinical decision-making process itself was unblinded.

5. **selective reporting: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** Although a trial registry or protocol was not provided for comparison, total duration of treatment is a standard, highly expected outcome measure for a trial evaluating "treatment efficiency." The reported results correspond to the pre-specified outcome measurements listed in the methods section, with no indication that data were selected based on the direction or statistical significance of the results.

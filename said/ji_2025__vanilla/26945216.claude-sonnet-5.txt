Relevant sentences:
"The participants were blind to the allocation to treatment groups after randomization, and hence, the study was a single blind trial." However, the trial had three distinct arms: standard care alone (control, no electrical stimulation device), TENS (device delivering a "strong but nonpainful TENS sensation"), and IFT (device delivering a "strong but nonpainful TENS sensation" via different electrode configuration). "not using a placebo for the patients of the control group could be considered as limitations of the present study." Additionally, TENS used a single channel with two electrodes placed specifically over the MTrP and acromial tendon insertion, while IFT used four electrodes in a quadripolar arrangement around the MTrP—visibly different electrode setups (Figures 1 and 2).

Analysis:
Although participants were described as "blind" to allocation, this is questionable given the trial design. The control group received no electrical stimulation device at all, while both active groups received a device producing a distinct sensory perception ("strong but nonpainful" electrical sensation). Participants in the control arm would clearly know they were not receiving electrical stimulation, since no device/electrodes were applied to them, whereas TENS/IFT participants would feel the electrical current sensation. Furthermore, even between the TENS and IFT groups, the number and placement of electrodes were visibly different (2 electrodes in a specific pattern vs. 4 electrodes in a square pattern), which participants could visually distinguish. There was no placebo/sham device used for the control group, as explicitly acknowledged by the authors as a limitation. Therefore, participants were very likely aware of which intervention they were receiving based on both the presence/absence of a device and the distinguishable electrode configurations and sensations experienced.

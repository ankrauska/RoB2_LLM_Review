## RoB 2 assessment

**Study design:** Individually randomized, parallel-group trial.  
**Comparison:** Spinomed orthosis versus posture-training support (PTS).  
**Result assessed:** Between-group comparison of the *immediate change* in balance outcomes while wearing the allocated orthosis versus not wearing it (Table 3), with particular emphasis on the stated main outcome, the sensory organization test balance score.  
**Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect).  
**Sources available for this assessment:** Article report and supplied tables/figures only. No trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, trial-registry record, or other prespecified analysis document was available in the supplied material.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Probably yes.**  
  The report states: *“The randomization procedure was to pick the name of the orthosis from a bag including the names of both orthoses.”* Drawing allocations from a bag is a procedure containing a random component. However, the report does not state the number of labels for each orthosis, whether labels were identical, whether selection occurred with or without replacement, who performed the draw, or whether the process was implemented consistently. The unequal final group sizes (Spinomed, n = 10; PTS, n = 8) are compatible with unrestricted random allocation in this small study and do not by themselves establish a failure of randomization.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  No information is provided on concealment of the bag-drawing process. In particular, the article does not report whether the allocation labels were opaque and indistinguishable, whether the bag was handled by an independent person, or whether the person recruiting/enrolling participants could identify or predict the forthcoming allocation before inclusion.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — Probably no.**  
  The article reports no statistically significant baseline differences between groups in age, weight, height, kyphosis angle, baseline balance score, baseline directional control, or baseline reaction time. The group sizes were 10 and 8, which is not a substantial imbalance for the described small sample. The available baseline information therefore does not suggest a problem with randomization.

**Judgement and motivation**

The allocation method involved a random element, and available baseline characteristics do not indicate that randomization failed. However, allocation concealment is not reported. Under RoB 2, absence of information on allocation concealment, despite apparently balanced baseline data, leads to **some concerns** rather than low risk of bias. The reporting ambiguity is a limitation: it is not possible to verify that recruitment or enrolment could not have been influenced by knowledge of the next assignment.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Participants wore either a Spinomed orthosis or a PTS. These are visibly and physically different orthoses; participant blinding was not reported and is not plausible from the described intervention procedures.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  A certified orthotist fitted the assigned orthosis and adjusted its straps. The person delivering the intervention would necessarily know which orthosis was fitted.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The intended intervention was immediate use of the allocated orthosis after a 1.5-hour adaptation period. The report describes that both orthoses were fitted by a certified orthotist, and balance was measured with and without the allocated orthosis. It does not report crossovers, use of the non-allocated orthosis, differential co-interventions, or implementation failures caused by the trial context. The study was explicitly designed to assess the immediate effect of wearing each allocated orthosis, without exercise.  
  The article does not provide detailed adherence or fidelity data; this is a reporting limitation. However, it does not provide evidence that trial-context deviations occurred.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  All 18 allocated participants completed the study and are represented in the analyses: 10 in the Spinomed group and 8 in the PTS group. The article compared within-person change with versus without orthosis and compared these changes between randomized groups using an independent-samples t-test. Participants were analysed according to their allocated orthosis group. No post-randomization exclusions, as-treated analyses, or per-protocol exclusions are described.

**Judgement and motivation**

Participants and intervention personnel were aware of the allocated orthosis. Nevertheless, for the immediate assignment effect assessed here, the report does not identify deviations from the intended intervention arising from the trial context that were likely to affect balance outcomes or that were unbalanced between groups. The reported analysis retained all allocated participants in their assigned groups. Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Yes.**  
  The article states that 18 participants completed the study procedure. The reported group sizes in the outcome tables equal the allocated group sizes: Spinomed, n = 10; PTS, n = 8. Table 3 reports the between-group comparison of changes for all 18 participants. No participant losses, withdrawals, exclusions after allocation, or missing balance measurements are reported.

**Judgement and motivation**

Data for the reported balance outcomes were available for all 18 randomized participants. There is no indication that any outcome measurements were missing or that missing data were imputed. Therefore, missing outcome data could not have materially biased the assessed result, and the domain is at **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  Balance score was measured by the NeuroCom EquiTest sensory organization test, and directional control and reaction time were measured using the NeuroCom Balance Master limits-of-stability test. The procedures are described in detail and were performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The article also cites previous evidence regarding reliability and validity of these measures. Nothing in the report indicates that the measurement systems were unsuitable for the stated balance outcomes.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  Both groups underwent the same balance-testing procedures, using the same equipment, testing conditions, rest intervals, and outcome definitions. The protocol reports random ordering of testing with and without the orthosis and random ordering of the sensory-organization-test stages, intended to limit learning and fatigue effects. There is no reported differential outcome-ascertainment method between intervention groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants? — No information.**  
  The article describes the study as *“single blinded”* but does not identify who was blinded. Thus, the report does not establish whether the testing operator was blinded to the orthosis worn by a participant.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  The reported outcomes are computer-derived values from standardized force-plate-based tests: total balance score, directional control, and reaction time. Although participants necessarily knew whether they were wearing an orthosis, the numerical outcomes were generated by the testing systems rather than by subjective interpretation of an assessor. The report does not identify an assessor judgment, clinical decision, or participant-reported outcome that could differentially determine the recorded numerical values.

**Judgement and motivation**

The balance outcomes were obtained with standardized, instrument-based procedures applied comparably to both groups. The article does not clarify the identity of the person who was “single blinded,” so assessor blinding cannot be confirmed. However, the assessed result relies on automated force-plate-derived measurements rather than subjective assessor judgments. On the information reported, knowledge of allocated orthosis was not likely to bias the recording or ascertainment of the numerical outcome measures. This domain is therefore judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis? — No information.**  
  No protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial registry record is available in the supplied material. Although the article identifies balance score as the main outcome measure and describes the planned statistical tests in its Methods section, this does not establish that the analysis plan was finalized before outcome data were available.

- **5.2 Is the result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  The study measured several balance-related outcomes: sensory organization test balance score, directional control, and reaction time. The article reports all three of these named variables. However, the sensory organization test also involved six sensory configurations, each repeated three times, and the limits-of-stability assessment involved eight directional stages. The report presents only aggregate measures and does not provide a prespecified hierarchy or complete reporting of all potentially eligible measurement-level results. In the absence of a pre-specified analysis document, it cannot be determined whether the reported aggregate outcomes were selected after results were known.

- **5.3 Is the result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The article reports paired within-group comparisons and independent-samples between-group comparisons of change scores. It does not provide a prospectively specified analysis plan establishing that change-score analysis, rather than another eligible analysis (for example, comparison of post-orthosis values adjusted for baseline values), was selected before unblinded outcome data were available. There is no direct evidence that results-based selection occurred, but the absence of a protocol or statistical analysis plan prevents excluding it.

**Judgement and motivation**

The publication identifies balance score as the main outcome and reports the three stated balance variables. However, no pre-specified protocol, registry entry, or statistical analysis plan was available to verify that the reported measurement and analysis were selected before unblinded outcome data were examined. There is no evidence of selective reporting sufficient for a high-risk judgement, but the absence of pre-specified analysis documentation creates **some concerns** about selection of the reported result.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement — **Some concerns**

The overall judgement is **some concerns**.

- Domain 1, bias arising from the randomization process, has **some concerns** because allocation concealment was not reported.
- Domain 5, bias in selection of the reported result, has **some concerns** because no prospectively finalized protocol or statistical analysis plan was available to verify that the reported result was selected without knowledge of unblinded outcome data.
- Domains 2, 3, and 4 are judged at low risk of bias for the immediate, instrument-based balance outcome result assessed.

There is no domain judged at high risk of bias. The two domains with some concerns do not, on the reported evidence alone, warrant escalation to high overall risk of bias.

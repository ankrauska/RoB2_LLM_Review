## RoB 2 assessment

**Study:** Harris and Carney, *Can We Modify Maladaptive Attributions for Fatigue?*  
**Design:** Individually randomized, parallel-group trial.  
**Experimental intervention:** Fatigue-information (FI) sheet describing common non-sleep-related causes of daytime fatigue, read with the investigator.  
**Comparator:** Sleep-control information sheet of comparable amount, containing generic information about sleep but not causes of fatigue.  
**Result assessed:** The effect of assignment to FI versus sleep-control information on the immediate post-intervention **NumberAttributionScore** (proportion of participant-generated fatigue attributions that were sleep-related), assessed through the group-by-time interaction: *F*(1,86) = 5.17, *p* = .026.  
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect).  
**Sources available:** Journal article only. No protocol, statistical analysis plan, trial registry entry, or other pre-specified analysis document was available in the supplied material.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states: “*a Microsoft Excel random number generator was used to randomize participants into the two conditions.*” This describes a sequence-generation process with a random component.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The report does not describe who generated the allocation sequence, whether allocation was centrally administered, whether the investigator recruiting/enrolling participants had access to the randomization list, or any concealment procedure such as sealed opaque envelopes or independent assignment. Random allocation generation alone does not establish allocation concealment.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No information.**  
  The authors state that there were no significant baseline group differences in demographics, insomnia, fatigue, depression, dysfunctional beliefs about sleep, or baseline LAT measures. However, baseline characteristics and scores are not reported separately by randomized group. Therefore, the available report does not permit an independent assessment of the size, direction, or clinical relevance of baseline balance. No evidence of a baseline imbalance is presented, but useful group-specific baseline information is unavailable.

**Judgement and motivation:**  
The allocation sequence was generated using a random-number generator, but the allocation-concealment process was not reported. Under RoB 2, absence of information about whether allocation was concealed prevents a low-risk judgement. The report also does not provide sufficient group-specific baseline data to evaluate whether there were imbalances suggesting a problem with randomization. Therefore, this domain is judged as raising **some concerns**.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Participants received visibly different information: either information about causes of fatigue or generic information about sleep. Blinding of participants was not described and would not be plausible from the intervention procedures as reported.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The investigator “*read the information sheet alongside each participant individually*.” The person delivering the intervention necessarily knew which information sheet was being administered.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report describes a single-session intervention in which the investigator read the allocated information with each participant. It reports no departures from the assigned FI or control information, no contamination between groups, no additional intervention delivered selectively to one group, and no differential co-interventions arising from the trial context.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  The principal analyses were mixed group-by-time ANOVAs using the final analysed sample of 88 participants, with 44 in each randomized group. Participants were analysed by their assigned intervention group. No as-treated analysis, naïve per-protocol analysis, or analysis reclassifying participants according to intervention received was reported.

**Judgement and motivation:**  
Although participants and the investigator delivering the information were aware of allocation, lack of blinding alone does not establish bias due to deviations from intended intervention for the effect of assignment. The article reports no trial-context deviations from the intended FI or control intervention that would have affected the result, and the reported analysis retained participants in their allocated groups. Accordingly, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

**Important boundary of this judgement:**  
The participants’ awareness of the intervention is highly relevant to the validity of their self-reported outcome responses. That concern is assessed under Domain 4, bias in measurement of the outcome, rather than under this domain.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Probably yes.**  
  The article reports that 93 individuals were initially involved, with five individuals excluded from analysis: two were under age 18, one did not complete demographic information, and two did not complete the outcome measures in a manner permitting a total score. The final analysed sample was 88 participants, 44 per group.

  The reported target outcome, NumberAttributionScore, was derived from the LAT. The two participants unable to provide usable outcome scores represent approximately 2% of the initially described 93 participants. The report does not indicate that outcome data were missing for any additional participants. This amount is sufficiently small that, on the information supplied, it is unlikely by itself to have materially changed the continuous outcome result.

- **3.2–3.4 — Not applicable.**  
  Because outcome data were available for nearly all participants for the assessed outcome, further missing-data questions are not required by the RoB 2 algorithm.

**Judgement and motivation:**  
The study provides an account of the five exclusions and retains 88 of 93 described participants. Only two participants were reported as unable to complete the outcome measures in a usable form. For this immediate continuous self-report outcome, the quantity of missing outcome data is small. The report does not provide exclusions separately by randomized group, nor does it explicitly state whether the five exclusions occurred before or after random assignment. This is a reporting limitation. Nevertheless, the small number of missing/unanalyzable outcome observations does not indicate a material risk that missing outcome data biased the assessed result. The domain is therefore judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  The LAT directly asks participants to list factors that account for their personal daytime fatigue, and the NumberAttributionScore is calculated as the proportion of listed attributions classified as sleep-related. This is directly aligned with the construct assessed in the study: self-reported fatigue attributions. The article does not report validation data for this investigator-developed measure, but does not provide evidence that the method was incapable of measuring the stated outcome.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  The same LAT was administered before and immediately after the intervention in both groups. The same coding procedure was reported for both groups. There is no report of different outcome forms, different thresholds, or different post-intervention assessment procedures between groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants? — Yes.**  
  For this participant-reported outcome, the participant is the outcome assessor. Participants knew whether they had just received information about multiple causes of fatigue or generic sleep information. The immediate post-intervention LAT was completed directly after receipt of the assigned information.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Yes.**  
  The FI intervention explicitly provided examples of non-sleep-related fatigue causes. The primary outcome then asked participants to generate and rate possible causes of their own fatigue. Knowledge of the just-provided content could influence which causes participants reported.

- **4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Yes.**  
  The outcome was measured immediately after an unblinded information intervention, and it directly assessed the content addressed by that intervention. The authors themselves acknowledge this limitation: “*it is also possible that demand characteristics or priming played a role in FI participants’ post-intervention responses on the LAT*.” They further state that “*the information-based intervention may have influenced participants to respond in a certain manner*.” This is direct recognition that awareness of intervention content could influence the assessed outcome.

**Judgement and motivation:**  
The result is based on an immediate participant-reported measure of fatigue attributions after participants were told which non-sleep causes of fatigue to consider. Since participants were aware of their assigned information, the outcome was inherently susceptible to priming, demand characteristics, and response changes driven by the content of the intervention rather than a durable or independently assessed change in personal attributions. The authors explicitly identify demand characteristics and priming as possible explanations. Under RoB 2, it is likely that outcome assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention received. This domain is judged at **high risk of bias**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  No protocol, statistical analysis plan, trial registration record, or dated pre-specified analysis document is available in the supplied report. The article describes hypotheses and planned use of mixed ANOVAs, but this does not establish that the analysis plan was finalized before the investigators had access to unblinded outcome data.

- **5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  The study used three related LAT-derived outcomes: NumberAttributionScore, FrequencyScore, and ImportanceScore. The article reports results for all three outcomes, which reduces concern that only one of those three reported measures was selected for reporting. However, without a pre-specified document describing the intended outcome measurement hierarchy, it cannot be determined whether other eligible outcome measures, time points, coding approaches, or measures were assessed but not fully reported.

- **5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The report states that mixed group-by-time ANOVAs were used and presents the interaction results. However, without a protocol or statistical analysis plan, it is not possible to verify whether alternative eligible analyses were conducted—for example, analyses with or without covariate adjustment, alternative scoring or coding decisions, alternative handling of exclusions, or alternative outcome definitions—and whether the reported result was selected after examination of the data.

**Judgement and motivation:**  
The article reports all three specified LAT outcomes, and there is no direct evidence that the NumberAttributionScore result was selectively reported because of its statistical significance or direction. However, there is no prospectively finalized analysis plan or protocol available to verify that the selected outcome and analysis were pre-specified, or to exclude selection from among multiple outcome measurements or analytic approaches. In accordance with RoB 2, this domain is judged as raising **some concerns** rather than high risk, because selective reporting is not demonstrated.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement — **High risk of bias**

The overall judgement is **high risk of bias** because the study is at **high risk of bias in Domain 4, bias in measurement of the outcome**.

The principal result depends on participants’ immediate self-report of the very attributions that the unblinded intervention explicitly instructed them to consider. Participants completed the post-intervention LAT immediately after receiving the information, and the authors acknowledge that demand characteristics and priming may have influenced FI participants’ responses. This creates a high risk that the observed group-by-time difference in reported fatigue attributions was affected by knowledge of the intervention received.

In addition, there are **some concerns** regarding allocation concealment (Domain 1) and selection of the reported result (Domain 5), because allocation concealment and a prospectively finalized analysis plan were not reported or available.

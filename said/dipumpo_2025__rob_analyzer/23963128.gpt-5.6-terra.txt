## RoB 2 assessment

**Target result assessed:** Effect of assignment to **direct, immediately available, or distant fellow supervision** during the first simulated scenario on residents’ subsequent **independent performance in the unsupervised scenario**, measured by the **Ottawa Crisis Management Global Rating Scale (Ottawa GRS)** (Table 3: direct 4.2 [SD 1.1], immediately available 4.5 [1.0], distant 4.1 [1.1]; one-way ANOVA *p* = 0.56).

**Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment to the intervention** (intention-to-treat effect).  
**Design:** Individually randomized, three-arm parallel-group simulation trial.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — No information.**  
  The article states only that “residents were randomized to one of three levels of supervision.” It does not describe how the allocation sequence was generated. There is no information on computer generation, random-number tables, blocking, stratification, or another method containing a random component.

- **1.2 Was allocation concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned? — No information.**  
  The report contains no description of allocation concealment. It does not state that a central randomization system, independent allocation process, pharmacy system, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes were used.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with randomization? — Probably no.**  
  All 53 recruited residents are represented in the three groups (18 direct, 16 immediately available, 19 distant). Table 1 shows differences in baseline characteristics, including age, prior ICU and simulation experience, specialty, and confidence scores, but no imbalance is reported as extreme or clearly incompatible with chance in this relatively small trial. The article does not present evidence of a substantive imbalance in an important prognostic factor that would indicate manipulation or failure of randomization.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Low risk of bias requires adequate allocation concealment. Because neither sequence generation nor allocation concealment is described, the randomization process cannot be verified. The available baseline data do not themselves indicate that randomization failed, but the lack of reporting on the two essential randomization procedures raises **some concerns**.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Residents necessarily knew whether the fellow was physically present from the start of the scenario, available by phone and able to attend within 1 minute, or available only by phone. The interventions were defined by physical proximity and could not be concealed from residents.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the intervention aware of assigned intervention? — Yes.**  
  The supervising fellows necessarily knew the supervision condition they were delivering. The direct-supervision fellow was in the room from the beginning, whereas fellows in the other conditions were initially available by telephone.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The article describes the intended operationalization of all three supervision conditions. Fellows were instructed to “balance the care of the patient and the teaching to the resident the same way they would in real life.” The reported differences in fellow involvement in the immediately available condition—two residents did not call the supervisor, four received telephone advice only, and 10 were assisted at the bedside—are consistent with the intended intervention strategy, which allowed a fellow to be present when needed. Similarly, all distant-supervision residents called their supervisor, as permitted by the distant-supervision condition. The report does not identify protocol-inconsistent, trial-context-induced deviations.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment? — Probably yes.**  
  The prespecified primary analysis for the unsupervised Ottawa GRS was a one-way ANOVA according to randomized level of supervision. The group sizes reported for this outcome remain 18, 16, and 19, totaling all 53 recruited residents. The report does not describe exclusions, reclassification according to the supervision actually received, or an as-treated/per-protocol analysis.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Participants and fellows were aware of supervision assignment, but this was intrinsic to the interventions being studied. For the effect of assignment, awareness creates bias only if protocol-inconsistent deviations arose because of the trial context and were likely to affect the outcome. The article does not report such deviations. The analysis retained the reported randomized group sizes and compared groups by assigned supervision strategy. Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  Independent performance was measured with the Ottawa Crisis Management Global Rating Scale. The authors state that this tool had been validated for assessing residents’ individual performance during ICU simulated scenarios. Two trained independent raters reviewed videotaped performance. Interrater reliability was reported as good (ICC = 0.75).

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  The same Ottawa GRS and videotaped assessment process were used across all three supervision groups during the unsupervised scenario. The article does not report different measurement procedures, thresholds, timing, or assessors by group.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by participants? — No information.**  
  The authors state that two independent raters reviewed and rated videotaped performance, but they do not report whether these raters were blinded to the resident’s initially assigned level of supervision. The report also does not state whether videos were coded, whether rating order was concealed, or whether raters had access to information that could identify the previous supervision condition.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Yes.**  
  The Ottawa GRS is an observer-rated scale covering leadership, problem solving, situational awareness, resource utilization, communication, and overall performance. These outcomes involve professional judgement. If raters knew a resident’s prior supervision condition, knowledge could influence ratings.

- **4.5 Is it likely that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  The article provides no evidence that raters knew group allocation or that ratings were influenced by expectations regarding any supervision condition. However, it also does not provide evidence that blinding was maintained.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The performance scale was appropriate and had reported validation and acceptable interrater reliability. However, the outcome is subjective and observer-rated, and the blinding status of the independent raters is not reported. The absence of information does not establish that ratings were biased, but it leaves a plausible risk that knowledge of prior supervision assignment could have affected assessment. This leads to **some concerns**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **5.1 Were the data analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The article describes a sample-size calculation and states that the “primary analysis” used a one-way ANOVA of the Ottawa GRS during the unsupervised scenario. However, no trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, trial registration record, or dated pre-specified analysis document is provided. Therefore, it cannot be established that the analysis plan was finalized before outcome data were available.

- **5.2 Is the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  Independent performance was assessed using several measures during the unsupervised scenario: Ottawa GRS, individual checklist score, duration of abnormal vital signs, and achievement of definitive management. The article reports all these measures in Table 3. Nevertheless, without a prospectively available protocol or analysis plan, it cannot be verified whether other eligible measurements, scenario-specific measurements, time points, or alternative scale summaries were assessed but not fully reported.

- **5.3 Is the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses? — No information.**  
  The report provides an unadjusted one-way ANOVA and also presents exploratory multivariable regression models. However, there is no pre-specified statistical analysis plan defining the primary analytical model, covariate handling, outcome summaries, or the intended approach to multiple analyses. The article itself notes that its final predictive regression models were selected using the data, and that resulting p-values would be larger than those presented. This does not directly demonstrate selective reporting of the Table 3 Ottawa GRS result, but it confirms that data-dependent analytical choices occurred elsewhere in the study.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The reported Ottawa GRS analysis is described as the primary analysis and the article reports several unsupervised-scenario outcomes. However, no prospectively finalized protocol or statistical analysis plan is available in the report. Consequently, it cannot be determined whether the reported result was selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses on the basis of the findings. This domain is judged as raising **some concerns**.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation for overall judgement:**  
No individual domain is judged at high risk of bias. However, the result has **some concerns in three domains**:

1. **Randomization process:** sequence generation and allocation concealment were not reported.  
2. **Measurement of outcome:** the subjective observer-rated primary outcome was assessed by raters whose blinding to prior supervision assignment was not reported.  
3. **Selection of reported result:** no prospectively finalized protocol or statistical analysis plan was available to verify the prespecified outcome measurement and analysis.

These multiple concerns affect key stages of the study: allocation, assessment of the primary outcome, and selection of the reported analysis. Taken together, they substantially lower confidence in the reported result for subsequent independent performance on the Ottawa GRS. Under RoB 2, the overall judgement is therefore **high risk of bias**.

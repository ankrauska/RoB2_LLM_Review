# ROB 2 Assessment Report: WRAP Randomized Controlled Trial

**Study:** Cook JA, et al. "Results of a Randomized Controlled Trial of Mental Illness Self-management Using Wellness Recovery Action Planning." Schizophr Bull. 2012.

**Outcome assessed:** BSI Global Symptom Severity (primary outcome) — Intention-to-treat effect of assignment to intervention.

---

## 1. Bias Arising from the Randomization Process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- **Sequence generation:** The article states randomization was performed using "a random allocation sequence programmed into CAPI administration software" (Signalling Q1.1 → Yes).
- **Allocation concealment:** The paper explicitly states this allowed "complete allocation concealment up to the point of assignment," with randomization performed by SRL staff at the end of each interview, so neither interviewers nor respondents knew the group assignment until after allocation (Signalling Q1.2 → Yes).
- **Baseline imbalance:** The authors state "the success of randomization was confirmed by the absence of statistically significant differences by study condition at baseline on all characteristics" (Table 1 data support this — no imbalance is evident) (Signalling Q1.3 → No).
- Despite adequate concealment and no evidence of baseline imbalance, the domain is rated "Some concerns" rather than "Low" because **no explicit description of the method used to generate the randomization sequence itself** (e.g., whether it was simple randomization, blocked, or stratified) is provided beyond "programmed into CAPI software." This lack of detail about how randomness was technically implemented within the software introduces ambiguity, even though the process appears procedurally sound.

**Limitation/Ambiguity:** The specific algorithm used within the CAPI software to generate the random sequence is not detailed, precluding a "Low risk" judgment despite good concealment practices.

---

## 2. Bias Due to Deviations from Intended Interventions

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- **Blinding of participants/personnel:** Participants and WRAP facilitators were necessarily aware of group assignment (this is an unblinded behavioral intervention comparing an active peer-led course to a wait-list). Only outcome assessors (interviewers) were blinded (Signalling Q2.1 & 2.2 → Yes, participants/personnel aware).
- **Deviations arising from trial context:** A substantial minority of control-condition subjects (41.9%–44.9%) reported attending peer-led support groups outside the study during the trial period. This is explicitly acknowledged by the authors as a "potential confound," since it may have exposed control participants "to some of the same active ingredients as those contained in the WRAP intervention." This constitutes a **non-protocol intervention** likely arising from the trial context (participants on a waitlist seeking similar support) (Signalling Q2.3 → Yes).
- **Effect on outcome and balance:** These deviations were **not balanced** between groups (peer support was much more available/utilized as a substitute in controls than experimentals received extra non-protocol input), and the authors themselves state this "may have underestimated the effects of WRAP." However, because this deviation would bias the result *conservatively* (toward the null, favoring underestimation rather than overestimation of the intervention's effect), and because the authors reran models controlling for exposure to peer support groups without a substantive change in results (Q2.4/2.5 → deviations likely affected outcome but sensitivity analysis suggests limited impact on point estimate direction).
- **Analysis approach:** The study explicitly used a full intention-to-treat approach: "all randomized participants" were retained in the MIXREG regression models regardless of intervention receipt (233/276 intervention arm actually received the intervention, but all 251 analyzed subjects from that arm entered ITT analysis) (Signalling Q2.6 → Yes, appropriate ITT analysis was used).

Given non-protocol contamination in the control group (identified and partially addressed via sensitivity analysis) but an appropriate ITT analytic approach, the domain is judged "Some concerns" rather than "Low" or "High."

**Limitation/Ambiguity:** The magnitude and content of "peer support" received by control participants is not well characterized (frequency, intensity, or specific overlap with WRAP content), making it impossible to fully quantify the resulting bias.

---

## 3. Bias Due to Missing Outcome Data

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:**
- Data available for the vast majority of participants: 88.2% completed T2 and 86.3% completed T3, for a combined attrition of only ~13.7% (Signalling Q3.1 — borderline, but very close to "nearly all").
- Critically, follow-up rates did **not differ significantly by study condition**: at T2, 89.2% (intervention) vs. 87.3% (control) completed (χ²=0.49, p=.29); at T3, 87.6% vs. 85.1% (χ²=0.39, p=.23). This indicates missingness is unlikely to be differentially related to intervention assignment (Signalling Q3.3/3.4 → unlikely that missingness depends on true outcome value in a way that differs by group).
- The paper explicitly used an ITT design including "all randomized participants" in MIXREG models, which use maximum-likelihood-based mixed-effects regression — a method that appropriately handles missing data under the assumption of data missing at random, rather than requiring complete-case analysis.
- Reasons for attrition (death, ill health, moving away, formal withdrawal) were reported and do not suggest differential informative dropout related to the outcome (i.e., not preferentially due to worsening or improving symptoms).

Given comparable and modest attrition across groups, and use of a full-information ITT/mixed-model estimation approach, risk of bias due to missing outcome data is judged **Low**.

---

## 4. Bias in Measurement of the Outcome

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- **Appropriateness of measurement method:** The BSI is a validated, widely used self-report instrument with "high concordance with clinician symptom assessment," so the method itself is not inappropriate (Signalling Q4.1 → No).
- **Comparability of measurement across groups:** The same BSI instrument, administered via identical structured telephone interviews at the same intervals, was used for both study arms (Signalling Q4.2 → No, measurement did not differ between groups).
- **Awareness of outcome assessors:** This is a **participant-reported outcome** — the outcome assessor is the participant, per RoB2 definitions for self-report scales. Interviewers were blinded to condition, but the participants themselves (the actual "outcome assessors" for the BSI) could not be blinded to their own study condition, since they knew whether they had attended WRAP or were waitlisted (Signalling Q4.3 → Yes, aware).
- **Potential and likely influence:** Because participants' self-reported symptom ratings could plausibly be influenced by their expectations/hopes regarding an active intervention versus a waitlist condition (i.e., psychological/placebo-type expectancy effects), there is a real possibility that assessment of this subjective outcome was influenced by knowledge of assignment (Signalling Q4.4 → Yes). However, there is no direct evidence (e.g., large unexplained discrepancies, implausible effect sizes) that this influence was substantial or dominant, and the effect sizes reported were modest and clinically plausible, and blinding of interviewers (with only 4% detected unblinding) partially mitigates concern (Signalling Q4.5 → Probably No/uncertain).

This results in a judgment of "Some concerns" rather than "High," since influence is plausible but not clearly demonstrated to have driven the result, and interviewer-level blinding was well maintained and monitored.

**Limitation/Ambiguity:** The paper cannot fully rule out participant expectancy bias inherent to unblinded self-report psychological outcomes in a wait-list design; this is an inherent structural limitation of the design rather than a reporting deficiency.

## 5. Bias in Selection of the Reported Result

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:**
- The trial was prospectively registered (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01024569), and the authors explicitly state: "all outcomes, hypotheses, and statistical analyses presented here were prespecified at the time the proposal was submitted to the federal government for funding consideration" (Signalling Q5.1 → Yes, pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded data available).
- The primary outcome (BSI Global Severity Index/Positive Symptom Total) and secondary outcomes (Hope Scale total and subscales, WHOQOL-BREF environment subscale) are all reported in full, including non-significant results (e.g., the "pathways" subscale of Hope showed no significant effect and this null result was transparently reported rather than omitted) (Signalling Q5.2 → No evidence of selective outcome-measurement reporting).
- A single, consistent analytic model (MIXREG mixed-effects random regression) was applied uniformly across all outcomes, with results reported regardless of statistical significance; sensitivity analyses (controlling for peer-support exposure) were also reported transparently and shown not to alter conclusions (Signalling Q5.3 → No evidence of selective analysis reporting).
- The protocol-registered outcomes align with those reported in the results section and in the linked registry results database.

Given full, transparent reporting of both significant and non-significant results consistent with pre-specified registration, this domain is judged **Low risk**.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall Judgment: Some concerns**

**Rationale (per RoB 2 mapping rules):**
- Domain 1 (Randomization): Some concerns
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions): Some concerns
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data): Low risk
- Domain 4 (Measurement of outcome): Some concerns
- Domain 5 (Selection of reported result): Low risk

No domain was judged to be at **High risk of bias**. However, **three domains (1, 2, and 4) were independently judged as "Some concerns."** Per RoB 2 guidance, "Some concerns in multiple domains may lead review authors to decide on an overall judgement of 'High' risk of bias." In this case, I have determined that the multiple "Some concerns" domains do **not** cumulatively rise to a "High" risk judgment, because:
1. The concerns in Domain 1 relate only to incomplete reporting of the specific randomization algorithm, not evidence of actual failure;
2. The concern in Domain 2 (control-group contamination via peer support) is explicitly addressed via sensitivity analysis by the authors, with no substantive change in results, and the likely direction of this bias is conservative (toward the null, underestimating WRAP's effect) rather than inflating it;
3. The concern in Domain 4 is an inherent structural feature of self-reported subjective outcomes in unblinded psychosocial trials, partially mitigated by blinded interviewers and low detected unblinding (4%).

Therefore, per Cochrane RoB 2 criteria, the overall judgment is **"Some concerns"** — the study raises legitimate concerns in multiple domains that should be considered when interpreting the size and certainty of the treatment effect, but does not meet the threshold for "High risk of bias" for this outcome.

---

**Stated Limitations/Ambiguities Found During Assessment (as required by RoB2 protocol, not assumptions):**
1. Precise sequence-generation algorithm within the CAPI randomization software is not described in detail.
2. The extent, frequency, and content overlap of peer-support-group attendance among control participants (used to assess deviation from intended intervention) is not quantified beyond overall attendance percentages.
3. No independent/objective corroboration of self-reported psychiatric symptom outcomes was collected (explicitly acknowledged as a limitation by the study authors themselves).

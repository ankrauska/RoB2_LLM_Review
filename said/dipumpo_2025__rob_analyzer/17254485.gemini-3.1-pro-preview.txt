Based on a thorough and detailed evaluation of the provided article using the RoB 2 tool, here is the structured Risk of Bias assessment:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study reports that participants were randomly assigned to the control or therapy group using a "random number table method" generated via Microsoft Excel, which indicates a random sequence generation process. However, there is no information provided regarding allocation concealment. Without a mechanism to conceal the allocation sequence (e.g., sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes or central randomization), personnel enrolling participants could potentially foresee the assigned groups. Baseline characteristics appear balanced, but the lack of allocation concealment raises some concerns.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (Effect of assignment to intervention)**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** Due to the nature of the interventions (immediate combined embolization and endoscopic variceal ligation versus embolization with delayed ligation), participants and clinical personnel could not be blinded to the assigned intervention. While there is no explicit evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that arose specifically because of the trial context, the study does not explicitly state whether an Intention-to-Treat (ITT) analysis was performed. In the control group, patients who failed initial embolization received immediate ligation, which reflects real-world clinical necessity rather than a protocol deviation, but the lack of blinding and unclear analytic strategy (ITT vs per-protocol) warrants some concerns. 

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study reports outcome data (rate of emergency hemostasis and rate of early rebleeding) for all randomized patients. The control group had 82 patients, and the therapy group had 90 patients; all 172 patients are accounted for in the initial hemostasis results. The follow-up for the early rebleeding outcome appears to be complete for those who achieved initial hemostasis. There is no evidence of missing outcome data or loss to follow-up that could bias the results.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The outcomes evaluated are emergency hemostasis and early rebleeding. These outcomes are defined by objective clinical and physiological criteria (e.g., re-hematemesis, dark stools, hemoglobin decrease of >20 g/L, signs of hypovolemic shock, or need for blood transfusion). Although outcome assessors were likely aware of the intervention received due to the lack of blinding, the objective nature of these outcomes makes it highly unlikely that the assessment was influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study does not reference a pre-published trial protocol, clinical trials registry entry, or a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available. Consequently, it is not fully possible to verify whether the reported outcomes and analyses were selected from multiple measurements or analyses. Although the outcomes reported (hemostasis and rebleeding) are standard and expected for this clinical condition, the absence of a pre-specified plan raises some concerns regarding the selection of the reported results.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to have some concerns across multiple domains (randomization process due to lack of allocation concealment, deviations from intended interventions due to lack of blinding and unclear ITT, and selection of the reported result due to lack of a pre-specified protocol). In accordance with the RoB 2 guidelines, having "Some concerns" in multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result leads to an overall judgment of High risk of bias.

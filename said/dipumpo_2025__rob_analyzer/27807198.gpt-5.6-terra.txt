## RoB 2 assessment: randomized double-blind phase II comparison of doxycycline versus placebo

**Result assessed:** Effect of assignment to doxycycline 100 mg/day versus placebo on **survival** in the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II trial. The numerical result assessed is the Cox-model hazard ratio reported for the randomized comparison: **HR 0.841, 95% CI 0.238 to 2.966**.  

**Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect).  

**Sources used:** Article report, study flow diagram/table, and the supplied EudraCT registry/protocol record. The observational compassionate-use comparison and the meta-analysis combining it with the randomized study are not randomized-trial results and are not used to judge this RCT result.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — No information.**  
  The article states that patients “were randomly assigned (ratio 1:1)” to doxycycline or placebo. However, it does not describe how the sequence was generated: there is no statement of computer generation, random-number table, random permuted blocks, minimization, or another process containing a random element. The registry confirms that the study was randomized but likewise does not provide the sequence-generation method. Under RoB 2, the mere assertion that a study was randomized is insufficient to confirm that the allocation sequence was random.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The article reports that “the randomisation list and the study medication were administered by the Charité, Universitätsmedizin, Berlin.” This identifies an external institution involved in administration, but does not establish that allocation was centrally assigned only after irrevocable participant enrolment, or otherwise concealed from persons recruiting participants. No adequate description of a remote allocation system, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or equivalent allocation-concealment procedure is reported.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — Probably no.**  
  Important baseline differences were present in this extremely small trial: median age at onset was 58 years in the doxycycline group and 72 years in the placebo group; median MMSE was 21 versus 16; and median time from onset to treatment/placebo was 95 versus 152 days. These differences are clinically relevant prognostic differences. However, the randomized sample comprised only 7 doxycycline and 5 analysed placebo participants. The report does not provide statistical evidence that these differences were beyond what could occur by chance, nor does it provide evidence of manipulation or a failed randomization process. Therefore, the reported baseline differences do not establish a randomization failure, but they reinforce the uncertainty created by the unreported sequence-generation and concealment methods.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Neither random sequence generation nor allocation concealment is adequately described. There is no direct evidence that allocation was not random or was unconcealed, and the observed baseline imbalances cannot definitively be distinguished from chance imbalances in a trial of this size. Following the RoB 2 algorithm, absent information about allocation concealment leads to **some concerns** when there is no demonstrated baseline indication of a failed randomization process.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably no.**  
  The article describes the trial as “double-blinded” and placebo-controlled. The registry also records the trial as double blind, with doxycycline and placebo each administered as hard oral capsules. No report indicates that participants were unblinded.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably no.**  
  The article and registry identify the study as double blinded. The randomization list and medication were administered through Charité, rather than being described as administered by the recruiting study physician. No information indicates that caregivers or those delivering the study medication knew assignments.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Not applicable.**  
  This question is not required because participants and trial personnel were apparently blinded.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Probably yes.**  
  The randomized comparison was analysed using a Cox proportional-hazards model according to assigned doxycycline versus placebo group. The four doxycycline-group participants who discontinued treatment were still included in the reported survival analysis: the article reports 7 treated participants in the analysis despite four discontinuations. One placebo participant was excluded because of dropout/loss, which is a missing-outcome-data issue addressed under Domain 3 rather than a deviation-from-intended-intervention exclusion. There is no indication that participants were analysed according to treatment actually received or that non-adherent participants were excluded from their randomized group.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The study was reported as double blind and placebo controlled, and there is no evidence of trial-context deviations from protocol that could have differentially affected survival. The reported Cox analysis retained the seven randomized doxycycline participants despite four treatment discontinuations and analysed groups according to their original random allocation. The omission of one placebo participant because of missing survival data is evaluated in Domain 3, not this domain. Therefore, for the effect of assignment to doxycycline versus placebo, this domain is at **low risk of bias**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — No.**  
  Thirteen participants were randomized: 7 to doxycycline and 6 to placebo. The flow diagram reports that one placebo recipient was lost and excluded from analysis. Thus, survival analysis included 7/7 doxycycline participants but only 5/6 placebo participants. In a trial with only 13 randomized participants and very few events expected to determine the survival estimate, a single omitted participant could materially affect the hazard ratio and confidence interval. The outcome data therefore were not available for all or nearly all randomized participants in the RoB 2 sense.

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — No.**  
  No sensitivity analysis, analysis accounting for missing survival data, or evidence that the omitted participant’s outcome could not materially affect the result is reported.

- **3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value? — Probably yes.**  
  The missing participant was described as having dropped out/lost to follow-up. The article does not state why this occurred, whether the participant was alive at the end of study, or the date and circumstances of their last contact. In a rapidly fatal disease, withdrawal or loss to follow-up may be related to clinical deterioration, survival status, or factors associated with survival. The reported information cannot rule out such a relationship.

- **3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value? — Yes.**  
  Missing survival data occurred only in the placebo group: 1/6 placebo participants versus 0/7 doxycycline participants. This differential missingness is especially consequential because the randomized comparison is based on only 12 analysed participants and because survival is the primary outcome. No reason for the loss and no outcome information for the missing placebo participant are reported. Under RoB 2, differential proportions of missing outcome data between groups support a judgement that missingness was likely related to the outcome’s true value.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The sole placebo-group dropout was excluded from the survival analysis, creating differential missing outcome data between groups. The report gives no reason for the dropout, no survival status, no censoring details, and no sensitivity analysis. Given the small total sample, one missing placebo participant can have a substantial effect on the survival estimate. The conditions for a RoB 2 **high risk of bias** judgement due to missing outcome data are therefore met.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  The primary outcome was survival, measured from randomization to death or end of study in the randomized trial. Death is an objective outcome, and no evidence suggests that the method used to determine death was unsuitable.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  Both groups underwent the same trial follow-up schedule: study visits every two months during the first six months and then every three months, with caregiver telephone contacts every two weeks. No group-specific surveillance procedure for death ascertainment is reported.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — No information.**  
  The article reports double blinding but does not specifically identify the person or source used to ascertain death dates or explicitly state that outcome assessors were blinded.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  Survival/death is an observer-reported outcome not requiring clinical judgement as to whether the event occurred. Knowledge of allocation is not expected to alter whether death occurred or its basic ascertainment, provided the same follow-up processes are used.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Survival is an objective outcome, the study used a common follow-up schedule across groups, and there is no evidence that death ascertainment differed by assignment. Although blinding of the individual who determined death status/date is not explicitly documented, assessment of death is not plausibly influenced by knowledge of doxycycline versus placebo assignment. This domain is therefore at **low risk of bias**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No.**  
  The EudraCT protocol specifies the primary endpoint as survival time **from symptom onset** until death. In contrast, the article explicitly defines randomized-trial survival follow-up as beginning at **randomization/first study medication**, reporting the randomized Cox-model HR using this analysis. Thus, the reported randomized primary survival result does not follow the prespecified endpoint time origin contained in the trial registry/protocol.

  The supplied registry record does not include a detailed, date-stamped statistical analysis plan specifying the Cox model, covariate adjustment, handling of loss to follow-up, censoring strategy, or rationale for changing the time origin from disease onset to randomization. Therefore, the reported analysis cannot be verified as conforming to a prespecified analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available.

- **5.2 Was the result likely selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — Probably no.**  
  The protocol and article identify survival as the primary outcome. The protocol’s definition differs from the published RCT analysis with respect to the time origin, but the available sources do not demonstrate that multiple survival measurements were analysed and that the randomization-based result was selected because it was more favourable. The unexplained change is nevertheless relevant to Question 5.1.

- **5.3 Was the reported result likely selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  No full statistical analysis plan is available, and the article does not state whether alternative analyses were performed—for example, survival from symptom onset versus randomization, adjusted versus unadjusted Cox models, or different approaches to the missing placebo participant. Because survival data can be analysed in multiple eligible ways, and the analysis intentions are not sufficiently documented, this cannot be assessed as low risk.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The registered protocol specifies survival from symptom onset as the primary endpoint, whereas the published randomized comparison uses survival from randomization/first medication. The article gives no explanation for this change and no prespecified statistical analysis plan is available. There is not enough evidence to conclude that the published result was selected because of its magnitude, direction, or statistical significance; therefore, the appropriate RoB 2 judgement is **some concerns**, rather than high risk.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation for overall judgement:**  
The randomized survival result is at **high risk of bias** in the domain of **missing outcome data**. One of six placebo-assigned participants was lost and excluded from the survival analysis, whereas all seven doxycycline-assigned participants were analysed. In this 13-participant trial, the missing placebo survival outcome could materially affect the estimated hazard ratio. No reason for dropout, survival status, censoring information, or sensitivity analysis was provided.

In addition, the result raises **some concerns** for the randomization process because sequence generation and allocation concealment were insufficiently described, and for selection of the reported result because the published randomized survival analysis began at randomization rather than at symptom onset as specified in the registry/protocol.

Under the prespecified RoB 2 overall rules, **high risk of bias in at least one domain requires an overall judgement of High risk of bias**.

Based on the detailed analysis of the provided randomized controlled trial article using the RoB 2 tool, here is the structured Risk of Bias (ROB) assessment:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
- **Motivation:** The allocation sequence was adequately generated using computer random sequence generation with block sizes of 6 and 4 (Signalling Question 1.1 = Yes). Allocation concealment was properly achieved using sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes (SQ 1.2 = Yes). Baseline characteristics between the two groups were evaluated (Table I) and showed no substantial imbalances that would suggest a problem with the randomization process (SQ 1.3 = No).

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (Effect of assignment to intervention)**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
- **Motivation:** While participants and personnel could not be blinded to the intervention due to its nature (SQ 2.1 and 2.2 = Yes), there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context (SQ 2.3 = No). The study analyzed all 32 randomized participants without any post-randomization exclusions or crossovers, acting in accordance with intention-to-treat principles (SQ 2.6 = Yes).

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
- **Motivation:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants. The study started with 32 patients (16 in each group), and there were zero dropouts or losses to follow-up (Figure 4: CONSORT flow diagram). All 32 surveys were successfully retrieved and analyzed (SQ 3.1 = Yes).

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** High risk of bias.
- **Motivation:** The primary and secondary outcomes were assessed via patient-reported questionnaires (subjective outcomes such as pain, acceptance, and satisfaction). Since blinding of the participants was impossible, the outcome assessors (the participants themselves) were fully aware of the intervention received (SQ 4.3 = Yes). Because one group received an invasive surgical adjunct (mini-implants inserted under local anesthesia) and the other did not, the subjective assessment of pain, swelling, and satisfaction is highly likely to have been influenced by the participants' knowledge of receiving this more invasive procedure (SQ 4.4 and 4.5 = Yes). 

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns.
- **Motivation:** The trial was not registered, and the protocol was not published prior to trial commencement. Therefore, there is no pre-specified statistical analysis plan available to confirm that the data were analyzed exactly as originally intended before unblinded outcome data became available (SQ 5.1 = No/No Information). Although the reported outcomes match the aims described in the methods, the lack of a pre-published protocol prevents a definitive conclusion that the reported results were not selected from multiple analyses or outcome measurements (SQ 5.2 and 5.3 = No information).

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** High risk of bias.
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at high risk of bias overall because it is at high risk of bias in at least one domain (Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome). The unblinded nature of the study, combined with entirely subjective, patient-reported outcomes comparing an invasive (mini-implants) versus non-invasive intervention, substantially lowers confidence in the results due to the high likelihood of measurement bias. Additionally, there are some concerns regarding the selection of the reported result due to the lack of prior trial registration and protocol publication.

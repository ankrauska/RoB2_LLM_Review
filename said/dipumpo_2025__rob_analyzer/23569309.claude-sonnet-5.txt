# RoB 2 Assessment Report
**Study:** KCSG-BR07-02 (Park et al., J Clin Oncol 2013) — Phase III RCT of maintenance paclitaxel/gemcitabine (PG) vs. observation after 6 cycles of first-line PG in metastatic breast cancer
**Outcome assessed:** Progression-free survival (PFS) after randomization — the primary endpoint
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect), as confirmed by the statement "All randomly assigned patients were included in the efficacy analysis according to the intent-to-treat principle"

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions/evidence:**
- Sequence generation: "The random permutation method was used for random assignment." → Random component present (Yes).
- Allocation concealment: "Registration and random assignment were coordinated centrally at the KCSG data center." This is a central, third-party randomization system independent of enrolling investigators, meeting the criteria for adequate concealment (Yes).
- Baseline imbalance: Table 1 shows that of ~17 baseline comparisons, only one ("Lung, hematogenous" metastases, P=.029) reached conventional statistical significance. A single significant result among many comparisons is compatible with chance (expected ~1 in 20 comparisons to be significant by chance). No imbalance in key prognostic factors (HR status, visceral disease, number of metastatic sites, ECOG PS, response to induction) was observed, and none is large enough or implausible enough (no P<.001) to suggest a randomization failure.

**Judgment rationale:** Adequate concealment + random sequence + baseline differences compatible with chance → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention) — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions/evidence:**
- The trial was open-label with no masking of participants or personnel (design module explicitly states "masking: NONE"; also implied by the nature of the intervention — active chemotherapy vs. observation, no placebo). Thus participants and treating physicians were aware of allocated intervention.
- Deviations arising from trial context: 33/116 (28%) patients in the maintenance arm discontinued chemotherapy "at the physician's discretion" in the absence of documented progression, compared with only 6/115 in the observation arm undergoing an early switch/local treatment (Table 2). This represents an unbalanced deviation from protocol-specified continuation until progression, plausibly influenced by clinician/patient awareness of ongoing toxicity and treatment assignment (a trial-context-driven deviation).
- Importantly, there was no cross-over of the observation arm to PG chemotherapy after progression ("PG combination chemotherapy was not given to any patient in the observation group after progression, which means that there was no crossover"), limiting one major source of dilution bias.
- Analysis: An intention-to-treat analysis was used, including all randomized participants, regardless of adherence or physician-directed early discontinuation. This satisfies the criterion for an "appropriate analysis" for the effect of assignment.

**Judgment rationale:** Although an appropriate ITT analysis was used (Part 2 = Low), the open-label design combined with unbalanced, physician-driven treatment discontinuations that could plausibly affect the outcome (Part 1) creates non-trivial concern. Per the RoB2 algorithm, unbalanced deviations arising from trial context in an unblinded study, even alongside appropriate ITT analysis, warrants at least "Some concerns." There is no clear evidence that these deviations were of low impact, and the paper does not report a sensitivity analysis addressing this.

**Limitation/ambiguity noted:** The report does not explicitly quantify the impact of the "physician discretion" discontinuations on the effect estimate, nor does it clarify whether this decision-making was protocol-defined or ad hoc — this ambiguity is reported as a limitation of the assessment rather than assumed.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions/evidence:**
- The CONSORT flow (Fig 1B) and Table 2 account for all 231 randomized patients with documented reasons for discontinuation (progression, withdrawal, adverse events, physician's discretion, other) in both arms.
- PFS was calculated "from the date of random assignment to the documented date of disease progression or the last visit date," with censoring applied appropriately for patients lost to follow-up, consistent with an ITT analysis incorporating all randomized patients (with censoring, not exclusion).
- There is no indication that a substantial number of participants had outcome data missing outright (as opposed to being censored per standard time-to-event methodology), and reasons for discontinuation/censoring do not appear differentially informative for one arm versus the other in a manner likely to bias PFS materially.

**Judgment rationale:** Outcome data (PFS) were available for essentially all randomized participants through appropriate censoring methodology → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions/evidence:**
- The method of measurement (RECIST version 1.0 imaging-based assessment) is appropriate in principle (not inherently invalid) — Q4.1: No.
- However, "PFS and the duration of response were assessed by investigators," and the trial had no blinding of outcome assessors (masking: NONE). Because the maintenance arm patients continued to attend for chemotherapy visits while observation-arm patients might have different follow-up intensity, and because progression determination via RECIST is a judgment-based radiological/clinical assessment (an "observer-reported outcome involving some judgement"), knowledge of intervention assignment could plausibly influence the timing or threshold for declaring progression.
- Q4.2: Ascertainment of the outcome could have differed between groups — the trial does not report that imaging/assessment schedules or thresholds were identical and independently verified, and no blinded independent central radiologic review is mentioned.
- Q4.3: Outcome assessors (investigators) were aware of the intervention received (Yes, given open-label design and lack of any blinded/independent assessment committee).
- Q4.4/4.5: Assessment of PFS is highly susceptible to influence from knowledge of treatment allocation (investigators aware that maintenance-arm patients were still on active treatment could be more inclined to delay progression calls, or conversely, closer surveillance of the maintenance arm through ongoing visits could lead to different detection patterns compared with the observation arm, which had less frequent contact). No safeguards (blinded central review, blinded outcome adjudication committee) were reported to mitigate this.

**Judgment rationale:** For a subjective, judgment-based outcome (radiographic assessment of progression) assessed by unblinded investigators without independent/blinded adjudication, the RoB2 algorithm defaults to **High risk of bias**, since knowledge of intervention likely could have influenced the outcome assessment and there is no evidence mitigating this concern (e.g., blinded central review).

**Limitation/ambiguity noted:** The manuscript does not explicitly state whether a blinded independent review committee assessed radiologic images; this is a reporting ambiguity, and in its absence, per RoB2 guidance, the default assumption is that outcome assessors were aware of allocation and could have been influenced, given the fully open-label design.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions/evidence:**
- The registered protocol (NCT00561119) pre-specifies PFS as the primary outcome with a defined time frame, matching the reported primary endpoint and the sample-size calculation described in the Statistical Methods section (90% power, 5% one-sided α, 20% difference in 6-month PFS rate).
- Secondary outcomes (OS, QoL, toxicity, duration of response) are pre-specified in the registry and are reported consistently in the paper (with QoL explicitly deferred to "a separate article," which is a pre-announced, transparent deferral rather than a selective suppression based on results).
- The statistical analysis method (Kaplan-Meier/log-rank, Cox model with pre-specified stratification covariates: visceral disease, age, menopausal status, performance status, number of metastatic sites, prior adjuvant taxane, disease response, HR status) is described in a single, consistent way without evidence of multiple alternative analyses being tried and only the most favorable reported.
- No indication of selective outcome-measurement reporting (e.g., different definitions/timepoints of PFS reported selectively) was found.

**Judgment rationale:** The reported PFS result corresponds to the pre-specified primary analysis with no evidence of selective reporting from multiple candidate analyses or measurements → **Low risk of bias**.

**Limitation/ambiguity noted:** A full, date-stamped statistical analysis plan (SAP) was not available for direct comparison; the judgment relies on consistency between the registered protocol, the methods section, and the reported results, which the RoB2 guidance considers acceptable ("Probably yes"/"Probably no" judgments) in the absence of a full SAP, but this is reported as a limitation of certainty rather than an assumption of fact.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement — **High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** Per RoB2 rules, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted when at least one domain is judged to be at high risk of bias. In this assessment:
- Domain 1 (randomization): Low risk
- Domain 2 (deviations from intended interventions): Some concerns
- Domain 3 (missing outcome data): Low risk
- Domain 4 (measurement of the outcome): **High risk**
- Domain 5 (selection of the reported result): Low risk

Because Domain 4 (measurement of the outcome) is judged at high risk of bias — driven by the open-label design combined with unblinded, judgment-based investigator assessment of the primary outcome (PFS) without independent/blinded radiological adjudication — the overall result for the primary PFS outcome must be classified as:

### **Overall Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Key drivers of this judgement:**
1. Lack of blinding of outcome assessors for a subjective, judgment-dependent endpoint (PFS via RECIST), with no reported blinded independent review — the principal driver of the "High" rating.
2. Compounding concern from Domain 2 (unblinded trial context leading to unbalanced, physician-directed early discontinuations in the maintenance arm), which, while mitigated by appropriate ITT analysis, still contributes "Some concerns" and reduces overall confidence in the result.

**Reported limitations/ambiguities of this assessment (explicitly, as required):**
- The manuscript does not specify whether an independent, blinded radiology review committee adjudicated progression events; absence of this information was treated conservatively per RoB2 guidance, contributing to the high-risk judgement in Domain 4.
- No full statistical analysis plan (SAP) date-stamped prior to unblinding was available for direct verification against the registered protocol in Domain 5; consistency between protocol and publication was used as the basis for judgement.
- The exact clinical rationale and pre-specification (or lack thereof) of "physician's discretion" as an allowable reason for treatment discontinuation in the maintenance arm was not detailed in the article, limiting precise characterization of Domain 2.

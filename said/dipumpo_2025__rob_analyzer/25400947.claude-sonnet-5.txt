# ROB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** "Using Celecoxib for the Suppression of Preterm Labor Instead of Magnesium Sulfate"
**Design:** Individually randomized parallel-group trial (single-blinded, as stated by authors)
**Comparison:** Experimental = Celecoxib (100 mg orally q12h); Comparator = MgSO4 (4g IV loading + 1g/hr maintenance)
**Outcome assessed:** Suppression of preterm labor / delivery before 48 hours (Table 1)
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (as reported — presumed intention-to-treat, since all 300/300 randomized participants appear in the final analysis)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **HIGH RISK**

- **Sequence generation (1.1):** The report states only that patients were "divided into two groups by simple random sampling." No description of the actual method used (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random number table) is provided. This is treated as **No information/Probably yes** at best, given the vague terminology.
- **Allocation concealment (1.2):** No information whatsoever is provided regarding concealment mechanisms (no sealed envelopes, no central randomization, no third-party allocation described). Judged as **No information**.
- **Baseline imbalance (1.3):** While age, parity, and history of preterm labor were balanced (P=0.194, 0.99, 1, respectively), **Table 3 shows a highly significant difference in baseline cervical effacement between groups (35.93% vs 42%, P≤0.0001)**. Cervical effacement is a key prognostic factor directly related to the probability of successful tocolysis/response to treatment. A difference this extreme (P≤0.0001) is very unlikely to be due to chance and is unlikely to be a chance finding; it raises a strong signal of a problem in the randomization process (e.g., failure of concealment allowing selective enrollment/allocation).

**Motivation for judgement:** Per RoB2 criteria, an imbalance in a key prognostic factor with a P-value this extreme, combined with the complete absence of information on allocation concealment, meets criteria for **High risk of bias** in this domain (Table 3 criteria: "baseline differences suggest a problem with randomization" together with no assurance of concealment).

**Limitation/ambiguity to report:** The manuscript does not clarify how "simple random sampling" was operationalized, nor does it describe any concealment procedure. This ambiguity is treated conservatively (as a marker of risk) rather than assumed to be adequate.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **SOME CONCERNS**

- **Awareness of assignment (2.1/2.2):** The two interventions differ fundamentally in route and mode of administration (IV infusion with toxicity monitoring vs. oral capsule) — it is not feasible for participants, treating physicians, or nursing staff to be unaware of the assigned intervention. Therefore, participants and personnel were **not blinded** (Yes/Probably yes).
- **Deviations arising from trial context (2.3–2.5):** The article does not report whether unblinded status led to differential co-interventions, differential thresholds for declaring "treatment failure," or differential ancillary care between arms. No explicit information is given on this point.
- **Analysis approach (2.6):** All 300 randomized participants per arm appear to have been retained in the analysis (Tables 1–3), consistent with an ITT-type analysis; no participants were reported as excluded post-randomization.

**Motivation for judgement:** Because blinding of participants/personnel was not possible/attempted and no information is available on whether this lack of blinding led to biased/differential co-interventions or decision-making regarding "arrest of contraction" (which is partly a clinical judgment), this domain is judged as **Some concerns** rather than Low or High, reflecting genuine ambiguity rather than a confirmed high-risk deviation.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **LOW RISK**

- Outcome data (delivery before/after 48 hours) are reported for all 300 participants in each arm (Table 1); no dropouts, withdrawals, or missing outcome data are mentioned anywhere in the manuscript.

**Motivation for judgement:** Since outcome data appear to be available for essentially all randomized participants, the risk of bias due to missing outcome data is **Low**.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **SOME CONCERNS**

- **Appropriateness of measurement method (4.1):** Delivery timing itself is an objective, easily verifiable outcome. However, the process leading to the classification of "successful suppression" also depended on repeated clinical assessment of uterine contractions, cervical dilation, and effacement — outcomes that require clinical judgment and were not stated to be assessed by blinded personnel.
- **Differential ascertainment (4.2):** Monitoring intensity differed substantially between arms (MgSO4 patients required close monitoring for toxicity — deep tendon reflexes, urine output, respiratory rate — whereas celecoxib patients were monitored for GI/skin symptoms). This creates potential for differential clinical attention/ascertainment between groups.
- **Blinding of assessors (4.3–4.5):** The study is described as "single-blinded," but the manuscript never specifies who was blinded (patient, assessor, or caregiver). Given the visibly different routes of administration (IV vs oral) and monitoring regimens, it is highly likely that the clinicians assessing "arrest of contraction" (an outcome involving clinical judgement) were aware of group assignment, and this awareness could plausibly influence assessment/decision-making (e.g., threshold for declaring failure).

**Motivation for judgement:** Because the outcome assessment plausibly involved unblinded clinical judgment, but there is no clear evidence that this influenced the result systematically, the domain is judged as **Some concerns** rather than High risk.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **SOME CONCERNS**

- No trial registration number, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced anywhere in the article.
- It cannot be determined whether other outcome measures/timepoints/analyses were collected but not reported, or whether the presented result (delivery before/after 48 hours, along with dilatation/effacement comparisons) was selected from among multiple possible analyses.
- The outcomes reported are plausible/expected core outcomes for this type of tocolysis trial, and no internal inconsistencies (e.g., differing outcome definitions across sections) are evident within the text.

**Motivation for judgement:** In the absence of any protocol/registration data to verify pre-specification, and per RoB2 guidance, this uncertainty defaults to **Some concerns** (not High, since there is no explicit evidence of selective reporting).

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement — **HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Rationale:** Per RoB2 rules, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted if the study is at high risk in *at least one* domain, or if it has "some concerns" for multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result.

In this study:
- **Domain 1 (randomization process) is judged High risk** due to the combination of (a) no information on allocation concealment, and (b) a highly significant (P≤0.0001) baseline imbalance in a key prognostic factor (cervical effacement) that is mechanistically linked to the primary outcome (successful arrest of preterm labor).
- This alone is sufficient to classify the overall result as **High risk of bias**.
- This is compounded by "Some concerns" in three further domains (deviations from intended interventions, measurement of the outcome, and selection of the reported result), which further diminishes confidence in the reliability of the reported effect estimate.

**Reported limitation/ambiguity:** The primary source of uncertainty in this assessment is the very limited methodological detail provided by the authors regarding (i) the actual method of sequence generation, (ii) the mechanism (if any) used to conceal allocation, and (iii) who exactly was blinded in this "single-blinded" design. These ambiguities, combined with the objectively documented and statistically extreme baseline imbalance in cervical effacement, are the basis for the overall "High risk of bias" judgement, and should be considered a limitation of the certainty of this assessment rather than a definitive proof of investigator misconduct.

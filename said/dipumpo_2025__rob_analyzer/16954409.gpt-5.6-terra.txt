## RoB 2 assessment

**Study:** *Comparison of different gonadotrophin preparations in intrauterine insemination cycles for the treatment of unexplained infertility: a prospective, randomized study.*

**Design:** Individually randomized, three-arm parallel-group trial.

**Result assessed:** Clinical pregnancy at 6 weeks after IUI, assessed by transvaginal ultrasound. The principal comparison is recombinant FSH (follitropin alfa; rFSH; Group I, 21/81 pregnancies) versus urinary FSH (uFSH; Group II, 11/80) and hMG (Group III, 10/80). The study reports an overall three-group comparison (*P* = 0.046).

**Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect).

**Sources available for assessment:** Published article, including reported baseline and outcome tables and CONSORT flow diagram. No trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, prospective registry record, or other source containing pre-specified analysis details was available in the supplied material.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states that the 241 couples were “prospectively randomized using computer-generated random numbers.” This is an explicitly random sequence-generation method.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The report states that women were allocated on day 2 or 3 of menstruation “with sealed envelopes by specialist nurse.” However, it does not state that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, opened sequentially, or opened only after irreversible enrolment. Therefore, the reported use of “sealed envelopes” does not meet the information required by RoB 2 to judge allocation concealment as adequate.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  Table I shows closely similar group sizes (81, 80 and 80) and no statistically significant between-group differences in mean age, duration of infertility, mean BMI, or BMI categories. No imbalance reported in these baseline characteristics indicates a problem with randomization.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Random sequence generation was adequately reported, and the reported baseline characteristics do not suggest an implementation failure of randomization. However, allocation concealment is insufficiently described. The article’s statement that sealed envelopes were used does not establish whether the envelopes prevented foreknowledge or manipulation of allocation. Under RoB 2, lack of information about allocation concealment, despite apparently balanced baseline characteristics, leads to **some concerns**.

---

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably yes.**  
  The study does not report participant blinding, placebo use, or masking of the gonadotrophin preparations. The three interventions were different preparations—follitropin alfa, highly purified urinary FSH, and hMG—administered as daily injections. In the absence of reported blinding procedures, participants were probably aware of the intervention received.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably yes.**  
  The report does not state that nurses, clinicians, or other personnel were blinded. The specialist nurse performed allocation using envelopes, and gonadotrophin doses were individually adjusted according to estradiol concentrations and ultrasound findings. No masking procedures are described.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — No information.**  
  The article does not report whether non-protocol co-interventions, differential monitoring, differential dose modification, switching between gonadotrophin preparations, or other deviations occurred because participants or personnel knew treatment allocation. It does report individualized dose adjustments and cycle cancellation criteria, but does not indicate whether their implementation differed systematically across groups because of the trial context.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  The clinical-pregnancy outcome is reported using all randomized participants as denominators: 21/81 in the rFSH group, 11/80 in the uFSH group, and 10/80 in the hMG group. Thus, the reported pregnancy rates are consistent with analysis according to randomized group for all 241 randomized women.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The trial was apparently unblinded for participants and personnel. In an unblinded trial, RoB 2 requires consideration of deviations from intended intervention arising from the trial context. The article does not provide sufficient information to determine whether such deviations occurred. The use of all randomized participants in the reported clinical-pregnancy denominators supports an appropriate analysis for the effect of assignment. Nevertheless, the lack of information on trial-context deviations in an apparently unblinded study results in **some concerns**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Yes.**  
  The study randomized 241 women: 81 to rFSH, 80 to uFSH, and 80 to hMG. The clinical-pregnancy results are reported with these same group denominators: 21/81, 11/80, and 10/80. The reported percentages correspond to these denominators. Thus, clinical-pregnancy data appear to have been included for all randomized participants.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The clinical-pregnancy analysis uses the full randomized sample of 241 women, with no outcome-data exclusions reported for this result. Although the supplied text does not provide detailed numerical follow-up information from the CONSORT figure, the outcome table explicitly reports clinical pregnancy using all randomized women as denominators. There is therefore no indication of missing clinical-pregnancy outcome data capable of materially affecting the intervention comparison. The domain is judged **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  Clinical pregnancy was documented by transvaginal ultrasound 6 weeks after IUI. This is an appropriate method for determining clinical pregnancy.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  The report specifies the same outcome definition and the same scheduled timing of ultrasound confirmation for all groups. It does not report differential follow-up procedures, thresholds, or ascertainment methods between groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — No information.**  
  The report does not state whether the personnel performing or interpreting transvaginal ultrasound were blinded to allocated gonadotrophin preparation.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  Clinical pregnancy documented by ultrasound is a relatively objective outcome. Although ultrasound assessment involves some clinical interpretation, knowledge of whether a woman received rFSH, uFSH, or hMG is not expected to materially alter identification of an intrauterine clinical pregnancy at 6 weeks.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The outcome was assessed by an appropriate, common method across all trial groups. Blinding of ultrasound outcome assessors was not reported, which is an ambiguity in the report. However, the outcome is ultrasound-confirmed clinical pregnancy, for which assessment is unlikely to be materially influenced by awareness of the allocated gonadotrophin preparation. Therefore, the risk of bias in outcome measurement is **low**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The article identifies clinical pregnancy rate as the primary outcome and states the study hypothesis and sample-size calculation. However, no protocol, trial registration record, or statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data became available is provided. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported analysis followed a prospectively finalized analysis plan.

- **5.2 Is the reported result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — Probably no.**  
  The report defines clinical pregnancy as pregnancy documented by transvaginal ultrasound 6 weeks after IUI and reports this outcome for all three groups. No alternative clinical-pregnancy definitions, measurement scales, or time points are reported in the supplied material as having been measured and selectively omitted.

- **5.3 Is the reported result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The report presents group-specific pregnancy rates and an overall three-group *P* value, but there is no pre-specified statistical analysis plan. The article does not establish whether alternative eligible analyses—such as pairwise rFSH-versus-uFSH and rFSH-versus-hMG comparisons, adjusted analyses, or alternative handling of cancelled cycles—were conducted and selectively unreported.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Clinical pregnancy was explicitly designated as the primary outcome, which reduces concern that an entirely unplanned outcome was selected. However, no prospectively finalized protocol or statistical analysis plan is available, and the article does not allow verification that the reported three-group analysis was selected without knowledge of results from among multiple possible eligible analyses. There is no direct evidence that selective reporting occurred; therefore the judgement is **some concerns**, rather than high risk of bias.

---

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation for overall judgement:**  
The study has **some concerns** in three domains:

1. **Randomization process:** allocation concealment was inadequately reported; use of sealed envelopes alone does not establish adequate concealment.  
2. **Deviations from intended interventions:** the trial appears unblinded, and the report provides no information allowing assessment of whether unblinded trial-context deviations occurred.  
3. **Selection of the reported result:** no prospectively finalized analysis plan, protocol, or registry record is available to verify that the reported analysis was not selected from multiple eligible analyses.

The study is at low risk of bias for missing clinical-pregnancy data and for measurement of ultrasound-confirmed clinical pregnancy. Nevertheless, the concerns occur in multiple RoB 2 domains and jointly substantially lower confidence in the reported clinical-pregnancy comparison. In accordance with the specified RoB 2 overall-judgement rule, the overall judgement is **high risk of bias**.

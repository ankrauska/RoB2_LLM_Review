# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Comparison of different gonadotrophin preparations in intrauterine insemination cycles for the treatment of unexplained infertility: a prospective, randomized study

**Design:** Individually-randomized, three-arm parallel-group trial (Follitropin α/rFSH vs uFSH vs hMG)

**Outcome assessed:** Clinical pregnancy rate (primary outcome)

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Sequence generation: The authors state patients were "prospectively randomized using computer-generated random numbers," which satisfies the criterion for a random component (signalling question 1.1 = Yes).
- Allocation concealment: Allocation was performed using "sealed envelopes" managed by a specialist nurse. The report does not specify whether these envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, and tamper-proof, nor whether they were opened sequentially only after participant enrolment. This is a well-recognized method susceptible to subversion if not rigorously safeguarded, and insufficient detail is provided to confirm adequate concealment (signalling question 1.2 = No information/Probably no).
- Baseline imbalance: Table I shows no significant differences in age, duration of infertility, BMI, or BMI categories between groups, consistent with successful randomization (signalling question 1.3 = No).
- **Ambiguity/Limitation:** The lack of detail regarding the envelope allocation procedure (sequential numbering, opacity, sealing, and irreversible assignment before opening) prevents a judgement of "Low risk of bias" for concealment. Per the RoB 2 algorithm, adequate random sequence generation combined with no information on concealment and no baseline imbalance issues leads to a judgement of "Some concerns."

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- This trial compares three active pharmacological formulations (rFSH, uFSH, hMG) rather than an intervention versus placebo/no treatment. Blinding of participants and personnel was not reported and was likely not undertaken given differences in drug names/formulations and dosing that would be visible to prescribers, but there is no biologically plausible motivation for differential deviations given that all three are gonadotrophin preparations administered with an identical injection protocol and monitoring strategy (dose titration by E2/USG, hCG trigger criteria).
- There is no evidence in the manuscript that non-protocol interventions, co-interventions, or differential clinical management occurred between groups because of trial context. The stimulation protocol (starting dose based on BMI, hCG trigger criteria, insemination timing) was applied identically across groups.
- Analysis: All 241 randomized patients appear to have been analysed according to their assigned group (81, 80, 80), consistent with an intention-to-treat approach. The CONSORT flow diagram indicates all randomized participants were analysed in their allocated arms with no reported crossovers or post-randomization exclusions.
- **Ambiguity/Limitation:** The report does not explicitly discuss blinding status of outcome-assessment personnel or whether clinicians managing stimulation were aware of group assignment (which is inherent given different named drugs/formulations); however, this is not judged to materially affect the objectively measured primary outcome (clinical pregnancy confirmed via ultrasound), so this does not elevate risk of bias for this domain.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- The CONSORT flow diagram and reported denominators (81, 80, 80 = 241 total) indicate that outcome data (clinical pregnancy, confirmed by transvaginal ultrasound 6 weeks after IUI) were available for all randomized participants in each arm. No losses to follow-up or exclusions from the pregnancy outcome analysis are reported.
- Because data appear complete for all randomized participants for the primary outcome, missingness could not have influenced the effect estimate (signalling question 3.1 = Yes).
- **Ambiguity/Limitation:** The CONSORT diagram figure legend generically states "Lost to follow-up" and "exclusions" boxes exist as a template feature; however, the actual reported numbers analysed (81/80/80) equal the randomized numbers, so no substantive missing outcome data are identifiable from the text or tables.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- The primary outcome (clinical pregnancy) was assessed via transvaginal ultrasonography, an objective method not substantially susceptible to observer-reported judgement bias (signalling question 4.1 = No, method not inappropriate).
- Measurement/ascertainment methods (serum hCG/progesterone at day 14, followed by TV-US confirmation at 6 weeks) were applied identically to all three groups, so ascertainment is unlikely to have differed between intervention groups (signalling question 4.2 = No).
- However, outcome assessors (clinicians performing TV-US and interpreting hCG/progesterone results) were not reported as blinded to intervention group assignment. Given that the three gonadotrophin preparations are commercially distinct products administered under an open-label design, it is highly likely that treating physicians/ultrasonographers were aware of group allocation.
- Although clinical pregnancy determined by ultrasound is a relatively objective outcome with limited room for subjective interpretation, the diagnosis of "clinical pregnancy" versus biochemical pregnancy or early miscarriage can, in some cases, involve an element of clinical judgement (e.g., timing of scan, interpretation of gestational sac/fetal heart activity). Because assessors' awareness of group assignment was not addressed, and there is no explicit statement of blinding, a judgement of "Some concerns" is warranted rather than "Low risk of bias."
- **Ambiguity/Limitation:** The manuscript provides no information regarding blinding of ultrasonographers, laboratory personnel, or clinicians involved in outcome verification. This absence of information, combined with the open-label nature of the trial (different named drug products), is reported here as a specific ambiguity precluding a "Low" risk judgement.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- No trial registration number, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan (SAP) is referenced or available in the manuscript to verify whether the outcomes and analyses reported correspond to pre-specified plans finalized before unblinded data were available (signalling question 5.1 = No information).
- The primary outcome (clinical pregnancy rate) is clearly pre-specified in the Methods and reported in full for all three groups without evidence of selective reporting among multiple pregnancy definitions or measurement time points.
- Secondary outcomes (duration of stimulation, gonadotrophin dose, follicle number, multiple pregnancy, miscarriage, OHSS) are all reported without apparent omission.
- Statistical methods used (chi-square, ANOVA, Fisher's exact test) appear applied consistently.
- **Ambiguity/Limitation:** In the absence of a registered protocol or SAP, it is not possible to confirm with certainty that no alternative analyses (e.g., adjusted vs. unadjusted models, alternative pregnancy definitions such as biochemical vs clinical) were conducted and selectively omitted. This lack of independently verifiable pre-specification is reported as the specific ambiguity underlying the "Some concerns" judgement, following RoB 2 guidance that absence of information on analysis intentions when multiple outcome measurements/analyses were plausible leads to "Some concerns."

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall Judgment: High risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
Applying the RoB 2 algorithm for reaching an overall judgement:
- Domain 1 (Randomization process): Some concerns
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions): Low risk
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data): Low risk
- Domain 4 (Measurement of outcome): Some concerns
- Domain 5 (Selection of reported result): Some concerns

No individual domain was judged to be at "High" risk of bias. However, according to RoB 2 rules, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted when a study has "some concerns" for **multiple domains** in a way that **substantially lowers confidence in the result**. In this study, three of five domains (randomization process, outcome measurement, and selection of the reported result) raise concerns simultaneously. These concerns are compounded by:
1. Lack of detail on allocation concealment methods (envelope security procedures unspecified),
2. Absence of blinding of outcome assessors in an open-label, multi-product comparison where clinical judgement plays some role in pregnancy diagnosis timing,
3. Absence of any registered protocol or SAP to verify that the reported primary/secondary outcomes and statistical analyses were pre-specified, and
4. The result of primary interest (clinical pregnancy rate, P = 0.04–0.046) is a borderline statistically significant finding, which the authors themselves acknowledge is limited by small sample size and "P value... close to significance" — a borderline result is particularly vulnerable to the compounding effect of multiple "some concerns" domains.

Given the convergence of these concerns across multiple domains and their collective potential to materially affect confidence in the borderline significant primary result, the overall risk of bias for this outcome is judged as **High**.

**Note on limitation of this assessment:** This judgement is based solely on the published journal article; no trial protocol, registration record, or statistical analysis plan was available for review. Several signalling questions (particularly regarding allocation concealment mechanics and blinding status of outcome assessors) were answered as "No information," and judgements were made based on the most plausible interpretation given the open-label, multi-formulation nature of the trial. This should be considered an inherent limitation of the assessment rather than a definitive finding of poor trial conduct.

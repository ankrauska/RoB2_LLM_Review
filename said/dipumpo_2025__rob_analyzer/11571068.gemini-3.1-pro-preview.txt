Based on the provided Cochrane Risk of Bias (RoB 2) guidance, here is the risk-of-bias assessment for the randomized controlled trial "Helping the female partners of men abusing alcohol: a comparison of three treatments" (Halford et al.).

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
*   **Judgment:** Some concerns
*   **Motivation:** The study reports that "Participants were assigned randomly via random number tables to one of three treatment conditions." This indicates a random component was used for sequence generation. However, the study does not report any information about allocation sequence concealment. Given that no information is provided regarding whether the allocation was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned (e.g., use of central randomization or sealed opaque envelopes), it is unclear if the recruiters were unaware of the upcoming assignments. There are no obvious severe baseline imbalances reported, but the lack of information on allocation concealment raises some concerns.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
*   **Judgment:** High risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The study interventions were behavioral (supportive counseling, stress management, alcohol-focused couples therapy), so neither participants nor therapists could be blinded to the assigned intervention. The paper notes an overall attrition of 22% (14 out of 61 participants dropped out). Importantly, it states: "Just over 20% of women dropped out of treatment, and most of these women reported they had left their partners soon after beginning the programme. Many women who dropped out told us that the sessions they attended assisted them to leave a relationship in which they had been very unhappy for many years... Unfortunately, all the women who dropped out from treatment declined further assessment." This indicates significant deviations from intended intervention (dropout) that were directly related to the trial context and likely affected the outcome. Furthermore, the analysis does not appear to follow intention-to-treat principles for estimating the assignment effect, as the dropouts were excluded from the follow-up analyses (e.g., "Forty-seven participants completed treatment and 44 of these participants provided assessed data at 6-month follow-up"). The failure to analyze a substantial portion of randomized participants (around 22-28% excluded from follow-up analyses) who dropped out for reasons linked to the intervention/trial context warrants a judgment of high risk.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
*   **Judgment:** High risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** Outcome data were not available for nearly all participants (only 44 of 61, or roughly 72%, provided 6-month follow-up data). There is no evidence presented that the result was not biased by missing outcome data (e.g., no multiple imputation or robust sensitivity analyses are mentioned). The reason for missingness (leaving the abusive partner/dropping out of treatment because the intervention helped them make that decision) is highly likely to depend on the true value of the outcomes (e.g., relationship satisfaction, distress, violence). Therefore, it is likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
*   **Judgment:** High risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The outcomes assessed include subjective, participant-reported measures such as psychological distress (GHQ), relationship satisfaction (DAS), and reports of the partner's drinking and physical aggression. Because blinding was not possible for these psychosocial interventions, the outcome assessors (the women themselves reporting via questionnaires) were aware of the intervention received. Assessment of these subjective outcomes is very likely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, especially considering the different therapeutic focuses (e.g., relationship focus vs. general support) and the highly distressed nature of the participants.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
*   **Judgment:** Some concerns
*   **Motivation:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial protocol finalized before unblinded data were available. While the reported outcomes (stress, drinking, relationship function) align with the stated aims in the methods, the absence of a pre-registered protocol makes it impossible to definitively confirm whether the reported data were selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses. Therefore, there is "No information" to firmly answer the signaling questions, leading to a default judgment of some concerns.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
*   **Judgment:** High risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at High risk of bias in multiple domains: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions, Bias due to missing outcome data, and Bias in measurement of the outcome. The lack of blinding combined with subjective self-reported outcomes, coupled with high, informative attrition where dropouts were excluded from the analyses, substantially lowers confidence in the results.

# Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) Assessment
## MI-GENES Study: Shared Decision-Making Following Disclosure of CHD Genetic Risk

**Outcome assessed:** Shared decision-making (SDM-Q score, patient-reported) and OPTION5 video-coded score
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)
**Comparison:** CRS (conventional risk score) vs. CRS*GRS (genetics-informed risk)

---

### 1. Bias Arising from the Randomization Process
**Judgment: SOME CONCERNS**

- **Sequence generation:** The allocation sequence was computer-generated using the Pocock and Simon minimization method, stratified for age, gender, and family history of CHD. Minimization methods generally incorporate a random element, so this is judged as *adequate* (Yes/Probably Yes to randomness).
- **Allocation concealment:** The report states that "one of the investigators (HJ) generated the random allocation sequence and study arm assignment," and the same investigator (HJ), along with EMB, recruited participants for the trial. This overlap between the person generating/controlling the allocation sequence and the person recruiting participants raises concern that allocation may not have been adequately concealed from those enrolling participants. No description of a remote/third-party allocation concealment mechanism (e.g., central randomization office, sequentially numbered opaque envelopes) is provided.
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table 1 shows no meaningful imbalances in age, sex, smoking, family history, BMI, blood pressure, lipid levels, or education between arms (all p>0.05, no evidence of substantial imbalance).
- **Conclusion:** Because sequence generation was random but allocation concealment is not clearly described and appears potentially compromised (same investigator involved in both sequence generation and recruitment), this domain is judged as **Some Concerns**. This is a specific ambiguity identified as a limitation of the assessment, since the article does not explicitly describe safeguards to prevent foreknowledge of assignment among recruiters.

---

### 2. Bias Due to Deviations from Intended Interventions (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)
**Judgment: LOW RISK**

- Participants and personnel (genetic counselors/physicians) were necessarily aware of the assigned intervention, since the content of the disclosure itself (CRS alone vs. CRS+GRS) constitutes the intervention. This is an open-label trial (masking: NONE per protocol), which is unavoidable and appropriate for the nature of the comparison (information disclosure).
- There is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention (i.e., failure to deliver the intended disclosure content, or trial-context-driven contamination) occurred. The intervention was standardized through use of the same modified Statin Choice decision aid, delivered with scripted language ("Out of 100 people like you…") in both arms, differing only in the risk information content.
- No indication of non-protocol interventions, crossovers, or implementation failures that would differentially affect the SDM/satisfaction outcome.
- Analysis: All 207 randomized/completed participants were analyzed according to assigned group (ITT-consistent); 206/207 completed the SDM survey and all 207 completed satisfaction surveys, analyzed in the group randomized.
- **Conclusion:** Because deviations from intended intervention did not arise from the trial context in a way likely to bias this particular outcome, and an appropriate (ITT-consistent) analysis was used, this domain is judged as **Low risk**.

---

### 3. Bias Due to Missing Outcome Data
**Judgment: LOW RISK**

- Of 207 randomized and analyzed participants, the SDM survey was completed by 206 (99.5%), and the physician visit satisfaction survey by all 207 (100%).
- The amount of missing data is negligible and could not plausibly have altered the direction or magnitude of the reported result.
- No differential dropout or missingness between arms is reported; the CONSORT flow diagram (Figure 4) confirms 103 vs. 104 participants completed the second visit in each arm with only trivial attrition (9 withdrawals prior to randomization, unrelated to intervention group).
- **Conclusion:** Missing outcome data are minimal and unlikely to bias the result — **Low risk**.

---

### 4. Bias in Measurement of the Outcome
**Judgment: SOME CONCERNS**

- **Method appropriateness:** The SDM-Q and CAHPS-adapted satisfaction surveys are validated instruments; the OPTION5 scale is a validated observer-rated tool. The method of measurement itself is not inappropriate.
- **Comparability of measurement across groups:** The same instruments were used identically in both arms; no evidence that measurement/ascertainment methods differed structurally between groups.
- **Outcome assessor and blinding:** For the SDM-Q and satisfaction surveys, the outcome assessor is the *participant*, who is inherently unblinded (aware of which type of risk information—conventional vs. genetic—was disclosed to them). This is a participant-reported outcome domain in which the assessment "could be influenced by knowledge of intervention received," per RoB 2 criteria for such outcomes.
- For the OPTION5 video analysis, the report does not state whether the single video coder (TSM) was blinded to intervention allocation while scoring the encounters. Absence of stated blinding of this assessor is a further source of uncertainty, though the observer-rated nature of OPTION5 (assessing conversational behaviors) involves some judgment and could theoretically be influenced by awareness of the study arm (e.g., recognizing genetic-risk-specific dialogue).
- However, there is no clear evidence or strong theoretical reason (e.g., strong preconceptions/vested interest in a particular direction) to believe that this awareness actually influenced scoring in one particular direction, and the results were null/similar between groups for both self-report and video-based measures.
- **Conclusion:** Because participants (as self-reported outcome assessors) and possibly the video coder were not blinded, and assessment could plausibly be influenced by this awareness even though there is no strong evidence it was, this domain is judged as **Some Concerns**. This reflects an ambiguity in the report (lack of explicit statement regarding blinding status of the OPTION5 video coder) which is reported here as a limitation of this assessment.

---

### 5. Bias in Selection of the Reported Result
**Judgment: LOW RISK**

- Both "Shared decision making (as assessed by survey)" and "Physician visit satisfaction (as assessed by survey)" are explicitly pre-specified as secondary outcomes in the registered trial protocol (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01936675), matching the outcomes reported in the manuscript.
- The SDM-Q is reported in full with all 11 individual items presented (Table 2), along with the overall composite score, without evidence of selective reporting of only favorable items.
- The OPTION5 scale and physician satisfaction survey are reported in their entirety (all 6 items in Table 3), with pre-specified scoring methodology (validated instruments, clearly described score transformation).
- No evidence of selective reporting of alternative analyses, subgroups, or cut-points; the analysis plan (video subsample of 80 matched encounters) is clearly described and appears to be a pre-planned sampling approach rather than a post hoc selection based on results.
- **Conclusion:** The reported results are consistent with pre-specified outcome measures and analyses, without indication of selective reporting — **Low risk**.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**OVERALL JUDGMENT: SOME CONCERNS**

**Rationale:** This trial was judged to be at "Some Concerns" in two domains — (1) Bias arising from the randomization process, due to insufficiently described allocation concealment and overlap between the individual generating the allocation sequence and the individual recruiting participants, and (4) Bias in measurement of the outcome, due to the inherently unblinded nature of participant-reported SDM/satisfaction outcomes and unclear blinding status of the OPTION5 video coder. No domain was judged to be at "High risk of bias." Per RoB 2 criteria, an overall judgment of "Some Concerns" applies when a study raises some concerns in at least one domain without being at high risk in any domain. While two domains show concerns rather than one, the concerns identified are of a mild-to-moderate nature (i.e., the ambiguities relate to incomplete reporting rather than clear evidence of problematic conduct, and the null/consistent results across independent measurement methods — self-report and independent video-coding — mitigate concern about differential bias), and therefore do not, in our judgment, escalate the overall risk to "High." 

**Limitation of this assessment:** Specific ambiguities were identified that could not be resolved from the published report and are noted as limitations of this assessment: (a) the process used to conceal the allocation sequence from study personnel/recruiters prior to assignment was not explicitly described, and the individual who generated the randomization sequence also participated in recruitment; and (b) blinding status of the individual who coded the OPTION5 video-recorded encounters was not reported.

Based on a detailed analysis of the provided article, here is the Risk of Bias assessment using the RoB 2 tool for randomized trials.

**Preliminary Considerations**
*   **Study design:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial.
*   **Interventions:** Experimental: Science-based causal explanations (SC). Comparators: Basic science before clinical concepts (BC), Clinical concepts before basic science (CB), Clinical feature list only (FL).
*   **Outcome being assessed:** Diagnostic test performance (delayed testing).
*   **Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect).

### 1. Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
*   **Judgment:** High risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The study states that "Participants were randomly allocated to one of four study conditions." However, there is **no information** provided about how the random allocation sequence was generated (Question 1.1: No information). More importantly, there is **no information** about whether the allocation sequence was concealed (Question 1.2: No information). Furthermore, five participants dropped out and two were excluded for spending less than five minutes, leaving unequal final numbers across groups (25 in FL, 21 in CB, 24 in BC, 25 in SC). Since both sequence generation and allocation concealment lack any descriptive details, and concealment is critical to prevent selection bias, the lack of information raises serious concerns. According to RoB 2 mapping, "No information" on allocation concealment typically leads to "Some concerns," but the overall lack of any detail regarding the randomization process, coupled with post-randomization exclusions (addressed partly in domain 2 but reflecting on the integrity of the process) and convenience sampling, elevates this to a high risk of bias/some concerns. Following strict RoB2 mapping: Q1.1 (NI), Q1.2 (NI), Q1.3 (NI/PN) -> Default mapping is **Some concerns**, but given the complete absence of methodological details regarding sequence generation and concealment, it raises substantial doubts. Judgment is **Some concerns**.

### 2. Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)
*   **Judgment:** Some concerns
*   **Motivation:** Participants and personnel (investigators/software program) were likely aware of the intervention received, as participants read distinct texts (Question 2.1: Probably Yes; Question 2.2: Probably Yes). However, because the intervention was a computer-based module delivered in a single session, deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context are unlikely (Question 2.3: Probably No). Regarding analysis (Question 2.6), 5 participants who did not complete the delayed session and 2 participants who spent less than 5 minutes on the delayed test were excluded from the analysis. This means the analysis was not a true Intention-to-Treat (ITT) analysis (Question 2.6: No). The exclusion of these 7 participants out of 101 initially enrolled is a small percentage (~7%), but the reasons for dropping out or rushing through the test could potentially be related to the assigned intervention (e.g., finding the text format frustrating). Therefore, there is potential for impact on the result (Question 2.7: Probably Yes / No Information). This leads to **Some concerns**.

### 3. Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
*   **Judgment:** Some concerns
*   **Motivation:** Data for the delayed diagnostic test were not available for all randomized participants. Five enrolled participants failed to complete the delayed session (3 from CB, 2 from BC) and 2 were excluded for spending less than five minutes on the test (1 from BC, 1 from CB). Therefore, data were missing for ~7% of the cohort (Question 3.1: No). There is no evidence provided that the result was not biased by missing data (e.g., no sensitivity analyses were performed) (Question 3.2: No). Missingness could potentially depend on the true value of the outcome, as students who found the material too difficult or unhelpful (leading to poor performance) might have been more likely to skip the delayed session or rush through it (Question 3.3: Probably Yes). While the numbers are small, the missingness is slightly imbalanced (concentrated in the CB and BC groups), raising **Some concerns**.

### 4. Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The method of measuring the outcome (a diagnostic test with clinical vignettes) was appropriate and previously validated (Question 4.1: No). The measurement of the outcome did not differ between intervention groups; all groups took the same computer-based tests (Question 4.2: No). The outcome assessors were essentially the computer program scoring the multiple-choice diagnostic test (no judgment involved), or if considering the participant as the assessor of their own knowledge, they were aware of their intervention (Question 4.3: Yes). However, because the test was an objective measure of diagnostic accuracy (identifying the correct diagnosis from a list), the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received (Question 4.4: Probably No). Thus, the risk of bias is **Low**.

### 5. Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
*   **Judgment:** Some concerns
*   **Motivation:** There is no indication that the trial was registered or that a pre-specified statistical analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available (Question 5.1: No / No information). The study reports the primary outcome (diagnostic test performance) across the groups. While there is no obvious evidence of selective reporting from multiple outcome measurements or analyses, the absence of a pre-specified protocol means we cannot be completely certain that other measures (e.g., time per vignette, which was collected) were not the primary intended outcome or that analyses were not adjusted based on the data. Therefore, lacking a protocol, the judgment is **Some concerns**.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement
*   **Judgment:** High risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The study is judged to have "Some concerns" across multiple domains: Randomization Process (complete lack of details on sequence generation and allocation concealment), Deviations from intended interventions (exclusion of participants from analysis), Missing outcome data (differential dropout/exclusions in specific groups without sensitivity analyses), and Selection of the reported result (lack of a pre-specified protocol). Under RoB 2 rules, if a study is judged to have some concerns for multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result, the overall judgment is **High risk of bias**. The combination of uncharacterized randomization, non-ITT analysis with differential missing data in the comparator groups, and lack of a protocol substantially lowers confidence in the precise effect estimate.

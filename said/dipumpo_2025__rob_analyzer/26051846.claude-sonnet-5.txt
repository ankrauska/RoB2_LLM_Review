# ROB 2 Assessment Report

## Preliminary Considerations
**Study:** Pérez-Merino et al., "Evaluation of the effectiveness of three physiotherapeutic treatments for subacromial impingement syndrome" (RCT, NCT01748188)
**Design:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial (3 arms)
**Comparison assessed:** Iontophoresis with dexketoprofen vs. Ultrasound (comparator/reference group), as representative of the primary trial comparison
**Outcome assessed:** VAS pain score at post-treatment (primary outcome, per trial registry)
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)
**Sources used:** Journal article (full text with tables), ClinicalTrials.gov registry record. No protocol, SAP, or other documents were available.

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **SOME CONCERNS**

**Signalling questions:**
- 1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? → **Yes.** "Each participant was assigned to one treatment group, at random, using a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method, applied by a statistician. The statistician was not involved in data collection or analysis."
- 1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled? → **No information.** The article describes sequence generation but gives no details on the concealment mechanism (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation, pharmacy-controlled dispensing). There is no description of how recruiters/enrollers were prevented from foreseeing upcoming allocations.
- 1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with randomization? → **No.** Table 1 shows no significant differences in age, sex, time from onset, dominant side, or tendon pathology type across groups (all p>0.4), fully compatible with chance.

**Judgement rationale:** Random sequence generation is adequately described, and baseline characteristics show no evidence of a failure of randomization. However, the complete absence of information about allocation concealment prevents a "Low risk" judgement, since it cannot be excluded that enrolling personnel/participants had foreknowledge of assignment. This maps to "Some concerns" per the RoB 2 algorithm (Table 4: NI on concealment + no baseline imbalance → Some concerns).

**Limitation noted:** No description of the concealment procedure was reported in the article or the registry; this ambiguity could not be resolved without further protocol documentation.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention) — **SOME CONCERNS**

**Signalling questions:**
- 2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention? → **Probably yes.** The trial is described as "single-blind" with masking limited to the participant category per the registry, yet the three interventions are physically and sensorially distinct: iontophoresis delivers a palpable galvanic electrical current for 20 minutes, whereas ultrasound/phonophoresis involves a probe with gel for 5 minutes. No sham-current or sham-ultrasound procedure is described to mask these evident sensory differences.
- 2.2 Were carers/people delivering the intervention aware? → **Yes.** The treating physiotherapists necessarily knew which modality they were administering (evident from the equipment used). The article clarifies this was mitigated by physiotherapists not collecting data or evaluating outcomes: "The physiotherapists did not collect data and did not evaluate the results."
- 2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention arising from trial context? → **No information.** No explicit mention of non-protocol interventions, crossover, or trial-context-driven deviations. Additionally, "No educational advice was provided, and participants were not given guidelines for performing exercises at home," reducing opportunity for context-driven behavioural deviation.
- 2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used? → **Probably yes.** Final analyzed sample sizes (32+33+34=99) equal the total enrolled/randomized sample (99), suggesting no post-randomization exclusions; however, an explicit ITT statement is not provided.

**Judgement rationale:** Although personnel awareness was mitigated by separating treatment delivery from outcome assessment, genuine blinding of participants is questionable given the readily distinguishable physical sensations of the three treatment modalities, and no masking strategy (e.g., sham electrical stimulation) is reported. There is no clear evidence, however, that this awareness led to documented trial-context-driven deviations affecting the outcome, and the analysed sample matches the randomized sample. This combination of an unresolved blinding concern with no confirmed detrimental deviation is consistent with "Some concerns."

**Limitation noted:** No explicit statement of ITT analysis; no report of dropout/withdrawal numbers by group; ambiguity in the true robustness of participant blinding across three physically distinguishable interventions was not addressed by the authors.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **LOW RISK**

**Signalling questions:**
- 3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? → **Probably yes.** Enrolled/analyzed participants (32 ultrasound, 33 phonophoresis, 34 iontophoresis = 99) match the total sample size of 99 reported in both the article and the trial registry ("Ninety-nine participants... were assigned at random to three intervention groups" and enrollment count = 99, ACTUAL). All three outcome measures (VAS, CMS, DASH) are reported with full descriptive statistics for all three time points and all three groups, with no indication of attrition.

**Judgement rationale:** The numbers randomized and analyzed are identical across the reported sample, and no missingness or dropout is described in the results section ("No adverse events were documented in any of the treatment groups"). This supports a low risk of bias due to missing outcome data.

**Limitation noted:** The referenced "Fig. A" (CONSORT flow diagram), which would explicitly confirm participant flow and any losses to follow-up, was in the supplementary material and not available for direct verification in the text provided; the judgement relies on the consistency of the reported sample sizes.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **HIGH RISK**

**Signalling questions:**
- 4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? → **No.** VAS is a validated, standard pain measurement tool.
- 4.2 Could measurement/ascertainment of the outcome have differed between groups? → **No.** Same VAS scale and time points used for all groups.
- 4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? → **Yes.** For VAS (and DASH), the outcome assessor is the **participant** (per RoB2 guidance, "For participant-reported outcomes, the outcome assessor is the participant, even if a blinded interviewer is... completing a questionnaire on their behalf"). As detailed in Domain 2, participants were very likely aware of their allocated intervention because of overtly distinguishable sensory experiences between electrical current (iontophoresis) versus ultrasound-based treatments (ultrasound/phonophoresis), with no sham procedure reported to mask this difference.
- 4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? → **Yes.** VAS is a subjective, participant-reported measure of pain, inherently susceptible to expectation and belief effects regarding "receiving a drug" (dexketoprofen) versus a device-only treatment.
- 4.5 Is it likely that assessment was influenced by knowledge of intervention received? → **Probably yes.** Given that two of the three arms involve administration of an active analgesic drug (dexketoprofen) via phonophoresis/iontophoresis, while the reference arm (ultrasound) does not, participants' expectations regarding drug- versus non-drug treatment are plausible, strong sources of differential reporting bias for a self-reported pain scale. The primary outcome for the trial (VAS) is thus at particular risk.

**Judgement rationale:** Although the CMS (partly clinician-assessed) benefited from having "the same blinded rehabilitation doctor" perform assessments at all visits, this blinding safeguard does not extend to the participant-reported outcomes (VAS – the trial's primary outcome – and DASH), where the assessor is the participant. Because participants were plausibly unblinded due to overt sensory/treatment differences, and because knowledge of "receiving a drug" versus "not receiving a drug" is likely to influence self-reported pain, this domain is judged at **High risk of bias** for the VAS outcome.

**Limitation noted:** The article does not report any attempt to verify or test the success of participant blinding (e.g., a post-trial blinding-success questionnaire), nor does it describe any sham procedure to make the three interventions sensorially indistinguishable. This ambiguity substantially limits confidence in the self-reported outcome data.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **LOW RISK**

**Signalling questions:**
- 5.1 Were the data analysed in accordance with a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? → **Probably yes.** The ClinicalTrials.gov registration (NCT01748188) pre-specifies VAS as the primary outcome and CMS/DASH as secondary outcomes, matching exactly what is reported in the article's Results (Tables 2 and 3), at the same three time points (baseline, post-treatment, 1-month post-treatment).
- 5.2 Was the result likely selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements? → **No.** VAS, CMS and DASH are reported in full and match the registered outcome measures; no alternative scales are mentioned or omitted.
- 5.3 Was the result likely selected from multiple eligible analyses? → **No.** A single, consistent statistical approach (repeated-measures ANOVA within groups; ANOVA with post hoc Scheffé test between groups) is applied uniformly and reported for all outcomes and all time points, including both statistically significant and non-significant findings (e.g., "no significant differences were detected between the groups" at 1-month follow-up).

**Judgement rationale:** The correspondence between the pre-registered outcomes/time points and those fully reported in the results, together with the consistent and comprehensive reporting of both significant and non-significant results, supports a low risk of selective outcome reporting.

**Limitation noted:** No full trial protocol or statistical analysis plan (SAP) was available for direct comparison beyond the registry summary; therefore, more granular pre-specification details (e.g., prespecified covariate adjustment) could not be verified.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement — **HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Summary of domain-level judgements:**

| Domain | Judgement |
|---|---|
| 1. Randomization process | Some concerns |
| 2. Deviations from intended interventions | Some concerns |
| 3. Missing outcome data | Low risk |
| 4. Measurement of the outcome | **High risk** |
| 5. Selection of the reported result | Low risk |

**Rationale for overall judgement:** Per the RoB 2 decision rule, a judgement of "High risk of bias" in **any single domain** mandates an overall "High risk of bias" judgement for the result, regardless of the status of other domains. Domain 4 (Measurement of the outcome) was judged as High risk because the trial's primary outcome (VAS pain score) is a participant-reported measure, and participants were very likely unblinded due to the overtly distinguishable physical/sensory nature of the three interventions (electrical current for iontophoresis vs. ultrasound-only vs. gel-mediated phonophoresis), combined with differential presence/absence of an active pharmacological agent (dexketoprofen) across arms — a scenario highly susceptible to expectation-driven reporting bias, with no evidence of any masking strategy to mitigate this. Additionally, "Some concerns" were identified independently in two further domains (randomization process, and deviations from intended interventions), which further diminishes confidence in the result, reinforcing the High risk of bias conclusion.

**Overall conclusion:** This result (VAS pain score, iontophoresis vs. ultrasound comparison) is judged to be at **High risk of bias**, driven principally by the likely lack of effective participant blinding for a subjective, self-reported primary outcome, compounded by unresolved uncertainty regarding allocation concealment and the completeness of intervention-fidelity reporting.

## Scope of the RoB 2 assessment

- **Study design:** Individually randomized, two-arm parallel-group RCT.
- **Comparison:** Forsus Fatigue Resistant Device alone (**FFRD**) versus FFRD with mandibular mini-implant anchorage (**FMI**).
- **Result assessed:** The effect of assignment to FMI versus FFRD on **patient-reported acceptance/functional experience at removal of the FFRD**, measured through the post-treatment questionnaire (including speech, eating, sleep, oral hygiene, pain, swelling, noticeability, external appearance, and satisfaction).
- **Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect).
- **Sources available:** The journal article, tables, figures, and questionnaire description. The trial was not registered and no protocol published before commencement was available.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The authors report that randomization used “a computer-generated list of random numbers” created using the Excel `RAND()` function. They also report random permuted blocks of sizes 4 and 6, with randomized block-size order. This includes a random component and is an acceptable random sequence-generation method.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — Probably yes.**  
  The article reports use of “sequentially numbered and sealed opaque envelopes,” concealed from researchers and assessors. Envelopes were taken by patients after informed consent had been signed. Although the report does not specify every extended safeguard recommended by RoB 2—such as tamper-proof sealing, irreversible assignment before opening, or sequential opening—it reports the principal features of an appropriate envelope-concealment process.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  Both groups included 16 participants, consistent with the intended 1:1 allocation. Baseline characteristics were reported for all randomized participants. Table I shows no striking imbalance in age, skeletal Class II characteristics, overjet, or mandibular crowding that clearly indicates a failure or manipulation of randomization. Differences in vertical cephalometric measures are not reported as statistically significant and, in this small sample, do not by themselves demonstrate a randomization problem.

**Motivation for judgement**  
The allocation sequence was random, allocation concealment was described using sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, and the reported baseline data do not reveal an imbalance clearly incompatible with chance. Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Blinding of patients was explicitly described as impossible. Participants in the FMI group received mini-implants, whereas those in the FFRD group did not; therefore, they necessarily knew which intervention they received.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Clinicians could not be blinded because the interventions were visibly and procedurally different.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The study reports appliance breakage in three participants in each group and separation of appliance components in five FMI participants and four FFRD participants. However, the article does not report that these events caused participants to cross over to the alternative intervention, discontinue allocated treatment, or receive non-protocol interventions that arose because of trial participation. The reported activation and repair procedures were part of appliance monitoring and management. No trial-context deviations likely to affect the questionnaire outcome were reported.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  The primary analysis was stated to be intention-to-treat. All 32 randomized participants completed the study, returned questionnaires, and were included in the analyses: 16 per group. Thus, no participant was excluded, analysed according to a different intervention, or lost after randomization.

**Motivation for judgement**  
Participants and clinicians were unblinded, but lack of blinding alone does not establish bias in this RoB 2 domain for the effect of assignment. The article does not report trial-context deviations from the intended interventions that would have affected outcomes differently between groups. Moreover, all randomized participants were analysed in their assigned groups, with no attrition or crossover reported. This domain is therefore at **low risk of bias**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Yes.**  
  The CONSORT flow diagram and results text state that all 32 randomized participants completed the study, that “the 32 surveys were retrieved,” and that there were “no dropouts.” Each group contributed questionnaire data for all 16 randomized participants.

- **3.2–3.4 — Not applicable.**  
  Since questionnaire outcome data were available for all randomized participants, further consideration of mechanisms and consequences of missing outcome data is not required.

**Motivation for judgement**  
There were no missing participant questionnaire data for the assessed result and no post-randomization losses. The authors reported that all questionnaires were collected on removal of the FFRD and all participants were included in analysis. Therefore, missing outcome data could not materially bias the result. This domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  The outcome was patient experience/acceptance, assessed using a questionnaire. A questionnaire is an appropriate general method for obtaining patient-reported experience outcomes. The questionnaire used a five-point Likert response format for most items and yes/no responses for three questions. It was modified from a questionnaire previously used to evaluate experiences with the FFRD. The report does not establish formal validation of the modified questionnaire, but it does not demonstrate that the instrument was inappropriate for measuring patient-reported acceptance.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  The same questionnaire was administered to both groups at the same treatment stage: the day the FFRD was removed. The same response scales and outcome-collection procedure were reported for both groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — Yes.**  
  For the main outcomes, the outcome assessor was the participant because the outcomes were patient-reported satisfaction, pain, functional limitations, noticeability, appearance, and related experiences. Participants could not be blinded to whether they had received mini-implants in addition to FFRD. The reported blinding of the person extracting questionnaire data and of the statistician does not blind the patient-reported outcome assessor.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Yes.**  
  The outcomes are subjective patient-reported evaluations. Knowledge of receiving mini-implant anchorage or conventional FFRD could influence how a participant reports satisfaction, pain, appearance, appliance noticeability, and functional limitations.

- **4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  The article does not report evidence that patients’ questionnaire responses were influenced by knowledge of assignment, nor does it demonstrate expectations or beliefs sufficiently strong to establish that such influence was likely. Both groups received active orthodontic treatment involving the FFRD, rather than an active treatment versus no-treatment comparison. Nevertheless, influence remains possible because participants were unblinded and outcomes were subjective.

**Motivation for judgement**  
The patient-reported questionnaire was administered identically in both groups, but patients were necessarily aware of their treatment assignment. The reported blinding of data extractors, outcome-data coders, and statistician cannot remove bias from a patient-reported outcome, because the participant is the assessor of her own experience. Since knowledge of assignment could influence these subjective assessments, but the report provides no direct evidence that it did so, this domain raises **some concerns** rather than high risk of bias.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The article explicitly states that the trial was not registered and that the protocol was not published before trial commencement. No dated statistical analysis plan is reported. Although the article describes planned primary and secondary outcomes and states that no outcome changes occurred after trial commencement, this does not establish that a sufficiently detailed analysis plan was finalized before outcome data were available.

- **5.2 Is the assessed result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  The patient-acceptance construct was assessed through multiple questionnaire items, including several domains of patient experience. The article reports results for the questionnaire items, but no prospective protocol or analysis plan is available to confirm whether all intended patient-acceptance measurements, time points, scoring approaches, or outcome definitions were specified before unblinded data were available.

- **5.3 Is the assessed result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The published report describes use of ridit analysis for the Likert outcomes and descriptive analyses for yes/no questions. However, without a pre-specified, date-stamped protocol or statistical analysis plan, it is not possible to establish whether this analytic approach, the reference-group choice, treatment of questionnaire items, or any alternative eligible analyses were determined before unblinded results were available.

**Motivation for judgement**  
There is no trial registration, prospectively available protocol, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan. The authors state that outcomes did not change after trial commencement, but this statement cannot verify that the reported questionnaire results and analyses were selected without knowledge of the data. There is no direct evidence that selective reporting occurred; therefore, the domain is not judged high risk. However, absence of prospectively documented analysis intentions creates **some concerns**.

---

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation for overall judgement**  
The study is judged at low risk of bias for randomization, deviations from intended interventions, and missing outcome data. However:

1. **Measurement of the outcome:** the key outcomes were subjective patient-reported questionnaire outcomes, and participants were necessarily unblinded to whether they had mini-implant anchorage. This creates some concerns that knowledge of intervention could have influenced reporting.
2. **Selection of the reported result:** the trial was unregistered and lacked a protocol published before commencement or a date-stamped pre-specified statistical analysis plan. Therefore, selective reporting of questionnaire outcomes or analyses cannot be excluded.

Under RoB 2, the presence of **some concerns in at least one domain**, with no domain judged high risk, leads to an overall judgement of **some concerns**.

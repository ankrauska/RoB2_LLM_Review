## RoB 2 assessment

**Study:** *A multi-center randomized controlled trial to compare a self-ligating bracket with a conventional bracket in a UK population: Part 1: Treatment efficiency* (Angle Orthodontist, 2016).

**Design:** Individually randomized, two-arm, parallel-group multicentre trial.

**Comparison:** SmartClip self-ligating brackets versus Victory conventional brackets.

**Result assessed:** Difference in **total treatment duration (months from fixed-appliance placement to debonding)** between SmartClip and Victory groups: SmartClip mean 25.12 months; Victory mean 25.80 months; reported difference 0.68 months (95% CI −1.4 to 2.7; P=.51).

**Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect).

**Sources available for this assessment:** The supplied journal article, tables, and figures only. No trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, or registry record was supplied.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states that allocation used “block randomization determined by a computer-generated random number table,” with stratification by operator and centre. A computer-generated random sequence is an adequate random sequence-generation method.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — Probably yes.**  
  The article states that allocation was placed in “a sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelope” and that this was opened after the patient had been accepted into the trial. It also states that the person generating the randomization did not participate in patient allocation. These details support allocation concealment. The report does not specify tamper-proof envelopes or confirm that they were opened strictly sequentially only after irreversible enrolment; however, the reported approach is sufficient to judge concealment as probably adequate.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — Probably no.**  
  The final analysis included 65 SmartClip and 70 Victory participants, which is broadly compatible with the planned approximately 1:1 allocation. Baseline age, sex, extraction status, and malocclusion categories were reported. Some numerical differences occurred, including Class I malocclusion (12 versus 23) and Class III malocclusion (8 versus 13), but the article provides no indication that these differences were implausible by chance, nor is there evidence of a substantial imbalance in a clearly identified key prognostic factor attributable to a failure of randomization.  

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The allocation sequence was computer-generated, and allocation was reported to be concealed using sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes administered independently of sequence generation. Reported baseline information does not demonstrate an imbalance clearly beyond that expected by chance. Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The report explicitly states that it was not possible to blind patients to bracket type.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The report explicitly states that clinicians could not be blinded to bracket type.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — No information.**  
  The trial used a standardized bonding procedure and intended archwire sequences, and it states that exceptions to the archwire sequence were recorded. However, the article does not report the number, nature, distribution, or consequences of any exceptions. It also does not report whether other non-protocol interventions, differential management, or differential treatment implementation occurred because clinicians and participants knew the allocated bracket type.

- **2.4–2.5 Were such deviations likely to affect the outcome and balanced between groups? — Not applicable / no information.**  
  Since the report does not establish whether trial-context deviations occurred, their effects and balance between groups cannot be assessed.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — No.**  
  The article explicitly states: “Due to the low number of dropouts, analysis was undertaken on a per protocol basis.” The CONSORT diagram indicates that 138 participants were randomized, but the final analysis included 135: 65 SmartClip and 70 Victory participants. One participant allocated to SmartClip did not receive fixed appliance treatment after allocation, and one participant in each group did not complete treatment. Thus, the reported treatment-duration result excluded randomized participants rather than analysing all randomized participants in their assigned groups.

- **2.7 Was there potential for a substantial impact of failure to analyse participants in the groups to which they were randomized? — Probably no.**  
  Three of 138 randomized participants (2.2%) were excluded. For a continuous outcome such as treatment duration, this small number alone does not establish a substantial impact on the reported comparison. However, the outcomes of these excluded participants are not available, and no intention-to-treat analysis was reported.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Both participants and clinicians were aware of the assigned bracket system. The report does not provide sufficient information to establish whether knowledge of assignment produced important trial-context deviations from intended treatment. More importantly, the reported treatment-duration result was based on a **per-protocol analysis**, not an intention-to-treat or modified intention-to-treat analysis. Although only three randomized participants were omitted and a substantial impact is not demonstrated, the departure from an analysis of all randomized participants raises **some concerns**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Yes.**  
  Of 138 randomized participants, 135 (97.8%) completed treatment and were analysed. Three participants were not included in the final analysis: one who declined fixed appliance treatment after allocation and two who did not complete treatment. Thus, treatment-duration data were available for 97.8% of randomized participants.

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — Not applicable.**  
  The amount of missing outcome data is sufficiently small for this continuous outcome to be considered unlikely to make an important difference to the estimated treatment-duration comparison.

- **3.3–3.4 Could missingness depend on its true value, and was this likely? — Not applicable.**  
  The report does not provide detailed reasons for the two non-completions. Nevertheless, with only three of 138 randomized participants lacking outcome data, the amount of missingness is small.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Treatment-duration data were available for 135/138 randomized participants. The 2.2% missing outcome-data proportion is small for the continuous treatment-duration outcome, and the article does not provide evidence that these missing data could materially alter the reported effect estimate. Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**. The per-protocol exclusion itself remains relevant to Domain 2, because it concerns the analysis population rather than solely the amount of missing outcome data.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  Treatment duration was defined as the time in months from fixed-appliance placement to debonding. This is an appropriate and directly relevant measure of treatment efficiency.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  The same definition of treatment duration was used for both groups, measured from the same clinical milestones: fixed-appliance placement and debonding. The report does not describe different measurement procedures, schedules, thresholds, or ascertainment methods by bracket group.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  The report states that data analysis was carried out with examiner blinding. It additionally states that appliance type was not recorded on data-collection sheets to minimize bias during data entry and analysis.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Not applicable / probably no.**  
  Treatment duration is based on dates of appliance placement and debonding rather than a participant-reported or judgement-dependent outcome. Moreover, the reported analysis was examiner-blinded.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The treatment-duration outcome was objectively defined and measured equivalently in both intervention groups. The article reports blinded data analysis and omission of appliance type from data-collection sheets. There is no evidence that measurement or ascertainment differed between groups or that outcome assessment was influenced by knowledge of treatment allocation. This domain is therefore at **low risk of bias**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  No protocol, statistical analysis plan, or prospectively registered analysis plan was available in the supplied material. The article describes planned outcome measures and statistical tests, but the article alone does not establish that these plans were finalized before unblinded outcome data became available.

- **5.2 Is the result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — Probably no.**  
  For treatment duration, the article specifies one operational measurement: months from fixed-appliance placement to debonding. No alternative time points or alternative instruments for treatment duration are reported. The result assessed corresponds to this stated measure.

- **5.3 Is the reported result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The article reports Welch two-sample t-tests for the primary between-group comparison and also reports analyses considering operator and centre. However, without a pre-specified analysis plan, it cannot be verified whether the selected unadjusted comparison, analysis population, handling of the three exclusions, or other analytic decisions were specified before the outcome data were available. The report’s explicit use of a per-protocol analysis also cannot be verified against a pre-specified plan.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The treatment-duration measure itself is clearly specified and there is no direct evidence that it was selected from multiple alternative eligible measurements. However, no pre-specified protocol or statistical analysis plan was available, and the article does not demonstrate that the reported analytic approach—including the per-protocol analysis—was finalized before unblinded results were available. Under RoB 2, this leads to **some concerns** about selection of the reported result.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation for overall judgement:**  
The study is judged at low risk of bias for randomization, missing outcome data, and measurement of treatment duration. However:

1. **Domain 2** raises some concerns because the treatment-duration result was analysed on a **per-protocol basis**, excluding three randomized participants, rather than estimating the effect of assignment using an intention-to-treat approach; and  
2. **Domain 5** raises some concerns because no pre-specified analysis plan, protocol, or trial registry record was available to verify that the reported analysis was specified before unblinded outcome data were available.

There is no domain judged at high risk of bias, and the concerns in Domains 2 and 5 do not, based on the information reported, substantially lower confidence enough to warrant a high overall judgement. Therefore, according to the stated RoB 2 overall-judgement rules, the overall risk of bias for the reported treatment-duration result is **Some concerns**.

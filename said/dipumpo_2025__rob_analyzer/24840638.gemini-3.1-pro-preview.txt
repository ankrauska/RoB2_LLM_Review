Based on a thorough and detailed assessment of the provided randomized controlled trial using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool, here is the structured Risk of Bias (ROB) report:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** High risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The allocation sequence was not adequately generated or concealed. The study used a highly predictable block randomization method: "randomly assigning 2 participants to the percentile group, 2 to the fixed criterion group and 1 to the random incentives group from each group of 5 participants completing intake." There is absolutely no information provided regarding allocation concealment (e.g., centralized randomization or opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes). Because the block size is small and fixed (blocks of 5) and the assignment rule is deterministic within those small blocks, personnel enrolling participants could easily predict the upcoming assignments if they were aware of previous ones. Furthermore, there was a statistically significant baseline imbalance in education levels between the groups, which, combined with the lack of concealment and predictable sequence, raises severe concerns that the randomization process was compromised.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Due to the nature of the behavioral intervention (contingency management), participants and trial personnel were aware of the assigned interventions. However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended interventions arose because of the trial context (e.g., administration of non-protocol interventions). The analysis appears to have included all randomized participants according to the group to which they were assigned, and the lack of blinding is an inherent and acceptable feature of this specific behavioral trial design that did not inherently lead to unbalanced non-protocol deviations.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** Outcome data were not available for all randomized participants across all 60 visits. The average number of delivered breath CO samples out of 60 was 42, 40, and 52 for the percentile, fixed criterion, and random incentive groups, respectively. In smoking cessation trials, missingness is very likely related to the true value of the outcome (participants who relapse or continue smoking are more likely to miss visits). Although the trial protocol appropriately handled missed visits by resetting the incentive value (effectively penalizing missed visits as positive smoking samples), the differential rate of visit attendance and the inherent relationship between missing a visit and the outcome variable (smoking status) raise some concerns about the potential for bias, as no specific sensitivity analyses were reported to prove the results were unaffected by the missing data. 

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The method of measuring the outcome (breath carbon monoxide using Vitalograph CO monitors) is an objective, standardized, and appropriate physiological measure for assessing recent smoking abstinence. While the outcome assessors and participants were aware of the assigned interventions, the objective nature of the carbon monoxide breathalyzer test leaves essentially no room for judgment. Therefore, it is highly unlikely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** There is no mention or reference to a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial protocol finalized before unblinded outcome data became available. The study reports several different analytical approaches to the outcome (e.g., time to first abstinent sample, total number of criterion samples, maintenance curves). Without a date-stamped, pre-registered protocol, it is impossible to verify whether the reported analyses were selected from multiple eligible data analyses based on the statistical significance or direction of the results. 

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** High risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at a high risk of bias overall. This is strictly driven by the "Bias arising from the randomization process" domain, where the highly predictable, small-block randomization method without any reported allocation concealment—coupled with an observed baseline imbalance—substantially lowers confidence in the baseline comparability of the groups. Furthermore, the presence of "Some concerns" in both the missing outcome data and selection of the reported result domains further limits the trustworthiness of the specific effect estimates reported.

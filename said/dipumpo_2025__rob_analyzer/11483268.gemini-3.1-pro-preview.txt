**Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2) Report**

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study report explicitly states that participants were "randomised to receive either Vaqta™ or Avaxim™"; however, it does not provide any details regarding the method used to generate the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers) or the mechanism used to conceal the allocation sequence until participants were enrolled and assigned to their respective interventions. Although baseline characteristics (age, sex, weight, and height) appear well-balanced between the two groups as shown in Table 1, suggesting no obvious problems with the randomization process, the complete lack of information on sequence generation and allocation concealment warrants a judgment of "Some concerns". 

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The trial is described as an "open study," meaning that both the participants and the trial personnel administering the vaccines were aware of the assigned interventions. While the administration of a single booster vaccine leaves limited room for deviations from the intended intervention that could arise from the trial context, the analysis raises concerns. The authors state that both intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol analyses were performed, but only the per-protocol results are reported for the primary immunogenicity outcome because the results were "close enough." Excluding participants from the primary analysis based on post-randomization criteria (6 subjects were excluded for various reasons, including lack of serological results and protocol violations) rather than presenting a full ITT analysis introduces a risk of bias, leading to a judgment of "Some concerns".

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** Of the 127 randomized participants, 127 were included in the safety analysis and 121 (95.3%) were included in the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis. Only 4 subjects were missing pre- and post-vaccination serological results, while 2 others were excluded for protocol violations. Because outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, and the proportion of missing data is too small to have a clinically meaningful impact on the estimated true effect of the vaccines, the risk of bias due to missing outcome data is judged to be low.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias (for the primary outcome: immunogenicity)
* **Motivation:** The primary evaluation criterion was the anti-HAV antibody geometric mean titre (GMT) measured via a standardized, automated, and objective serological assay (modified HAVAB™ assay). Although the study was open-label (participants and personnel were unblinded), the measurement of antibody titres is an objective laboratory parameter that does not involve subjective judgment. Therefore, the assessment of this outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. *(Note: If the assessment were focused on the secondary outcome of reactogenicity/adverse events, which were self-reported via diary cards in an unblinded setting, there would be a higher risk of bias).*

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study report does not mention a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial protocol that was finalized prior to the unblinding of the outcome data. Although the reported outcomes (GMT, seroconversion rates, and standard reactogenicity) are expected and standard for vaccine trials, it is not possible to verify whether the data were analyzed strictly in accordance with pre-specified intentions or whether selective reporting of multiple eligible analyses occurred. Consequently, a judgment of "Some concerns" is applied.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Overall Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "Some concerns" in Domain 1 (due to a lack of reporting on sequence generation and allocation concealment), Domain 2 (due to the open-label design combined with the reporting of only a per-protocol analysis), and Domain 5 (due to the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan). While these issues raise some concerns regarding the internal validity of the study, there is no direct evidence of severe bias (e.g., no gross baseline imbalances or massive attrition) that would definitively place any single domain at a "High risk of bias". Therefore, the overall risk of bias for the primary outcome is judged as having "Some concerns."

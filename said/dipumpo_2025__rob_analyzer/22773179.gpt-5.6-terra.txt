## RoB 2 assessment

**Result assessed:** Effect of assignment to an **aspheric ablation profile (ASA)** versus a **non-aspheric/tissue-saving profile (TSA)** on **change in ocular spherical aberration (SA) at the 6-mm analysis zone, measured 3 months after LASIK**. This is a randomized paired-eye study: 15 participants contributed one ASA-treated eye and one TSA-treated eye. The assessment concerns the **effect of assignment to intervention**.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and motivation**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states that, for each patient, one eye received ASA and the other TSA, “**assigned at random using Microsoft Excel’s random number function**.” This is a computer-generated method with a random component.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The report does not state who generated the Excel random allocation, whether the sequence was secured, or whether the surgeon or recruiting personnel could foresee which eye would receive each profile before assignment. No central allocation, independent allocation process, or sealed-envelope method is reported.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  The reported preoperative spherical equivalent was similar between groups: −2.43 ± 1.53 D for ASA and −2.54 ± 1.47 D for TSA (*p* = 0.87). Preoperative SA at 6 mm was also very similar: 0.118 ± 0.094 µm in ASA and 0.123 ± 0.095 µm in TSA. No substantial imbalance in group sizes was present (15 eyes per group), and the reported baseline data do not indicate a problem with randomization.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Although a random sequence was reported and the reported baseline measures do not suggest failed randomization, allocation concealment was not described. Under RoB 2, lack of information on concealment, despite apparently comparable baseline values, leads to **some concerns** rather than low risk.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and motivation**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — No information.**  
  The article does not state whether patients were informed which ablation profile was used in each eye, nor whether masking of patients was attempted.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — No information.**  
  The report does not state whether the surgeon, laser operator, follow-up personnel, or other staff were masked to the assigned ablation profile.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — No information.**  
  No deviations, crossovers, profile substitutions, retreatments before the 3-month assessment, or non-protocol co-interventions are reported. However, the article does not explicitly report whether such deviations were assessed or absent.

- **2.4–2.5 Were deviations likely to affect the outcome and were they balanced? — Not assessable from the report.**  
  These questions cannot be resolved because the report does not provide information on whether deviations occurred.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Probably yes.**  
  All 30 randomized eyes appear in the 3-month results. The article reports a paired-eye design and states that Student’s paired *t*-tests were used for between-group comparisons of preoperative spherical equivalent and change in RAWS. There is no report that eyes were analysed according to a profile other than the one assigned, or that randomized eyes were excluded because of post-randomization events.

- **2.7 Potential substantial impact of failure to analyse according to randomization — Not applicable.**  
  No evidence is presented that eyes were switched between ASA and TSA or excluded from the assigned-profile analysis.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The analysis appears to retain the randomized eyes in their assigned groups, and no actual profile crossover or exclusion is reported. However, the blinding status of participants and personnel is not reported, and the report does not explicitly document whether deviations from the assigned treatment protocol occurred. In accordance with RoB 2, uncertainty about deviations arising in an unblinded or incompletely reported trial context results in **some concerns**.

A further reporting limitation is that the statistical methods do not clearly specify the method used for every comparison displayed in Figure 7 across the 0.5-mm analysis zones. This does not itself establish that participants were analysed outside their randomized groups, but limits certainty about the analysis of the paired-eye data.

The 3-month SA results and refractive outcome figures are reported for both groups, and the figures specify that they include data from 30 eyes at 3 months postoperatively. No loss to follow-up, missing SA measurement, withdrawn eye, or excluded randomized eye is reported.

- **3.2–3.4 — Not applicable.**

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The report provides 3-month outcome data for all 30 randomized eyes and gives no indication of missing SA measurements or post-randomization exclusions. Therefore, missing data could not plausibly have materially affected the estimated comparison for SA at 6 mm.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and motivation**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  SA was measured using the Carl Zeiss Meditec wavefront-supported custom ablation aberrometer. Measurements were undertaken before surgery and 3 months after surgery under cycloplegia using tropicamide 0.5%. Wavefront examinations were repeated until a 7-mm pupil diameter was obtained, and SA was calculated over analysis zones from 3 to 7 mm. These procedures directly measure the stated optical outcome.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  The same aberrometer, cycloplegic procedure, analysis-zone range, and follow-up time point were reported for both ASA and TSA eyes. No different measurement procedure, threshold, timing, or ascertainment pathway is described between groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — No information.**  
  The report does not say whether the personnel conducting or processing wavefront measurements were masked to ASA/TSA assignment.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  SA is an instrument-derived optical measurement rather than a participant-reported symptom or a clinician judgement outcome. The article describes a standardized aberrometric measurement and numerical analysis of wavefront data. Although assessor masking is not reported, there is no indication that knowledge of profile assignment could materially alter the machine-derived SA measurement under the same measurement protocol.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The outcome was measured with the same objective instrument and standardized procedure in both randomized groups. The absence of reported assessor masking is a reporting limitation, but the outcome is an objective aberration measurement with little scope for differential judgement. Therefore, the risk of material bias in outcome measurement is judged **low**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and motivation**

- **5.1 Were the data analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The article does not cite or provide a prospectively registered protocol, a statistical analysis plan, or a trial-registry record. Ethics approval and informed consent are reported, but these do not establish that the reported analysis was pre-specified before outcome data were available.

- **5.2 Is the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  SA was evaluated at multiple analysis zones, from 3 to 7 mm in 0.5-mm intervals. The article reports the comparative pattern in Figure 7 and reports selected pre/post values at 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7 mm in Table 1. However, without a pre-specified plan it cannot be established whether the 6-mm result, the analysis-zone range, or the specific presentation of results was selected after observing the data.

- **5.3 Is the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses? — No information.**  
  The article presents several related analyses: pre/post SA at different analysis zones, change in SA, SA induction per dioptre, RAWS, coma, and total higher-order aberration RMS. It also applies ANOVA with Bonferroni adjustment and paired *t*-tests for specified comparisons. In the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan, it cannot be confirmed that the reported comparative result was selected without reference to its magnitude, direction, or statistical significance.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
There is no accessible prospectively finalized protocol or statistical analysis plan. The outcome domain was evaluated at several pupil/analysis-zone diameters and with several related summary approaches. The available report does not demonstrate selective reporting, but it also cannot exclude it. Therefore, this domain has **some concerns**, rather than high risk, because there is no direct evidence that a favourable result was selected from multiple analyses.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Overall judgement: High risk of bias**

**Rationale:**  
The study has **some concerns** in three domains:

1. **Randomization process:** allocation concealment was not reported.  
2. **Deviations from intended interventions:** masking and information on possible trial-context deviations were not reported.  
3. **Selection of the reported result:** no pre-specified protocol or analysis plan was available, despite multiple SA analysis zones and related analyses.

The missing-data domain and outcome-measurement domain are judged at low risk. However, the accumulation of concerns across randomization concealment, deviations from intended intervention, and selective reporting substantially lowers confidence in the reported comparative SA result. Under the specified RoB 2 overall rule, **some concerns in multiple domains that substantially lower confidence in the result warrant an overall judgement of high risk of bias**.

# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Emollients in a propanol-based hand rub can significantly decrease irritant contact dermatitis
**Design:** Individually-randomized cross-over (within-subject, left/right cubital fossa) trial
**Comparison:** Propanol-based hand rub WITH emollients vs. propanol-based hand rub WITHOUT emollients
**Outcome assessed:** Sum score of erythema and dryness (ITT population, primary outcome)
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Sequence generation: "Randomization was performed via randomly permuted blocks of fixed size using validated software RANCODE 3.6," prepared by the Department of Statistics and Data Management — a clearly random, computer-generated process (signalling Q1.1: Yes).
- Allocation concealment: Randomization scheme was prepared centrally by an independent statistics unit, blinding codes were sealed until closure of the final database, and product assignment (A/B) to left/right cubital fossa was concealed from investigators and participants until unblinding (Q1.2: Yes).
- Baseline imbalance: Because this is a within-subject, split-body design (each subject receives both products), baseline comparability between "groups" is guaranteed by design; no imbalance in prognostic factors between comparison arms is possible (Q1.3: No).

No indication of manipulation or problems with the process was found.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- The study is described as "double-blind." Application was performed by a study nurse, and visual assessment for erythema/dryness was performed by "the same investigator," blinded to product identity (codes A/B were sealed until database closure). Thus participants, personnel (applying nurse) and outcome assessors were blinded to intervention allocation (Q2.1/Q2.2: No/Probably no).
- Because both participants and personnel were blinded, deviations from intended intervention arising from the trial context are unlikely (Q2.3: No/Probably no).
- Analysis: Results are presented for the ITT population, with missing data for 3 participants imputed via Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF). This is consistent with an appropriate ITT-oriented analytic approach retaining all randomized participants (Q2.6: Yes/Probably yes).
- 3 participants were excluded during the study due to protocol violations (used for the per-protocol analysis only), but the ITT population (n=35) result — the one being assessed here — retained all randomized subjects.

**Limitation noted:** The double-blind label is not elaborated for the study nurse who applied the products (whether s/he could distinguish products by odor/texture is not explicitly discussed), but no evidence of unblinding or contextual deviation was reported.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Outcome data were not fully available for all randomized participants: 3 of 35 subjects (8.6%) were excluded during the study due to protocol violations (intake of antiphlogistics in 1 case, disinfection of the fossa cubitalis in 2 cases), and their missing data points were handled via LOCF imputation for the ITT analysis (signalling Q3.1: No/Probably no — data not available for all/nearly all; complete case number reduced).
- LOCF is a single-imputation method that does not adequately correct for bias due to missing data, and is not, per RoB2 guidance, presumed to remove bias unless clearly justified (Q3.2: No evidence provided that this approach corrects for bias).
- Reasons for missingness (antiphlogistic drug intake possibly related to skin symptoms; disinfection of the test area, which could directly alter/mask the erythema/dryness outcome) raise the possibility that missingness could be related to the true value of the outcome, i.e. not clearly unrelated to prognosis for the outcome (Q3.3: Yes/Probably yes, could depend on true value).
- However, there is no strong or explicit evidence that missingness differed systematically between the two study arms/tolerabilities (in fact, since this is a within-subject paired design, both arms were affected equally for excluded subjects), and the amount of missing data is small (3/35, 8.6%), which somewhat mitigates the likely impact. There is no clear evidence, however, that this was proven not to bias the result (no sensitivity analysis reported beyond LOCF).

This leads to a judgment of "Some concerns" rather than "High risk," because the proportion of missing data is small and the paired/within-subject nature of the design limits opportunity for differential missingness between arms, but there is insufficient certainty that missingness was fully unrelated to the true outcome value.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Method of measurement: Visual assessment of erythema and dryness using a standardized, pre-defined ordinal scale (modified from Fitzpatrick et al.) is an accepted and appropriate method for this type of dermatological outcome (Q4.1: No/Probably no — not inappropriate).
- Measurement/ascertainment did not differ between intervention groups: Both preparations were assessed identically, at the same time points, using the same procedure and the same blinded assessor for both cubital fossae in the same subject (Q4.2: No).
- Outcome assessors: "All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator," and product codes were sealed until database closure — thus the outcome assessor (dermatologist) was blinded to intervention assignment (Q4.3: No — assessor unaware).
- Because assessment was blinded, potential influence of knowledge of intervention assignment on the outcome measurement is not a concern (Q4.4/Q4.5: Not applicable, given effective blinding of assessor).

This is a well-controlled domain: standardized visual scale, blinded single assessor, identical assessment procedure across arms.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- The primary outcome (sum score of erythema and dryness) and statistical analysis plan (non-inferiority testing via Fieller's method, one-sided 97.5% CI, non-inferiority margin of 1.2, sample size calculation) were specified with considerable detail in the Methods section, suggesting a pre-specified analytic approach (Q5.1: Probably yes) — however, no trial registry entry, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan (SAP) is cited or made available for independent verification. The article does not reference a trial registration number (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov), which was already a possibility/expectation at the time. This limits our ability to confirm that outcome measurements and analyses reported (erythema score, dryness score, and their combined sum score) were not selected post hoc from among multiple analyzed alternatives.
- Multiple related outcome measures were reported (erythema alone, dryness alone, the combined sum score, ITT and PP populations, atopic/non-atopic subgroups), and the primary outcome is reported as the "sum score," combining two components (erythema and dryness) that could conceivably have been analyzed and reported separately or with different weighting. Without an available pre-registered protocol/SAP dated prior to unblinding, it is not possible to fully exclude selective emphasis on the combined score presentation (Q5.2/Q5.3: No information).
- The Wilcoxon test result reported for the ITT population sum score (P=0.022) is reported alongside the non-inferiority-based Fieller confidence interval, and the switch from a non-inferiority to a superiority framework is explicitly justified in the text (citing regulatory guidance for switching between non-inferiority and superiority without inflation of Type 1 error), which somewhat mitigates concern about this specific switch, but does not fully resolve uncertainty about outcome/analysis selection for other secondary measures.

**Explicit ambiguity/limitation:** No protocol or trial registry record was cited or accessible for verification; therefore, judgments on this domain rely solely on internal consistency of the Methods and Results sections rather than external confirmation of pre-specification.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall Judgment: Some concerns**

**Rationale (per RoB 2 rules):**
- Domain 1 (Randomization process): Low risk
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions): Low risk
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data): Some concerns
- Domain 4 (Measurement of the outcome): Low risk
- Domain 5 (Selection of the reported result): Some concerns

No domain was judged to be at **High risk of bias**. However, two domains (Domain 3 and Domain 5) were judged to raise **"Some concerns."** According to the RoB 2 decision rules, since at least one domain raises some concerns and no domain is at high risk, the outcome is not automatically escalated to "High risk" — this would only be considered if concerns across multiple domains combined to substantially lower confidence in the result. In this instance, the "some concerns" in the missing-data domain relate to a small (8.6%) and adequately handled/documented degree of missingness within a rigorously blinded, within-subject paired design, and the "some concerns" in the selection-of-result domain stem mainly from lack of accessible external protocol documentation rather than from clear internal evidence of outcome-switching. These do not, in combination, appear severe enough to substantially undermine confidence in the primary result.

**Final overall judgement: Some concerns.**

**Key limitations/ambiguities identified during this assessment (reported per instructions, not resolved by assumption):**
1. No trial registration number or publicly available protocol/statistical analysis plan was cited in the article, limiting verification of pre-specification of outcomes and analyses (relevant to Domain 5).
2. The method used to handle missing data (LOCF) is a single-imputation approach not considered by RoB 2 guidance to reliably correct for bias, and no dedicated sensitivity analysis addressing missing data assumptions was reported (relevant to Domain 3).
3. Details on whether the treating/applying study nurse could distinguish the two formulations by sensory cues (odor, texture) were not explicitly reported, though no evidence of unblinding was found.

Based on a detailed analysis of the provided article using the RoB 2 framework, here is the structured Risk of Bias (RoB) assessment:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The study is a long-term follow-up of the randomized PORTEC-2 trial. While the specific details of the sequence generation and allocation concealment are not fully detailed in this specific secondary publication, the primary publications of the PORTEC-2 trial establish that it was a rigorously conducted, centrally randomized multicenter trial. The baseline characteristics of the current participants were compared and showed no significant imbalances that would suggest a problem with the randomization process between the intervention groups.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Given the nature of the interventions (external beam radiation therapy vs. vaginal brachytherapy), blinding of participants and personnel was not possible. However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended interventions occurred that were unbalanced between the groups or arose because of the trial context. The analysis appropriately focused on the groups as randomized (intention-to-treat approach), and the lack of blinding is unlikely to have led to non-protocol interventions that would systematically bias the long-term survivorship and health-related quality of life (HRQL) outcomes being assessed.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The response rate among eligible survivors was 77% (205 out of 265) at 7 years and 67% (80 out of 119) at 10 years. The authors noted that responders were slightly younger and had fewer comorbidities compared with the entire original PORTEC-2 cohort. Because the outcomes are health-related quality of life and symptom burdens, it is plausible that missingness (e.g., failure to return the questionnaire due to declining health, severe comorbidities, or death from intercurrent disease) could depend on the true value of the outcomes. While the response rate is reasonable for a 7- to 10-year follow-up, the differential baseline characteristics between responders and the original cohort raise some concerns about whether the missing data could bias the HRQL estimates. 

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The outcomes evaluated in this study are exclusively participant-reported outcomes (PROMs) assessing HRQL, bowel, bladder, and sexual symptoms using the EORTC QLQ-C30, specific subscales, and the Impact of Cancer Questionnaire. Since the participants were not blinded to their assigned intervention (EBRT vs. VBT), they were aware of the treatment they received. According to the RoB 2 guidance, for participant-reported outcomes where the assessor (the participant) is not blinded, there is a possibility that the assessment of the outcome could be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. Although the assessment occurred 7 to 10 years post-treatment—which might attenuate acute biases related to treatment expectations—the subjective nature of symptom reporting in an unblinded setting warrants "some concerns."

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The data were analyzed in accordance with standardized EORTC Quality of Life Group guidelines. The authors reported on all prespecified domains of the EORTC QLQ-C30, the symptom subscales, and the IOCv2 questionnaires. There is no evidence that the reported numerical results were selectively chosen from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple eligible analyses based on statistical significance or direction of the effect. The statistical models (linear mixed models and ordinal logistic regression) are standard for longitudinal HRQL data.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "Some concerns" in at least one domain (Bias due to missing outcome data and Bias in measurement of the outcome), but it is not judged to be at high risk of bias for any domain. The long-term follow-up inherently introduces attrition that may be related to the health status of the participants, and the reliance on unblinded self-reported questionnaires for subjective symptoms slightly lowers the confidence in the exact magnitude of the results, though it does not completely invalidate them. Therefore, following the strict RoB 2 mapping rules, the overall judgment is "Some concerns."

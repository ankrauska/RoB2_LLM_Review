Based on a detailed evaluation using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool for randomized trials, here is the structured Risk of Bias assessment for the provided article:

**1. Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
*   **Motivation:** The study used a sex-stratified randomization sequence and successfully concealed the allocation using sealed envelopes. Although some minor baseline differences in joint mobility were noted (Table 1), these are compatible with chance for a relatively small sample size (n=54), and the authors verified that the groups evolved similarly. There is no evidence suggesting a problem with the randomization process. 

**2. Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (Effect of assignment to intervention)**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
*   **Motivation:** Participants were blinded using a placebo ultrasound (device off). While the physiotherapist applying the treatment was not blinded, both groups followed a very strict and identical parallel protocol (microwave, massage, isometric exercises) differing only in the active/inactive state of the ultrasound device. There is no evidence that the lack of blinding of the therapist led to non-protocol deviations or compromised the interventions. Furthermore, the analysis was explicitly stated to be done by intention-to-treat (ITT).

**3. Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
*   **Motivation:** The study authors explicitly stated in the Methods section that "During the study no patient was lost or dropped out." Therefore, outcome data were available for 100% of the randomized participants, eliminating any risk of bias due to missing data.

**4. Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
*   **Motivation:** The methods of outcome measurement (VAS, algometer, inclinometer) were appropriate and comparable across groups. The study utilized an independent, blinded assessor ("A physiotherapist appraiser assessed the patients’ pain... Another physiotherapist executed the interventions"). Because the patients and the outcome assessor were effectively blinded to the intervention received, the assessment of the outcomes was highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the group assignment.

**5. Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result**
*   **Judgment:** Some concerns.
*   **Motivation:** While the reported results perfectly match the outcome measurements and time points specified in the Methods section (measured at baseline, session 10, and session 25), there is no information indicating that the trial was prospectively registered or that a pre-specified statistical analysis plan was finalized before unblinded data were available. Due to the absence of a publicly available protocol to definitively rule out selective reporting, a judgment of 'some concerns' is standard per the RoB 2 algorithm. 

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
*   **Judgment:** Some concerns.
*   **Motivation:** The study is rigorously designed in its execution, demonstrating low risk across the randomization, deviations from intervention, missing data, and outcome measurement domains. However, because there is no confirmed pre-specified protocol available to completely rule out selective reporting, the study is judged to raise "some concerns" in at least one domain (Domain 5), but it is not at high risk of bias for any domain.

Based on a thorough and detailed assessment using the RoB 2 tool for the provided randomized controlled trial (CTO-IVUS study), here is the structured Risk of Bias report for the primary composite outcome (Major Adverse Cardiac Events - MACE) at 12 months based on the intention-to-treat effect:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Participants were randomly assigned using an interactive web-based response system, which ensures both a random sequence generation and adequate allocation concealment since the randomization is centrally controlled and tamper-proof. Furthermore, an assessment of baseline clinical and angiographic characteristics (Table 1) shows that the groups were well-matched with no substantial imbalances that would suggest a problem with the randomization process. 

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Due to the nature of the intervention (IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided procedure), the operators performing the procedures could not be blinded. However, deviations in the procedure itself (such as higher frequency of high-pressure post-stent balloon dilation in the IVUS group) reflect the intended protocol of using IVUS to optimize stent deployment, rather than non-protocol deviations arising from the trial context. The study explicitly states that the intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses. Given that all randomized patients were analyzed in the groups they were originally assigned to, an appropriate analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Outcome data were available for nearly all participants randomized. The trial reports a follow-up completion rate of 99.5% in the IVUS-guided group (200 out of 201 patients) and 100% in the angiography-guided group (201 out of 201 patients) at 12 months. The proportion of missing outcome data is negligible (1 patient out of 402) and could not have made an important difference to the estimated effect of the intervention.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The outcome definitions (cardiac death, MI, target-vessel revascularization) were strictly predefined and measured appropriately according to standard guidelines (e.g., Academic Research Consortium). Ascertainment of the outcome did not differ between the intervention groups. Most importantly, all clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical event committee that was explicitly blinded to the randomization assignment, ensuring that the outcome assessors' judgments were not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The study protocol was pre-registered (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT01563952) prior to study completion. The primary endpoint (cardiac death) and the secondary endpoint (MACE) analyzed and reported in the published manuscript align perfectly with the pre-specified outcomes in the registry. There is no evidence that the results were selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the domain, nor were they selected from multiple eligible analyses based on the nature of the findings.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at low risk of bias across all five domains for the evaluation of the specified outcome. The randomization was properly conducted and concealed, missing data was essentially non-existent, the ITT analysis was appropriate, outcomes were adjudicated by a blinded independent committee, and the reported results strictly matched the pre-registered protocol.

Here is the detailed Risk of Bias (RoB 2) assessment for the provided randomized controlled trial article:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study used a "randomization automated system on a one-to-one basis," which ensures an adequate random sequence generation. Allocation concealment was properly implemented, as the "randomization list was held securely, and released only after study completion." Furthermore, baseline characteristics (such as age and dosage of methadone) showed no significant differences between the two groups, indicating no apparent problems with the randomization process.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study was double-blind, and both gabapentin and placebo were "dispensed in identical appearing capsules." Because both the participants and the trial personnel were unaware of the assigned interventions, it is highly unlikely that any deviations from the intended interventions arose due to the trial context or lack of blinding. 

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** There is a clear imbalance in missing outcome data between the two arms. By the end of the trial (Day 18), 3 out of 20 participants (15%) in the placebo group were excluded from the analysis, whereas 0 participants (0%) dropped out of the gabapentin group. The stated reasons for dropout in the placebo group were "non-compliance with the scheduled treatment programme and self-medication with illicit and/or over-the-counter (OTC) drugs." The authors explicitly state that this non-compliance "may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms." Because the dropout is unbalanced between groups and the reason for missingness is directly linked to the true value of the outcome (worse withdrawal symptoms), the missingness mechanism is informative and introduces a high risk of bias.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The outcome was measured using the Subjective Opiate Withdrawal Scale (SOWS), which is a valid and reliable indicator of withdrawal severity. Although this is a self-reported subjective outcome, the successful implementation of double-blinding using identical capsules ensures that the participants (who act as the outcome assessors here) were unaware of their assigned intervention, minimizing the risk that their knowledge influenced the assessment.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The trial reports the results across all the evaluated time-points and specific symptoms as described in the methods. However, there is no mention of a pre-specified, publicly available statistical analysis plan, trial registry entry, or published protocol that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. Without a pre-registered protocol, it cannot be fully verified whether the reported analyses and outcome measurements were selected from multiple eligible options based on the results.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at high risk of bias due to Domain 3 (missing outcome data). The disproportionate dropout rate in the placebo group—driven directly by the severity of the measured outcome (withdrawal symptoms)—violates the assumption of data missing at random and substantially lowers confidence in the final reported effect estimates.

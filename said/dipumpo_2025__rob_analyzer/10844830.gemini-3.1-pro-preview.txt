Based on the comprehensive application of the RoB 2 tool guidelines to the provided article evaluating the effect of inspired oxygen fraction (FIO2) after cardiopulmonary bypass, here is the detailed Risk of Bias (ROB) assessment for the outcome of venous admixture and Big ET-1 concentrations 20 minutes after weaning from the CPB machine (effect of assignment to intervention).

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study states that "Sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random 20 consecutive adults," which indicates a random component was used for sequence generation. However, there is insufficient detail on whether the allocation sequence was adequately concealed (e.g., whether the envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, and tamper-proof). Additionally, there are notable baseline imbalances between the two small groups (n=10 each), such as gender distribution (9/1 Male/Female in Group 1 vs. 5/5 in Group 2), type of surgery (Coronary vs. Valve), cardiopulmonary bypass times (116 vs. 84 mins), and significantly different pre-bypass pulmonary artery pressures (25 vs. 19 mm Hg). These imbalances, coupled with the lack of detail on allocation concealment methods, raise some concerns about the randomization process. 

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The trial involves adjusting the FIO2 on a ventilator while patients are under general anesthesia. Therefore, participants were completely unaware of their assigned intervention. While the healthcare providers adjusting the ventilator were inevitably aware of the intervention, there is no evidence to suggest that this knowledge led to deviations from the intended interventions that were inconsistent with the trial protocol and arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, all 20 randomized patients appear to have been analyzed in the groups to which they were assigned, aligning with intention-to-treat principles.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Outcome data for the variables of interest (venous admixture, Big ET-1, and ET-1 concentrations) appear to have been available and analyzed for all randomized participants (n=20) at the specified time points. There is no indication of participant dropout, loss to follow-up, or missing data that would compromise the completeness of the dataset or suggest that missingness could depend on the true value of the outcome.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The methods used to measure the outcomes (venous admixture calculated from standard blood gas analysis equations, and peptide concentrations measured via specific enzyme immunoassays) are appropriate and were applied consistently across both intervention groups. Although the personnel obtaining the samples and possibly analyzing them were likely aware of the intervention received, these outcomes are objective laboratory measurements. It is highly unlikely that the assessment of these objective outcomes could have been influenced by knowledge of the assigned FIO2 level.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** There is no mention of a pre-published trial protocol or a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available. While the reported outcomes (venous admixture and specific endothelial peptides) align with the objectives stated in the introduction, the absence of a registered protocol makes it impossible to definitively rule out whether the reported results were selected from among multiple eligible measurements (e.g., different time points not reported) or multiple eligible analyses based on the statistical significance of the findings.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise some concerns in at least one domain (Bias arising from the randomization process due to potential issues with allocation concealment and visible baseline imbalances; and Bias in selection of the reported result due to the lack of a pre-specified analysis plan), but it is not judged to be at high risk of bias for any single domain. Therefore, the overall risk of bias is classified as "Some concerns."

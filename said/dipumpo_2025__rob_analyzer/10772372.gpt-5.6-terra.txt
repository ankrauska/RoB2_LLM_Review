## RoB 2 assessment

**Study assessed:** *Impact of Pharmacy Counseling on Compliance and Effectiveness of Combination Lipid-Lowering Therapy in Patients Undergoing Coronary Artery Revascularization* (Pharmacotherapy 2000;20:410–416).

**Design:** Individually randomized, parallel-group controlled trial; 30 participants were randomized 1:1 to weekly pharmacist telephone contact for 12 weeks or no telephone contact. All participants received lovastatin plus colestipol.

**Result assessed:** The effect of **assignment** to weekly telephone follow-up versus no telephone follow-up on **long-term medication compliance at 2 years**, based on pharmacy refill records (including the reported proportion compliant with both agents). This is the outcome most directly corresponding to the stated purpose of evaluating telephone follow-up on compliance over two years.

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to the intervention (intention-to-treat effect).

**Important reporting limitation:** The supplied article text refers to Tables 1–5, but the numerical tables themselves are not included. Therefore, exact group-specific compliance, refill, lipid, and baseline values cannot be independently examined. Judgements below are limited strictly to the narrative report provided.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes**  
  The Methods state: *“Patients were randomized to telephone contact or no telephone contact using a computer-generated list of random numbers.”* This is an explicitly random sequence-generation method.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information**  
  The article does not describe who held the computer-generated list, who enrolled patients, who assigned the intervention, whether allocation was centrally administered, or whether any other mechanism prevented foreknowledge of the next allocation. A computer-generated sequence alone does not demonstrate allocation concealment.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — Probably no**  
  The article reports that *“Baseline characteristics were similar for both groups (Table 1).”* The actual Table 1 data are not available in the supplied text, so the balance cannot be independently verified. Nevertheless, the report does not identify an imbalance suggesting a failure of randomization, and group sizes were equal (15 per group).

**Motivation for judgement**

The random sequence was adequately generated. However, allocation concealment was not reported. Under RoB 2, where concealment information is absent but no problematic baseline imbalance is reported, the appropriate judgement is **some concerns**, rather than low risk. The missing Table 1 prevents independent confirmation that no clinically important baseline imbalance existed.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes**  
  Participants assigned to the intervention received weekly telephone calls from a pharmacist, whereas control participants did not. Participants therefore necessarily knew whether they received telephone follow-up.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes**  
  The pharmacist delivering the telephone intervention necessarily knew which participants were assigned to telephone contact. The study does not state that other treating clinicians were blinded.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no**  
  The telephone calls were the intended experimental intervention, not a deviation from it. The article does not report non-protocol co-interventions, implementation failures, differential treatment outside the study protocol, or contamination of the no-contact group with pharmacist telephone follow-up.

- **2.4 Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome? — Not applicable**  
  No deviations arising from the trial context were reported.

- **2.5 Were these deviations balanced between groups? — Not applicable**  
  No relevant deviations were reported.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes**  
  Fifteen participants were randomized to each group, and the report states that *“none of [the 30 patients] was lost to follow-up.”* The reported comparison is between the randomized telephone-contact and no-contact groups. The long-term outcome was compared using the proportion compliant in each group. There is no report that participants were analysed according to treatment received rather than random assignment, or that eligible randomized participants were excluded.

- **2.7 Was there potential for a substantial impact of failure to analyse participants in the groups to which they were randomized? — Not applicable**  
  The reported analysis does not indicate a departure from randomized-group analysis.

**Motivation for judgement**

Lack of blinding was unavoidable because receipt of telephone counseling is apparent to both participants and the pharmacist. However, for the effect of assignment to telephone follow-up, awareness alone does not establish bias. RoB 2 requires evidence that awareness caused deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context, affected the outcome, and were unbalanced. Such deviations are not reported.

All 30 randomized participants were retained through follow-up, and the study reports comparisons according to randomized groups. Therefore, there is no identified risk of bias from deviations from the intended interventions for the assignment effect.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Yes**  
  The Results state: *“Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up.”* Long-term compliance was assessed through pharmacy refill records at one and two years. The report presents results for the two randomized groups and does not describe missing compliance data.

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — Not applicable**  
  Outcome data were reported as available for all randomized participants.

- **3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value? — Not applicable**  
  The report indicates no loss to follow-up.

- **3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value? — Not applicable**  
  The report indicates no missing outcome data.

**Motivation for judgement**

The article explicitly reports that no participant was lost to follow-up. It describes refill-record assessments at both one and two years and does not report exclusions from these assessments. Consequently, missing outcome data are not an identified source of bias for the long-term compliance result.

A limitation is that the numerical tables are absent from the supplied material, so the completeness of the group-specific outcome denominators cannot be independently checked. Nonetheless, the explicit statement that none of the participants was lost to follow-up supports a **low-risk** judgement under RoB 2.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no**  
  Long-term compliance was assessed from pharmacy refill information: *“pharmacies at which patients filled their prescriptions were contacted at 1 and 2 years to document refill information.”* The predefined threshold was filling at least 80% of prescriptions. Refill records are an objective source for whether prescriptions were filled.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no**  
  The same stated long-term method—contacting pharmacies to document refill information—was used for both randomized groups at the same follow-up points. The report does not describe different ascertainment methods, thresholds, or schedules between groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants? — No information**  
  The article does not state whether the person contacting pharmacies or extracting refill data was blinded to telephone-contact allocation.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no**  
  The long-term outcome was based on pharmacy refill records and a prespecified numerical threshold. Although the assessor’s blinding status is not reported, determining whether prescriptions were filled is not described as requiring subjective clinical judgement. The report does not indicate that the assessor had discretion to classify pharmacy records differently by group.

- **4.5 Was assessment of the outcome likely to have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Not applicable**  
  Knowledge of intervention allocation was not likely to influence the objective ascertainment of whether a prescription was refilled.

**Motivation for judgement**

The outcome was assessed using pharmacy refill records, rather than patient self-report alone. The same definition of noncompliance—failure to fill at least 80% of prescriptions—was stated for both groups. Although the article does not report whether assessors were blinded, the outcome is based on an external record and a numerical rule, with little scope for subjective outcome-assessment bias. No evidence indicates differential outcome ascertainment between groups.

This judgement concerns the risk of biased *measurement*, not whether prescription refilling perfectly represents medication ingestion. The latter is a limitation of the compliance measure but is not, by itself, evidence of differential measurement bias under RoB 2.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information**  
  No trial protocol, trial registration record, statistical analysis plan, or date-stamped pre-specified analysis plan is provided. The article describes its statistical methods but does not establish that these were finalized before unblinded outcome data became available.

- **5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information**  
  Medication compliance was assessed at several time points: six weeks, 12 weeks, one year, and two years. It was also assessed separately for lovastatin and colestipol, and through different methods (pill/packet counts early and refill records later). The report includes both early and long-term findings, but no pre-specified plan establishes which time point, drug-specific outcome, or combined compliance outcome was primary for the reported long-term result.

- **5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information**  
  The study reports several related analyses: percentage compliance for each drug, the number classified as compliant using the ≥80% threshold, LDL target achievement, and lipid changes at multiple times. No pre-specified analytical plan is available to determine whether the reported two-year comparison was selected from multiple possible analyses on the basis of the observed findings.

**Motivation for judgement**

The article reports several outcomes and multiple follow-up times, including short-term compliance, one-year compliance, two-year compliance, individual-drug compliance, combined-drug compliance, lipid concentrations, and the proportion reaching LDL targets. The report does not provide a protocol or analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available. There is no direct evidence that the reported long-term compliance result was selectively chosen because it was statistically significant; however, the available report is insufficient to exclude selection from multiple eligible time points, outcome measurements, and analyses.

In accordance with RoB 2, this absence of prespecified analysis information leads to **some concerns**, not high risk, because selective reporting is not directly demonstrated.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Overall judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation**

The study is judged:

- **Some concerns** for bias arising from the randomization process, because allocation concealment was not reported;
- **Low risk** for deviations from intended interventions, because no relevant trial-context deviations were reported and the analysis appears to retain the randomized groups;
- **Low risk** for missing outcome data, because the study reports no losses to follow-up;
- **Low risk** for measurement of the long-term compliance outcome, because pharmacy refill records and the same predefined threshold were used for both groups;
- **Some concerns** for selection of the reported result, because no protocol or pre-specified statistical analysis plan was available and several eligible compliance measures, time points, and related analyses were reported.

Under the specified RoB 2 overall rules, the study has **some concerns** overall because it has some concerns in at least one domain, but no domain is judged at high risk of bias.

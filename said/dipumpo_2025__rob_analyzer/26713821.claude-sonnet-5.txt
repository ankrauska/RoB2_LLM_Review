# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Preliminary considerations:**
- Study design: Individually-randomized parallel-group trial
- Experimental intervention: Conventional medical care + nutritional counselling (caloric restriction)
- Comparator: Conventional medical care alone
- Outcome assessed: Cognitive change (composite domains: verbal memory, executive function, language/fluency, global cognition) at 12 months
- Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (ITT-type)
- Sources used: Journal article, trial registry record (NCT01286389)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgement: Some concerns**

Motivation: The article states patients were subject to "random allocation" 1:1 to groups, but no description is provided of the method used to generate the allocation sequence (no mention of computer randomization, random number tables, etc.) and no description of allocation concealment (no mention of sealed envelopes, central randomization, or third-party allocation). Table 1 shows baseline characteristics were well balanced between groups (all P>.05), which is compatible with chance and does not itself suggest a problem with randomization. However, because sequence generation and allocation concealment are both unreported ("No information"), and the baseline balance alone cannot compensate for this lack of methodological detail, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk." This is reported as a specific ambiguity/limitation: the manuscript provides no methodological detail on sequence generation or concealment procedures.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**Judgement: Some concerns**

Motivation: 
- Participants and personnel (nutritionists, geriatricians delivering care) could not be blinded, given the nature of the intervention (group nutritional counselling sessions vs. conventional care alone); this is inherent to behavioral/dietary interventions and was not disguised as a placebo condition. The protocol registry indicates only "outcome assessor" masking, confirming participants/personnel were unblinded.
- The authors explicitly report that the intervention did not achieve its intended contrast: both groups lost similar amounts of weight (intensive: −2.1 kg/m²; conventional: −1.3 kg/m²; P=.428), acknowledged by the authors as a major limitation ("weight loss difference between groups was lower than expected... nutritional counseling was a mild intervention" and possible contamination from motivational information given during recruitment to both groups). This indicates a substantial deviation from intended intervention contrast, likely arising from trial context (awareness of obesity risks in both arms), which could affect between-group comparisons for the originally-intended between group ITT effect.
- However, the authors appropriately pivoted their primary analysis away from the group-based ITT comparison to a continuous BMI-change exposure analysis, and used generalized linear models adjusting for relevant covariates (gender, education, physical activity, baseline test, age, APOE4) for the outcome of interest (association between ΔBMI and cognitive change), which is analytically appropriate for that continuous relationship. Analysis for the group-level comparison itself (GEE with group×time interaction) followed reasonable ITT-type principles (all randomized retained where possible).
- Given the deviations (contamination between arms) are acknowledged, likely affected the result (attenuating group differences), and are balanced across groups (both groups affected similarly per authors' description), this is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

This is reported as a specific ambiguity: the manuscript does not clarify whether care providers/nutritionists interacted with comparator group at all in ways that could have equalized intervention delivery.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgement: Low risk**

Motivation: Of 80 randomized patients, 75 completed follow-up (37/40 conventional, 38/40 intensive) — a low overall attrition rate (6.25%). Figure 1 (flow chart) documents this. Reasons for the 5 dropouts are given: 4 dropped out and 1 died of pneumonia — reasons unrelated to the true value of the cognitive outcome or to a differential mechanism between groups. Given the small proportion of missing data (<10%) and documented, plausible, non-outcome-related reasons, it is unlikely that missingness depended on the true value of the outcome. The proportion of missing data is small enough that it could not have made an important difference to the estimated effect.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgement: Some concerns**

Motivation:
- The neuropsychological battery used validated, appropriate instruments (RAVLT, TMT, CAMcog, MoCA, verbal fluency, Wisconsin Card Sorting), so the method of measuring outcomes was not inappropriate.
- Measurement/ascertainment methods did not differ between intervention groups (same tests administered to both groups at same time points).
- The protocol indicates outcome assessors were blinded ("masking: SINGLE; who masked: OUTCOMES_ASSESSOR"). However, several outcomes are participant-reported or involve subjective components (e.g., IQCODE - informant-reported cognitive complaints; self-reported dietary recalls used in correlational analyses) and, more importantly, many neuropsychological tests require face-to-face administration by an assessor who could become unblinded due to visible weight loss in participants (a highly noticeable physical change), potentially compromising the blinding despite intended masking of assessors.
- Because participants obviously knew their allocation (nutritional counselling attendance) and interacted with assessors, and visible weight loss could reveal group assignment to assessors administering subjective/semi-subjective tests (e.g., executive function tests requiring some judgment, verbal fluency scoring), there is a plausible risk that outcome assessment could have been influenced by (indirect) knowledge of intervention/weight status, although no direct evidence is provided that this occurred.
- Given uncertainty about whether true blinding of assessors was maintained throughout (not explicitly confirmed as successful in the article, only stated in registry), and the semi-subjective nature of several outcome measures, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

This is reported as a specific ambiguity: the article itself does not explicitly describe blinding procedures for outcome assessors or confirm that blinding was successfully maintained (only the trial registry mentions "outcomes assessor" masking), and does not address risk of unblinding via visible weight change.

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgement: High risk**

Motivation:
- The trial registry (NCT01286389) lists three primary outcomes: "Neuropsychological test performance," "Diagnosis of dementia," and "Weight loss." The publication does not report the pre-specified outcome "Diagnosis of dementia" at all, and does not report results according to the pre-specified group-based primary comparison for most cognitive outcomes as originally intended.
- Critically, the authors explicitly state a deviation from the pre-specified analytic strategy: "we decided to a posteriori pool the longitudinal analysis between groups and to test for the effects of change of BMI as a continuous variable" — a switch from the original between-groups intent-to-treat comparison (specified in the design as an RCT with 2 randomized arms) to a secondary/exploratory correlational analysis performed after observing the actual results (ie., that the two groups did not differ sufficiently in weight loss). This is a clear, self-acknowledged post-hoc change in analysis strategy driven by the results themselves.
- Multiple cognitive domain outcomes, multiple biomarker correlations, and multiple sub-group interaction analyses (age, APOE4 status) were explored, with selective emphasis on statistically significant findings (eg., "for verbal memory and language, there was interaction between BMI change and age," "for executive function... interaction between APOE4 status and BMI change") being highlighted in the abstract/conclusions while non-significant findings (e.g., attention domain) are dismissed briefly ("data not shown").
- No trial protocol or statistical analysis plan (SAP) was available/cited to confirm which analyses were pre-specified versus determined post-hoc; the only prospectively registered document (trial registry) confirms outcomes were altered/reduced in the final publication (dementia diagnosis outcome dropped; group-wise comparison de-emphasized in favor of continuous exposure analysis).
- Given the explicit, results-driven change in primary analytic approach and selective reporting/emphasis of statistically significant subgroup interactions without multiplicity correction (acknowledged by authors: "it was not possible to carry a multiple comparisons correction"), this domain is judged **High risk of bias**.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgement: HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

Rationale: According to RoB 2 rules, a judgement of High risk of bias in any single domain results in an overall High risk of bias judgement. In this study, Domain 5 (Bias in selection of the reported result) was judged as High risk due to the explicit, self-reported post-hoc shift from the pre-specified group-wise ITT comparison to an exploratory continuous-exposure analysis strategy driven by the observed lack of between group difference in weight loss, combined with non-reporting of a pre-specified primary outcome ("diagnosis of dementia") and lack of multiplicity correction across numerous subgroup/domain analyses. Additionally, "Some concerns" were present in three further domains (randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, and measurement of outcome), which independently would also support escalation toward a high risk judgement given the cumulative effect on confidence in the result. 

**Key limitations/ambiguities explicitly noted (as required, not assumed):**
1. No description of sequence generation method or allocation concealment procedure was reported in the article.
2. The intended between-group intervention contrast was not achieved (similar weight loss in both arms), a limitation explicitly acknowledged by the authors, raising concern about contamination/trial-context deviations.
3. Blinding of outcome assessors is stated only in the trial registry (not the article) and is not confirmed as successfully maintained, particularly given that assessors could plausibly detect visible weight loss.
4. A pre-registered primary outcome ("diagnosis of dementia") was not reported in the publication, and the primary analytic strategy was changed post-hoc from a group-based comparison to a continuous BMI-change exposure analysis, explicitly because the original comparison did not show the expected effect.

Here is the detailed Risk of Bias (RoB 2) assessment for the provided randomized controlled trial, evaluating the effect of assignment to the intervention (intention-to-treat effect) for the primary outcome (severity of depressive symptoms measured by the Beck Depression Inventory [BDI]).

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study states that participants "were randomly allocated to the treatment or wait-list group in consecutive order." While it mentions random allocation, the phrase "in consecutive order" is ambiguous and the exact method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated random numbers) is not described. Furthermore, while the automated nature of the online survey (OPST software) implies that allocation might have been concealed from the investigators, there is no explicit information provided regarding allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics appear balanced (Table 1), suggesting no obvious failures in the randomization process, but the lack of methodological details warrants a judgment of "Some concerns". 

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** Participants and trial personnel were inherently aware of the assigned interventions due to the nature of the trial (online therapy vs. wait-list control). However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended interventions arose because of the trial context that would compromise the intention-to-treat effect. The authors correctly performed an intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis utilizing multiple imputation to account for missing data, ensuring that participants were analyzed in the groups to which they were initially randomized regardless of their adherence.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The overall completion rate was 81.9% (85.7% in the wait-list group and 78.1% in the Deprexis group), meaning outcome data were not available for all participants. However, there were no major differences in the dropout rates between the groups, and baseline characteristics of dropouts did not differ significantly from completers on the primary outcome variables. Crucially, the authors employed a robust ITT analysis using multiple imputation (with 100 imputed datasets) to account for missing outcome data. This provides sufficient evidence that the result was not substantially biased by the missing outcome data.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The primary outcome (BDI) is a participant-reported outcome, meaning the study participants are the outcome assessors. Because the trial compared an active psychological intervention to a wait-list control, the participants were fully aware of the intervention they received. In trials with subjective, self-reported outcomes and a wait-list/no-treatment control group, the assessment of the outcome is highly likely to be influenced by the knowledge of the intervention received (e.g., due to expectation bias, disappointment in the wait-list group, or placebo effects in the active group). According to the RoB 2 guidance, this leads to a high risk of bias in the measurement of the outcome.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The trial was prospectively registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01401296) prior to the completion of data collection. The primary outcome (BDI) reported in the manuscript aligns with the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registry. There is no evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the depression domain, nor is there evidence that it was selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data based on the statistical significance or direction of the results.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Overall Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at high risk of bias overall. While the study performed well in utilizing appropriate ITT analyses to handle missing data and adhering to pre-specified reporting plans, it is judged to be at High risk of bias in Domain 4 (Bias in measurement of the outcome). This is a common and often unavoidable limitation in unblinded psychotherapy trials employing a wait-list control design combined with self-reported primary outcome measures, as the lack of blinding strongly influences the subjective assessment of symptoms. Furthermore, there are "Some concerns" regarding the lack of reporting details for the randomization and allocation concealment processes (Domain 1). According to the RoB 2 framework, a high risk of bias in at least one domain automatically leads to an overall judgment of High risk of bias.

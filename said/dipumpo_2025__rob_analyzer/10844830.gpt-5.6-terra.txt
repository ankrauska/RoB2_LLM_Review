## Scope of this RoB 2 assessment

RoB 2 is result-specific. This assessment addresses the **effect of assignment to high versus moderate inspired oxygen fraction after cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB)** on the article’s principal pulmonary gas-exchange result: **venous admixture measured 20 minutes after discontinuation of CPB**.

- **Experimental intervention:** FIO₂ 1.0 after CPB (group 1; n=10).
- **Comparator:** FIO₂ 0.35 after CPB (group 2; n=10).
- **Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to the intervention (intention-to-treat effect).
- **Source assessed:** The submitted journal article and its tables/figure only. No protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial-registry record was available in the supplied material.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — No information.**  
  The report states that “sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random” the 20 consecutive patients. However, it does not describe how the allocation sequence was generated. It does not state that computer-generated random numbers, a random-number table, random permuted blocks, or another method containing a random component was used. Under RoB 2, calling allocation “random” without describing sequence generation is insufficient to establish that the sequence itself was random.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The article reports only “sealed envelopes.” It does not state that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, opened sequentially, or opened only after irreversible participant enrolment. Thus, the information is insufficient to judge allocation concealment as adequate.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — Probably no.**  
  Baseline data are reported for both groups. Some differences are present, including sex distribution (9/1 vs 5/5), mean pulmonary artery pressure (25 vs 19 mm Hg), procedure type, and CPB duration (116 vs 84 minutes). However, the trial is very small (10 participants per group), no statistical evidence is reported that these baseline differences are beyond what could arise by chance, and the report does not demonstrate an imbalance in a key prognostic factor that is clearly too large to be compatible with chance or indicative of manipulation of allocation. CPB time is also a perioperative rather than a pre-randomization baseline characteristic. Therefore, the available baseline data do not establish a failure of randomization.

**Motivation for judgement**  
A low-risk judgement requires adequate allocation concealment. The reporting of sealed envelopes alone does not establish adequate concealment, and the method used to generate the allocation sequence is not reported. There is no affirmative evidence that randomization failed, but the unreported sequence-generation and concealment procedures raise concern. Therefore, this domain is judged as **some concerns**, rather than high risk.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably no.**  
  The relevant measurement was taken 20 minutes after CPB, during ongoing general anaesthesia and mechanical ventilation. Participants were therefore not in a position to be aware of the FIO₂ to which they had been assigned during this intraoperative period.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The intervention was the ventilator FIO₂ setting. Anaesthesia personnel delivering mechanical ventilation would necessarily have known the setting.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report states that, except for FIO₂, the ventilatory mode did not differ from pre-bypass settings “in order to preserve equal ventilatory conditions between the groups.” It also states that operations were performed using standardized CPB and that postoperative care was standardized. No non-protocol co-intervention, differential ventilation strategy, or failure to implement the allocated FIO₂ regimen is reported for the 20-minute post-CPB assessment.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Probably yes.**  
  The groups were analysed according to their allocated FIO₂ strategy. The reported between-group comparison used the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and the results are presented for the randomized groups. There is no reported crossover between FIO₂ groups or exclusion from the venous-admixture analysis.

**Motivation for judgement**  
Although personnel were aware of FIO₂ allocation, awareness alone does not establish bias for the effect of assignment. The report describes the ventilation conditions as otherwise equal and gives no evidence that deviations from the intended strategy occurred because of the trial context and affected venous admixture. The venous-admixture result was analysed by randomized group. This domain is therefore at **low risk of bias**.

**Important reporting limitation**  
The report notes that one participant in group 2 had persistently high arterial ET-1 values and that “these data were excluded from the statistical analysis.” This explicitly concerns ET-1 values and does not state that the participant’s venous-admixture data were excluded. Therefore, it cannot be used as evidence of exclusion from the venous-admixture result assessed here. It would require separate consideration for an assessment targeting the ET-1 outcome.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  Venous admixture was calculated from arterial and mixed venous blood samples using the standard oxygen-content shunt equation. Blood-gas and haemoximetry measurements are described, as is the equation used. The measurement approach is described sufficiently to show how the reported variable was derived.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Yes.**  
  The calculation of venous admixture was made under substantially different FIO₂ conditions: FIO₂ 1.0 in group 1 and FIO₂ 0.35 in group 2. The authors themselves state that their “oxygen shunt” was not a pure shunt because it included regions with low ventilation/perfusion ratios. They further state that calculation during pure oxygen breathing “eliminated any contribution to the shunt value from regions with low ventilation/perfusion ratios.” Therefore, the physiological components contributing to the calculated venous-admixture value differed systematically between groups as a consequence of the different oxygen concentrations at the time of assessment.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of intervention received? — Not applicable to the high-risk judgement.**  
  The high-risk judgement arises from the differential measurement/ascertainment issue in question 4.2. In addition, the numerical blood-gas and haemoximetry measurements are largely instrument-based and not principally dependent on subjective assessor judgement.

**Motivation for judgement**  
The concern is not that the laboratory instruments were invalid or that assessors subjectively interpreted the outcome differently. The concern is that venous admixture was calculated under different oxygen fractions, and the study itself explains that the calculation has a different physiological meaning under pure oxygen than under a moderate FIO₂. Specifically, pure oxygen eliminates the contribution of low ventilation/perfusion regions to the oxygen-shunt calculation, whereas this contribution remains under FIO₂ 0.35. Thus, measurement/ascertainment of the reported venous-admixture outcome could and did differ systematically between the intervention groups.

Under RoB 2, when outcome measurement or ascertainment could differ between intervention groups, this domain is at **high risk of bias**. The observed between-group difference in calculated venous admixture cannot be cleanly separated from the different measurement conditions created by the assigned FIO₂ levels.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

  No protocol, trial registration, or statistical analysis plan is provided. The article does not state that the analysis plan was finalized before outcome data were examined.

- **5.2 Is the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  Venous admixture was assessed at several reported time points: before CPB, 20 minutes after CPB, 90 minutes after ICU arrival, and on postoperative day 1. The article highlights the immediate post-CPB result, but no pre-specified primary time point is identified in the supplied report. Therefore, it is not possible to exclude selection of the reported time point from multiple eligible measurements.

- **5.3 Is the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The report describes repeated-measures Friedman analysis, Wilcoxon rank-sum testing between groups, and post-hoc Tukey testing “if appropriate.” No pre-specified plan identifies which analysis, comparison, time point, or adjustment strategy was primary. There is no evidence that the reported result was selected based on its statistical significance, but there is also no information that excludes this possibility.

**Motivation for judgement**  
No evidence of actual selective reporting is presented. However, the absence of a prospectively finalized analysis plan, together with multiple measurement times and potential analytic choices, means that the risk of selection of the reported immediate post-CPB result cannot be excluded. This domain is therefore judged as **some concerns**.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation for overall judgement**  
The result is at **high risk of bias overall** because it is judged to be at **high risk of bias in Domain 4 (bias in measurement of the outcome)**. The venous-admixture calculation was performed under the intervention-specific FIO₂ levels, and the report acknowledges that pure oxygen versus moderate oxygen changes which physiological components contribute to the calculated value. This creates systematically different outcome-measurement conditions between the groups.

In addition, there are **some concerns** regarding the randomization process because neither random sequence generation nor adequate allocation concealment is adequately reported, and there are **some concerns** regarding selection of the reported result because no prospectively finalized analysis plan or pre-specified primary time point is available.

Therefore, for the assessed result—**venous admixture 20 minutes after CPB comparing FIO₂ 1.0 with FIO₂ 0.35**—the definitive RoB 2 judgement is **high risk of bias**.

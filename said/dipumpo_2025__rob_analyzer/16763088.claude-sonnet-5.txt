# ROB 2 ANALYSIS REPORT

**Study:** Randomised clinical trial investigating bond failure rates using a self-etching primer (Transbond™ Plus SEP) vs. conventional etch and primer system
**Design:** Individually-randomized, split-mouth, cross-quadrant trial
**Outcome assessed:** Bracket bond failure rate over 12 months
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias Arising from the Randomization Process

**Judgment: SOME CONCERNS**

**Motivation:**
- **Sequence generation (Signalling Q1.1 – Yes):** The allocation sequence was generated using a documented block randomization method (Roberts and Torgerson, 1998), guaranteeing balanced allocation (5:5) within blocks of 10 consecutive subjects for the upper right quadrant. This constitutes an adequate random component.
- **Allocation concealment (Signalling Q1.2 – Probably Yes/Some concerns):** The article states "operators were unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial," suggesting concealment prior to enrolment. However, no explicit description is given of the concealment mechanism (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation), and given that a **fixed block size of 10 was used and disclosed in the methodology**, there is a theoretical risk that the last allocations within each block could become predictable to those enrolling participants, particularly since the study was conducted at a single site with a small number of operators (n=2). This raises a concern regarding the robustness of concealment.
- **Baseline imbalance (Signalling Q1.3 – No):** Table 1 shows no major baseline imbalances that would suggest problems with randomization (this is a split-mouth design, so each patient serves as their own control, largely eliminating patient-level confounding for the primary comparison).

**Limitation/Ambiguity:** The report does not provide sufficient detail on the concealment mechanism (e.g., whether allocation was performed via sealed opaque envelopes, a third party, or other mechanism). This lack of detail, combined with the disclosed fixed block size, prevents a "Low risk" judgment and is reported here as a specific ambiguity affecting the assessment.

---

## 2. Bias Due to Deviations from Intended Interventions

**Judgment: SOME CONCERNS**

**Motivation:**
- **Blinding of participants (Signalling Q2.1 – No):** Patients were unaware of which system was used on each side of the mouth ("patients were unaware which system had been used on each side of the mouth").
- **Blinding of personnel (Signalling Q2.2 – Yes):** The article explicitly states: "it was not possible to blind the operators to the type of system being used" because the two systems had different modes of application. This is an inherent and unavoidable limitation of comparing two etching systems with different clinical application protocols.
- **Deviations arising from trial context (Signalling Q2.3 – Probably No):** There is no strong evidence that unblinded operators introduced non-protocol co-interventions inconsistent with the study protocol. The application technique for each system followed standard manufacturer instructions consistently.
- **Appropriate analysis (Signalling Q2.6 – Yes):** All patients were followed for the full 12-month period (with one exception clearly documented and retained in the analysis with partial data), and the analysis appears to include all randomized units (brackets) according to their assigned etching system — consistent with an intention-to-treat-like approach at the bracket level.

**Rationale for "Some Concerns" rather than "Low":** Although lack of blinding of personnel was unavoidable given the nature of the intervention (different application techniques), the operators' awareness of the etching system being used introduces a theoretical risk of differential handling/care during bonding (e.g., number of manipulations, moisture control, pressure applied), which could influence the true bond failure outcome — an objective outcome, but one still susceptible to technique-dependent execution. Because personnel were not blinded and this could plausibly influence a technique-sensitive outcome, but there is no direct evidence that it did bias the result, this is judged as "Some Concerns" rather than "Low."

---

## 3. Bias Due to Missing Outcome Data

**Judgment: LOW RISK**

**Motivation:**
- **Signalling Q3.1 (Yes):** "All patients were observed for the entire 12-month period apart from one 13-year-old male who moved to another city after 7 months of observation, during which time no bracket failures occurred." This represents a loss of only 1/39 patients (~2.6%), and his data were retained and included in the survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier), which correctly accounts for censored data.
- The use of **Kaplan-Meier survival analysis** appropriately handles the one instance of censored follow-up (via right-censoring), which is a statistically appropriate method for handling this type of missing data in time-to-event outcomes.
- No other missing outcome data are reported; the full sample of 661 brackets across 38 completing patients (plus partial data from the 39th) appears to be accounted for in the results.

**Conclusion:** The amount of missing data is minimal and unlikely to have influenced the result, and appropriate statistical methods (Kaplan-Meier, Cox regression) were used to handle the one censored case.

---

## 4. Bias in Measurement of the Outcome

**Judgment: LOW RISK**

**Motivation:**
- **Appropriateness of measurement method (Signalling Q4.1 – No):** Bond failure was assessed as a binary, objective, and clinically observable event (whether the bracket had detached from the tooth) — a low-judgement, objective outcome. The adhesive remnant index (ARI, Årtun and Bergland, 1984) used to characterize the failure site is a well-established validated scoring method.
- **Differential ascertainment between groups (Signalling Q4.2 – No):** Given the split-mouth design, both etching systems were assessed within the same patient, at the same visits, by the same examiners, using identical assessment criteria and identical follow-up schedules. There is no indication that ascertainment of failure differed systematically between the two etching systems.
- **Blinding of outcome assessors (Signalling Q4.3):** Although operators were aware of which etching system was applied to which quadrant (unavoidable, as noted above), bracket failure/detachment is an objective, easily verifiable outcome (a bracket is either bonded or not) that leaves very little room for subjective interpretation or assessor judgement bias, unlike outcomes such as pain or quality-of-life scores.
- **Q4.4/4.5:** Because this is an observer-reported outcome not substantively involving judgement (a bracket is objectively either still bonded or has failed), knowledge of intervention assignment during assessment is very unlikely to have influenced classification of the primary outcome (bond failure/survival).

**Note:** Scoring of the ARI (adhesive remnant index) involves marginally more subjective judgement than failure/non-failure per se, but this is a secondary descriptive outcome and unlikely to materially bias the primary failure-rate comparison.

## 5. Bias in Selection of the Reported Result

**Judgment: SOME CONCERNS**

**Motivation:**
- There is **no evidence that the study was prospectively registered**, and no trial protocol or statistical analysis plan (SAP) is referenced, cited, or made available in the article. This is a limitation reported strictly as found: the article provides no information confirming that the outcomes and analyses reported (bracket failure rate, Cox regression, subgroup analyses by gender, age, operator, tooth location, number of manipulations, and ARI scoring) were pre-specified prior to unblinded data being available.
- **Signalling Q5.1 (No information):** No pre-specified analysis plan is cited or provided.
- **Signalling Q5.2/5.3 (No information):** Multiple subgroup and exploratory analyses are reported (gender, age, operator, tooth location, number of adjustments, ARI), some of which are explicitly noted by the authors as not tested statistically "due to low power" (e.g., tooth position differences), suggesting selective statistical testing/reporting decisions were made post hoc based on the data patterns observed, though the authors are transparent about this limitation.
- The primary outcome (bracket failure rate) and its comparison appear to be clearly and completely reported, with a sample-size/power calculation reported a priori (302 brackets per group needed for 80% power), which supports some assurance that the primary comparison was planned in advance. However, the number of secondary/subgroup analyses without clear prior specification raises the potential (though not confirmed) for selective emphasis on statistically significant findings (e.g., gender effect emerging only in survival analysis but not logistic regression, as acknowledged by the authors themselves: "This may be a question of the different power of the two different models").

**Limitation explicitly noted:** No independent trial registry entry, protocol, or SAP was available for cross-checking; this ambiguity is reported as a limitation of the assessment rather than assumed to indicate bias.

## 6. OVERALL RISK-OF-BIAS JUDGEMENT

**Overall judgment: HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Justification (per Table 1 of RoB 2 guidance):**

| Domain | Judgment |
|---|---|
| 1. Randomization process | Some concerns |
| 2. Deviations from intended interventions | Some concerns |
| 3. Missing outcome data | Low risk |
| 4. Measurement of outcome | Low risk |
| 5. Selection of reported result | Some concerns |

While no single domain was rated "High risk" in isolation, **three of the five domains ("Randomization process," "Deviations from intended interventions," and "Selection of the reported result") were judged as "Some concerns."** Per the RoB 2 overall algorithm, when a study has "some concerns" in **multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result**, the overall judgement should be escalated to **High risk of bias**. Here, the combination of (a) unclear/undisclosed allocation concealment mechanism combined with a disclosed fixed block size, (b) unavoidable lack of blinding of personnel in a technique-sensitive procedure, and (c) absence of a pre-specified/registered analysis plan with several post hoc subgroup analyses, cumulatively lowers confidence in the reliability and robustness of the reported treatment effect (11.2 vs 3.9% failure rate; HR 0.4, 95% CI 0.2–0.8), justifying an overall classification of **High risk of bias**.

**Reported limitation of this assessment:** This judgment is constrained by the absence of a publicly available trial protocol, registry record, or statistical analysis plan, which precluded verification of pre-specification of outcomes/analyses and full assessment of the allocation concealment procedure. This ambiguity is reported here as a limitation rather than assumed to reflect a specific bias.

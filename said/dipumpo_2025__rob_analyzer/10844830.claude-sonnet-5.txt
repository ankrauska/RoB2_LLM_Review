# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** "Inspired oxygen fraction after cardiopulmonary bypass: effects on pulmonary function with regard to endothelin-1 concentrations and venous admixture"
**Design:** Individually randomized parallel-group trial (n=20; 10 vs 10)
**Effect of interest assessed:** Effect of assignment to intervention (high vs moderate FIO2 after CPB)
**Outcomes assessed:** Arterial/mixed venous Big ET-1, ET-1, and venous admixture (Qs/Qt)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process
**Judgement: Some concerns**

- **Sequence generation:** The article states only that "sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random 20 consecutive adults." No description of how the random sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated numbers, random number table). This is insufficient to confirm a truly random process → answer "No information/Probably yes" is not strongly supportable given the brief description.
- **Allocation concealment:** Only "sealed envelopes" are mentioned, with no confirmation that they were opaque, sequentially numbered, or that opening was sequential and irreversible. This falls short of the extended/minimal criteria for adequate concealment (Table 2 of RoB 2 guidance).
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table 2 reveals notable imbalances: sex distribution (9M/1F vs 5M/5F), mean pulmonary artery pressure (25 vs 19 mmHg — explicitly acknowledged by the authors as "indicating the difficulty of getting a good match in this kind of study"), and CPB duration (116 vs 84 min). Given the very small sample size (n=10/group), these differences could plausibly reflect a problem with the randomization process rather than pure chance, though a formal statistical excess of imbalance cannot be firmly established from the data provided.

**Motivation:** The combination of an inadequately described allocation concealment method and clinically relevant (and author-acknowledged) baseline imbalances in a very small trial raises concerns about randomization integrity, without clear evidence of major failure. This meets the "Some concerns" criteria (inadequate/unclear concealment plus baseline imbalance signals).

**Limitation/ambiguity:** No further methodological detail regarding envelope characteristics (opacity, sequential numbering, tamper-proofing) was reported, and this ambiguity could not be resolved as no protocol or supplementary methods were available.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)
**Judgement: Some concerns**

- **Blinding of participants/personnel:** The intervention (FIO2 setting) was open-label by necessity, as clinicians actively titrate the ventilator FIO2; no blinding of clinical staff is reported or plausible.
- **Deviations from trial context:** Because FIO2 is a directly clinician-administered/titrated parameter (not a behavioral or psychological intervention), the risk of trial-context-driven deviations (e.g., participants seeking non-protocol interventions) is low. Co-interventions (fentanyl/midazolam dosing, ventilatory settings) were stated to be standardized and equivalent between groups except for FIO2.
- **Analysis:** All 20 randomized patients appear to have been analyzed in their assigned groups (ITT-consistent), **except** that one outlier patient in Group 2 was excluded from the ET-1 statistical analysis post hoc ("data were excluded from the statistical analysis in order to simplify statistical interpretation"). This is a deviation from ITT principles and constitutes an inappropriate, results-based post-randomization exclusion for at least the ET-1 outcome.

**Motivation:** While lack of blinding is not likely to have introduced trial-context bias given the objective/physiological nature of the intervention and outcomes, the explicit post hoc exclusion of an outlier's data for the ET-1 analysis constitutes an inappropriate analytical deviation from full ITT principles, without pre-specified justification. This impacts one of the three co-primary outcomes (ET-1) specifically.

**Limitation:** No trial protocol or pre-specified statistical analysis plan was available to confirm whether outlier-exclusion rules were pre-specified; this is treated as an unplanned, results-influenced exclusion based on the text provided.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data
**Judgement: Some concerns**

- Data appear available for all 20 randomized participants for venous admixture and Big ET-1 outcomes.
- However, for **arterial ET-1**, one participant in Group 2 was excluded due to being an "outlier" with persistently high values, and no clear methodological or biological justification (other than statistical convenience) was provided ("Comparisons with the patient's data revealed no explanation for this finding").
- No sensitivity analysis was performed to assess robustness of the ET-1 result to inclusion/exclusion of this participant.

**Motivation:** With a sample size of only 10 per group, exclusion of a single participant represents 10% of one arm's data for a specific outcome (ET-1), which could plausibly have a substantial impact on this result. The reason for exclusion (extreme/outlying value) is plausibly related to the true value of the outcome, raising concern that missingness may not be at random with respect to the outcome.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome
**Judgement: Low risk**

- **Outcome type:** ET-1, Big ET-1 (via standardized sandwich enzyme immunoassay with reported sensitivity/reproducibility data) and venous admixture (calculated from standardized arterial/mixed venous blood gas measurements) are objective, laboratory/biochemical measures not involving subjective clinical judgement.
- **Assessor blinding:** Although it is unclear whether laboratory personnel performing ET-1/Big ET-1 assays were explicitly blinded to group allocation, the nature of the assay (automated colorimetric ELISA reading) makes it highly unlikely that knowledge of group assignment could influence the numerical result.
- **Measurement method consistency:** The same measurement protocol, timing, and equipment were used identically across both groups at each time point (Table 1), and there is no indication that outcome ascertainment differed by group beyond the FIO2 intervention itself.

**Motivation:** Given the objective, automated, and standardized nature of the outcome measurements, and no plausible mechanism for assessor-based influence on these biochemical/gas-exchange values, risk of bias in outcome measurement is judged low despite the absence of explicit statements about blinding of laboratory personnel.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result
**Judgement: Some concerns**

- No trial registration, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or available for comparison against the reported results.
- Multiple outcomes/time-points were measured (Big ET-1, ET-1, venous admixture at four time points), and results are reported comprehensively across all time points and both peptides, which is reassuring.
- However, the **post hoc exclusion of an outlier data point** specifically for the ET-1 analysis, explicitly justified by data pattern rather than a pre-specified rule, raises concern that this represents a results-driven analytical decision (selection of the analysis or effective post hoc trimming of the dataset), which could have inflated between-group homogeneity in ET-1 findings by removing a value contrary to the analysis's need for "simplicity."

**Motivation:** In the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan, and given a clear instance of unplanned exclusion of data justified only by convenience for interpretation, there is a realistic possibility that outcome selection or analytical choices were influenced by the observed results, particularly for the ET-1 outcome.

**Limitation:** No protocol/registry was available to confirm or refute pre-specification; the judgement is based solely on internal inconsistencies described within the article itself.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgement: High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** Although no single domain was rated as unambiguously "High risk," the study shows "Some concerns" across **multiple domains** (Domain 1 – randomization process; Domain 2 – deviations from intended intervention/analysis; Domain 3 – missing outcome data; Domain 5 – selection of the reported result). Per RoB 2 guidance (Table 1), when a study exhibits "some concerns" for multiple domains in a way that cumulatively and substantially lowers confidence in the result—particularly given (a) unclear allocation concealment combined with notable, author-acknowledged baseline imbalances in a very small sample (n=20 total), and (b) an unplanned, results-influenced exclusion of outcome data affecting the ET-1 result with no sensitivity analysis to test robustness—the cumulative effect is judged sufficient to warrant an overall classification of **High risk of bias** for this trial's results, especially regarding the ET-1 endpoint. The venous admixture and Big ET-1 findings, while less affected by the missing-data/selection concerns, remain subject to the same randomization-process and small-sample-size concerns that pervade the entire trial.

**Key overall limitations/ambiguities to note:**
1. No trial protocol, registration, or statistical analysis plan available for verification of pre-specified methods.
2. Sequence generation and allocation concealment procedures were described in only minimal detail ("sealed envelopes"), precluding a definitive "low risk" judgement for randomization.
3. Very small sample size (n=10/group) amplifies the potential impact of both baseline imbalances and the single-participant exclusion on effect estimates.
4. Justification for excluding the ET-1 outlier was explicitly stated as being "to simplify statistical interpretation," which is a data-driven rather than pre-specified rationale.

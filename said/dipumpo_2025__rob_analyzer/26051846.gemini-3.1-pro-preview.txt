Here is the detailed Risk of Bias (RoB 2) assessment for the provided randomized controlled trial.

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study reports using an adequate random sequence generation method ("random permuted blocks method, applied by a statistician"). However, there is insufficient information regarding how the allocation sequence was concealed at the point of participant enrolment (e.g., whether opaque sequentially numbered envelopes or a central remote system was used). While Table 1 shows no significant baseline imbalances, the lack of explicit details on allocation concealment raises some concerns that the recruiters could have potentially foreseen the assignments.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (Effect of assignment to intervention)**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The trial is described as "single-blind," and while the assessing doctor was blinded, the participants and physiotherapists delivering the interventions likely were not. The treatment modalities varied noticeably in their administration (e.g., 5 minutes for ultrasound and phonophoresis versus 20 minutes of galvanic direct current for iontophoresis). Therefore, participants were likely aware of their assigned intervention, which could influence their expectations and behavior (trial context). However, there is no evidence that these deviations led to an unbalanced implementation of non-protocol interventions, and it appears that all randomized participants were included in the analysis (99 randomized and 99 analyzed), somewhat mitigating the risk.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The trial randomized 99 participants (32 in ultrasound, 33 in phonophoresis, and 34 in iontophoresis). The results reported in the tables reflect data for the full randomized sample, and no dropouts, losses to follow-up, or missing data are mentioned. Therefore, outcome data were available for all or nearly all randomized participants.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The outcomes evaluated included both objective/clinician-assessed measures (Constant-Murley Scale) and subjective/participant-reported measures (VAS for pain, DASH questionnaire). The report explicitly states that the rehabilitation doctor who performed the assessments was blinded to the treatment groups. However, for the participant-reported outcomes (VAS, DASH), the participants act as the outcome assessors. Given that participants were likely aware of their intervention (due to the stark differences in application times and methods between ultrasound and iontophoresis), their knowledge of the received intervention could have influenced their subjective reporting of pain and upper limb disability. 

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study was registered in advance on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01748188). A comparison with the provided protocol summary demonstrates that the trial analyzed and reported the specified primary outcome (VAS) and secondary outcomes (DASH and Constant scores) exactly as planned. The data were evaluated at the pre-specified time points (post-treatment and 1-month post-treatment), with no indication that multiple outcome measurements or analyses were generated and selectively reported based on statistical significance.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "Some concerns" overall. This is driven by a lack of methodological detail regarding allocation concealment (Domain 1), the high likelihood that participants were not effectively blinded to the drastically different interventions which could influence behaviors (Domain 2), and the potential for this lack of participant blinding to influence the subjective, self-reported outcome measures like the VAS and DASH (Domain 4). However, there is no domain judged as being at a definitive "High risk of bias."

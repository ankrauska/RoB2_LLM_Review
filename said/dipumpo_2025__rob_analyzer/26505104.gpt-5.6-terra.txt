## RoB 2 assessment

**Study design:** Individually randomized, parallel-group educational trial.  
**Result assessed:** The reported primary diagnostic-learning result: the condition-by-time interaction for diagnostic-test performance (immediate versus 1-week delayed testing), comparing the four randomized instructional conditions (SC, BC, CB, and FL): *F*(3,88) = 3.05, *P* < .03. The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to the allocated instructional condition**.

**Important limitation of the assessment:** RoB 2 is designed for a specific comparison between two interventions and a specific outcome result. The article reports a four-group omnibus interaction, rather than one uniquely defined pairwise intervention effect. This assessment therefore addresses the reported omnibus diagnostic-performance result, including the reported comparisons involving the SC condition. No protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial-registry record is available in the supplied report.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — No information.**  
  The article states only that “participants were randomly allocated to one of four study conditions.” It does not report how the allocation sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, block randomization).

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  There is no description of allocation concealment, central randomization, use of an independent allocator, or sealed opaque envelopes.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No information.**  
  The report provides limited descriptive information on participant profession and on the number who completed rheumatology versus neurology materials, but it does not present baseline characteristics by randomized group. Consequently, it is not possible to determine whether relevant baseline differences were compatible with chance or indicated a problem with randomization.

**Motivation for judgment:**  
The study is described as randomized, but the report does not provide sufficient information to establish that the allocation sequence was random or that allocation was concealed. No usable baseline-comparability table is reported. Under RoB 2, absence of information on concealment and absence of usable baseline data leads to **some concerns**, rather than low risk of bias. There is no direct evidence that allocation was non-random or manipulated; therefore, high risk is not justified for this domain.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgment: High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Participants received visibly different instructional materials: a clinical-feature list, science-based causal explanations, or separated basic-science and clinical-feature explanations in different orders. These conditions could not realistically be concealed from participants.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably yes.**  
  The study was completed individually using a custom computer program. Although the report does not explicitly identify personnel involved in delivery, the intervention materials necessarily differed by assigned condition.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report does not identify crossovers, contamination, differential co-interventions, failure to deliver the allocated learning material, or other trial-context deviations from the instructional conditions. The study involved a short, computer-based learning intervention delivered individually.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — No.**  
  The final diagnostic analysis did not include all randomized participants. Five enrolled participants who did not complete the delayed session were excluded. In addition, two participants—one from BC and one from CB—were excluded after the investigators inspected their delayed diagnostic-test time and performance. The report states that these participants spent less than five minutes on the delayed test and had scores three standard deviations below their respective group means, including scores of 0% and 8%. These were post-randomization exclusions of participants with observed outcome data, not exclusions due solely to unavailable outcome data.

- **2.7 Was there potential for a substantial impact of failure to analyse participants in the groups to which they were randomized? — Probably yes.**  
  The two additionally excluded participants had extremely low delayed-test scores and were excluded from the BC and CB groups, which are two groups contributing to the reported condition-by-time interaction and post hoc comparisons. With approximately 21–25 analysed participants per group, removal of one very low-scoring participant from each of two groups could materially alter group means, estimates of decline over time, and the reported between-condition contrasts. The exclusions were based on post-randomization outcome-related information.

**Motivation for judgment:**  
Knowledge of allocation alone does not establish bias in the effect of assignment, because the article does not report trial-context deviations from the allocated instructional materials. However, the reported analysis excluded randomized participants after inspection of their delayed-test behaviour and poor outcome performance. This is not a full intention-to-treat analysis, nor a modified intention-to-treat analysis limited only to missing outcome data. The exclusion of two participants with extremely low scores from particular intervention groups could have a substantial impact on the reported diagnostic-performance interaction and pairwise comparisons. Therefore, this domain is at **high risk of bias**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgment: High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — No.**  
  Five participants did not complete delayed testing and were excluded: three from CB and two from BC. Thus, delayed diagnostic data were missing for 5 participants. The report also contains inconsistent participant accounting: it states a final sample of 94 participants but lists group totals of 25 FL, 21 CB, 24 BC, and 25 SC, which sum to 95. The figure further reports SC as 26 at immediate testing and 25 at delayed testing, but the text does not explain this apparent change. Therefore, the exact number and distribution of participants with delayed diagnostic data cannot be established consistently from the report.

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — No.**  
  No sensitivity analysis, imputation analysis, inverse-probability weighting, or other analysis addressing the impact of missing delayed outcomes was reported.

- **3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value? — Yes.**  
  The report gives no reasons for the five participants’ failure to complete the delayed session. In an educational study measuring retention and diagnostic performance, withdrawal or non-return for delayed testing could plausibly be related to engagement, learning, retention, or other factors related to the delayed diagnostic outcome.

- **3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value? — Probably yes.**  
  Missing delayed-session data were not balanced across the reported groups: three participants were missing from CB and two from BC, whereas none were reported missing from FL. The report does not provide reasons for non-completion or analyses showing that missingness was unrelated to delayed diagnostic performance. Moreover, the internal inconsistency in reported group totals prevents a reliable reconstruction of missing data across all groups.

**Motivation for judgment:**  
The reported diagnostic result depends on delayed testing, yet outcome data were unavailable for participants who did not return after one week. The reasons for non-completion are not reported. Missingness was concentrated in the BC and CB conditions, and participant-flow reporting is internally inconsistent. There is no analysis demonstrating that plausible missing outcomes would not alter the result. In accordance with RoB 2, missing outcome data are at **high risk of bias** when outcome data are not available for nearly all randomized participants, missingness could and is likely to depend on the true outcome value, and there is no evidence that the result is robust to missing data.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  The diagnostic test consisted of 12 written clinical vignettes, each requiring selection of the correct diagnosis from four alternatives. The materials and cases were developed by a clinician and reviewed by an independent clinical expert. The article also states that these materials had been used in previous related studies and had demonstrated “adequate measurement properties” for the stated experimental purpose.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  All conditions completed the same type of diagnostic test under the same computerized study procedure. The report describes counterbalanced versions of the test administered at immediate and delayed testing; it does not report differential assessment procedures by instructional condition.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — No / not material to the result.**  
  The outcome was the number or percentage of correct answers on a multiple-choice diagnostic test completed through a computer program. Scoring of correct versus incorrect responses is objective. The report does not indicate that subjective assessor judgment was used to score the diagnostic test.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — No.**  
  The outcome involved objectively scored responses to fixed-answer diagnostic vignettes. Although participants knew the form of their instructional material, knowledge of allocation could not alter which answer was designated correct or the scoring of the response.

**Motivation for judgment:**  
The diagnostic outcome was measured using the same objectively scored multiple-choice test format across groups. The report does not indicate differential outcome ascertainment or subjective assessor judgment that could be influenced by knowledge of the assigned instructional condition. Therefore, there is **low risk of bias** in measurement of the diagnostic-test outcome.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The article describes its intended primary analysis as a mixed repeated-measures ANCOVA, but no protocol, trial registration, or dated statistical analysis plan is reported or available in the supplied material. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported analysis was finalized before investigators had access to unblinded outcome data.

- **5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  The study measured diagnostic performance at immediate and delayed testing, used two counterbalanced diagnostic-test versions, and included two sets of learning materials (neurology and rheumatology). The article reports the condition-by-time interaction and selected post hoc contrasts, but no pre-specified document is available to determine whether this result was selected from multiple eligible time points, outcome definitions, material sets, or subgroup-specific results on the basis of the findings.

- **5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The report states that covariates were selected “based on the correlations between performances on the diagnostic and memory test as well as between performance and time per vignette.” This describes data-based selection of covariates, but it does not establish which analyses were planned in advance, what alternative models were considered, or whether alternative adjusted and unadjusted analyses were conducted. The report also presents post hoc least-significant-difference comparisons. Without a protocol or SAP, selection of the reported analysis cannot be excluded.

**Motivation for judgment:**  
There is no evidence of a pre-specified, dated analysis plan finalized before access to unblinded results. The diagnostic outcome was assessed at multiple time points, with alternative test versions and two content areas, and the final model included covariates selected on the basis of observed correlations. The report does not provide direct evidence that the reported significant interaction or post hoc comparisons were selected because of their results; therefore, high risk is not established. Nevertheless, the absence of pre-specified analysis intentions creates **some concerns** about selective reporting of the reported result.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgment  
**Overall judgment: High risk of bias**

**Motivation:**  
The study is judged at **high risk of bias** in:

1. **Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**, because the reported analysis excluded two randomized participants with observed, extremely low delayed diagnostic-test scores after inspection of post-randomization outcome-related information; and  
2. **Bias due to missing outcome data**, because delayed diagnostic data were missing for participants who did not return, missingness was unevenly distributed across groups, reasons for missingness were not reported, no robustness analysis was provided, and participant-flow totals are internally inconsistent.

The study also has **some concerns** regarding the randomization process and selection of the reported result. Although measurement of the diagnostic-test outcome is judged at low risk of bias, the high-risk judgments in Domains 2 and 3 require an overall judgment of **high risk of bias** under RoB 2.

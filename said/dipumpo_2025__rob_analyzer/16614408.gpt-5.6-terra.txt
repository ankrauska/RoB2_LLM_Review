## RoB 2 assessment

**Result assessed:** effect of assignment to fish-oil supplementation containing 3.6 g/day of (n-3) fatty acids versus olive-oil placebo on **plasma 8-iso-prostaglandin F2α (8-iso-PGF2α) after 3 months**, combined across the two background-diet groups. This is the principal reported positive finding (P = 0.015).  
**Design:** multicentre, individually randomized 2×2 factorial parallel-group trial (SAFA versus MUFA diet; fish oil versus placebo).  
**Effect of interest:** effect of **assignment** to fish-oil rather than placebo (intention-to-treat effect).  
**Sources available for this assessment:** the submitted journal article only. No protocol, statistical analysis plan, trial-registry record, or CONSORT participant-flow diagram was available in the supplied material.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

* **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?** **No information.** The article says participants “were randomly assigned” to the diet and then had a “2nd random assignment” to fish oil or placebo, but it does not describe how either random sequence was generated (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, blocks, stratification).
* **1.2 Was allocation concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?** **No information.** No mechanism for allocation concealment is reported. There is no reference to central randomization, an independent pharmacy, a remote allocation system, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes.
* **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process?** **Probably no.** Table 1 presents broadly similar baseline age, BMI, lipid concentrations, glucose, and blood-pressure values across the four randomized groups. No marked imbalance or substantial departure from the intended approximately equal group sizes is apparent (42, 41, 40, and 39 participants).

**Motivation for judgment**

The reported baseline data do not indicate that randomization failed. However, the article provides no usable information on either random-sequence generation or allocation concealment. In particular, the statement that participants were “randomly assigned” is insufficient under RoB 2 to establish that the sequence had a random component or that recruiters could not foresee allocations. Because baseline balance is compatible with chance but allocation concealment is unreported, the RoB 2 algorithm gives **some concerns** rather than low risk of bias.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

* **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?** **No information.** The article identifies fish-oil capsules and olive-oil placebo capsules but does not state that participants were blinded, whether the capsules were matched in appearance/taste/smell, or whether blinding was assessed.
* **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial?** **No information.** No information is provided on blinding of investigators, dietitians, research personnel, or staff providing capsules.
* **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?** **No information.** The article does not report capsule discontinuation, switching, use of prohibited supplements, use of fish products, contamination between groups, or other protocol deviations. Participants were instructed not to use nutritional supplements and not to consume fatty fish/products, but adherence to these instructions and deviations are not reported.
* **2.4–2.5** **Not applicable/not answerable**, because deviations arising from the trial context were not documented.
* **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?** **No information.** The article reports a model including diet, (n-3) supplementation, their interaction, centre, age, sex, and baseline outcome value. However, it does not explicitly state that all randomized participants with available outcome data were analysed according to their original fish-oil/placebo assignment. It does not state whether participants were excluded after randomization, whether data were analysed as treated, or whether the analysis was intention-to-treat.
* **2.7 Was there potential for a substantial impact of failure to analyse participants in the randomized groups?** **No information.** There is no participant-flow information, no number analysed for 8-iso-PGF2α in each group, and no information on exclusions or treatment discontinuations. Therefore, the possible impact cannot be assessed.

**Motivation for judgment**

The biomarker analysis provides indirect evidence that fish-oil intake increased—serum phospholipid fatty-acid changes are said to have reflected increased EPA/DHA intake—but this does not establish that all randomized participants were retained and analysed in their assigned groups. More importantly, the report does not identify the analysis population for the assessed outcome and does not document an intention-to-treat or modified intention-to-treat analysis.

Under RoB 2 for the effect of assignment, when the appropriateness of the analysis is not established (**2.6: no information**) and the potential impact of exclusions or analysis in the wrong group also cannot be excluded (**2.7: no information**), the domain is judged **high risk of bias**. This judgment is driven by the absence of information required to verify that the reported result estimates the effect of randomized assignment, not by an assumption that deviations or incorrect analysis actually occurred.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

* **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, randomized participants?** **No information.** Although 162 people were randomized, 8-iso-PGF2α was analysed only in samples from Finland, Australia, and Sweden. Samples from Denmark and Italy were “inadequate” for isoprostane analysis because of inappropriate sample handling and/or storage. The article does not give the number of participants from each centre, nor the number with baseline and 3-month 8-iso-PGF2α values in each randomized group. There is also no participant-flow diagram or report of withdrawals/loss to follow-up.
* **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?** **No.** No sensitivity analysis, analysis addressing missing outcomes, or other evidence demonstrating robustness to missing data is presented.
* **3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?** **No information.** The stated centre-level sample inadequacy relates to sample handling/storage, but participant-level completeness and the reasons for any unavailable baseline or 3-month values are not reported. Thus, it cannot be established that missingness was wholly unrelated to the underlying outcome.
* **3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?** **No information.** The available report does not supply the necessary participant-level extent, reasons, or between-group distribution of missing data.

**Motivation for judgment**

The trial randomized 162 participants, but the 8-iso-PGF2α outcome was unavailable from two of five centres due to sample-handling/storage problems. The exact participant count affected, its distribution among the four groups, and whether all participants at the remaining centres had 3-month measurements are not reported. Table 3 continues to show the randomized-group sizes in its headings, but it does not state that these are the numbers actually contributing 8-iso-PGF2α data.

The report also gives no withdrawals, no numbers analysed, no reasons for participant-level missingness, and no sensitivity analysis. Consequently, it is impossible to establish that outcome data were available for all or nearly all relevant randomized participants, or that any missing data could not materially alter the intervention comparison. Following RoB 2, absence of information on the extent of missing outcome data generally leads to a **high risk-of-bias** judgment in this domain.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

* **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?** **Probably no.** Plasma 8-iso-PGF2α was measured using a reported validated radioimmunoassay developed by Basu, with stated antibody cross-reactivity and a detection limit of 23 pmol/L.
* **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?** **Probably no.** The outcome was a plasma biomarker measured at the same scheduled time points—baseline and 3 months—in the randomized groups. The report does not describe different measurement methods, different thresholds, or differential outcome-ascertainment opportunities by fish-oil/placebo allocation.
* **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of intervention received?** **No information.** The report does not state whether laboratory personnel conducting the radioimmunoassay were blinded to allocation.
* **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?** **Probably no.** This was an assay-based plasma biomarker with specified laboratory procedures, not a participant-reported outcome or a clinical judgment outcome. The article does not indicate that its measurement required subjective assessor judgment susceptible to knowledge of allocation.
* **4.5** **Not applicable.**

**Motivation for judgment**

The outcome was measured with a validated biochemical assay, and no differential measurement procedure between fish-oil and placebo groups is reported. Although laboratory blinding is not described, the assessed outcome is an assay-derived concentration rather than an outcome requiring subjective clinical or participant judgment. On the evidence available, knowledge of allocation would not be expected to influence the measurement itself. Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

This judgment does not resolve the separate problem that samples from two centres were inadequate; that issue concerns availability of outcome data and is addressed in Domain 3.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

* **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available?** **No information.** No protocol, registry entry, or dated statistical analysis plan is provided. The article describes the statistical model used, but this does not establish that it was pre-specified before access to unblinded outcome data.
* **5.2 Is the result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?** **No information.** The article reports 8-iso-PGF2α at baseline and 3 months. No pre-specified outcome hierarchy or complete analysis plan is available to establish whether other time points, assays, biomarker definitions, or eligible measurements were collected and selectively omitted.
* **5.3 Is the result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?** **No information.** Several analytically plausible approaches existed, including analyses of 3-month values versus changes from baseline, covariate-adjusted versus unadjusted analyses, factorial interaction versus main-effect models, and different approaches to centre-related missing samples. The article reports one adjusted model but no pre-specified plan or comprehensive set of analyses from which to determine whether the reported result was selected based on its findings.

**Motivation for judgment**

The article identifies changes in 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α as “main outcome variables,” but no prospectively finalized analysis plan is available. It therefore cannot be verified that the selected 8-iso-PGF2α analysis, its 3-month time point, its model specification, and the handling of incomplete centre data were pre-specified. The available report does not provide affirmative evidence that the result was selected on the basis of its statistical significance or magnitude; thus high risk is not justified. However, the lack of a protocol/SAP and lack of information concerning eligible measurements and analyses generate **some concerns**.

---

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgment — **High risk of bias**

The overall judgment is **high risk of bias** for the reported fish-oil versus placebo effect on plasma 8-iso-PGF2α at 3 months.

This follows directly from the RoB 2 rules because:

* Domain 2 (**deviations from intended interventions**) is at **high risk of bias**, because the report does not establish that the effect was estimated using an appropriate intention-to-treat–consistent analysis and provides no information allowing the potential impact of exclusions or incorrect analysis grouping to be assessed.
* Domain 3 (**missing outcome data**) is at **high risk of bias**, because 8-iso-PGF2α samples from two centres were unusable, and the article does not report the participant numbers analysed, participant flow, missing-data distribution by randomized group, participant-level reasons for missingness, or robustness analyses.
* Domains 1 and 5 raise **some concerns** because allocation generation/concealment and pre-specified outcome-analysis intentions were not reported.
* Only Domain 4 is at **low risk of bias**.

Accordingly, the reported statistically significant reduction in plasma 8-iso-PGF2α with (n-3) fatty-acid supplementation cannot be considered at low risk of bias on the basis of the supplied article alone.

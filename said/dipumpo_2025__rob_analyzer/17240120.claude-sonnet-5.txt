# ROB 2 Assessment: Escitalopram Relapse Prevention in OCD

**Study design**: Randomized (double-blind) discontinuation/relapse-prevention trial, parallel-group, following an open-label lead-in phase. Assessment applies to the double-blind randomized comparison (escitalopram vs. placebo), primary outcome: time to relapse (ITT population, n=320).

---

## Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:** Sequence generation was via a "computer-generated randomisation list" — an adequate random method. Allocation concealment was achieved through sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes, with randomization numbers unknown to investigators and assigned in blocks of four at each centre — meeting minimal criteria for adequate concealment. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) between the escitalopram and placebo double-blind groups appear well balanced (age, sex, race, age of onset, duration of illness), with the paper explicitly stating "There were no differences in demographics and clinical parameters between the two treatment groups in the randomisation period." No evidence of problematic imbalance is apparent. Therefore, all criteria for low risk are met.

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:** Participants, carers, and outcome assessors were blinded throughout the double-blind phase ("All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study"); tablets were identical in appearance/taste/smell, and a tapering strategy (20mg→10mg→placebo) was implemented specifically to minimize discontinuation-related unblinding/deviations. The authors explicitly investigated whether discontinuation symptoms confounded results by censoring early relapses (7- and 14-day cut-offs) and found the treatment effect remained robust, indicating deviations from intended intervention (if any) did not materially bias the assignment effect. The primary analysis used the ITT population ("all randomised patients who took at least one dose of trial medication and had at least one post-randomisation efficacy assessment"), consistent with an appropriate intention-to-treat approach. A secondary per-protocol analysis was also reported and was consistent with the primary analysis, further supporting robustness. No evidence of unbalanced non-protocol interventions or failure of blinding is reported.

---

## Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:** The ITT population comprised all 320 randomized patients (minus 2 excluded for not meeting response criteria at randomization — a protocol violation issue rather than missing data per se). Overall withdrawal rates (excluding relapses) were similar between arms (7.9% escitalopram vs. 8.9% placebo), suggesting non-differential loss unrelated to the outcome. However, patients who relapsed were withdrawn from the study by design, and LOCF (last observation carried forward) was used for continuous secondary efficacy endpoints (Y-BOCS, NIMH-OCS, CGI) — a method not guaranteed to correct for bias due to missing data, as it assumes no further symptom change after dropout, which may not hold in a discontinuation trial where placebo patients are more likely to deteriorate after leaving the study. The primary outcome (time-to-relapse) was analyzed using survival methods (log-rank/Cox), which handle censoring appropriately if censoring is non-informative; however, the paper does not explicitly demonstrate that censoring (e.g., due to withdrawal for AEs) was unrelated to the outcome. Given the reliance on LOCF for secondary continuous outcomes and incomplete detail on informativeness of censoring for the time-to-event primary analysis, there remains some uncertainty, though the risk appears modest given balanced withdrawal rates. This is reported as a limitation/ambiguity rather than a definitive high-risk judgment.

---

## Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:** The primary outcome (Y-BOCS-based relapse) is an observer-reported outcome involving some clinical judgement, assessed by raters who underwent standardized "rater training sessions" to increase inter-rater reliability, chaired by an experienced psychiatrist, and patient ratings were performed by the same investigator at each visit whenever possible — enhancing consistency across arms. Outcome assessors (investigators) were blinded to treatment assignment throughout the double-blind period, as were participants, with identical-appearing tablets. The measurement method (Y-BOCS) is a validated, standard, appropriate instrument for OCD symptom severity and is not inappropriate for the outcome. Measurement schedules were identical between the two study arms (assessed at the same time points regardless of assignment), so ascertainment did not differ by group. Given effective blinding was maintained and confirmed by symmetric assessment protocols, and given the outcome, while involving some judgement, is unlikely to have been heavily influenced given blinding integrity was reinforced by the tapering strategy to prevent unblinding via discontinuation symptoms, risk of bias is judged low.

---

## Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:** The primary outcome (time to relapse) and its analysis method (two-tailed log-rank test) were specified prospectively ("The prospectively defined primary analysis of efficacy was the time to relapse"), and this matches sample size/power calculations described in the Methods, supporting low risk for the primary result specifically. However, the paper explicitly reports a trial registry/protocol was not directly referenced or provided in the article, and no explicit statement is made confirming that the full analysis plan was finalized and date-stamped before unblinding of outcome data was made available to investigators — this document itself is not accessible to verify. Additionally, several post-hoc analyses are described within the paper itself (e.g., "post-hoc analyses based only on the Y-BOCS relapse criterion," subgroup analyses by age/gender/baseline severity/dose), which, while reported transparently and consistent with the main findings, raise the general possibility that other unreported analyses might have been conducted and not published if unfavourable. Without access to a pre-registered protocol or statistical analysis plan for independent verification, there is insufficient information to fully rule out selective reporting, though no direct evidence of it was found in the article. This ambiguity is reported explicitly as a limitation of this assessment, since I could not verify the trial registration record independently.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgment: Some concerns**

**Rationale:** Domains 1, 2, and 4 were judged as low risk of bias. Domains 3 (missing outcome data) and 5 (selection of the reported result) were judged as "some concerns" due to (a) reliance on LOCF imputation for secondary continuous outcomes and incomplete information on informativeness of censoring in the primary time-to-event analysis, and (b) inability to verify, from the article alone, that the full statistical analysis plan was pre-specified and finalized prior to unblinding, combined with the presence of multiple post-hoc/subgroup analyses reported alongside the primary result. Per RoB 2 rules, since no domain was rated "high risk" but at least one domain (and in this case two) raised "some concerns" without substantially undermining confidence to the point of high risk, the overall judgement is **Some concerns**.

**Stated limitation/ambiguity:** This assessment was conducted solely on the basis of the published journal article; no trial protocol, registry entry, or statistical analysis plan was available for cross-checking, which limits certainty regarding Domain 5 in particular, and partly Domain 3.

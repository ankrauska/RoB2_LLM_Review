Based on a thorough and detailed Risk of Bias (ROB) assessment of the provided article using the RoB 2 tool, here is the structured report evaluating each of the 5 domains and the final overall judgment:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Participants were randomly assigned to either DCS or placebo by a data manager who had no interaction with the participants, and identical-appearing capsules were used, ensuring allocation concealment. Although the specific method of random sequence generation is not explicitly detailed, the involvement of an independent data manager implies a random component. Baseline characteristics were largely balanced; the reported difference in age was investigated by the authors and found not to explain the group effects, indicating no severe baseline imbalances that would suggest a compromised randomization process.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The study utilized identical-appearing capsules for both the DCS and placebo groups. Both the participants and the experimenters were effectively blinded, which was confirmed by an assessment showing neither could discern the treatment assignment above chance. Because the intervention was a single-dose administration under observation before the exposure task, deviations from the intended intervention were highly unlikely. Although 4 participants dropped out and were excluded from the analysis (analyzing only those completing Visit 2), this relates to missing data rather than post-randomization deviations from the assigned intervention regimen.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Out of the 34 randomized participants, 4 (2 in the DCS group and 2 in the placebo group) did not return for the second visit and were not analyzed. The amount of missing data is relatively small (~11%), and crucially, the dropouts are perfectly balanced between the two intervention groups. The reasons for missingness (unreachable, preference not to continue) do not show a differential pattern between groups that would suggest the missingness heavily depended on the true value of the outcome in a biased direction.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The primary outcomes were subjective anxiety measured by the Subjective Units of Distress Scale (SUDS) and the Social Phobia Scale. While these are participant-reported outcomes (meaning the participant is the outcome assessor and involves subjective judgment), the participants were effectively blinded to the intervention received. Therefore, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. The measurement instruments themselves are standard and appropriate.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** There is no mention in the article of a pre-specified, publicly available, and date-stamped trial registry record or statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available. While the outcomes reported (SUDS and Social Phobia Scale) align with the stated objectives of the study, the absence of an a priori protocol makes it impossible to definitively rule out that the specific analytical methods (e.g., using a 5-point drop for categorical response, or the specific covariates used) or the reported time points were not selected from among multiple analyses based on the statistical significance of the results.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Overall Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "Some concerns" in Domain 5 (Bias in selection of the reported result) due to the lack of evidence of a pre-specified and registered statistical analysis plan. All other domains (randomization, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, and measurement of the outcome) are judged to be at a low risk of bias. According to the specified rules, a study that raises some concerns in at least one domain, but is not at high risk in any domain, receives an overall judgment of "Some concerns."

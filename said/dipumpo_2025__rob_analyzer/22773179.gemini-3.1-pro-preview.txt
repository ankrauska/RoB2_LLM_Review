Based on the thorough evaluation of the provided article using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool, here is the detailed risk-of-bias assessment:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study describes a paired-eye randomized design where one eye received the ASA profile and the other received the TSA profile. Sequence generation was adequately performed using "Microsoft Excel’s random number function" (Question 1.1: Yes). However, the report lacks information on whether the allocation sequence was concealed from the investigators before enrolling and assigning the eyes (Question 1.2: No information). In paired-eye trials, once the first eye's treatment is revealed, the second eye's treatment is known, making allocation concealment for the first eye critical. Baseline characteristics (preoperative spherical equivalent) between the two eyes were comparable and no significant imbalances suggesting a problem with the randomization process were found (Question 1.3: No). Due to the lack of information on allocation concealment, this domain raises some concerns.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The interventions were performed using an automated excimer laser (MEL80) programmed for either an ASA or TSA profile. While the surgeons were inherently aware of the assigned intervention (Question 2.2: Yes), there is no evidence that this knowledge led to deviations from the intended interventions that would be inconsistent with the trial protocol (Question 2.3: No). The laser ablation is a standard programmed procedure, making context-driven deviations unlikely to affect the outcome. Furthermore, all 30 eyes completed the intervention and were analyzed appropriately in their respective groups (Question 2.6: Yes).

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The study enrolled 15 patients (30 eyes) and reports 3-month postoperative refractive and wavefront outcomes for all participants. There is no mention of dropouts, loss to follow-up, or excluded eyes in the results ("100% of eyes were 20/25 or better"). Therefore, outcome data were available for all randomized participants (Question 3.1: Yes).

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The outcome of interest (spherical aberration) was measured using an objective, automated device (the wavefront supported custom ablation aberrometer) (Question 4.1: No). The method of ascertainment was identical for both intervention groups (Question 4.2: No). Because the outcome is observer-independent and relies on an automated diagnostic test, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, regardless of whether the personnel performing the test were blinded (Question 4.4: No).

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** There is no mention of a pre-specified, publicly available study protocol or statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available (Question 5.1: No information). While the authors comprehensively reported the spherical aberration outcomes across various pupil diameters and calculated the area under the curve (RAWS parameter) as intended, the absence of a registered protocol makes it impossible to definitively rule out whether the reported outcomes or analysis methods were selected from multiple eligible options based on the results (Question 5.2 and 5.3: No information). This leads to a judgment of some concerns.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "some concerns" in at least one domain (Domain 1: Randomization process due to unclear allocation concealment; Domain 5: Selection of the reported result due to the lack of a pre-specified protocol). However, it is not judged to be at high risk of bias in any single domain. Therefore, following the RoB 2 rules, the overall risk-of-bias judgment is "Some concerns."

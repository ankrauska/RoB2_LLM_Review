## RoB 2 assessment

**Result assessed:** 12-month bracket failure/survival comparing **Transbond™ Plus Self-Etching Primer (SEP)** (experimental intervention) with the **conventional 37% phosphoric-acid etch plus Transbond™ MIP primer** (comparator). The principal numerical result is the Cox-model estimate: conventional etch/primer versus SEP, **hazard ratio 0.4 (95% CI 0.2 to 0.8)**, with reported bracket-failure rates of **3.9%** and **11.2%**, respectively.

**Design:** randomized split-mouth cross-quadrant clinical trial in 39 patients (661 brackets). The assessment applies the five RoB 2 domains supplied. The report does not provide a protocol, statistical analysis plan, trial registry entry, or other pre-results source.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Probably yes.**  
  The article states that “**randomization for allocation … was undertaken using a block randomization method**.” It further describes that each block of 10 consecutive participants contained five participants with SEP allocated to the upper right quadrant and five with SEP allocated to the upper left quadrant. Although the precise method used to randomize the order within blocks is not reported, the study explicitly describes the method as block randomization and cites a source on randomization methods.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — Probably yes.**  
  The report states that “**the operators were unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial**.” This supports concealment from the treating operators until enrolment. The article does not describe the physical or administrative mechanism used to conceal the allocation sequence; therefore, “probably yes,” rather than unequivocal “yes,” is appropriate.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  This was a split-mouth cross-quadrant design: both interventions were used within each participant. Therefore, participant-level characteristics, including age, sex, and operator, were inherently shared between the compared intervention conditions. The bracket distributions shown in the tooth-location table are also essentially balanced between systems: 331 brackets in the SEP condition and 330 in the conventional condition, inferred from the reported denominators. No imbalance is reported that indicates manipulation or failure of randomization.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The study reports block randomization, operator concealment until participant entry, and no evidence of a baseline imbalance indicating a flawed allocation process. The exact sequence-generation and allocation-concealment procedures are incompletely described, but the reported information supports a low-risk judgement rather than raising material concern.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Effect of interest:** effect of assignment of brackets/teeth to SEP versus conventional etch-and-primer treatment.

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The article states that patients “**were unaware which system had been used on each side of the mouth**.” However, because the two treatments had different clinical application procedures, this statement establishes intended patient blinding but does not establish that blinding was necessarily maintained. The report nevertheless directly states that patients were unaware, so the more relevant concern in this domain is the operator awareness.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The report explicitly states that “**it was not possible to blind the operators to the type of system being used**,” because the systems had different modes of application.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — No information.**  
  The report gives standardized bonding procedures for both systems and records the number of bracket manipulations before curing. However, it does not report whether there were deviations from the assigned bonding procedures, non-protocol co-interventions, differential clinical management after bonding, or other deviations arising because operators knew which system was being used.

- **2.4–2.5 Were deviations likely to affect the outcome and balanced between groups? — Not assessable.**  
  These questions cannot be resolved because the report provides no information on whether relevant trial-context deviations occurred.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Probably yes.**  
  The trial reports the outcomes for all 661 bonded brackets according to the etch/primer system used. It used logistic regression and Cox proportional-hazards regression, both “**adjusted for the clustering within mouths**.” One participant moved after seven months, but the participant’s observed data were retained in the survival analysis. There is no reported post-randomization exclusion, as-treated analysis, or reassignment of brackets between intervention groups.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The operators were necessarily unblinded. Lack of operator blinding does not itself establish bias, but it creates the possibility that protocol implementation or co-interventions could have differed by bonding system. The article does not report whether such deviations occurred. The analysis appears to retain brackets in their allocated treatment groups and accounts for within-mouth clustering, which reduces concern about inappropriate analysis. Nevertheless, the absence of information on whether unblinded operators introduced differential deviations from the intended procedures leads to **some concerns**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Probably yes.**  
  The report states that “**all patients were observed for the entire 12-month period apart from one 13-year-old male who moved to another city after 7 months of observation**.” Thus, 38 of 39 participants completed the full 12-month observation period. The patient who moved had no bracket failures before leaving, and the observed follow-up was included in the survival analysis.

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — Not applicable.**  
  Because outcome data were available for nearly all participants and brackets, no further demonstration is required under the RoB 2 algorithm.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Only one of 39 participants had incomplete follow-up, and that participant contributed seven months of observed survival time without a bracket failure. The report does not state the number of brackets contributed by this participant, so the exact number of brackets with incomplete 12-month observation is not available. However, the amount of incomplete follow-up is small, the participant’s available data were retained, and there is no evidence of differential loss to follow-up by intervention condition: each participant received both systems under the split-mouth design. This supports a **low-risk** judgement.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  The outcome was bracket bond failure. For each failure, the investigators recorded the tooth, the date of failure, and the adhesive remnant index. A bracket failure is a directly observable clinical event, and no evidence is reported that the outcome definition or measurement method was unsuitable.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  Both etching/priming systems were applied in the same participants, and all patients were monitored during the same orthodontic-treatment period. The report does not identify separate follow-up schedules, thresholds, examination methods, or ascertainment processes for SEP versus conventional-system brackets.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — No information.**  
  The article states that operators could not be blinded during bonding, but it does not identify who assessed and recorded subsequent bracket failures or whether the assessor was blinded to the etch/primer system.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  The primary outcome is whether a bracket detached or failed. This is an objective physical event with little scope for assessor judgement. Although exact timing of a failure may depend on when it is detected, the event itself is not a subjective patient-reported or judgement-based outcome.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The outcome was measured using an appropriate and comparable clinical method across the two within-patient treatment conditions. The identity and blinding status of the person recording failures are not reported. However, bracket detachment is an objectively observable event, and the report provides no evidence that its ascertainment differed between the two systems. Accordingly, lack of explicit assessor-blinding information does not create a material risk of measurement bias for this result.

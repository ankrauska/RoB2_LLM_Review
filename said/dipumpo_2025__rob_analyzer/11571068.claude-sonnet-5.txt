# ROB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Halford et al. – "Helping the female partners of men abusing alcohol: a comparison of three treatments"
**Design:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial (3 arms: supportive counselling, stress management, alcohol-focused couple therapy [AFCT])
**Effect of interest assessed:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect), as the analyses reported are based on completers/available cases rather than a defined per-protocol adherence strategy.
**Outcomes assessed (illustrative, primary distress/relationship/drinking outcomes):** RSS (women's stress/burden), GHQ (psychological distress), DAS (relationship satisfaction), male drinking (mean daily drinks, KAT, % days intoxicated), CTS (partner violence).

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q1.1 (random sequence): The paper states participants were "assigned randomly via random number tables" — this is an adequate description of a truly random sequence generation method. → **Yes**
- Signalling Q1.2 (allocation concealment): No information is provided on how the sequence was concealed from the researchers/therapists enrolling participants (e.g., no mention of sequentially numbered, sealed envelopes, or centralized allocation). The paper simply states random number tables were used without describing the mechanism preventing foreknowledge of assignment. → **No information**
- Signalling Q1.3 (baseline imbalance): One-way ANOVAs showed no significant differences between conditions on demographic variables (age, years married, number of children) or on baseline GHQ, RSS, DAS, MSI, CTS, or drinking measures. No evidence of imbalance suggestive of a randomization failure. → **No**

Because allocation concealment is not reported (no information) and there is no baseline imbalance suggestive of a problem, per the RoB 2 algorithm (no information on concealment + no baseline imbalance problem) → **Some concerns**.

**Limitation/ambiguity to report:** The article gives no description of the mechanism used to conceal the allocation sequence from personnel enrolling participants. This ambiguity prevents a "Low risk" judgment.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgment: High risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q2.1/2.2 (awareness of assignment): Given the nature of the interventions (supportive counselling vs. stress management vs. couple therapy, involving distinct manual-based procedures and, in AFCT, attempts to engage the male partner), it was not possible to blind participants, therapists, or those delivering the interventions. → **Yes** (aware)
- Signalling Q2.3 (deviations arising from trial context): There is clear evidence of a major deviation from intended intervention in the AFCT arm: only 6 of 21 women assigned to AFCT had partners who engaged in couple therapy; for the remaining 15, women received the stress management treatment instead of the assigned AFCT protocol. This is an explicit, protocol-driven substitution rather than a chance event, and it is clearly related to the intervention/trial context (male partner's willingness to attend, itself possibly influenced by the treatment condition and non-blinded delivery). → **Yes**
- Signalling Q2.4 (likely to affect outcome): Given that the primary distinguishing component of AFCT (male engagement) affects drinking and relationship outcomes, this deviation plausibly affects the outcomes assessed (relationship satisfaction, male drinking). → **Yes**
- Signalling Q2.5 (balanced between groups): This deviation is specific to the AFCT arm (not comparably present in the other two arms) and therefore unbalanced. → **No**
- Signalling Q2.6 (appropriate analysis – ITT): The analyses appear to be based on those who completed treatment and had available data (47/61 completed treatment; 44/61 had 6-month data), not on a full ITT analysis of all randomized participants analyzed as assigned. The AFCT arm's substantial crossover to the stress-management protocol was not accounted for via an appropriate ITT-adjusted or instrumental variable analysis. Participants who received the "wrong" (substituted) intervention were analyzed nominally within the assigned label, but this may dilute/distort the true assignment effect and is not addressed statistically. → **No/Probably no**
- Signalling Q2.7 (potential for substantial impact): With 15/21 (71%) of the AFCT arm receiving substituted treatment rather than the intended intervention, and no correction in analysis, the potential impact on the result is substantial. → **Yes**

Given: participants/personnel aware + unbalanced deviations arising from trial context, likely affecting outcome + inappropriate/no correction analysis with substantial potential impact → **High risk of bias**.

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q3.1: Outcome data were not available for all or nearly all randomized participants. 61 were randomized; only 47 completed treatment (77%) and only 44 (72%) provided 6-month follow-up data — an overall attrition of 22%, which cannot be considered "nearly all." → **No**
- Signalling Q3.2 (evidence result not biased): No sensitivity analyses addressing plausible values for missing data were reported by the authors (although a dropout-by-condition chi-square was performed, showing no significant differential dropout by condition — but this does not equate to a sensitivity analysis regarding the relationship between missingness and the true outcome value). → **No**
- Signalling Q3.3 (could missingness depend on true value?): The authors explicitly report that most dropouts left because they had left their partners/relationship, often citing that treatment helped them decide to leave – i.e., dropout is plausibly related to the true underlying construct (relationship distress, motivation, or improvement) being measured. This is a face-valid mechanism connecting missingness to outcome. → **Yes**
- Signalling Q3.4 (likely dependent on true value): The chi-square analysis found no statistically significant differential dropout by treatment condition, and no significant difference in reasons distinguishing arms was noted; however, the authors themselves caution that "the small number of dropouts overall give this analysis low power to detect differential attrition." There is no strong affirmative evidence that missingness related differentially by arm-specific true outcome status, but plausibility remains and cannot be excluded due to the underpowered dropout analysis. → **Probably no/No information** (concern present but not definitively "likely")

This pattern (missingness could depend on true value, but it is not established to be likely, particularly not differentially by arm) maps to **Some concerns**.

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: High risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q4.1 (appropriateness of measurement method): The measures used (RSS, GHQ, DAS, KAT, CTS) are validated, appropriate instruments for the outcomes of interest. → **No** (not inappropriate)
- Signalling Q4.2 (differential ascertainment between groups): Measurement methods were the same standardized instruments applied identically across arms; no evidence that measurement/ascertainment methods differed structurally between groups. → **No**
- Signalling Q4.3 (outcome assessors aware of intervention assignment): Nearly all outcome measures in this trial are **participant self-report** measures (RSS, GHQ, DAS, KAT, CTS) or **spouse-report of partner's drinking**. The outcome assessor here is the woman herself (or her report about her husband). Given the woman was clearly aware of her own treatment assignment (unblinded, distinct manual-based interventions), the "outcome assessor" (participant) was aware of intervention status. → **Yes**
- Signalling Q4.4 (could assessment be influenced by knowledge of assignment): For participant-reported outcomes, the assessment is potentially influenced by knowledge of intervention received (per RoB2 guidance, this defaults to at least "Some concerns"). → **Yes**
- Signalling Q4.5 (likely influenced): Given that the study directly compared "active" structured behavioral treatments (stress management, AFCT) against a supportive counselling condition explicitly designed as a "plausible contact control," and given strong demand characteristics/expectancy differences plausible between a specific skills-based therapy versus generic supportive counselling, together with women's own self-report of burden, distress, and relationship satisfaction (all subject to expectancy and social desirability effects), it is likely that self-report assessment of subjective outcomes (RSS, GHQ, DAS) was influenced by knowledge of the assigned condition, particularly given no blinding of participants was possible or attempted. Additionally, spouse-report of partner's drinking is not an objective/independently verified assessment and is similarly susceptible to expectancy effects related to treatment condition. → **Yes**

Because it is judged likely that assessment of the (predominantly self-reported/subjective) outcomes was influenced by knowledge of intervention received → **High risk of bias**.

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q5.1 (pre-specified analysis plan available before unblinded data): No trial registration, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or available in the article. There is no way to verify whether the reported outcomes/analyses were pre-specified before outcome data were examined. → **No information**
- Signalling Q5.2 (selection from multiple eligible outcome measurements): Multiple outcome measures were used per domain (e.g., drinking assessed via daily monitoring diaries, KAT, and % days intoxicated) and results were somewhat inconsistent across these overlapping measures (e.g., significant reduction in reported days intoxicated and mean daily drinks, but non-significant on KAT). All results appear to have been reported (not selectively suppressed), which reduces (but does not eliminate, given absence of a protocol) concern here. → **Probably no**, but with residual uncertainty due to lack of protocol.
- Signalling Q5.3 (selection from multiple eligible analyses): Various ANOVA/ANCOVA models were run (e.g., an additional ANCOVA for days intoxicated was performed only after the initial ANOVA yielded a significant main effect, adjusting for baseline as covariate). While this appears to be a reasonable follow-up analysis, the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan means it cannot be verified whether this and other analytic choices (e.g., separate ANOVAs per outcome rather than a global model, choice of clinical-significance thresholds) were decided a priori or were influenced by the pattern of results. → **No information/Probably no**

Given the absence of a registered protocol/SAP (no information on Q5.1) combined with plausible but unconfirmed multiplicity in outcome measures and analyses (no strong evidence of selection, but cannot be excluded) → **Some concerns**.

**Limitation to report:** No trial registry, protocol or statistical analysis plan was available for cross-checking; judgments here rely solely on internal consistency of the published report.

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall Judgment: HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Rationale:**
Per the RoB 2 decision rules, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted if the study is judged high risk in **at least one domain**, or has "some concerns" in multiple domains such that confidence in the result is substantially undermined.

In this study:
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions) was judged **High risk**, primarily due to the substantial, unbalanced, unaddressed crossover/substitution in the AFCT arm (only 6/21 men engaged, with the remainder receiving a different treatment than assigned, with no appropriate ITT-consistent statistical correction).
- Domain 4 (Measurement of outcome) was also judged **High risk**, due to reliance on unblinded, subjective self-report outcomes highly susceptible to expectancy/demand effects in a design with no blinding possible.
- Additionally, "Some concerns" were present in Domains 1, 3, and 5.

Given two independent domains at High risk of bias plus additional concerns in three further domains, the overall result is judged to be at **High risk of bias**. This is consistent with the authors' own acknowledgment of significant limitations (low statistical power, substantial non-engagement of male partners in AFCT, high attrition with plausible informative dropout, and reliance on self-report/spouse-report measures).

**Key ambiguities/limitations explicitly noted (as no further clarification could be sought):**
1. No description of allocation concealment procedures.
2. No trial registration or protocol/SAP was available to verify pre-specification of outcomes and analyses.
3. Substantial (71%) non-adherence to assigned AFCT condition was not addressed through an appropriate causal/ITT-consistent analytic method.
4. All primary outcomes were based on non-blinded self-report or spouse-report, with no independent/objective corroboration (e.g., biological verification of alcohol consumption).

# ROB 2 Assessment: MADIT-CRT Trial (Moss et al., NEJM)

**Outcome assessed:** Death from any cause or nonfatal heart-failure event, whichever came first (primary composite end point); HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.52–0.84), P=0.001
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat)

---

## Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

- **Sequence generation (1.1):** The report states patients were "randomly assigned in a 3:2 ratio" using "an algorithm that ensured near balance in each stratum" (stratified by center and ischemic status). No further detail on the randomization sequence-generation method (e.g., computer-generated numbers) is provided in the article or protocol excerpt → answer: **Probably yes** (given publication in a major journal with word-count constraints and a well-resourced academic coordinating center, a random component is plausible, but explicit method is not described).
- **Allocation concealment (1.2):** No explicit description of the mechanism used to conceal allocation from enrolling investigators is provided (e.g., central randomization system, sealed envelopes) → **No information**.
- **Baseline imbalance (1.3):** Table 1 shows baseline characteristics that are well balanced between groups (age, sex, ejection fraction, QRS duration, medications, comorbidities all closely similar) with no evidence of imbalance suggestive of a flawed randomization process → **No** (compatible with chance).

**Judgment rationale:** Baseline characteristics appear balanced, consistent with adequate randomization; however, the absence of explicit reporting on allocation-concealment mechanisms precludes a "Low risk" judgment. Per the RoB 2 algorithm, "No information" on concealment combined with no evidence of imbalance defaults to **Some concerns**.

**Limitation/ambiguity noted:** The trial report and available protocol do not describe the specific method of allocation concealment (e.g., whether a centralized/third-party system was used). This is reported as an assessed ambiguity, not assumed.

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention) — **High risk**

- **2.1 Participants aware of assigned intervention?** Yes. The trial had **no masking** ("Masking: NONE" per protocol registry), and CRT device implantation is a distinguishable invasive procedure (device is either single- or dual-chamber with a coronary sinus lead) — participants were necessarily aware.
- **2.2 Personnel/carers aware?** Yes — explicitly stated: "The treating physicians were aware of study-group assignments."
- **2.3 Deviations arising from trial context?** Yes. The article explicitly reports 173 crossovers (91 ICD-only patients received CRT-ICD, 82 CRT-ICD patients received ICD-only) and states some crossovers occurred "after a heart-failure event" or "at the physician's discretion." Critically, the diagnosis of the primary heart-failure component of the composite endpoint was made "by physicians who were aware of study-group assignments." The authors themselves acknowledge in the Discussion: "It is possible that the investigators' knowledge of study-group assignment contributed in some way to the lower frequency of heart failure in the CRT–ICD group."
- **2.4 Likely to have affected the outcome?** Yes — this directly concerns the primary outcome (heart-failure diagnosis/hospitalization decisions), a component highly susceptible to clinical judgment and treatment expectation.
- **2.5 Balanced between groups?** No — crossover numbers differed between groups (91 vs. 82, in different directions and for different reasons: many ICD-only crossovers occurred specifically *after* a heart-failure event, which could differentially affect subsequent event counting/care).
- **2.6 Appropriate analysis?** Yes — analysis was intention-to-treat, as explicitly stated ("Data analysis was performed according to the intention-to-treat principle").

**Judgment rationale:** Although the statistical analysis was appropriately ITT (Part 2 = Low), Part 1 is High risk because (a) participants and treating/diagnosing physicians were unblinded, (b) deviations (differential crossover, and non-blinded heart-failure diagnosis by treating physicians) demonstrably arose from trial context, (c) these deviations plausibly affected the primary (subjective, physician-driven) heart-failure component of the outcome, and (d) they were not balanced between groups. Per RoB 2 algorithm, High risk in Part 1 drives the domain to **High risk** overall, irrespective of appropriate ITT analysis.

---

## Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data — **Low risk**

- **3.1 Outcome data available for all/nearly all randomized participants?** Yes. All 1820 randomized patients were followed and included in the primary analysis (device implantation rates 98.4–99%; loss to follow-up/withdrawal was small: 44/1089 [4.0%] in CRT-ICD and 55/731 [7.5%] in ICD-only, and the composite endpoint incorporates death, so even those lost would generally have contributed some follow-up time before censoring). The CONSORT-style flow data (participant flow module) confirm 1089 and 731 "completed" the overall study with 0 formally listed as "not completed."
- Time-to-event analysis (Cox regression, log-rank test) was used, appropriate for handling censoring, and the group-sequential design’s prespecified event-driven monitoring supports non-informative censoring in this context.

**Judgment rationale:** The proportion of missing/incomplete data is small and does not appear differentially related to the true outcome value in a way likely to bias the composite death/HF endpoint. → **Low risk**.

---

## Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome — **High risk**

- **4.1 Method of measuring outcome inappropriate?** No — clinical/adjudicated diagnosis of HF and death are appropriate outcome measures for this composite.
- **4.2 Could measurement/ascertainment differ between groups?** Potentially — CRT-ICD patients underwent more frequent device-related follow-up and testing due to the additional device programming/optimization visits, which could increase detection opportunities for HF symptoms; however, this is not strongly emphasized in the article.
- **4.3 Outcome assessors aware of intervention?** Yes, explicitly: "The diagnosis of heart failure...was made by physicians who were aware of study-group assignments," although final adjudication was performed by a committee unaware of assignments.
- **4.4 Could assessment be influenced by knowledge of assignment?** Yes — heart failure is an "observer-reported outcome involving judgment" (signs/symptoms, response to therapy), a domain highly susceptible to influence by unblinded assessors, especially the decision to hospitalize or intensify diuretic therapy, which are clinician-driven judgment calls.
- **4.5 Likely that assessment was influenced?** Yes — the authors themselves state this is a plausible source of bias in the Discussion, and the heart-failure component (driving 41% of the treatment effect) was diagnosed by unblinded physicians, even though later "adjudicated" by a blinded committee. Adjudication of events reported by unblinded clinicians does not fully correct for bias introduced at the point of initial diagnosis/treatment-decision (e.g., decision to treat with IV diuretics, which itself defines an "event").

**Judgment rationale:** Given that (a) the primary outcome is a composite substantially driven by the subjective/judgment-based heart-failure component, (b) initial diagnosis/care decisions were made by unblinded treating physicians, and (c) the authors themselves flag this as a likely source of bias potentially favoring CRT-ICD, this domain is judged **High risk**, despite blinded final-event adjudication (adjudication addresses classification of events already flagged by unblinded caregivers, not the initial detection/decision to treat).

---

## Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

- **5.1 Pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded data available?** The trial had a detailed prespecified statistical analysis plan (group-sequential Wang–Tsiatis design, prespecified primary/secondary/tertiary analyses, prespecified subgroups) referenced to a previously published design paper (Moss et al. 2005) → **Probably yes** for the primary composite endpoint itself.
- **5.2 Selection from multiple eligible outcome measurements?** No clear evidence of this for the primary endpoint, which was consistently defined.
- **5.3 Selection from multiple eligible analyses?** Some concern: multiple subgroup analyses were conducted (10 prespecified categorical subgroups), two significant interactions (sex, QRS ≥150ms) are highlighted, and "exploratory" (i.e., non-prespecified, post hoc) subgroup analyses are also presented (e.g., women without QRS effect, NYHA III/IV history, atrial fibrillation history) without clear a priori specification, and are discussed prominently in the results, raising some possibility of selective emphasis on favorable findings post hoc. Additionally, the tertiary echocardiographic analyses were explicitly stated to be conducted and reported after unblinding ("Of the tertiary analyses, echocardiographic changes at 1 year are reported here"), and the echocardiographic subset (746/1089 paired studies) was "not randomly selected," as acknowledged by the authors themselves.

**Judgment rationale:** For the primary composite endpoint, no clear evidence of selective reporting was found. However, the presence of exploratory (non-prespecified) subgroup analyses and post hoc echocardiographic analyses conducted after unblinding introduces some concern regarding selection of favorable results, even though this does not appear to affect the primary endpoint result itself. → **Some concerns**.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement: **HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Rationale:** Per RoB 2 rules, an overall judgement of High risk of bias is warranted if the study is at High risk of bias in at least one domain. In this trial:
- Domain 2 (deviations from intended interventions) is **High risk**, driven by unblinded treating physicians making initial heart-failure diagnoses/treatment decisions (differentially non-blinded, with corroborating differential crossover rates), a concern explicitly acknowledged by the trial authors themselves.
- Domain 4 (measurement of outcome) is also **High risk**, for closely related reasons — the subjective, judgment-dependent heart-failure component of the composite primary outcome was ascertained by unblinded clinicians, even though later adjudicated by a blinded committee.
- Additional "Some concerns" ratings in Domains 1 and 5 further reduce confidence in the primary result.

Given High risk of bias in two related domains (2 and 4), both concerning the same underlying mechanism (lack of blinding of the treating/diagnosing physicians, a study design feature that the authors themselves flag as a limitation), the overall risk of bias for this outcome (composite death/heart-failure event) is judged to be **High**.

**Key limitation/ambiguity to report:** The article does not provide sufficient detail on allocation-concealment methods, precluding a "Low risk" judgment for Domain 1. This ambiguity is reported here as a limitation of the assessment rather than assumed in either direction.

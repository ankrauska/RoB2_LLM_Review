## Scope of this RoB 2 assessment

- **Study design:** Individually randomized, open-label, parallel-group trial.
- **Comparison:** Vaqta™ booster versus Avaxim™ booster in adults previously primed with Avaxim™.
- **Result assessed:** The primary immunogenicity result at approximately 1 month after booster vaccination: post-booster anti-HAV geometric mean titre (GMT), reported in the per-protocol population (Vaqta: 7262 mIU/mL; Avaxim: 5131 mIU/mL; upper one-sided 97.5% CI for GMT ratio Avaxim/Vaqta: 0.72).
- **Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect). This is the appropriate effect for the randomized comparison of booster strategies.

The assessment is based on the article and tables supplied. No protocol, statistical analysis plan, trial registry record, or additional trial report was available.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and support**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — No information.**  
  The report states that subjects “were randomised to receive either Vaqta™ or Avaxim™,” but does not describe how the random sequence was generated. There is no report of a computer-generated sequence, random-number table, blocked randomization, or another method containing a random component.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The article does not describe allocation concealment. There is no information on central randomization, pharmacy-controlled allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or another method preventing recruiters from knowing forthcoming assignments.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  The reported baseline characteristics were similar between groups: mean age was 38.4 versus 38.2 years; 55% versus 52% were male; mean weight was 75.8 versus 74.7 kg; and mean height was identical (172.9 cm). Group sizes were also close to the intended apparent 1:1 allocation (64 versus 63). Nothing in Table 1 indicates an imbalance incompatible with chance.

**Motivation for judgement**

The absence of baseline imbalance does not establish that allocation was adequately concealed. Because neither random-sequence generation nor allocation concealment is reported, the randomization process cannot be verified. Under RoB 2, when allocation concealment is not reported but baseline characteristics do not suggest a problem, the appropriate judgement is **some concerns**, rather than low risk or high risk.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and support**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The study is explicitly described as “open.” Participants therefore were aware of whether they received Vaqta™ or Avaxim™.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Since this was an open study and no masking procedures are described, personnel administering the booster vaccines were aware of the assigned intervention.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report identifies exclusions from the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis: one participant had previously received a hepatitis A booster, one travelled to India between visits, and four lacked both pre- and post-vaccination serological results. These reported circumstances do not establish deviations arising from the open-label trial context, such as differential co-interventions or differential implementation caused by knowledge of treatment assignment.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — No.**  
  The primary immunogenicity results presented in Table 2 are explicitly based on the **per-protocol** population: 59 participants in the Vaqta group and 62 in the Avaxim group. The authors state: “Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed,” but that “only the per-protocol results” were reported.  
  For the effect of assignment to intervention, a per-protocol analysis that excludes randomized participants after randomization is not the appropriate principal analysis. An intention-to-treat analysis should retain participants in their randomized groups.

- **2.7 Was there potential for a substantial impact of the failure to analyse participants in the groups to which they were randomized? — Probably no.**  
  Six of 127 randomized participants were excluded from the reported per-protocol immunogenicity analysis (4.7%). Four lacked paired serological results; one had received an additional hepatitis A booster; and one travelled to India between visits. The authors state that the intention-to-treat and per-protocol results were “close enough,” although the numerical intention-to-treat result is not reported. The excluded proportion is limited, and the reported upper confidence limit (0.72) was substantially below the prespecified non-inferiority limit of 4. However, the absence of the numerical ITT analysis remains a limitation.

**Motivation for judgement**

The study was open-label, but the article does not document trial-context deviations that were likely to affect antibody titres. The principal concern is analytical: the reported primary immunogenicity result is per-protocol rather than intention-to-treat, despite the effect of interest being the effect of random assignment. The limited number of exclusions and the authors’ statement that ITT and per-protocol analyses were close do not support a conclusion of high risk; however, they do not remove the concern created by reporting only the per-protocol result. Therefore, this domain is judged as **some concerns**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and support**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Probably yes.**  
  Of 127 randomized participants, four did not have both pre- and post-vaccination serological results available. Thus, paired serological data were available for at least 123/127 randomized participants (96.9%). The article reports that the remaining two per-protocol exclusions were due to an additional hepatitis A booster and travel to India, rather than absence of serology.

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — Not applicable.**  
  The amount of missing paired serological outcome data was sufficiently limited for the continuous GMT outcome to be considered available for nearly all randomized participants.

**Motivation for judgement**

Only four randomized participants lacked paired serological measurements. For a continuous outcome, availability of outcome data for approximately 95% or more of participants will often be sufficient for a low-risk judgement under RoB 2. The report does not provide reasons for the missing serological data, but the number missing is small (3.1% of randomized participants), and there is no reported differential missingness between intervention groups. On the available evidence, these missing data could not reasonably be expected to make an important difference to the reported GMT comparison. This domain is therefore judged **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and support**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  Anti-HAV antibody concentration was measured with a quantitative modified HAVAB™ assay, with results expressed in mIU/mL. The report describes use of a standard curve based on WHO standard serum immune globulin and states that levels were measured down to 6 mIU/mL. No evidence is provided that this assay was unsuitable for measuring post-vaccination anti-HAV antibody titres.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  The same serological outcome and laboratory method were used for both groups, at the same stated assessment time point: 21–35 days after booster vaccination.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of intervention received? — No information.**  
  The report does not state whether the Merck Research Laboratories personnel conducting the serological evaluations were blinded to booster assignment.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  The outcome was a quantitative laboratory antibody measurement using a described assay. This is an observer-reported laboratory outcome with little or no scope for subjective judgement by the assessor. The absence of reported blinding of laboratory personnel is therefore not expected to influence the measured antibody titre.

**Motivation for judgement**

The primary outcome was measured by the same quantitative serological assay in both groups. Although laboratory assessor blinding was not reported, the objective assay-based outcome is not plausibly susceptible to differential subjective outcome assessment based on knowledge of vaccine assignment. There is also no indication that outcome ascertainment differed between groups. This domain is judged **low risk of bias**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and support**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  No protocol, statistical analysis plan, or dated registry record is available in the supplied material. Although the article states that the primary evaluation criterion was the comparison of post-booster anti-HAV GMTs, it does not establish that the full analysis plan—including the analysis population and treatment of exclusions—was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

- **5.2 Is the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — Probably no.**  
  The report identifies a single primary immunogenicity assessment: anti-HAV GMT approximately 1 month after booster vaccination. Pre-booster antibody measurement was used to characterize baseline status rather than as an alternative primary post-booster outcome measurement. The article does not identify multiple post-booster time points or multiple antibody assays from which the reported result was selected.

- **5.3 Is the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The authors explicitly state that both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed, but only the per-protocol results are presented: “The results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported.” Numerical ITT results are not provided. Without a pre-specified, dated analysis plan, it cannot be established whether reporting the per-protocol analysis rather than the ITT analysis followed a pre-specified choice rather than post-result selection.

**Motivation for judgement**

The report provides no prospectively finalized analysis plan. Importantly, it confirms that more than one analysis population was used but reports only the per-protocol results. The statement that the analyses were “close enough” does not supply the omitted ITT estimates, confidence intervals, or a pre-specified rationale for selecting per-protocol results for publication. There is not sufficient evidence to conclude that reporting was selected on the basis of direction, magnitude, or statistical significance; therefore high risk is not justified. Nonetheless, the absence of the ITT result and absence of a pre-specified analysis plan raise **some concerns**.

---

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Overall judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation**

The primary post-booster immunogenicity result has:

- **Some concerns** for bias arising from the randomization process, because sequence generation and allocation concealment were not reported;
- **Some concerns** for deviations from intended interventions, because the reported primary immunogenicity result is per-protocol rather than ITT and excludes six randomized participants;
- **Low risk** for missing outcome data;
- **Low risk** for measurement of the objective laboratory antibody outcome; and
- **Some concerns** for selection of the reported result, because both ITT and per-protocol analyses were said to have been undertaken but only the per-protocol analysis was reported, with no available pre-specified analysis plan.

No individual domain reaches high risk on the information available. However, there are **some concerns in multiple domains**—randomization, analysis of deviations from assignment, and selection/reporting of the analysis—and these concerns jointly substantially lower confidence in the reported primary immunogenicity result. In accordance with the stated RoB 2 overall-judgement rules, the overall judgement is therefore **high risk of bias**.

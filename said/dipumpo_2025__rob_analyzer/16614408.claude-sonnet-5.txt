# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Dietary (n-3) Fatty Acids Reduce Plasma F2-Isoprostanes but Not Prostaglandin F2α in Healthy Humans (KANWU study substudy)

**Design:** Individually-randomized, 2×2 factorial parallel-group trial (SAFA vs MUFA background diet; fish-oil (n-3) vs placebo supplementation)

**Outcome assessed:** Plasma 8-iso-PGF2α (primary lipid peroxidation biomarker) and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α — comparison: (n-3) fatty acid supplementation vs placebo, effect of assignment to intervention (ITT-type analysis implied)

**Note on ambiguity:** No trial protocol, registration record, or statistical analysis plan was accessible from the article; assessment is based solely on the published manuscript text. This is a limitation explicitly reported here rather than assumed.

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

The article states participants were "randomly assigned" to SAFA/MUFA diets and, within each, randomly assigned again to (n-3) or placebo capsules, but **no description of the method of random sequence generation** (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number tables) is provided, nor is there any description of **allocation concealment** (e.g., central randomization, sealed envelopes). Table 1 shows baseline clinical characteristics that appear broadly similar across the four groups, with no significant imbalances reported, suggesting no evidence of randomization failure. However, because no information is given on sequence generation or allocation concealment methods, and this is a multicenter trial with no elaboration on how randomization was implemented at each site, there is insufficient information to confirm adequate concealment.
**Judgment: Some concerns** — driven by absence of information on sequence generation and allocation concealment (signalling questions 1.1 and 1.2 = No information; baseline balance appears adequate, 1.3 = No).

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **Some concerns**

The supplement arms compared fish-oil capsules against placebo (olive oil) capsules, described as intended to be indistinguishable, implying an attempt at blinding of participants and personnel. However, the article does **not explicitly report blinding status** of participants, care providers, or those administering the capsules ("double-blind" is never explicitly stated), nor does it report any assessment of the success of blinding. Given fish-oil capsules can have distinctive taste/odor/eructation effects, deviations (e.g., participants guessing allocation) cannot be excluded. No information is given about co-interventions, compliance monitoring (e.g., capsule counts, phospholipid fatty acid changes were reported in a companion paper but not directly linked here to compliance-adjusted analysis), or exclusions from analysis. The analysis appears to include all randomized participants per group (n = 42/41/40/39, matching original randomization numbers in Tables 1–3), consistent with an ITT-type approach, and no naïve per-protocol or as-treated analysis is reported.
**Judgment: Some concerns** — lack of explicit statement on blinding of participants/personnel and lack of information on deviations arising from the trial context, despite an apparently appropriate (ITT) analytic approach.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Some concerns**

The results tables (Table 3) present baseline and 3-month values for the biomarkers based on the full randomized samples per arm (n = 42, 41, 40, 39), suggesting data were available for essentially all randomized participants for the primary lipid peroxidation outcomes. However, the manuscript explicitly states that **plasma samples from 2 of the 5 centers (Denmark and Italy) were "unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage."** This constitutes substantial missing outcome data for the key biomarker (8-iso-PGF2α), analyzed only in 3 of 5 centers, with no quantification of how many randomized participants were thereby excluded from this specific analysis, no reasons unrelated to the outcome value provided beyond "handling/storage," and no sensitivity analysis addressing the potential impact of this loss. It cannot be excluded that missingness (due to sample handling/storage issues occurring in specific centers) is associated with outcome status or that the amount of missing data was small enough to preclude bias, since a large proportion of the total sample (2 out of 5 centers) was excluded from this specific endpoint.
**Judgment: Some concerns** — data missing for a non-trivial subset (2/5 centers) for the primary outcome (8-iso-PGF2α), with no sensitivity analysis, though the reason given (technical handling/storage) appears plausibly unrelated to the true outcome value and unrelated to intervention arm, precluding escalation to "High risk."

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **Low risk**

The outcome (8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α) was measured using a validated radioimmunoassay (RIA) with a well-described antibody cross-reactivity profile and detection limits, indicating an appropriate and objective measurement method. This is a laboratory-based, objective biochemical assay (not requiring subjective judgement by an outcome assessor and not a participant-reported outcome), performed presumably blind to intervention allocation as is standard practice for centralized biochemical assays, and there is no indication that measurement or ascertainment methods differed between intervention groups (all samples analyzed with the same RIA method across each analyzing center). No information suggests laboratory personnel performing the assay were aware of, or influenced by, treatment allocation, and the objective/automated nature of the RIA outcome makes influence from knowledge of allocation implausible.
**Judgment: Low risk** — objective, validated biochemical assay, unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

No trial protocol or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is cited, referenced, or available for comparison against the reported results, precluding confirmation of whether the outcome measures and analyses reported (e.g., choice of 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α as primary lipid peroxidation biomarkers, choice of covariate-adjusted statistical model, choice to report both AOC with and without uric acid adjustment, and with and without lipid adjustment) were fully pre-specified before unblinded data were available. The manuscript reports multiple related outcomes and multiple alternative analyses (e.g., AOC "with" and "without" uric acid, "with" and "without" lipid-adjustment), and the specific version of AOC that reached statistical significance (lipid-adjusted, without uric acid) is highlighted, raising the possibility — though not confirmed — of selective emphasis among multiple analytic options. However, the paper's primary hypothesis and primary comparison (8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α as pre-specified nonenzymatic/enzymatic lipid peroxidation markers) appear consistent with the stated study aim.
**Judgment: Some concerns** — absence of an accessible pre-specified analysis plan/protocol combined with reporting of multiple alternative outcome metrics/analyses for secondary endpoints (AOC).

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement — **High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** No single domain was rated "High risk" in isolation. However, per RoB 2 rules, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted when a study is judged to have "some concerns" for multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result. In this study, **four of the five domains (Randomization process, Deviations from intended interventions, Missing outcome data, and Selection of the reported result)** were each judged as "Some concerns," reflecting a consistent pattern of incomplete methodological reporting (absence of sequence generation/allocation concealment details, absence of explicit blinding confirmation, substantial unexplained loss of outcome data from 2 of 5 study centers for the primary endpoint, and absence of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan). The cumulative effect of these multiple concerns — particularly the loss of isoprostane data from 40% of study centers combined with unclear blinding and unclear allocation concealment — substantially lowers confidence in the reported effect of (n-3) fatty acid supplementation on plasma 8-iso-PGF2α. Therefore, the overall result is judged to be at **High risk of bias**.

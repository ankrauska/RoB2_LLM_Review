## RoB 2 assessment

**Assessment target.** This RoB 2 assessment concerns the randomized comparison among participants with residual vein thrombosis (RVT) at 6 months: **continuation of nadroparin for a further 6 months (group A1, n=119) versus discontinuation of LMWH (group A2, n=123)**, for **recurrent venous thromboembolism (VTE)**. The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect).

The comparison involving group B (no RVT) is not randomized and is therefore not the randomized comparison assessed here.

There is an important reporting ambiguity: the abstract reports an adjusted HR of **1.37 (95% CI 0.7–2.5)** for A2 versus A1, whereas the Results section reports **1.58 (95% CI 0.8–3.0)**. The article also describes the primary endpoint as recurrence from completion of LMWH, but states that analyses were from random assignment and reports results over differing calendar follow-up periods. These discrepancies are considered in Domain 5.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — No information.**  
  The report states that random assignment was “determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10.” It does not state how the order of allocations within blocks was generated, nor does it explicitly describe a random component such as computer-generated random numbers or random permuted blocks. Calling the process “random assignment” is insufficient by itself under RoB 2 to establish that the allocation sequence was random.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  Sealed envelopes were used, but the article does not report that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, opened sequentially, and opened only after irreversible participant enrolment. These details are needed to support adequate concealment when envelopes are used. The fixed block size of 10, together with lack of detail on envelope safeguards, does not provide adequate assurance that forthcoming allocations could not have been anticipated.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — Probably no.**  
  The reported baseline characteristics of A1 and A2 appear broadly similar, including age, sex, body mass index, recent surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, metastatic disease, and index-DVT characteristics. No major imbalance in a key reported prognostic factor is evident. However, the article presents P values primarily across all three groups, including the non-randomized group B, rather than a direct A1-versus-A2 balance assessment.

**Motivation for judgement**  
A low-risk judgement requires adequate allocation concealment. The envelope procedure is insufficiently described to establish concealment, and the random component of sequence generation is not reported. There is no clear baseline evidence that randomization failed, but the absence of adequate information about allocation concealment leads to **some concerns**.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The report explicitly states that patients were not masked to treatment or RVT status.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Treating physicians were also not masked.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — No information.**  
  The article reports high compliance in A1: 15 of 119 participants interrupted LMWH for fewer than 5 days during study treatment. However, it does not provide sufficient information on other potentially relevant deviations arising from the open-label trial context, such as differential co-interventions, differential use of anticoagulation outside the protocol, or management changes related to knowledge of assigned treatment.

- **2.4–2.5 Were such deviations likely to affect the outcome, and were they balanced? — Not applicable.**  
  No specific trial-context deviations are reported from which these questions can be determined.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  The investigators state that data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis. The randomized groups and recurrence events are reported for all 119 A1 and 123 A2 participants. There is no report that participants were analyzed according to treatment received or excluded because of non-adherence.

**Motivation for judgement**  
The intended comparison was open-label, so both participants and treating clinicians knew intervention assignment. The intention-to-treat analytical approach is appropriate for the effect of assignment. However, in an unblinded trial, information is required to establish whether trial-context deviations occurred and whether they could have affected recurrent-VTE risk. The report does not provide this information. Therefore, this domain raises **some concerns**, rather than high risk, because no actual important imbalance in deviations is documented.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **3.1 Were data for recurrent VTE available for all, or nearly all, randomized participants? — Probably yes.**  
  The report accounts for the full randomized samples of 119 participants in A1 and 123 in A2 in the recurrence analyses. It reports all recurrent-VTE events, major bleeding events, and deaths. Participants were scheduled for follow-up every 3 months, and the report states that all enrolled participants were observed for 1 year after LMWH discontinuation. No participants are reported as lost to follow-up or as having an unknown VTE outcome.

  The numbers remaining under follow-up decline over time, but the report identifies deaths due to cancer progression and recurrent events. It does not report unexplained missing outcome data or attrition.

- **3.2–3.4 — Not applicable.**  
  Because recurrent-VTE outcome ascertainment appears available for all or nearly all randomized participants, further questions regarding the relation of missingness to the true outcome value are not required.

**Motivation for judgement**  
The article does not report loss to follow-up or missing recurrent-VTE outcome data. Deaths are documented and adjudicated rather than being unreported losses. Although the different scheduled follow-up durations across groups complicate interpretation of the reported endpoint, that is an issue of outcome definition/reporting rather than evidence that outcome data were missing. For the missing-data domain, the evidence supports **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring recurrent VTE inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  Recurrent DVT was assessed using objective compression ultrasonography criteria: a previously compressible segment becoming non-compressible, or an increase of more than 4 mm in residual thrombus diameter during compression. Uncertain cases were reassessed after 5–7 days or evaluated using contrast venography. Suspected pulmonary embolism was assessed using objective diagnostic algorithms including clinical probability, ventilation-perfusion scanning, or helical computed tomography.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  The same diagnostic criteria and testing procedures were specified for all groups. There is no reported use of systematically different outcome-measurement methods or thresholds between A1 and A2.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — No.**  
  All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee unaware of group assignment, RVT results, participant name, and enrolling centre.

- **4.4–4.5 — Not applicable.**  
  Since the central outcome-adjudication committee was blinded, the subsequent questions concerning influence of knowledge of treatment assignment are not required.

**Motivation for judgement**  
Although participants and treating physicians were unblinded, the outcome was based on objective diagnostic criteria and centrally adjudicated by assessors blinded to treatment assignment and RVT status. The report provides no evidence that outcome measurement procedures differed between randomized groups. This supports a judgement of **low risk of bias** in outcome measurement.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **5.1 Were the data producing the result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The trial registry record identifies recurrent DVT and/or PE as the primary outcome but does not provide a detailed, date-stamped statistical analysis plan. No full protocol or statistical analysis plan is available in the supplied material. Thus, it cannot be determined whether the particular time origin, follow-up period, covariate adjustment, handling of deaths, or reported HR were specified before unblinded outcome data were available.

- **5.2 Was the reported result likely selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or time points? — No information.**  
  The article reports recurrent VTE at multiple periods: during the first 6 months after random assignment, between 6 and 12 months after random assignment, and in terms of 12-month cumulative recurrence. It also describes its primary endpoint as recurrence “from completion of LMWH,” while stating that analyses were from random assignment. The available information does not establish which specific time point and estimand were pre-specified for the principal A2-versus-A1 comparison.

- **5.3 Was the reported result likely selected from multiple eligible analyses? — No information.**  
  The article reports adjusted HRs, but the adjustment variables are stated only generally (age, sex, previous surgery, metastasis), and no pre-specified analysis plan is available. In addition, the A2-versus-A1 adjusted HR is inconsistent between the abstract (**1.37, 95% CI 0.7–2.5**) and the Results section (**1.58, 95% CI 0.8–3.0**), despite apparently referring to the same comparison. The source does not explain this discrepancy.

**Motivation for judgement**  
There is no evidence of a protocol or statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available that specifies the exact recurrence endpoint, time origin, follow-up window, and analysis used for the reported result. The inconsistency in the reported A2-versus-A1 HR and the ambiguity between recurrence from randomization versus recurrence after LMWH discontinuation prevent confirmation that the reported result was selected according to pre-specified intentions. There is not sufficient evidence that result selection occurred on the basis of findings; therefore the judgement is **some concerns**, not high risk.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation for overall judgement**  
The study is at **some concerns** in multiple domains:

1. **Randomization process:** inadequate reporting of random-sequence generation and envelope-based allocation concealment.  
2. **Deviations from intended interventions:** unblinded participants and clinicians, with insufficient information to rule out trial-context deviations affecting recurrent-VTE risk.  
3. **Selection of the reported result:** no available pre-specified detailed analysis plan, ambiguity in the specified primary endpoint and time origin, and inconsistent A2-versus-A1 HRs between the abstract and Results section.

Under the specified RoB 2 overall rules, multiple domains with some concerns can warrant an overall judgement of **high risk of bias** when they substantially lower confidence in the result. Here, the combination of uncertainty regarding allocation concealment, open-label management without adequate information on deviations, and ambiguity/inconsistency in reporting of the primary randomized comparison substantially lowers confidence in the reported A2-versus-A1 recurrent-VTE estimate.

Based on the thorough and detailed analysis of the provided article ("Effects of bright and blue light on acoustic reaction time and maximum handgrip strength in male athletes: a randomized controlled trial") and its associated protocol (NCT02203539), following the Cochrane RoB 2 guidelines, here is the structured Risk of Bias assessment report:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The study utilized a random allocation sequence generated via an online tool applying permuted block randomization with varying block sizes (3, 6, and 9). The list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered sealed opaque envelopes, ensuring adequate allocation concealment until participants were enrolled and assigned (Signalling Questions 1.1 and 1.2 = Yes). Baseline characteristics (Table 1) show no substantial imbalances across the three groups, indicating no apparent problem with the randomization process (Question 1.3 = No).

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** Due to the nature of the intervention (light exposure), participants were not blinded. The test supervisor was blinded to the assigned light condition during the tests. However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context that would likely affect the outcomes (reaction time and handgrip strength) differently between groups. Two participants were excluded retrospectively due to invalid measurements in the baseline fitness test (not related to the intervention or due to the trial context). An appropriate analysis approach was used.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** Seventy-four participants were randomized. Two were excluded due to invalid baseline screening data, leaving 72 participants. Outcome data (reaction time and handgrip strength) were available for all of the remaining 72 participants. There was no missing outcome data that could bias the result (Question 3.1 = Yes). 

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The methods for measuring reaction time (computer-based task) and handgrip strength (hydraulic hand dynamometer) were appropriate and standardized across all groups. The test supervisor (outcome assessor) was blinded to the light condition each participant was exposed to. Although participants were not blinded to the type of light they received, the outcomes (simple acoustic reaction time and maximum handgrip strength) are objective physiological and psychomotor measures, and it is unlikely that their assessment was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The outcomes assessed (reaction time and maximum handgrip strength) were pre-specified as secondary outcomes in the trial registry (NCT02203539). The data were analyzed in accordance with the pre-specified plan. There is no evidence that the reported numerical results were selected on the basis of the results from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple eligible analyses. The statistical methods were pre-specified and reported transparently.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at low risk of bias across all five domains for the outcomes assessed (reaction time and handgrip strength). Randomization and allocation concealment were robust, there were no important deviations from the intended interventions, missing data was not an issue, outcome measurement was objective and assessor-blinded, and results were reported in accordance with the pre-specified protocol. Therefore, the overall risk of bias is low.

# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Huang LY, et al. "Embolization combined with endoscopic variceal ligation for the treatment of esophagogastric variceal bleeding in patients with cirrhosis." Chin Med J 2007;120(1):36-40.

**Design:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial
**Experimental intervention:** Simultaneous embolization (Histoacryl) of gastric fundus varices + EVL of esophageal varices
**Comparator intervention:** Embolization of gastric fundus varices, followed by EVL 2 months later
**Outcomes assessed:** (1) Rate of emergency hemostasis; (2) Rate of early rebleeding
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q1.1 (Was the allocation sequence random?): The authors state "A random number table was produced through Microsoft Excel. The patients were divided into a control group and a therapy group following the random number table." This indicates a genuine random component → **Yes**.
- Signalling Q1.2 (Was the allocation sequence concealed?): The article provides **no information** on allocation concealment. There is no mention of a central randomization service, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or any other concealment mechanism. Given the small, single-center, non-CONSORT-adherent nature of this report, and no protocol/registry available for cross-checking, this must be scored as **No information**.
- Signalling Q1.3 (Did baseline differences suggest a problem with randomization?): The baseline table shows no statistically significant differences between groups for sex, age, active bleeding, red sign, Child-Pugh score, portal vein width, number/degree of esophageal varices, or GOV classification (all P>0.05). This is compatible with chance → **No**.

**Reasoning for domain judgment:** Random sequence generation was adequate, and no baseline imbalance is evident; however, the complete absence of information regarding allocation concealment prevents a "Low risk" judgment. Per the RoB2 algorithm, "No information" on concealment combined with no evidence of baseline imbalance maps to **Some concerns**.

**Limitation noted:** No trial registration, protocol, or statistical analysis plan was available/cited; assessment relies solely on the published article, limiting verification of concealment procedures.

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q2.1/2.2 (Were participants/personnel aware of assigned intervention?): This trial was **not blinded** — by nature of the intervention (timing of procedures: same-session vs. staged 2 months apart), blinding of patients and endoscopists was impossible → **Yes** (aware).
- Signalling Q2.3 (Deviations from intended intervention because of trial context?): No explicit deviations from protocol are reported in either arm; both groups received their assigned procedures as planned (immediate combined therapy vs. staged therapy). There is no evidence that trial context (e.g., recruitment/engagement effects) caused non-protocol interventions → **No**.
- Since no deviations related to trial context are reported, Q2.4/2.5 are not applicable.
- Signalling Q2.6 (Appropriate analysis – ITT?): All 90 and 82 randomized patients appear to be included and analyzed in their assigned groups for hemostasis outcome; no patients were excluded from primary analysis based on treatment received. This is consistent with an ITT-type analysis → **Yes**.

**Reasoning for domain judgment:** Lack of blinding is unavoidable given the nature of the procedures (open-label, since blinding of an endoscopic procedure timing is not feasible) and does not, by itself, elevate risk to "High" because outcomes (hemostasis, rebleeding) are relatively objective (or semi-objective) endpoints assessed via defined clinical/laboratory criteria rather than subjective participant-reported outcomes. However, because participants/personnel were not blinded and there is no explicit statement ruling out any influence of trial context on management decisions (e.g., threshold for administering rescue EVL, use of somatostatin, decisions on rebleeding management), **Some concerns** is assigned rather than "Low."

**Limitation noted:** The report does not discuss whether lack of blinding influenced any subjective clinical decision-making (e.g., threshold to diagnose rebleeding, decision to escalate to rescue EVL) between groups.

## Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q3.1 (Were data available for all/nearly all randomized participants?): Outcome data (hemostasis, rebleeding, complications) are reported for **all 90 patients** in the therapy group and **all 82 patients** in the control group. No dropouts, losses to follow-up, or exclusions are described. The full randomized cohort is accounted for in both the emergency hemostasis and early-rebleeding analyses → **Yes**.

**Reasoning for domain judgment:** With complete outcome data available for all randomized participants across both outcomes examined, there is no potential for bias due to missing outcome data.

**Limitation noted:** Although data appear complete, no CONSORT flow diagram is provided, and the exact number of patients screened/excluded before randomization is not detailed, slightly limiting ability to fully confirm no post-randomization attrition occurred beyond what is narratively described.

---

## Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q4.1 (Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?): Outcomes (hemostasis defined by cessation of active bleeding on endoscopy; rebleeding defined by hematemesis/melena plus hemoglobin drop >20g/L or hypovolemic shock or need for transfusion) are clearly and objectively defined a priori → **No**.
- Signalling Q4.2 (Could measurement/ascertainment differ between groups?): Both groups underwent endoscopic assessment using the same objective criteria; ascertainment procedures do not appear to differ structurally between groups → **No/Probably no**.
- Signalling Q4.3 (Were outcome assessors aware of intervention received?): Given the open-label design and the fact that the treating endoscopist and clinical team (who would also adjudicate rebleeding/hemostasis) were the same individuals performing the procedures, outcome assessors were **not blinded** → **Yes**.
- Signalling Q4.4 (Could assessment be influenced by knowledge of intervention?): The hemostasis and rebleeding outcomes have a component of clinical judgment (e.g., timing/threshold to diagnose rebleeding, decision to classify hemostasis as achieved, decision to intervene with rescue procedures), which could plausibly be influenced by awareness of group assignment, especially given the trial's hypothesis-driven design favoring the combined approach → **Yes**.
- Signalling Q4.5 (Is it likely that assessment was influenced?): There is no specific evidence (e.g., independent/blinded adjudication committee) that assessment was influenced, but no safeguard (blinded adjudication) is reported either → **No information/Probably no**, insufficient to escalate to "High."

**Reasoning for domain judgment:** Because outcome assessors (the treating physicians) were unblinded and outcomes involve some element of clinical judgment (particularly "early rebleeding," which required clinical determination of hemoglobin drop thresholds and shock signs), there is potential for influence of knowledge of intervention assignment on outcome ascertainment. No blinded/independent outcome adjudication committee is mentioned. This is not judged as definitely "likely" to have biased results (hence not "High"), but the potential cannot be excluded, warranting **Some concerns**.

**Limitation noted:** The article does not report whether outcome assessment (particularly rebleeding diagnosis) was performed or adjudicated by personnel independent of those delivering treatment.

## Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling Q5.1 (Was the outcome analyzed per a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded data were available?): No trial registration, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced anywhere in the article. It is impossible to verify whether the reported outcomes (rate of emergency hemostasis, rate of early rebleeding) and their definitions were pre-specified prior to unblinded data availability → **No information**.
- Signalling Q5.2 (Selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements?): No evidence of multiple outcome measurements/timepoints being collected and selectively reported; however, without a protocol, this cannot be fully verified → **No information**.
- Signalling Q5.3 (Selected from multiple eligible analyses?): Only single, clearly defined analyses (Chi-square for the two key outcomes) are presented; no evidence of multiple alternative analyses (e.g., different rebleeding definitions/time windows) being tested and selectively reported, but again this cannot be verified without the protocol → **No information**.

**Reasoning for domain judgment:** In the absence of a registered protocol or statistical analysis plan, there is no way to confirm that the reported results are free from selective outcome reporting or selective analysis reporting. According to the RoB2 algorithm, when information is insufficient to answer these signalling questions definitively but there is no explicit evidence of selection, the default judgment is **Some concerns** rather than "High" or "Low."

**Limitation noted:** No trial registry entry, protocol, or SAP was identifiable/cited in the article; this is a significant transparency limitation preventing a more definitive judgment on this domain.

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgment: High risk of bias**

**Rationale:**
Applying the Cochrane RoB 2 algorithm for reaching an overall judgement:
- Domain 1 (Randomization process): Some concerns
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions): Some concerns
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data): Low risk
- Domain 4 (Measurement of outcome): Some concerns
- Domain 5 (Selection of reported result): Some concerns

No individual domain was rated "High risk" in isolation. However, per RoB 2 guidance (Table 1), an overall rating of "High risk of bias" is warranted when "the study is judged to have some concerns for multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result." In this study, **four of the five domains** were rated "Some concerns," including critical domains such as allocation concealment (unknown), lack of blinding of outcome assessors combined with judgment-dependent outcomes (rebleeding, hemostasis), and complete absence of a pre-specified/registered protocol to rule out selective reporting. The cumulative effect of these multiple, simultaneous uncertainties — particularly the unblinded, single-center, non-pre-registered design with judgment-dependent outcome ascertainment — substantially lowers confidence in the reported treatment effect estimates (rate of emergency hemostasis and rate of early rebleeding).

Therefore, the overall risk-of-bias judgment for this result is **High risk of bias**.

**General limitations affecting the assessment (to be reported transparently):**
1. Only the published journal article was available for this assessment; no trial protocol, registration record, or statistical analysis plan could be consulted, which limited definitive judgments in Domains 1, 2, and 5.
2. The trial was conducted and published in 2007, prior to widespread adoption of trial registration and CONSORT reporting standards, which likely explains several reporting gaps (e.g., allocation concealment, blinded outcome adjudication).
3. No CONSORT participant flow diagram was provided, limiting full verification of participant flow, though narrative data suggest complete outcome ascertainment.

## RoB 2 assessment

**Result assessed:** Postoperative serum NT-proBNP measured 24 hours after ICU arrival: MgSO₄ group mean 1566.4 (SEM 15.9) versus placebo group mean 2632.1 (SEM 29.2), *P* = 0.002 (Table 3).

**Comparison:** Intravenous magnesium sulfate infusion (15 mg/kg/hour from induction of anaesthesia to skin closure) versus equal-volume intravenous normal saline placebo.

**Trial design:** Individually randomized, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial; 88 participants (44 per group).

**Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment** to magnesium sulfate versus placebo at baseline (intention-to-treat effect).

**Sources used:** The submitted journal article and supplied tables. Although the article reports an Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials registration number (IRCT201205069650N1), no registry record, protocol, or statistical analysis plan was supplied or available within the material assessed.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states that participants “were allocated into one of the two groups based on a computer random numbers table.” This is a random method of sequence generation.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The article does not describe allocation concealment. It does not report central randomization, pharmacy-controlled allocation, sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes, or another procedure preventing personnel enrolling participants from foreseeing allocations. Reporting that the trial was double-blind does not establish that allocation was concealed before assignment.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  Table 1 reports no statistically significant baseline differences in age, body surface area, Euroscore, weight, ejection fraction, ESR, haematocrit, creatinine, magnesium, or ionized calcium. The groups were also similar in CPB time, aortic cross-clamp time, and number of grafts. There is no reported baseline imbalance clearly beyond that compatible with chance.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The random sequence-generation method was appropriate, and the reported baseline data do not indicate a failed randomization process. However, allocation concealment was not reported. Under RoB 2, lack of information on concealment prevents a low-risk judgement when the concealment procedure cannot reasonably be inferred. Therefore, this domain raises **some concerns**.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably no.**  
  The study is described as double-blind, and both groups received an infusion via a 50-mL syringe: magnesium sulfate in the intervention group and the same volume of saline placebo in the control group. Participants were under general anaesthesia while the infusion was administered, from anaesthetic induction to skin closure.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — No information.**  
  The report uses the term “double-blind,” but does not state specifically which personnel were blinded, such as the anaesthesiologists administering the infusion, surgeons, ICU personnel, laboratory personnel, or outcome-related care providers. No method for preserving the blind is reported.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — No information.**  
  The report states that anaesthesia, surgery, CPB methods, and postoperative care were made “as similar as possible,” but does not provide data on treatment adherence, actual administration of the allocated infusion, unintended co-interventions, or departures from the protocol by group. It is therefore not possible to establish whether relevant deviations arose because of the trial context.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Probably yes.**  
  The reported NT-proBNP comparison is between the two allocated groups, each reported as containing 44 participants, using Student’s *t*-test. Thus, the reported analysis appears to retain participants in their randomized groups.

  However, the methods also state that patients requiring “triple inotropic agent therapy for CPB weaning were excluded from the study” and that the number excluded in each group was registered. No participant flow diagram or results for these exclusions are reported. This creates ambiguity regarding whether any randomized participants were excluded after randomization. The reported sample sizes remain 44 per group, matching the 88 enrolled participants, but the report does not explicitly resolve this discrepancy.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The placebo-controlled design and identical-volume infusions support participant blinding, but the report does not specify whether the anaesthesiologists, other treating personnel, and outcome-related care staff were blinded. Nor does it report whether deviations or differential co-interventions occurred. The planned exclusion of patients requiring triple inotropic therapy is a post-randomization exclusion criterion; although the presented group sizes imply that 44 participants per group were analysed, the article does not report whether exclusions actually occurred. These reporting limitations prevent a low-risk judgement. There is not sufficient evidence that deviations caused a material bias in the NT-proBNP result; therefore, this domain is judged as **some concerns**, rather than high risk.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Probably yes.**  
  Eighty-eight patients were enrolled and randomly allocated, with 44 participants assigned to each group. Table 3 presents postoperative NT-proBNP results for the MgSO₄ group (*n* = 44) and placebo group (*n* = 44), accounting for all 88 enrolled participants.

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — Not applicable.**  
  The available group sizes for the postoperative NT-proBNP analysis equal the reported randomized group sizes.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
For the assessed outcome, postoperative NT-proBNP values are reported for 44 participants in each of the two randomized groups, corresponding to all 88 enrolled participants. No missing NT-proBNP data, differential attrition, or imputation method is reported. Although the article does not provide a CONSORT participant-flow diagram and includes an unclear statement about excluding patients needing triple inotropic therapy, the reported outcome table explicitly retains 44 participants in each group. On the information available for this specific outcome, missing data are not sufficient to create a material risk of bias. This domain is therefore judged **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  NT-proBNP was measured with an electrochemiluminescence immunoassay using the Elecsys 2010 system. The article reports a coefficient of variation of 2.2–3.2% and a detection range of 5–35,000 pg/mL. The postoperative values reported in Table 3 were within this detection range.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  The report states that samples were obtained at the same prespecified time points: before anaesthesia and 24 hours after ICU arrival. It also reports consistent sampling temperature, centrifugation, refrigeration, storage, and laboratory processing throughout the study. The same assay was used for both groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants? — No information.**  
  The article does not explicitly state whether laboratory staff measuring NT-proBNP were blinded to treatment allocation.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably no.**  
  NT-proBNP is an objectively measured laboratory biomarker generated by an automated electrochemiluminescence immunoassay. The result is not a participant-reported outcome, a clinician judgement, or a treatment decision. Knowledge of allocated treatment would not plausibly alter the biochemical concentration in a stored blood sample or the automated assay result.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The outcome was measured using an appropriate and objective laboratory assay, with the same sampling schedule and processing procedures reported for both groups. Although laboratory assessor blinding was not explicitly reported, the objective automated nature of the assay means that knowledge of intervention allocation was unlikely to influence outcome measurement. Therefore, this domain is at **low risk of bias**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The trial is reported as registered (IRCT201205069650N1), but the registry record, trial protocol, and statistical analysis plan were not available in the supplied materials. The article does not state that an analysis plan was finalized before outcome data were unblinded. Furthermore, the article states that recruitment began in January 2010, whereas the identifier includes “20120506”; the submitted report does not provide sufficient information to establish the timing of registration or whether prospective outcome-analysis specifications existed.

- **5.2 Is the reported postoperative NT-proBNP result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  NT-proBNP was measured preoperatively and at 24 hours after ICU arrival. The authors acknowledge as a limitation that they assessed only “two points” and did not evaluate the biomarker trend or area under the curve. Without a protocol, registry outcome specification, or statistical analysis plan, it cannot be verified whether the 24-hour postoperative measurement was the prespecified primary time point or whether other intended postoperative NT-proBNP time points were measured or analysed.

- **5.3 Is the reported result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The reported analysis is a between-group Student’s *t*-test of postoperative NT-proBNP values. The report does not provide a pre-specified statistical analysis plan to establish whether this unadjusted post-treatment comparison was selected in preference to other eligible analyses, such as analysis of change from baseline, baseline-adjusted analysis, or alternative handling of the repeated baseline and postoperative measurements.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The study report alone does not allow confirmation that the reported 24-hour NT-proBNP analysis was pre-specified before unblinded data became available. The existence of a registration number does not itself establish pre-specification, because the relevant registry content and timing were not available for review. Since postoperative NT-proBNP could be measured at multiple time points and analysed in several eligible ways, the absence of a protocol or analysis plan creates concern regarding selective choice of the reported measurement or analysis. There is no direct evidence that the result was selected because of its statistical significance or direction; consequently, the appropriate judgement is **some concerns**, not high risk.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Overall judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation for overall judgement:**  
The result is at low risk of bias for missing outcome data and measurement of NT-proBNP. However:

- there are **some concerns** about the randomization process because allocation concealment was not reported;
- there are **some concerns** regarding deviations from intended interventions because the identities of blinded personnel, protocol deviations, and the handling of the stated post-randomization exclusion criterion were insufficiently reported; and
- there are **some concerns** about selection of the reported result because no protocol, registry record, or pre-specified statistical analysis plan was available to establish that the postoperative NT-proBNP time point and analysis were selected independently of the results.

Under the specified RoB 2 overall rule, **some concerns in multiple domains that substantially lower confidence in the result warrant an overall judgement of high risk of bias**. The key limitation is insufficient reporting of allocation concealment, blinding/deviations, participant exclusions, and pre-specified outcome-analysis intentions.

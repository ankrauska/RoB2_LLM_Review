# ROB 2 Assessment Report

**Preliminary Considerations**
- **Study design:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial (3 arms: Functional-order, Random, Blocked practice)
- **Experimental/Comparator interventions:** Functional-order practice vs. Random practice vs. Blocked practice
- **Outcome assessed:** Change (learning) scores on checklist, final product analysis, global rating, and drilling time, at immediate and delayed post-test
- **Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (ITT-type effect, no adherence issues expected in this lab setting)
- **Source used:** Journal article only (no protocol, no registration, no SAP available)

---

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process — Judgement: SOME CONCERNS**

The article states participants "were randomly assigned to three experimental groups" but provides **no description whatsoever** of the method used to generate the allocation sequence (no mention of computer randomization, random number tables, or any random component) and **no information on allocation concealment** (e.g., sequentially numbered envelopes, central randomization). No baseline characteristics of the three groups (e.g., prior experience, age, sex, baseline pre-test scores by group) are reported for comparison, so it is not possible to judge whether the groups were balanced at baseline.

- Signalling Q1.1 (random sequence?): No information → Probably Yes (given the explicit use of the term "randomly assigned"), but insufficient detail to confirm.
- Signalling Q1.2 (allocation concealed?): No information.
- Signalling Q1.3 (baseline imbalance suggesting problem?): No information (no baseline data reported).

**Motivation:** Per the RoB2 algorithm, when there is no information on allocation concealment (and no information on baseline imbalance), the default judgement is "Some concerns." This reflects the ambiguity due to absent methodological detail, not evidence of an actual flaw.

**Limitation noted:** The complete absence of a description of randomization procedure and allocation concealment mechanism is a critical reporting gap that prevents a "Low risk" judgement.

---

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — Judgement: LOW RISK**

This domain concerns deviations from assigned intervention (practice schedule) during the acquisition phase.

- Signalling Q2.1 (participants aware of assignment?): Yes — inherently, participants know which practice schedule (order of skill practice) they are undergoing, as this is a behavioral/educational intervention that cannot be blinded to the participant.
- Signalling Q2.2 (personnel aware?): Yes — the supervising orthopedic faculty providing feedback were necessarily aware of which schedule was being followed, as they administered the sessions.
- Signalling Q2.3 (deviations arising from trial context?): No — there is no indication that participants failed to follow their assigned schedules, crossed over to another arm, or that non-protocol interventions occurred. The task structure (rigid timed sessions) inherently prevented deviation.
- Signalling Q2.6 (appropriate analysis?): Yes — difference scores (post-test minus pre-test) were analyzed via ANOVA including presumably all randomized participants; no exclusions are reported.

**Motivation:** Although blinding of participants/personnel was not possible (as is typical/necessary in this type of educational intervention research), there is no evidence that deviations from assigned intervention occurred due to the trial context, and the analysis approach (comparing groups as assigned) is appropriate for estimating the assignment effect. Lack of blinding here is not expected to bias this type of skill-acquisition outcome, as participants could not "switch" practice schedules once assigned. Thus, this domain warrants a Low risk of bias judgement.

**Limitation noted:** No explicit confirmation is given that all 28 randomized participants completed the study without protocol deviations (e.g., withdrawal mid-session); this is inferred by absence of any reported dropout, which introduces mild ambiguity but does not shift the domain-level judgement given the tightly controlled experimental setting.

---

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data — Judgement: SOME CONCERNS**

The article reports outcomes from "28 first- and second-year medical students" who were assigned to three groups, but **does not report the number of participants per group**, nor does it provide a CONSORT-type flow diagram or any explicit statement that all 28 participants completed all three testing points (pre-test, immediate post-test, delayed post-test).

- Signalling Q3.1 (data available for all/nearly all randomized?): No information — no participant flow or per-group sample sizes are reported.
- Signalling Q3.2 (evidence result not biased by missing data?): No information/not applicable — no sensitivity analyses reported.
- Signalling Q3.3 (could missingness depend on true outcome value?): No information — cannot be excluded, as no missing data disclosure is provided at all.

**Motivation:** According to RoB2 guidance, absence of information about the extent of missing outcome data typically leads to a judgement of high risk of bias; however, given the tightly controlled, single-session laboratory nature of the study (participants tested within the same day, immediately supervised), it is plausible that attrition was minimal or absent. In the absence of any explicit statement confirming complete data for all 28 participants across the three measurement points and per group, a "Some Concerns" judgement is assigned rather than "High," acknowledging the plausibility of low dropout in this brief controlled setting, while still flagging the reporting gap as a genuine limitation.

**Limitation noted:** The failure to report the number of participants per group and confirm completeness of outcome data collection for all three test points is a significant reporting ambiguity.

---

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — Judgement: SOME CONCERNS**

Outcomes were assessed by "two experienced orthopedic surgeons" using checklists, final product analysis, and global ratings.

- Signalling Q4.1 (method inappropriate?): No — established, validated-type methods (checklist, final product analysis based on Szalay et al., global rating based on Reznick et al.) were used, consistent with surgical skills assessment literature.
- Signalling Q4.2 (measurement could differ between groups?): No — the same standardized tests and evaluation instruments were applied identically across groups and testing time points.
- Signalling Q4.3 (outcome assessors aware of intervention group?): **No information.** The article does not state whether the two orthopedic surgeon-raters were blinded to group assignment during checklist/final product/global rating scoring. Given that the raters directly observed the test performance sessions or products, and given the practice regimen (e.g., blocked vs. random) might be inferable from performance style, blinding status is unclear.
- Signalling Q4.4 (could assessment be influenced by knowledge of intervention?): Yes/Probably Yes — these are observer-reported outcomes involving judgement (checklist scoring, final product analysis, global ratings), which are all susceptible to influence by rater expectation if unblinded.
- Signalling Q4.5 (likely influenced?): No information — cannot be determined from the reported text whether raters had strong prior expectations (e.g., regarding which schedule "should" produce better learning) that could have influenced scoring.

**Motivation:** Because the outcome assessors' blinding status is not reported, and the outcome measures (checklist, final product, global rating) inherently involve subjective judgement susceptible to bias if raters were aware of group assignment, this domain is judged as "Some Concerns" rather than "Low risk." This is compounded by the fact that the same faculty who provided feedback during acquisition (and thus knew group assignment) may have overlapped with, or influenced, the outcome assessors (though this is not explicitly stated).

**Limitation noted:** No statement is provided regarding blinding of outcome assessors to intervention group assignment; this is a critical ambiguity for a domain heavily dependent on subjective, judgement-based scoring instruments.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result — Judgement: SOME CONCERNS**

No trial registration, protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or available for this study, and no pre-specified analysis plan is described.

- Signalling Q5.1 (analysis pre-specified before unblinded data available?): No information — no protocol or SAP is cited.
- Signalling Q5.2 (result selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements?): No information — Multiple outcome measures (checklist, final product analysis, global rating, drilling time) were reported, and results for each are presented with differing significance (checklist and final product analysis were "significant," while global rating and drilling time were "not significant"). All these appear to be reported (not selectively suppressed), which somewhat mitigates concern, but the lack of a pre-specified primary outcome raises the possibility that the "narrative emphasis" of the conclusions (favoring functional-order and random over blocked practice) was shaped post hoc by which measures reached significance.
- Signalling Q5.3 (result selected from multiple eligible analyses?): No information — no evidence of alternative statistical models tried and discarded, but this cannot be ruled out due to absence of a pre-specified plan.

**Motivation:** Since all four outcome measures appear to have been reported (with both significant and non-significant results disclosed transparently), there is no direct evidence of selective outcome suppression. However, the complete absence of a pre-specified, time-stamped analysis plan or trial registration means it is impossible to confirm that the analytic approach, choice of measures, and framing of "learning" (difference scores) were decided prior to data unblinding. This uncertainty warrants a "Some Concerns" judgement rather than "Low risk."

**Limitation noted:** No trial registration or protocol is available to verify that the analysis plan and outcome selection were finalized prior to data collection/analysis; this is reported as an inherent limitation of the assessment rather than an assumption of malpractice.

## 6. OVERALL RISK-OF-BIAS JUDGEMENT: **HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Summary of domain judgements:**
1. Bias arising from randomization process: **Some concerns**
2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions: **Low risk**
3. Bias due to missing outcome data: **Some concerns**
4. Bias in measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**
5. Bias in selection of the reported result: **Some concerns**

**Overall Motivation:** Following the RoB2 decision rule, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted because the study has been judged to raise "Some Concerns" in **multiple domains simultaneously** (Domains 1, 3, 4, and 5), and this pattern of multiple, compounding uncertainties (regarding randomization/allocation concealment methodology, completeness of outcome data reporting, blinding status of subjective outcome assessors, and absence of any pre-registered analysis plan) substantially lowers overall confidence in the trustworthiness of the reported result. Per RoB2 rules: *"Some concerns for multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result"* triggers a "High risk of bias" overall judgement, even though no single domain was independently rated as "High."

**Key limitations explicitly flagged for this assessment (ambiguities in reporting, not assumptions):**
- No description of random sequence generation or allocation concealment method.
- No baseline characteristic data by group reported.
- No per-group sample sizes or explicit confirmation of complete outcome data for all 28 participants across all three test points.
- No statement regarding blinding status of the two outcome-assessing orthopedic surgeons.
- No trial protocol, registration, or statistical analysis plan available to confirm pre-specification of analyses and outcomes.

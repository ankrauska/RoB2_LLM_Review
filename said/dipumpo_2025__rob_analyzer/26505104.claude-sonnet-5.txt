# ROB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Kulasegaram et al. – "Cause and Effect: Testing a Mechanism and Method for the Cognitive Integration of Basic Science"
**Design:** Individually randomized, 4-arm parallel experimental trial (SC, BC, CB, FL)
**Outcome assessed:** Diagnostic test performance (delayed testing) — the group × time interaction effect (F[3,88]=3.05, P<.03), which is the study's primary reported finding
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat-type effect), since this is a single-exposure educational/cognitive experiment

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions:**
- 1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? → *No information*. The report states only that "participants were randomly allocated to one of four study conditions," with no description of the method used (e.g., computer-generated randomization, block size, stratification).
- 1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed? → *No information*. No mention of concealment mechanisms (e.g., use of a central/automated system). Given that recruitment appears to have used a "custom-designed computer program," it is plausible that allocation was computer-generated and automatically concealed, but this is not explicitly confirmed.
- 1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem? → *No information/Probably no*. Group sizes were reasonably similar (25/24/21/25 initially), and there is no formal statistical comparison of baseline characteristics (e.g., prior knowledge, program type) presented, although a slight imbalance in nursing/PA student distribution across groups is noted descriptively without testing.

**Judgment rationale:** Because there is no information on sequence generation or allocation concealment, and no baseline imbalance data are reported to reassure the reader, this maps to "Some concerns" per the RoB 2 decision algorithm (No information on both signalling questions 1.1/1.2 with no evidence of imbalance defaults to "Some concerns" rather than "High").

**Reported ambiguity/limitation:** The absence of any description of sequence generation or concealment methods is a genuine reporting gap that could not be resolved from the text.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions:**
- 2.1/2.2 Were participants/personnel aware of assigned intervention? → *Yes*, inherently, since the "intervention" is the reading material itself, delivered directly via computer program; there is no third-party "care provider" to blind. However, this is a design feature intrinsic to a cognitive/educational lab experiment rather than a source of trial-context bias, since there is no "usual care" or clinical decision-making context in which awareness could distort delivery.
- 2.3 Did deviations arise due to trial context? → *No*. Delivery was standardized via a computer program with a fixed 30-minute learning cap and no interpersonal delivery; no evidence of non-protocol content or engagement effects influencing exposure.
- 2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used? → *Yes*. All randomized participants who completed testing were analyzed in their assigned groups; there is no naïve "per-protocol" or "as-treated" analysis switching participants between groups.
- 2.7 N/A.

**Judgment rationale:** Because the intervention is a fixed, self-contained reading exposure delivered identically by a computer program (removing the possibility of "personnel" behaving differently by group) and analysis followed group assignment, deviations from intended intervention affecting the outcome are unlikely. The exclusion of 2 outlier participants (extremely short time-on-task, <5 minutes, >3 SD below group mean) is addressed more appropriately under missing-data considerations (Domain 3) since it reflects incomplete/invalid task engagement rather than protocol deviation per se.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions:**
- 3.1 Were outcome data available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? → *No*. Of 99 enrolled, 5 failed to complete the delayed session (3 from CB, 2 from BC, 0 from SC, 0 from FL), and an additional 2 participants (1 BC, 1 CB) were excluded post hoc due to extremely short completion times and outlier-level performance (0% and 8%). Final analytic sample was 94, with attrition/exclusion concentrated entirely in the CB and BC arms.
- 3.2 Is there evidence the result was not biased by missing data? → *No*. No sensitivity analysis or imputation approach was reported to test robustness to the missing/excluded data.
- 3.3 Could missingness depend on the true outcome value? → *Yes*. The two additional post hoc exclusions were explicitly performance-based (participants scoring 3 SD below their group mean), meaning that exclusion decisions were direct functions of the outcome itself.
- 3.4 Is it likely that missingness depended on the true outcome value? → *Yes*. The differential loss (100% of dropouts/exclusions occurring in the CB and BC arms, which are precisely the arms showing the largest performance decline at delayed testing) raises strong concern that the pattern of missingness is related both to intervention group and the true (poor) outcome value, which could have exaggerated the between-group difference in decline that constitutes the study's key finding.

**Judgment rationale:** This directly matches the RoB 2 "High risk" criterion: outcome data were not available for all/nearly all participants, no evidence assuages bias, missingness could depend on the true value, and it is likely that missingness did depend on the true value and differed systematically between the compared arms.

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions:**
- 4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? → *No*. The diagnostic test used validated multiple-choice vignette items with pre-established use in prior studies by the same research group, appropriate for measuring diagnostic accuracy.
- 4.2 Could measurement differ between intervention groups? → *No*. All groups completed the same computer-administered, forced-choice diagnostic test format under identical procedural conditions and time constraints.
- 4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of intervention received? → *Not applicable/No*, in the meaningful sense — the diagnostic outcome is objectively scored (correct/incorrect selection from four options) by the computer program, with no room for subjective assessor judgment, and no human rater who could be influenced by knowledge of group assignment.
- 4.4/4.5 N/A given objective, automatically scored outcome.

**Judgment rationale:** The outcome is an objective, automatically/computer-scored measure with no subjective assessor interpretation, minimizing risk of differential measurement bias by group.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions:**
- 5.1 Was the analysis conducted per a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? → *No/No information*. No trial registration, protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced anywhere in the manuscript.
- 5.2 Was the result selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements? → *No information*, though the diagnostic test appears to be the single pre-planned primary measure.
- 5.3 Was the result selected from multiple eligible analyses? → *Yes/Probably yes*. The Methods section explicitly states: "Appropriate covariates chosen were based on the correlations between performances on the diagnostic and memory test as well as between performance and time per vignette." This confirms that the ANCOVA covariate structure was determined by inspecting associations with the (unblinded) outcome data themselves, rather than being pre-specified. This is a clear, self-reported instance of data-driven model selection that could influence the magnitude/significance of the key interaction effect (the paper's central finding).

**Judgment rationale:** This maps directly onto the RoB 2 "High risk" criterion for signalling question 5.3 — the analytic model (specifically covariate selection in the ANCOVA driving the key interaction result) was explicitly chosen based on the data's own correlational structure rather than a prior plan, creating a meaningful risk that the reported result was favorably selected from multiple possible analytic specifications.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement — **High risk of bias**

**Summary:**
| Domain | Judgment |
|---|---|
| 1. Randomization process | Some concerns |
| 2. Deviations from intended interventions | Low risk |
| 3. Missing outcome data | High risk |
| 4. Measurement of outcome | Low risk |
| 5. Selection of reported result | High risk |

**Rationale for overall judgment:** Per RoB 2 rules, a "High risk of bias" judgement in *any single domain* is sufficient to classify the overall result as High risk of bias. Here, **two** domains (Domain 3 – Missing outcome data, and Domain 5 – Selection of the reported result) were independently judged High risk. The differential, outcome-related attrition/exclusion concentrated in the CB and BC arms (precisely the arms exhibiting the key delayed-performance decline reported as the main finding), combined with explicitly data-driven covariate selection in the primary ANCOVA model, together substantially undermine confidence in the reported group × time interaction effect that anchors the study's central conclusions about causal explanations (SC) versus other integration formats.

**Stated limitation/ambiguity:** The manuscript does not report a registered protocol or statistical analysis plan, precluding verification of whether the ANCOVA model (including covariate selection) was specified prior to data unblinding. Additionally, the manuscript does not report formal statistical comparison of baseline characteristics across the four randomized groups, limiting assessment of randomization integrity. These represent genuine reporting gaps in the source article rather than assumptions made in this assessment.

# ROB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** QEEG indexed frontal connectivity effects of transcranial pulsed current stimulation (tPCS): A sham-controlled mechanistic trial

**Design:** Individually-randomized, double-blinded, sham-controlled, 4-arm parallel trial (sham, 1 Hz, 100 Hz, random 1–5 Hz) in 38 healthy volunteers.

**Outcome assessed:** Interhemispheric theta coherence (primary result highlighted by authors) — effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect).

**Result assessed:** Random frequency vs sham: mean difference in theta coherence = 0.089 points (95% CI 0.01 to 0.1693), p=0.0284.

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

- **Sequence generation:** The paper states "Randomization was completed using blocks of four," implying an allocation sequence with a random component. However, no explicit description of the random number generation method (e.g., computer-generated, random number table) is provided. → Signalling question 1.1: **Probably yes** (block randomization generally implies a random component, but details are absent).
- **Allocation concealment:** No information is provided on how the allocation sequence was concealed from the investigator/enrolling personnel prior to assignment (e.g., no description of sealed envelopes, central randomization, or pharmacy-controlled dispensing). → Signalling question 1.2: **No information**.
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table 1 shows no statistically significant differences in demographic characteristics (age, sex, MMSE) or baseline behavioral task performance across groups. This is compatible with chance. → Signalling question 1.3: **No**.

**Motivation:** Because allocation concealment is not reported and there is no information to confirm it was adequate, combined with reasonable (but not fully detailed) evidence of a random sequence and no evidence of baseline imbalance, this maps to "Some concerns" per the RoB 2 algorithm (No information on concealment + baseline differences compatible with chance = Some concerns).

**Limitation reported:** The article does not specify the method of allocation concealment, which prevents a "Low risk" judgment.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention) — **Low risk**

- **Blinding of participants:** Both participants and outcome assessors were blinded ("double-blinded"); the sham arm received 30 seconds of stimulation before automatic shut-off to mimic active stimulation, a common and adequate blinding strategy for CES/tPCS-type devices. → Signalling question 2.1: **Probably no** (blinding validation analysis showed subjects could not guess assignment beyond chance, p=0.679, supporting successful blinding).
- **Blinding of personnel:** The investigator assessing outcomes was also blinded ("the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention"). → Signalling question 2.2: **Probably no**.
- **Deviations from intended intervention arising from trial context:** No evidence of non-protocol interventions, crossovers, or contamination is reported; this is a single-session laboratory experiment with immediate EEG outcome measurement, minimizing opportunity for such deviations. → 2.3: **No**.
- **Analysis:** All 38 subjects who completed stimulation were analyzed in their assigned groups (with one exclusion for technical/EEG artifact reasons unrelated to intervention assignment or outcome value — pre-specified data quality criterion). This is consistent with an appropriate (ITT-consistent) analysis. → 2.6: **Yes**.

**Motivation:** Successful double-blinding, empirically validated, together with an appropriate analytic approach and no plausible mechanism for context-driven deviations in this single-session mechanistic experiment, supports a low risk of bias judgment for this domain.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Some concerns**

- Of 40 randomized/enrolled subjects, 2 withdrew consent before receiving stimulation and were not analyzed; a further 1 subject (from the Random Frequency group) was excluded post-hoc due to "excessive artifacts in the EEG recording."
- **3.1:** Data were not available for all randomized participants (38/40, and effectively 37/40 analyzed for coherence given the artifact exclusion) → **No/Probably no**.
- **3.2:** No sensitivity analysis was reported to demonstrate that the result is robust to this missing/excluded data → **No**.
- **3.3:** The EEG artifact-based exclusion could plausibly be independent of the true coherence value (technical/recording issue), but this cannot be verified from the report, and the exclusion occurred specifically and only in the Random Frequency group (the group ultimately reported as showing the significant effect), raising some concern about differential handling → **Probably yes**.
- **3.4:** There is no indication that this exclusion was outcome-dependent (e.g., related to poor coherence values) rather than a genuine technical artifact issue, but the report gives no explicit reasoning ruling this out → **Probably no** (insufficient evidence that missingness related to true outcome value, but not fully reassuring either).

**Motivation:** The overall amount of missing/excluded data is small (3/40, or 1/11 in the affected arm), and the reported reason (EEG artifact) is plausible and unrelated to coherence values. However, the lack of any sensitivity analysis, the imbalance in exclusion occurring in the very arm central to the primary reported finding, and absence of a formal ITT reporting framework (no CONSORT flow diagram) prevent a "Low risk" judgment.

**Limitation reported:** No CONSORT-style flow diagram was provided; reasons for the two pre-stimulation withdrawals are not detailed, and no sensitivity analysis for the excluded EEG dataset is presented.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **Low risk**

- **Appropriateness of measurement method:** Theta interhemispheric coherence was computed using magnitude-square coherence via Welch's averaged modified periodogram method, a standard, validated, and quantitative qEEG method. → 4.1: **No**.
- **Comparability of measurement across groups:** The same 64-channel EGI EEG system, same electrode pairs, same recording protocol (eyes open/closed, pre/post), and same analytic pipeline were applied identically across all four groups. → 4.2: **No**.
- **Outcome assessor blinding:** The outcome is a fully automated, objective, computer-derived quantitative signal-processing measure (coherence calculation), not subject to subjective interpretation by a human rater; also, the investigator assessing outcomes was reported blinded. → 4.3: **No** (assessor unaware, and the "assessor" is essentially objective algorithmic quantification).
- **Potential for knowledge of intervention to influence the outcome:** Given the fully automated and objective nature of the qEEG coherence calculation, this outcome type is classified as an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement," meaning knowledge of allocation could not have plausibly influenced the result. → 4.4/4.5: **No/Not applicable**.

**Motivation:** The objective, automated, and consistent methodology used to derive the coherence metric across all groups, combined with confirmed blinding, supports a low risk of bias for this domain.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

- **Pre-specified analysis plan:** No trial registration, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or cited anywhere in the article. There is no way to verify whether the analysis was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. → 5.1: **No information**.
- **Selection among multiple eligible outcome measurements:** The study measured multiple bands (alpha, theta) and four sub-bands (low-alpha, high-alpha, low-beta, high-beta), for both power and coherence, under two conditions (eyes open/closed), and reports the study explicitly as an "exploratory analysis." Ultimately, only theta and low-alpha coherence findings are highlighted as the "main findings," while several non-significant comparisons (e.g., high-alpha, low-beta, high-beta coherence; theta/beta power) are reported but de-emphasized. The explicit self-description as "exploratory" raises the possibility that the reported significant results were highlighted from a larger set of tested comparisons without correction for multiple comparisons. → 5.2: **Probably yes**.
- **Selection among multiple eligible analyses:** Multiple post-hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted (random vs sham, 1Hz vs sham, 100Hz vs sham, and additional exploratory pairwise comparisons such as random vs 1Hz and random vs 100Hz) without clear pre-specification or correction for multiplicity, and results are interpreted narratively as confirmatory despite the exploratory design. → 5.3: **Probably yes**.

**Motivation:** The complete absence of a registered protocol or SAP, combined with the study's self-acknowledged "exploratory" nature, multiple outcome measures/sub-bands tested, and multiple uncorrected post-hoc comparisons from which the significant theta/low-alpha coherence findings were foregrounded, raises credible concern for selective emphasis/reporting bias — although it is not possible to definitively confirm this is a "high risk" scenario absent explicit evidence of the exclusion or non-reporting of unfavorable comparisons.

**Limitation reported:** No trial registration number, protocol, or SAP is cited in the manuscript, precluding verification of pre-specification.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement — **HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Summary of domain judgments:**
1. Randomization process: Some concerns
2. Deviations from intended interventions: Low risk
3. Missing outcome data: Some concerns
4. Measurement of outcome: Low risk
5. Selection of reported result: Some concerns

**Justification:** Per RoB 2 rules, a study is judged "High risk of bias" if it has "some concerns" in multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result, even without any single domain rated "High." In this study, three of five domains (randomization process, missing outcome data, and selection of the reported result) were judged as "Some concerns." Critically, the combination of (a) unverifiable allocation concealment, (b) an unbalanced exclusion of outcome data specifically within the arm reporting the key positive finding without sensitivity analysis, and (c) an explicitly "exploratory" analytic approach involving multiple uncorrected comparisons and no available pre-registered protocol, cumulatively and substantially undermines confidence in the trustworthiness of the primary reported result (increased theta/low-alpha coherence with random-frequency tPCS). Therefore, the overall judgement for this result is **High risk of bias**.

**Overall limitation to report:** This assessment is limited by the absence of a publicly available trial registration, protocol, or statistical analysis plan, which prevented verification of pre-specification of outcomes and analyses; this ambiguity was resolved conservatively in accordance with RoB 2 guidance by treating undocumented aspects as "no information" and factoring the exploratory nature explicitly stated by the authors into the final judgement.

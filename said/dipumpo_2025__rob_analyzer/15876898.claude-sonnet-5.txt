# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Influence of Topiramate on Olanzapine-Related Adiposity in Women (RCT, 10-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled)
**Comparison:** Topiramate (n=25) vs Placebo (n=18)
**Outcome assessed:** Body weight change (primary outcome); also referenced where relevant to other domains
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect), as no per-protocol methodology is described

---

## 1. Bias Arising from the Randomization Process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- The article states "The randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration" but provides no description of the method used to generate the allocation sequence (no mention of random number tables, computer generation, or any random component). This is insufficient to answer "Yes" to sequence randomness with confidence.
- Allocation concealment appears plausible ("carried out confidentially by the clinic administration," tablets "supplied in numbered boxes"), which suggests some attempt at concealment, but no explicit description of the concealment mechanism (e.g., sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed containers, or independent randomization office procedures) is provided.
- Regarding baseline imbalance: Table 1 shows groups reasonably similar in age, height, initial weight, weight gain, and diagnosis distribution. However, the intended allocation was 25 vs 24 (approximately 1:1), but 6 dropouts occurred exclusively from the placebo group, leaving 25 vs 18 in the final analysis — this is a substantial imbalance in final group sizes, though it reflects post-randomization attrition rather than a failure of the randomization process itself (addressed under missing data domain instead).
- No statistical comparison (e.g., p-values) of baseline characteristics between groups is reported, limiting the ability to assess whether imbalances are compatible with chance.

**Conclusion:** Because sequence generation is not described (no information) and allocation concealment procedures are only vaguely described ("confidentially"), and because baseline data - while looking similar - were not formally tested for imbalance, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," per the algorithm (No information on both signalling questions defaults to "Some concerns").

## 2. Bias Due to Deviations from Intended Interventions (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:**
- Both participants and clinicians/personnel were blinded ("double-blind... Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment"), using matching placebo tablets in identical, numbered boxes.
- No evidence is reported of deviations from intended intervention arising because of the trial context (e.g., no reported crossovers, no non-protocol interventions specifically driven by unblinding or trial engagement effects). Concomitant medications and olanzapine doses "remained constant" during the study for both groups, indicating no differential co-intervention.
- The analysis excluded 6 dropouts (all from the placebo group) from the final analysis of 43/49 randomized subjects. This constitutes an analysis that is not a full ITT analysis (it does not include all randomized participants), but it fits the description of a modified ITT (mITT) analysis, since dropouts are due to missing outcome data (failure to appear) rather than for other reasons (deviations in intervention). Per RoB 2 guidance, an mITT excluding those with missing outcome data is considered an acceptable analysis for this domain (bias due to missing data is captured in Domain 3), not deviation from intended intervention.
- There is no indication that participants failed to adhere to their assigned medication or that treatment implementation failed.

**Conclusion:** Given effective blinding of participants and personnel, no evidence of trial-context-driven deviations, and an essentially appropriate (mITT) analytic approach for the ITT effect, this domain is judged as "Low risk."

## 3. Bias Due to Missing Outcome Data

**Judgment: High risk**

**Motivation:**
- Of 49 randomized participants (25 TG + 24 PG), 6 dropped out — "Six subjects who failed to appear for more than one evaluation (all from the placebo group) dropped out of the study, and their data were not further analyzed." This represents 100% of missing data occurring in the placebo arm (6/24 = 25% attrition in placebo vs 0% in topiramate group).
- This is a substantial and highly asymmetric proportion of missing outcome data between the two intervention groups (0% vs 25%), which is a strong signal of potential bias, since the trial result could be highly sensitive to the reasons underlying this differential dropout.
- The reason given ("failed to appear for more than one evaluation") does not exclude the possibility that these withdrawals relate to the true value of the outcome (e.g., participants who did not respond well to placebo, continued gaining weight, or were dissatisfied with lack of improvement may have been more likely to drop out) — a plausible and common phenomenon in trials of adiposity/weight loss interventions where lack of perceived benefit drives withdrawal.
- No sensitivity analyses (e.g., best-worst case scenarios, imputation methods) were performed to assess the robustness of the result to these missing data.
- No further details on individual reasons for dropout are provided beyond "failed to appear," precluding a determination that missingness was unrelated to the outcome.

**Conclusion:** Because outcome data were not available for all or nearly all randomized participants, no evidence exists that the result was robust to missing data, missingness could plausibly depend on the true outcome value, and the differential dropout occurred entirely in one arm — this domain is judged as "High risk of bias."

## 4. Bias in Measurement of the Outcome

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:**
- The primary outcome (body weight) was measured using a presumably objective, standardized method (weighing), which is not a method involving significant assessor judgment and is generally not susceptible to influence by knowledge of intervention assignment when a calibrated scale is used consistently.
- Measurement or ascertainment of the outcome did not differ between the two intervention groups; the assessment schedule ("Every 2 weeks, subjects were seen, weighed and tested...") was identical for both arms.
- Both participants and clinicians remained blinded throughout the 10-week trial ("Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment"), reducing the risk that weight measurement (an outcome assessor-reported outcome with little room for judgment) was influenced by knowledge of allocation.
- For the secondary self-reported outcomes (SF-36, EWL-60-S, Bf-S), these are participant-reported outcomes potentially susceptible to unblinding via side effects (e.g., topiramate-associated dizziness, paresthesia could theoretically unblind some participants); however, the study reports only "isolated cases" of side effects, and no evidence is provided that blinding was compromised or that this affected outcome reporting.

**Conclusion:** Given effective blinding, an objective primary outcome measure (weight), and identical measurement procedures across groups, this domain is judged as "Low risk of bias" for the primary outcome of body weight.

## 5. Bias in Selection of the Reported Result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- No trial registration, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or cited anywhere in the article. There is no way to verify whether the outcome measures and analyses reported (weight, and the 8 SF-36 subscales, 6 EWL-60-S subscales, and Bf-S) correspond to a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded data were available.
- The study reports "intermediate results were not analyzed during the course of the trial," which somewhat mitigates concern about interim-data-driven selection, but this is an author statement without independent verification (e.g., no trial registry date-stamp).
- Multiple outcome measures/scales and subscales were assessed (8 SF-36 domains, 6 EWL-60-S domains, Bf-S), and all are reported with significance testing; there is no evidence of selective non-reporting of specific subscales for the psychometric instruments, as all planned scales appear to be presented in full (Tables 2 and 3).
- For the primary outcome of body weight, only one measurement method (weight in kg) and one analysis approach (Mann-Whitney U test with mean difference and 95% CI) are used; there is no indication of multiple competing analytic approaches from which the reported result might have been selected.
- However, the complete absence of any pre-registration or protocol reference prevents a "Low risk" judgment, as it cannot be confirmed that outcome measurements/analyses were not selected on the basis of results.

**Conclusion:** Due to lack of information regarding pre-specification of the analysis plan (no protocol/registry cited), but with no overt evidence of selective reporting among multiple eligible outcomes/analyses, this domain is judged as "Some concerns."

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall Judgment: High risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
Per the RoB 2 algorithm, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted because:
1. **Domain 3 (Missing Outcome Data)** was independently judged as "High risk of bias" due to substantial, entirely one-sided attrition (25% dropout in the placebo group vs 0% in the topiramate group), with no sensitivity analyses to assess robustness and plausible outcome-dependent reasons for dropout.
2. Per RoB 2 rules, "The study is judged to be at high risk of bias in at least one domain for this result" is sufficient to classify the overall result as "High risk of bias," regardless of favorable judgments in other domains (Domains 2 and 4 were "Low risk"; Domains 1 and 5 were "Some concerns").

**Note on limitations of this assessment:** This judgment is based solely on the information reported in the published article. No trial protocol, registration record, or statistical analysis plan was available for cross-verification, which limited definitive assessment of Domains 1 (randomization process details) and 5 (selective reporting). These ambiguities are reported as limitations rather than assumptions, in accordance with RoB 2 methodology. Should such supplementary documentation become available, judgments for these domains may be revised.

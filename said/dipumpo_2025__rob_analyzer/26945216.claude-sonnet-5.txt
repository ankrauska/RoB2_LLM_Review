# ROB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Comparison of TENS vs IFT (both plus standard care) vs standard care alone in Myofascial Pain Syndrome (upper trapezius). Outcomes assessed: Pain intensity (VAS) and Cervical ROM, at multiple time points up to 1 week post-treatment.
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (ITT-type, all 105 randomized participants completed and were analyzed).

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

- **Sequence generation:** Reported as "computerized block randomization" — this indicates an adequate random component (Signalling Q1.1: Yes).
- **Allocation concealment:** Authors state "allocation group concealed in envelopes" but do not specify whether envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, or sealed. This lacks the detail required by the minimal criteria (Table 2) for a judgement of adequate concealment (Signalling Q1.2: No information/Probably no).
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table 1 shows a statistically significant difference in "affected side" (right vs left trapezius) between groups (χ²=5.952, P=0.015), which is unlikely to be a key prognostic factor for outcome and is unlikely to bias the effect materially. Age, sex, and baseline pain/ROM were balanced (all P>0.05).
- **Critical concern:** The principal investigator both administered treatments and performed randomization-related enrollment procedures, raising a possibility that concealment could have been compromised, but this is not explicitly reported, only inferable.

**Motivation:** Because allocation concealment is inadequately described (no detail on envelope characteristics, sequential numbering, opacity, or sealing) and there is no information proving that concealment was successfully maintained, despite the sequence being adequately random, this domain is judged as **Some concerns**, per the algorithm (Table 4: random=Yes, concealment=No information, baseline imbalance=No/minor → Some concerns).

**Limitation noted:** The paper does not elaborate on the method of concealment beyond stating "concealed in envelopes," creating ambiguity that could not be resolved from the text.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention) — **High risk of bias**

- **Blinding of participants:** Not possible — this was explicitly a "single-blind" trial; participants were aware of which physical modality (TENS, IFT, or none) they received, since these are physically distinguishable interventions (Signalling Q2.1: Yes).
- **Blinding of personnel/therapist:** The same principal investigator delivered **all** interventions and was **not blinded**; this same individual also served as the outcome assessor (explicitly stated: "The principal investigator acted as both outcome assessor... and as therapist administering all study treatment") (Signalling Q2.2: Yes).
- **Deviations from intended intervention due to trial context:** Because the sole investigator delivering treatment and taking outcome measurements was aware of group assignment and was not using a placebo device/control for the standard-care-only arm, there is a substantive risk that enthusiasm, expectation, differential encouragement, or subtle variability in delivery (e.g., during myofascial release, quality/duration of hot pack, verbal encouragement, or exercise instruction) systematically differed between groups. The authors themselves acknowledge in the discussion: "carrying out the clinical procedures and measurements by the principal investigator and not using a placebo for the patients of the control group could be considered as limitations of the present study" (Signalling Q2.3: Yes, deviations likely arose from trial context — lack of equipoise/lack of blinding of the treating clinician).
- **Effect on outcome & balance:** These deviations plausibly affected the outcome measures (both self-reported pain via VAS and clinician-measured ROM via goniometer, both of which have subjective components) and were **not balanced** across groups, since the non-blinded therapist knew which "active" modality (or none) was being delivered (Signalling Q2.4/2.5: Yes/Unbalanced).
- **Analysis:** All 105 randomized participants were analyzed in their randomized groups (ITT-consistent, no dropouts) (Signalling Q2.6: Yes — appropriate).

**Motivation:** Despite an appropriate analysis (all participants retained), the combination of (a) unblinded participants, (b) an unblinded single treating/assessing therapist, (c) no placebo/sham control for the control arm, and (d) explicit author acknowledgment of this limitation, indicates that deviations from intended intervention arising from trial context were likely, affected the outcome, and were not balanced between groups. Per Table 6 (Part 1), this maps to **High risk of bias**.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Low risk of bias**

- All 105 participants who were randomized (35 per group) completed the study and were included in the final analysis, as depicted in the CONSORT flow chart (Figure 3) and confirmed in the Results ("All completed the study and were entered into the analysis").
- There were no dropouts, exclusions, or missing measurements at any of the four assessment time points (baseline, immediately after first treatment, before eighth treatment, 1 week after eighth treatment).

**Motivation:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants (Signalling Q3.1: Yes), satisfying the criterion for low risk of bias in this domain regardless of further questions.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **High risk of bias**

- **Appropriateness of measurement method:** The methods used (100-mm VAS for pain, universal goniometer for ROM) are validated and appropriate tools (Signalling Q4.1: No).
- **Comparability of measurement across groups:** Measurement procedures were identical in timing and method across groups (Signalling Q4.2: No).
- **Blinding of outcome assessor:** The principal investigator, who was aware of participants' treatment allocation (since she personally administered the differing treatments), was **also the sole outcome assessor** for both pain (VAS, a participant-reported outcome but recorded/administered by the assessor) and ROM (an observer-reported outcome involving judgement — goniometric measurement requires positioning and reading, which can be influenced by expectation) (Signalling Q4.3: Yes — assessor aware).
- **Potential for influence:** Both outcome types (self-reported pain and clinician-measured ROM requiring manual goniometer placement/reading) are susceptible to conscious or unconscious influence by an unblinded outcome assessor, particularly one who also personally delivered the intervention and thus had direct expectations regarding relative efficacy of TENS vs. IFT vs. control (Signalling Q4.4: Yes).
- **Likelihood of actual influence:** Given (a) the assessor's dual role as therapist and outcome assessor, (b) no independent/blinded assessor was used, (c) the study's own hypothesis-driven framing favoring active modalities, and (d) the subjective/judgement-based nature of goniometric ROM measurement, it is likely that assessment was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received (Signalling Q4.5: Yes).

**Motivation:** The combination of an unblinded, single assessor who was also the treating therapist, applied to outcomes with a judgement component, and no corroborating blinded assessment, leads to a judgement of **High risk of bias** for this domain (Table 12: aware=Yes, could be influenced=Yes, likely influenced=Yes → High).

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

- No trial protocol, prospectively registered statistical analysis plan (SAP), or detailed pre-specified outcome list (beyond registry title) was referenced or made available in the article beyond a mention that the study "was registered as a clinical trial with the Sri Lanka Clinical Trial Registry." No registry number, date of registration, or accessible protocol is provided in the text to verify whether outcome measures/analyses were pre-specified before unblinded data were available (Signalling Q5.1: No information).
- Outcomes reported (VAS pain, three specific cervical ROM measures at four time points) appear reasonably comprehensive and consistently reported for all groups and time points without evidence of selective omission of specific measured domains or scales. There is no indication of multiple alternative scales/outcomes being measured but selectively unreported (Signalling Q5.2 & Q5.3: Probably no, but cannot be verified against a registered protocol).

**Motivation:** Because there is no accessible trial registry entry, protocol, or SAP referenced with sufficient detail to confirm that the analyses reported correspond exactly to pre-specified plans finalized before unblinded data were available, but there is also no overt evidence of selective outcome/analysis reporting, this domain is judged as **Some concerns** (per Table 14: no information on pre-specification with no evidence of selection from multiple outcomes/analyses → Some concerns).

**Limitation noted:** The absence of an accessible/verifiable trial registration record or protocol in the manuscript prevents a definitive judgement of low risk in this domain.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement — **High Risk of Bias**

**Summary of domain judgements:**
1. Randomization process: Some concerns
2. Deviations from intended interventions: **High risk**
3. Missing outcome data: Low risk
4. Measurement of the outcome: **High risk**
5. Selection of the reported result: Some concerns

**Overall justification:** According to RoB 2 rules, a judgement of **High risk of bias** in any single domain is sufficient to classify the overall result as **High risk of bias**. In this study, two domains (Domain 2 — deviations from intended interventions, and Domain 4 — measurement of the outcome) were independently judged as High risk, driven principally by the fact that the same unblinded principal investigator served simultaneously as treatment administrator and outcome assessor, with no placebo/sham control and no independent blinded assessment. This single methodological feature undermines confidence in the trial's results for both outcomes assessed (pain intensity and cervical ROM). Combined with "Some concerns" in the randomization and outcome-reporting domains, the overall risk of bias for this result is **High**.

Based on a detailed assessment of the provided article using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool, here is the structured Risk of Bias (RoB) report for the effect of n-3 fatty acid supplementation on plasma F2-isoprostanes (and other lipid peroxidation markers):

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study states that participants were "randomly assigned" to the background diets and subsequently to the fish-oil or placebo capsules. However, the report lacks specific details on the method of random sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated) and whether the allocation sequence was adequately concealed (e.g., central randomization or opaque sealed envelopes). Although Table 1 shows no significant baseline imbalances, the absence of methodological details regarding the randomization and concealment processes warrants a judgment of "Some concerns."

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study used placebo capsules containing olive oil to match the fish-oil capsules, suggesting an effort to blind participants. However, the report does not explicitly state whether the trial was double-blinded (i.e., whether the personnel delivering the intervention and the trial investigators were blinded). Additionally, there is no explicit mention of an intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis or how adherence was monitored and accounted for in the statistical models, raising some concerns about potential deviations from the intended interventions.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** For the primary marker 8-iso-PGF2a, the authors explicitly state that samples from two centers (Denmark and Italy) could not be analyzed due to "inappropriate sample handling and/or storage." While this constitutes missing outcome data, the reason for missingness (logistical/handling error) is clearly unrelated to the true value of the outcomes. For the other markers (e.g., 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2a), data from all centers were analyzed. Therefore, the missingness does not introduce systematic bias.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The outcomes were measured using validated, objective laboratory methods (RIA and ELISA assays) on plasma samples. Although it is not explicitly stated whether the laboratory technicians were blinded to the intervention assignments, the automated and objective nature of these biochemical assays makes it highly unlikely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** There is no reference to a publicly available, pre-specified trial protocol or statistical analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded data were available. While the reported outcomes align with those described in the methods section and there is no obvious evidence of selective reporting of multiple measurements or analyses, the absence of a pre-registered protocol makes it impossible to definitively rule out selective reporting.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "Some concerns" in multiple domains (Domain 1: Randomization process; Domain 2: Deviations from intended interventions; Domain 5: Selection of the reported result). Because these concerns are primarily due to a lack of reporting details rather than clear evidence of flawed methodology, they do not individually or collectively warrant a "High risk of bias" judgment. Thus, the overall judgment remains at "Some concerns."

## RoB 2 assessment

**Study:** *Comparison of the Effectiveness of Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation and Interferential Therapy on the Upper Trapezius in Myofascial Pain Syndrome: A Randomized Controlled Study.*

**Design:** Individually randomized, three-arm, parallel-group trial.

**Result assessed:** Effect of **assignment to TENS plus standard care versus IFT plus standard care** on **patient-reported pain intensity (0–100 mm visual analogue scale) at 1 week after the eighth treatment** (4-week intervention plus 1-week follow-up). The reported mean reduction from baseline was 45.3 mm (SD 8.4) in the TENS group and 31.8 mm (SD 6.2) in the IFT group; the report states that the TENS group had a significantly larger reduction than the IFT group.

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect).

**Sources available for this assessment:** The supplied journal article, including its tables and CONSORT flow-chart description. The article states that the trial was registered in the Sri Lanka Clinical Trial Registry, but no registry number, protocol, statistical analysis plan, or registry record was supplied; therefore these sources could not be used to verify pre-specification.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states that participants were allocated using “**computerized block randomization**.” This describes a random allocation process.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — Probably yes.**  
  The article states that the “**allocation group [was] concealed in envelops [envelopes]**.” It does not report whether envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, opened sequentially, or opened only after irrevocable enrolment. Nevertheless, the report expressly describes allocation concealment using envelopes and does not report evidence that recruiters could foresee assignments.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process? — Probably no.**  
  Groups had equal numbers randomized (35 in each group), consistent with the planned allocation. Baseline pain intensity and all baseline cervical range-of-motion measures were reported as not statistically significantly different between groups. Age and sex were also reported as not significantly different. There was a statistically significant difference in affected side across groups (P = 0.015), but the report does not establish that affected side was a key prognostic factor for the assessed pain outcome, nor does a single imbalance establish a failure of randomization.

**Motivation:**  
Computerized block randomization supports a random sequence. Allocation concealment was reported, albeit with insufficient operational detail to provide the strongest assurance of concealment. The equal group sizes and the reported baseline data do not show an imbalance pattern that clearly indicates a compromised randomization process. Therefore, for the assessed result, the domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — No.**  
  The report states: “**The participants were blind to the allocation to treatment groups after randomization**.”

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The principal investigator administered all interventions. TENS and IFT involved different electrode arrangements and different electrical-current delivery procedures. The therapist necessarily knew which modality was being administered.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The article describes a predefined treatment schedule: eight clinic sessions over four weeks, standard care for all groups, and TENS or IFT according to allocation. It reports that all 105 participants completed the study. It does not report protocol-inconsistent co-interventions, crossovers, failures to deliver allocated modality, or deviations caused by knowledge of allocation.

- **2.4–2.5 — Not applicable.**  
  Since no deviations arising from the trial context were identified, their possible impact and balance between groups were not further assessed.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  The report states that all 105 enrolled participants completed the study and were entered into the analysis. Each randomized group retained 35 participants. Outcomes were compared between the randomized groups using analysis of variance and Tukey post-hoc tests. No as-treated analysis, per-protocol exclusion, or post-randomization removal of eligible participants is reported.

**Motivation:**  
Although the treating investigator was aware of allocation, the article does not report trial-context deviations from the intended interventions that could have biased the comparison. All randomized participants were reported to have completed follow-up and been analysed in their assigned groups. For the effect of assignment to TENS plus standard care versus IFT plus standard care, the analysis is consistent with an intention-to-treat comparison. This domain is therefore judged at **low risk of bias**.

- **3.2–3.4 — Not applicable.**

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  Pain intensity at rest was measured using a 100-mm visual analogue scale with explicit anchors: “no pain at all” and “worst pain imaginable.” The report identifies this as the pain measure and cites a reference concerning visual analogue scale reliability. Nothing in the report indicates that the measurement scale was unsuitable for measuring pain intensity.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  The same visual analogue scale was used in all groups at baseline, immediately after treatment one, before treatment eight, and one week after treatment eight. The article does not describe different pain-assessment methods, thresholds, or measurement time points between groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants? — No.**  
  Pain intensity was a participant-reported outcome. The report states that participants were blinded to treatment-group allocation after randomization.

- **4.4–4.5 — Not applicable.**  
  Because the participant, who was the outcome assessor for this self-reported pain outcome, was stated to be blinded to group allocation, assessment bias arising from knowledge of intervention received was not identified.

**Motivation:**  
The outcome was measured using the same specified visual analogue scale at the same time points across groups. For this patient-reported pain outcome, the relevant outcome assessor was the participant. The article explicitly states that participants were blinded to allocation. Therefore, based on what is reported, measurement of the pain outcome was unlikely to have been differentially influenced by knowledge of intervention received. This domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

**Assessment limitation:**  
The article does not describe how participant blinding was maintained despite the different electrode configurations and stimulation procedures used for TENS and IFT, and it does not report a formal assessment of the success of blinding. This absence is a reporting limitation; however, the article explicitly reports that participants were blinded, and no evidence is reported that blinding failed.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis? — No information.**  
  The article states that the study was registered in the Sri Lanka Clinical Trial Registry, but neither a registry identifier nor the registry record is reported in the supplied material. No protocol and no date-stamped statistical analysis plan are available. Therefore, it cannot be determined whether the reported pain analysis at one-week follow-up was specified before unblinded outcome data became available.

- **5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — Probably no.**  
  Pain intensity was measured at baseline and at three stated post-baseline time points: immediately after the first treatment, before the eighth treatment, and one week after the eighth treatment. Table 2 reports the pain results for each of these time points. Thus, the supplied report presents the results across the reported follow-up measurements rather than reporting only the one-week result.

- **5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The report presents baseline pain values and change-from-baseline analyses using one-way ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc tests. However, without a protocol or pre-specified statistical analysis plan, it is not possible to establish whether other eligible analyses—for example, analyses of post-treatment pain values rather than change scores, analyses adjusted for baseline pain, repeated-measures analyses, or alternative handling of the repeated assessments—were planned or performed and not reported.

**Motivation:**  
No direct evidence establishes that the one-week pain result was selected because it was favourable to TENS. However, the absence of an accessible, date-stamped protocol or statistical analysis plan prevents confirmation that the chosen analysis was pre-specified and that selection from multiple eligible analytical approaches did not occur. Under RoB 2, this creates **some concerns** in this domain rather than high risk, because selective reporting is not demonstrated.

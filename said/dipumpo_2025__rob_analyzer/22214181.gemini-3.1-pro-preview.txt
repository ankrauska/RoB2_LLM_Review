Based on a detailed analysis of the provided article using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool, here is the structured Risk of Bias (ROB) assessment for the study.

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
**Judgment:** Some concerns
**Motivation:** The authors state that "a Microsoft Excel random number generator was used to randomize participants into the two conditions." However, there is no information provided regarding allocation sequence concealment (e.g., whether the sequence was concealed from the investigator enrolling the participants until the moment of assignment). Although baseline characteristics were well-balanced between the groups, the lack of detail on allocation concealment raises some concerns that the person enrolling participants could have foreseen the upcoming assignment.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
**Judgment:** Low risk of bias
**Motivation:** The intervention consisted of reading an information sheet. Although the investigator and participants were not blinded to the information being read, there is no evidence that any deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. The intervention was delivered in a single 1-hour session, making non-adherence or significant deviations from the protocol highly unlikely to affect the outcome. 

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
**Judgment:** Low risk of bias
**Motivation:** Data were available for nearly all randomized participants. Out of the initial pool, only five participants were excluded (two due to age, one missing demographics, and two for incomplete outcome measures). This represents a very small fraction of the total sample, and such a small amount of missing data is highly unlikely to bias the intervention effect estimate.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
**Judgment:** Some concerns
**Motivation:** The primary outcome (the List of Attributions Task) is a participant-reported measure. Participants were necessarily aware of the information they had just received (either fatigue information or sleep information). While the independent coder of the written responses was blinded to the study hypotheses and participant allocation, the participants themselves (the initial outcome assessors for self-reported measures) were not blinded to their intervention. Because the outcome is highly subjective and asks directly about the topics just discussed in the intervention, there is some concern that participants' responses could have been influenced by their knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
**Judgment:** Some concerns
**Motivation:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial registry entry finalized before unblinded data were available. While the reported analyses align with the described methodology for scoring the LAT (NumberAttributionScore, FrequencyScore, ImportanceScore), the absence of a date-stamped, pre-registered protocol means it is not possible to definitively rule out that the reported outcome measurements or analyses were selected based on the results.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
**Judgment:** Some concerns
**Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "Some concerns" overall. While the study demonstrates low risk in missing outcome data and deviations from intended interventions, it raises some concerns in the domains of the randomization process (due to unreported allocation concealment), measurement of the outcome (due to unblinded participant-reported subjective outcomes), and selection of the reported result (due to the lack of a pre-registered analysis plan). Because no single domain was judged as having a high risk of bias, but multiple domains raise some concerns, the overall confidence in the result is moderately lowered but not entirely compromised.

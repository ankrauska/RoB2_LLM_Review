Based on a thorough analysis of the provided article and the trial protocol (NCT00668343), here is the detailed Risk of Bias (RoB 2) assessment for the primary outcome (total attack/relapse rate). The assessment evaluates the effect of assignment to the intervention (the ‘intention-to-treat’ effect).

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study used an adequate method for random sequence generation, stating that "A computer-based blocking method (four blocks) was used for the randomization". Allocation concealment was achieved as "Separate physicians were assigned to randomly allocate patients" and "all medications were given in similar packages". Baseline characteristics (Table 1) were balanced between the simvastatin and placebo groups, indicating no apparent problems with the randomization process. 

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The trial was double-blinded. The report states that patients were unaware of their group assignment, medications were dispensed in identical packages, and the evaluating neurologists were blinded. Because participants, carers, and personnel were successfully blinded, deviations from intended interventions arising from the trial context are unlikely. Furthermore, the authors state that "analyses were performed according to intention-to-treat methods".

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** Of the 85 randomized patients, 14 withdrew from the study (16.5% dropout rate). The reasons for missing outcome data were directly related to the outcome of interest: 6 patients withdrew because they experienced two relapses (the primary endpoint), and 1 due to chronic exacerbation. Although the authors state they used intention-to-treat and Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) methods, LOCF does not adequately correct for bias when data are Missing Not At Random (MNAR). Because missingness depended on the true value of the outcome (experiencing relapses), this raises some concerns, although the absolute number of missing patients was relatively small and relatively balanced (although slightly higher in the placebo group).

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The methods of measuring the outcome (clinical evaluation of relapses by neurologists and MRI scans evaluated by radiologists) were appropriate. Assessors were strictly blinded to the intervention assignment, as explicitly stated: "Four neurologists were asked to... perform the follow-up examinations. They were blinded to the patients’ assignment". Therefore, it is highly unlikely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The reported data correspond exactly to the pre-specified primary outcome in the provided trial registry protocol (NCT00668343), which is the "attack number" at 1 year. There is no evidence that the reported clinical or MRI results were selected from multiple eligible measurements or multiple analysis methods based on the statistical significance of the findings.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "some concerns" for the primary outcome result due to the domain of missing outcome data (Domain 3), as a notable portion of patient withdrawals was explicitly driven by the occurrence of the primary outcome (relapses/exacerbations). All other domains were judged to be at low risk of bias. According to the RoB 2 rules, a study with some concerns in at least one domain, but no high risk in any domain, receives an overall judgment of "Some concerns".

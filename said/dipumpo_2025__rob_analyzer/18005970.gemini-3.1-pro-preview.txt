Based on a thorough analysis of the provided article using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool guidelines, here is the structured Risk of Bias assessment:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
*   **Judgment:** Some concerns
*   **Motivation:** The study states that students were randomly allocated using a "randomisation table" (Signalling question 1.1: Yes). Baseline characteristics between the trial and control groups within each campus showed no significant differences, suggesting no obvious problems with the randomization process (1.3: No). However, there is absolutely no information provided regarding whether the allocation sequence was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions (1.2: No information). Because the allocation concealment is unclear, the algorithm defaults to "Some concerns."

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** Due to the nature of the educational intervention, both participants (students) and personnel (tutors) were aware of the assigned intervention (2.1 and 2.2: Yes). However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context (2.3: Probably no). The teaching practices were standardized for the trial, and the data analysis included all 144 eligible and randomized students, representing a true Intention-To-Treat (ITT) analysis with no exclusions (2.6: Yes). Therefore, the lack of blinding did not lead to a risk of material bias.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** Data for the diagnostic reasoning outcomes were available for all participants randomized. A total of 144 students were eligible and randomized (82 in Trento, 62 in Udine), and the results in the tables and flowchart demonstrate that exactly 144 students were evaluated at Phase 1 and Phase 2. There was zero attrition or missing data (3.1: Yes).

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The method of measuring the outcome (simulated case evaluation) was appropriate and identical across all groups (4.1 and 4.2: No). Crucially, the outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention received. The text explicitly states, "Two researchers did blind evaluation of the problems individualised by the students and these were compared to the ‘standard’" (4.3: No). Because the assessors were blinded, the assessment could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
*   **Judgment:** High risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** There is no indication that the study was analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available (5.1: No information). Furthermore, there is clear evidence of data-driven analytical choices. In Section 4.2.1, the authors state: "The median values of error in Phases 1 and 2 were regarded as reference 'cut-off' points to estimate the differences within campuses and between the groups." Dichotomizing a continuous outcome (number of errors) based on the sample's median *after* seeing the data implies that the numerical result being assessed was selected from multiple eligible analyses (continuous vs. various categorical cut-points) on the basis of the results (5.3: Yes).

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
*   **Judgment:** High risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** According to the RoB 2 mapping rules, if a study is judged to be at "High risk of bias" in at least one domain for a result, the overall risk of bias must be judged as High. This trial is at high risk of bias in Domain 5 (Selection of the reported result) due to the retrospective, data-driven categorization of the primary outcome (using sample medians as cut-offs), which substantially lowers confidence in the reported odds ratios. Additionally, there are "Some concerns" regarding the randomization process (Domain 1).

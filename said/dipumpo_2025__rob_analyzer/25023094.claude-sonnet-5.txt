# RISK OF BIAS 2 (RoB 2) ASSESSMENT
**Study:** "Efficacy of Therapeutic Ultrasound in Pain and Joint Mobility in Whiplash Traumatic Acute and Subacute Phases"
**Design:** Individually randomized, two-arm, placebo-controlled parallel trial (blinded participants and blinded outcome assessor)
**Outcome assessed:** Pain (VAS/algometer) and joint mobility (inclinometer) at discharge (post 25 sessions / 20 days after US completion) — the result reported as statistically significant
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (the authors state "the analysis was done by intention to treat")

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **SOME CONCERNS**

- **Sequence generation:** The paper states, "the distribution of subjects to the two treatment groups was performed using sex-stratified randomization." A random component is implied, but the actual method used to generate the random sequence (e.g., computer-generated numbers, random number table) is not described. Answer: *Probably yes*.
- **Allocation concealment:** "the allocation sequence was blinded through the use of sealed envelopes." No further detail is given on whether envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, or tamper-proof (minimal criteria for adequate concealment per RoB2 Table 2). Answer: *Probably yes*, but with residual uncertainty.
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table 1 reports no statistically significant baseline differences (all p>0.05), although several mobility variables approached significance (extension p=0.054; right side bending p=0.060; left side bending p=0.050). These are compatible with chance and the authors explicitly justify this. Answer: *No*.
- **Judgement rationale:** Because the allocation concealment method (sealed envelopes) lacks the specific procedural detail (sequential numbering, opacity, irreversible assignment) required for full confidence, and because the sequence-generation method within strata is not explicitly described, this domain cannot be rated "Low." No evidence indicates the randomization was subverted, so it is not "High." → **Some concerns.**

**Ambiguity/Limitation:** The manuscript does not specify how the random sequence was generated within the sex-stratified design, nor whether the sealed envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, and opened only after irreversible participant assignment.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention) — **SOME CONCERNS**

- **Blinding of participants:** Explicitly described as blinded ("blinding of patients"); the control group received sham (inactive) ultrasound, indistinguishable from active US. Answer to Q2.1: *No* (unaware).
- **Blinding of personnel/carers:** The authors explicitly state as a limitation: *"The blinding of the physiotherapist who applied the treatment was not possible."* Answer to Q2.2: *Yes* (aware).
- **Deviations arising from trial context:** No information is provided on whether the unblinded treating physiotherapist introduced non-protocol interventions or differential implementation between groups. Given detailed standardized protocols (identical massage/exercise sequences, only ultrasound on/off differing), no explicit evidence of contextual deviation is reported, but this cannot be confirmed. Answer to Q2.3: *No information*.
- **Analysis:** The article explicitly states, "The analysis was done by intention to treat," and there were zero dropouts, supporting an appropriate ITT analysis. Answer to Q2.6: *Yes*.
- **Judgement rationale:** Awareness of intervention by the treating (non-blinded) personnel, combined with no information on whether this awareness led to protocol deviations affecting the outcome, produces at least "Some concerns" per the RoB2 algorithm (Q2.1/2.2 = Yes/NI → default "Some concerns"), even though the appropriate ITT analysis mitigates the concern. → **Some concerns.**

**Ambiguity/Limitation:** The report does not describe whether the unblinded physiotherapist's awareness of allocation influenced delivery of non-US components (massage intensity, exercise supervision, verbal encouragement), which could differentially affect the outcome.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **LOW RISK**

- The manuscript explicitly states: *"During the study no patient was lost or dropped out."*
- All 54 randomized participants (27 per group) appear to have complete data for all time points (pre-test, session 5, session 10, discharge) as shown in Tables 1–3.
- Answer to Q3.1: *Yes* (data available for all participants).
- **Judgement rationale:** Complete outcome data for all randomized participants eliminates the possibility of bias due to missing outcome data. → **Low risk.**

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **LOW RISK**

- **Appropriateness of measurement method:** VAS, algometer, and inclinometer are validated, standard instruments for pain and mobility assessment in musculoskeletal literature. Answer to Q4.1: *No* (not inappropriate).
- **Comparability of measurement across groups:** Both groups underwent identical assessment procedures and timing (pre-test, 6th, 11th, and 25th sessions), by the same appraiser method for both arms. Answer to Q4.2: *No*.
- **Blinding of outcome assessor:** The paper explicitly states the study had a "blind assessor," and this assessor was different from the treating physiotherapist ("A physiotherapist appraiser assessed the patients' pain..."; "another physiotherapist executed the interventions"). Answer to Q4.3: *No* (assessor unaware).
- Additionally, because participants themselves were blinded (VAS is a participant-reported outcome), self-report of pain is unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of allocation.
- **Judgement rationale:** Both the independent outcome assessor and the participants were blinded to intervention allocation, and the measurement methods and timing were identical between groups. → **Low risk.**

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **SOME CONCERNS**

- No trial registration record, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or cited anywhere in the manuscript. It is therefore not possible to verify whether the reported outcome measurements/analyses were pre-specified before unblinded data were available. Answer to Q5.1: *No information*.
- Positively, the study reports **all** pre-defined outcome variables (VAS, 4-point algometry, 6-direction inclinometry) at **all** three assessed time points (post-10-session, discharge) in full, in Tables 1–3, with no apparent omissions, unusual composite constructions, or selective sub-scale reporting. This reduces (but does not eliminate) concern of selective outcome/analysis reporting. Answers to Q5.2/Q5.3: *No information* (cannot be verified against a pre-specified plan, but no internal evidence of selection is apparent).
- **Judgement rationale:** Absence of a registered protocol/SAP to confirm that reported analyses match pre-specified plans, combined with no information to rule out selective reporting, defaults to "Some concerns" per the RoB2 algorithm (Table 14: any answer for 5.1 combined with "No information" for 5.2/5.3 → Some concerns). → **Some concerns.**

**Ambiguity/Limitation:** No trial registry entry, protocol, or SAP was cited in the article, precluding verification of whether the analyses/outcomes reported were pre-specified or selected post hoc based on results.

---

## 6. OVERALL RISK-OF-BIAS JUDGEMENT — **HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Summary of domain-level judgements:**

| Domain | Judgement |
|---|---|
| 1. Randomization process | Some concerns |
| 2. Deviations from intended interventions | Some concerns |
| 3. Missing outcome data | Low risk |
| 4. Measurement of the outcome | Low risk |
| 5. Selection of the reported result | Some concerns |

**Rationale for overall judgement:**
No single domain was rated "High risk of bias" in isolation. However, per the RoB 2 framework, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted when a study is rated "Some concerns" in **multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result**. In this study:

1. The randomization process lacks critical procedural detail (sequence-generation method, envelope security features);
2. The treating physiotherapist was explicitly unblinded, with no information available on whether this influenced non-ultrasound components of care administered differentially between groups; and
3. No trial registration, protocol, or statistical analysis plan is available to confirm that the reported outcomes/analyses were not selectively chosen based on results.

The convergence of these three "Some concerns" domains — spanning the foundational integrity of randomization, the conduct of the intervention, and the transparency of outcome reporting — cumulatively undermines confidence in the reported treatment effect (particularly the key discharge-phase pain finding), despite the reassuring low-risk ratings for missing data and outcome measurement (attributable to good blinding of participants/assessors and zero attrition).

**Final judgement: High risk of bias.**

*Note: This assessment is based solely on the information reported in the published article. No trial protocol, registry record, or statistical analysis plan was available for cross-checking; where information was insufficient to make a definitive judgement, this has been explicitly flagged as a limitation within each domain rather than assumed.*

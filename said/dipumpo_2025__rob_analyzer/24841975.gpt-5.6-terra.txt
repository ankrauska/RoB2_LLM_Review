## Scope of this RoB 2 assessment

- **Study design:** Individually randomized, parallel-group, open-label phase III trial.  
- **Comparison:** Elacytarabine versus investigator-selected salvage treatment (selected before randomization).  
- **Result assessed:** Primary outcome, **overall survival (OS)** in the intention-to-treat population: HR 0.97 (95% CI 0.77–1.22), median OS 3.5 versus 3.3 months.  
- **Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment** to elacytarabine versus assignment to investigator’s preselected control treatment.  
- **Sources available for this assessment:** The supplied journal article, including its tables and figures. Although a ClinicalTrials.gov identifier is reported, no registry record, protocol, or statistical analysis plan was supplied for direct examination.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states that eligible patients “were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System.” Randomization used blocks of four patients within strata defined by prior treatment intensity and relapse/refractory status. This is a random allocation approach.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — Yes.**  
  Assignment occurred through a centralized Interactive Web Response System after eligibility assessment. This is a centrally administered allocation method and supports concealment from enrolling personnel up to assignment.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  Most baseline characteristics were comparable between groups. There was a statistically significant imbalance in the proportion aged 70 years or older: 30% in the elacytarabine group versus 18% in control (P = .007). However, the groups had nearly identical total sample sizes (191 versus 190), and the other reported baseline differences did not demonstrate a pattern of substantial imbalance. A single observed difference among numerous baseline comparisons does not, by itself, demonstrate a failure of randomization under RoB 2. The authors also reported an age-adjusted OS HR of 0.98, similar to the unadjusted HR of 0.97.

**Motivation for judgement**  
The randomized sequence was generated and implemented centrally, allocation was concealed, and the available baseline data do not show an imbalance pattern that indicates manipulation or failure of randomization. The imbalance in age ≥70 years is noted but is not sufficient, on the evidence reported, to indicate a problematic randomization process.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The study is explicitly described as open-label.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  As an open-label trial involving visibly distinct treatment strategies, carers and treating personnel were aware of assigned treatment.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report identifies six participants in the control arm who received treatment differing from the investigator’s pre-specified choice. However, it does not state that these departures arose because of the trial context, knowledge of treatment allocation, or a systematic failure to implement the protocol. The report also states that 95% of patients receiving one treatment course and 77% receiving a second course received at least 80% of assigned dose(s). No differential, trial-context-related co-interventions or protocol deviations likely to affect OS are reported.

- **2.4 and 2.5 — Not applicable.**  
  There is no reported evidence of deviations arising because of the trial context that would require assessment of their likely effect on OS or their balance between groups.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  The primary analysis was performed in the stated intention-to-treat population of all 381 randomized patients: 191 allocated to elacytarabine and 190 allocated to investigator’s choice. OS was analysed by Kaplan–Meier methods, log-rank testing, and Cox regression. Participants were analysed according to randomized assignment.

- **2.7 — Not applicable.**  
  The reported primary analysis was appropriate for the effect of assignment.

**Motivation for judgement**  
Although the trial was open-label, the outcome assessed is OS and the report does not identify unbalanced deviations arising from the trial context that would plausibly alter this outcome. The few reported departures from preselected control therapy do not establish a systematic or trial-context-related problem. Most importantly, the primary OS result was based on the full randomized intention-to-treat population, preserving the randomized comparison.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Yes.**  
  All 381 randomized participants were included in the ITT population. At the analysis cutoff, 302 deaths had occurred. Of the remainder, 74 participants were alive and censored administratively at the cutoff date, while only five participants were reported as lost to follow-up or withdrawn: one in the elacytarabine arm and four in the control arm.

- **3.2 to 3.4 — Not applicable.**  
  The amount of genuinely missing survival-status information was very small. Administrative censoring for participants alive at the predefined event-based cutoff is not missing outcome data. The five participants lost to follow-up represent approximately 1.3% of the randomized population.

**Motivation for judgement**  
Survival status was available or appropriately administratively censored for nearly all randomized participants. The small number lost to follow-up is insufficient, based on the reported data, to have materially changed the OS comparison. Although the report does not give detailed reasons for loss to follow-up, only one participant was lost in the experimental arm and four in the control arm, while 302 deaths were observed. Thus, missing outcome data are unlikely to cause material bias in the primary OS result.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  The primary outcome was overall survival, a clear and appropriate outcome for relapsed/refractory AML. It was measured as time from randomization/treatment assignment to death, with censoring at a prespecified analysis cutoff or last known alive date for those lost to follow-up.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  The report describes the same survival definition and censoring rules for both groups. No group-specific survival ascertainment procedures are reported.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — Yes.**  
  The study was open-label. However, for OS the relevant ascertainment is death status, which is an observer-reported outcome not requiring clinical judgement in its determination.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — No.**  
  All-cause death is an objective outcome. Knowledge of allocation does not plausibly alter whether a participant died. The fact that subsequent care, including transplantation, may have occurred does not make death ascertainment subjective. The primary reported OS analysis was not censored at transplantation; a separate post hoc/secondary analysis censored for transplantation, but this is not the primary result assessed here.

- **4.5 — Not applicable.**

**Motivation for judgement**  
Overall survival is objective and its ascertainment does not involve subjective interpretation. The trial’s open-label design therefore does not create a material risk of measurement bias for the primary OS result. The report provides no evidence that survival ascertainment differed between groups.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The article identifies OS as the primary endpoint and describes the planned event-driven analysis: 300 events were required, and the analysis was conducted when the 300th event was reported. However, no dated protocol or statistical analysis plan was available in the supplied material to verify that the precise analysis of the reported OS result was finalized before unblinded outcome data became available.

- **5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — Probably no.**  
  The outcome is overall survival, based on death from any cause. The article consistently identifies OS as the primary endpoint and reports the prespecified median OS, HR, confidence interval, and log-rank P value. No alternative OS definitions or selectively reported survival time points are identified in the article.

- **5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The article reports several OS analyses: the primary ITT analysis; an age-adjusted Cox analysis; an analysis censoring participants at stem-cell transplantation; subgroup analyses; and analyses by individual investigator-choice treatments. The primary result is clearly identified, but without a protocol or dated SAP it cannot be verified whether the analysis selection and handling of all analytic decisions were completely prespecified before unblinded data were examined.

**Motivation for judgement**  
The report is consistent in describing OS as the primary endpoint and clearly presents the ITT primary analysis. There is no direct evidence that the reported HR of 0.97 was selected because of its result. Nevertheless, RoB 2 requires confirmation from a pre-unblinding analysis plan to judge this domain at low risk. Because neither a protocol nor SAP was available for inspection, and multiple OS analyses were reported, there are some concerns regarding selection of the reported result. This judgement reflects unavailable prespecification documentation, not evidence of selective reporting.

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation for overall judgement**  
The trial is judged at low risk of bias for:

There are **some concerns** in the domain of selection of the reported result because the supplied evidence does not include a dated protocol or statistical analysis plan permitting verification that the precise primary OS analysis was fully prespecified before unblinded outcome data were available.

Under the specified RoB 2 overall rules, the result is therefore judged to have **Some concerns**, because at least one domain raises some concerns and no domain is at high risk of bias.

Based on the thorough assessment of the provided randomized controlled trial article and the Cochrane RoB 2 tool guidelines, here is the detailed risk-of-bias evaluation:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study utilized an adequate random sequence generation process, as randomization was performed by a researcher not involved in data collection using participant identification numbers. Allocation was adequately concealed, as the study was double-blind and all research assistants, technicians, and participants were blinded to the allocation. Baseline characteristics were generally balanced between the fish oil and control groups, with a slight difference in skin color that is compatible with chance and does not suggest a subversion of the randomization process.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The trial was double-blind, utilizing identical capsules (in color, shape, size, and packaging) for both the fish oil and placebo groups, ensuring that participants, carers, and personnel were unaware of the assigned interventions. There is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, the researchers conducted an Intention-to-Treat (ITT) analysis to estimate the effect of assignment to the intervention, which is the appropriate analytical approach.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** There is a substantial amount of missing outcome data. Of the 60 women randomized (32 in the fish oil group, 28 in the control group), 28 women (nearly 50%) did not complete the trial. The proportion of missing data also differed between groups (approx. 53% attrition in the fish oil group vs. 39% in the control group). Given the high attrition rate and the nature of the condition being studied (depression, where dropout is frequently related to the true value of the mood outcome), it is likely that missingness depended on the true value of the outcome. There is no evidence from sensitivity analyses or rigorous imputation models to demonstrate that the massive loss to follow-up did not bias the results. 

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The outcome was measured using the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS), which is an appropriate and validated tool for assessing depressive symptoms in this population. The measurement methods were identical across both intervention groups. Since the study was double-blinded, the outcome assessors (the participants self-reporting their symptoms and the blinded researchers) were unaware of the intervention received, eliminating the risk that knowledge of the intervention could have influenced the outcome assessment.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study was retrospectively registered (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT01660165 on 24 May 2012, while enrollment started in November 2009), and the provided protocol metadata categorizes the study design as an "OBSERVATIONAL" cohort rather than an RCT. Because the trial was nested within a cohort and retrospectively registered, there is no verifiable pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available to ensure that the reported analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses of women with a previous history of depression) were not selected based on the results. However, they did report the stated primary and secondary outcomes transparently across all time points, avoiding a "High risk" judgment.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at high risk of bias overall because it was evaluated as having a "High risk of bias" in at least one domain (Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data). The extremely high and unbalanced attrition rates fundamentally compromise the integrity of the intention-to-treat estimates. Additionally, "Some concerns" were raised in Domain 5 due to retrospective trial registration and lack of a prospective RCT-specific analysis plan.

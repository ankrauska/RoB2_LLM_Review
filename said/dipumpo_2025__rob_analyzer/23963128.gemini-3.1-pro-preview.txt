Based on the detailed assessment using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool for the provided randomized controlled trial, here is the structured Risk of Bias report.

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns.
- **Motivation:** The study states that "Residents were randomized to one of three levels of supervision." However, there is no detailed information provided regarding the method used to generate the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers) or whether the allocation sequence was adequately concealed prior to assignment. While baseline characteristics across groups (Table 1) do not show definitive evidence of severe imbalance indicative of a compromised randomization process, the complete lack of methodological details regarding sequence generation and concealment leads to a judgment of "some concerns." 

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
- **Motivation:** Due to the nature of the intervention (physical proximity of the supervisor), both the participants (residents) and personnel (supervisors/confederates) were inherently aware of the assigned intervention group during the supervised scenario. However, the outcomes of primary interest (learning and resident independent performance) were evaluated during the subsequent *unsupervised* scenarios. There is no evidence that there were deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context that would affect this specific subsequent evaluation. Furthermore, the analysis appears to include all 53 randomized residents in their respective groups, consistent with an appropriate analysis strategy.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias.
- **Motivation:** The study recruited 53 residents. The data presented in the results and tables (Direct n=18, Immediately Available n=16, Distant n=19) account for all 53 randomized participants. Therefore, outcome data were available for all randomized participants, meaning there is no risk of bias introduced by missing outcome data.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns.
- **Motivation:** The primary outcome (independent performance during the unsupervised scenario) was measured using the Ottawa GRS and checklists by "two independent raters... who independently reviewed and rated all the videotaped performance." While the method of measurement is appropriate and was applied consistently, the text does not specify whether these independent raters were blinded to the residents' prior intervention assignment. Because assessing non-technical skills and clinical actions via a global rating scale involves some subjective judgment, if the raters were aware of the prior supervision level, their assessment could potentially be influenced. Without explicit confirmation of rater blinding, this raises some concerns.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns.
- **Motivation:** There is no indication that the trial was registered (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov) or that a pre-specified statistical analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available for analysis. While the outcomes reported in the results closely align with those described in the methods section, without a pre-specified protocol it is impossible to definitively rule out that the reported measurements or analysis models (e.g., the specific multivariable linear regression models chosen based on the best R-square) were selected from multiple eligible analyses based on the statistical results. 

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns.
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "some concerns" in multiple domains (randomization process, measurement of the outcome, and selection of the reported result). These concerns primarily stem from a lack of reporting detail regarding sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of the video assessors, and the absence of a pre-published trial protocol or statistical analysis plan. However, there is no direct evidence of high risk of bias in any single domain that would substantially lower confidence to the point of a "High risk" overall judgment.

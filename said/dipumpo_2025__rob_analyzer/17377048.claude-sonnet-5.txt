# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Coronary Artery Calcium Scoring: Influence of Reconstruction Interval and Reconstruction Increment Using 64-MDCT

**Preliminary note on applicability:** This is an unconventional application of RoB 2, as the study does not evaluate a clinical/therapeutic intervention with patient-relevant outcomes, but instead randomizes patients to two CT acquisition protocols (Group A: 64×0.6 mm collimation; Group B: 20×1.2 mm collimation) to compare calcium-scoring reproducibility across reconstruction intervals/increments. I have applied the tool as instructed, defining Group A as "experimental" and Group B as "comparator," and the outcome assessed as the Agatston/volumetric calcium score (and its coefficient of variation). This mismatch between the tool's intended use (clinical outcome trials) and the study design is itself reported below as a limitation of the assessment, per instructions, rather than assumed away.

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

- The article states only that "patients were assigned to two groups at random," with no description of the method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number table) and no description of allocation concealment procedures.
- Signalling question 1.1 (sequence random?): No information beyond the bare assertion of randomization.
- Signalling question 1.2 (allocation concealed?): No information provided.
- Signalling question 1.3 (baseline imbalance suggesting problem?): Age was compared between groups (Mann-Whitney U, p > 0.05) and found not significantly different; no other baseline prognostic factors (sex distribution, heart rate, extent of calcification risk factors) were formally compared, but no evidence of gross imbalance is reported.
- Because there is no information on sequence generation or allocation concealment, and no signal of baseline imbalance, this maps to "Some concerns" rather than "Low" or "High" risk, per the RoB 2 decision algorithm (no information on concealment + baseline differences compatible with chance/no information = Some concerns).

**Reported limitation:** Absence of any description of randomization or concealment methodology precludes a "Low risk" judgement.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **Low risk**

- This domain is atypical here because the "intervention" is an imaging acquisition protocol rather than a therapeutic intervention delivered to a live participant over time; there is no scope for participant non-adherence, co-interventions, or behaviourally-mediated deviations.
- All patients underwent the CT protocol as assigned (collimation setting), with no reported crossover, protocol deviation, or failure to implement the assigned acquisition parameters.
- All post-acquisition reconstructions (varying reconstruction interval and increment) were applied uniformly within each assigned collimation group, per protocol.
- The exclusion of 4/40 patients (no calcium detected on any reconstruction) is unrelated to deviation from assigned intervention; this is addressed under missing data (Domain 3), not deviations from intended intervention.
- Analysis was conducted according to assigned group (Group A vs. B) with no evidence of a "per-protocol" or "as-treated" reallocation.

**Judgement:** Low risk of bias for this domain.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Low risk**

- Of 40 enrolled patients, 4 (10%) were excluded because "no coronary calcium was detected on any of the reconstructions." This exclusion appears evenly distributed (18 patients analyzed in each of Group A and Group B, from an original 20 per group), suggesting no differential loss between comparison groups.
- The reason for exclusion (absence of detectable calcium) is not an informative missingness mechanism in the conventional sense (it is not a case of *unmeasured* outcome data — the true value was ascertainable, i.e., zero, but these patients were excluded from the variability/coefficient-of-variation analyses because CV cannot be meaningfully computed on a zero-score case). This is a reasonable, pre-specifiable, and symmetric exclusion rather than bias-inducing loss to follow-up.
- No further missing data across the five reconstruction time points or two reconstruction increments were reported for the 36 analyzed patients; all patients contributed data to all 10 reconstructions (5 intervals × 2 increments) per protocol.

**Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as outcome data were available for nearly all randomized/analyzed participants, and exclusions were symmetric and unrelated to differential missingness driven by the true value of the comparative outcome (protocol/increment effect).

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **High risk**

- The outcome (Agatston/volumetric calcium score) was derived from an automated workstation algorithm but required a subsequent manual step in which "a radiologist with 4 years of experience... and a cardiologist with 11 years of experience... differentiated between calcium and noise by placing regions of interest around the CAC." This is an observer-reported outcome **involving judgement**.
- Critically, blinding of these assessors to the reconstruction parameters (interval and increment) being scored was neither reported nor plausible, since the assessors were reviewing the reconstructed images directly and reconstruction increment/interval is often visually apparent (e.g., different slice thickness, different apparent noise/motion artifact). There is no mention of blinding procedures, randomized/blinded order of reading, or independent adjudication blinded to acquisition parameters.
- Because the central research question is whether reconstruction parameters *change* the measured score, and the same assessors scored all reconstructions for a given patient with full awareness of which reconstruction (interval/increment) they were evaluating, there is a plausible mechanism by which expectation or prior knowledge of the reconstruction setting could influence the manual differentiation of calcium from noise (e.g., anticipating more artifact at 50% vs. 65-70% intervals based on prior literature cited in the Discussion).
- This is not a case of an objective, non-judgement outcome (e.g., all-cause mortality); it explicitly requires subjective discrimination between "calcium" and "noise," which is the exact category of outcome RoB 2 identifies as being at higher risk when assessors are unblinded.

**Judgement:** High risk of bias, because (a) the method involves assessor judgement, (b) assessors were not blinded to the reconstruction parameter being evaluated, and (c) it is plausible that this awareness influenced the calcium/noise discrimination, particularly for borderline/small lesions (as the authors themselves note that overlapping increments revealed "very small calcified lesions" not seen otherwise — indicating measurement sensitivity varied with known reconstruction parameters).

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

- No trial registration, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or available for comparison against the reported results.
- However, within the report itself, results appear to be reported comprehensively and consistently across both pre-specified metrics (Agatston score and volumetric score), for both groups, across both reconstruction increments, including both statistically significant and non-significant findings (e.g., non-significant Agatston differences and significant volumetric differences are both reported transparently), which somewhat mitigates concern about selective outcome reporting.
- Nonetheless, the absence of a documented a priori analysis plan (specifying in advance which comparisons, subgroups, or thresholds would be examined) means it cannot be confirmed that the analyses presented represent the full set of pre-specified comparisons, or that no alternative analyses (e.g., other percentile thresholds, other statistical tests) were conducted but not reported.

**Judgement:** Some concerns, due to lack of information about a pre-specified analysis plan, despite no overt internal evidence of selective reporting.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement — **High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** Per the RoB 2 algorithm, a judgement of "High risk of bias" in any single domain results in an overall "High risk of bias" judgement, irrespective of the ratings in other domains. Here, Domain 4 (bias in measurement of the outcome) was judged **High risk**, driven by the unblinded, judgement-dependent nature of the calcium-versus-noise discrimination central to the primary outcome. This is compounded by "Some concerns" in two additional domains (randomization process, and selection of the reported result), which further lowers confidence in the trustworthiness of the reported result.

**Summary of domain judgements:**
| Domain | Judgement |
|---|---|
| 1. Randomization process | Some concerns |
| 2. Deviations from intended interventions | Low risk |
| 3. Missing outcome data | Low risk |
| 4. Measurement of the outcome | High risk |
| 5. Selection of the reported result | Some concerns |
| **Overall** | **High risk of bias** |

**Reported limitations/ambiguities identified during assessment (not assumed, but explicitly noted):**
1. No description of the method of random sequence generation or allocation concealment was provided in the article.
2. No blinding procedures for the manual calcium/noise differentiation step were described, and given the study design, blinding to reconstruction parameters may not have been feasible, but this was not discussed or mitigated (e.g., via blinded independent readers) by the authors.
3. No trial protocol, registration, or statistical analysis plan was available for cross-checking against the reported outcomes, limiting assessment of selective reporting.
4. The study is fundamentally a technical/methodological comparison rather than a clinical-outcome RCT, which limits the direct applicability of some RoB 2 constructs (e.g., "deviations from intended intervention" in the clinical sense); this assessment applied the tool's logic as closely as possible to the imaging-protocol context, and this adaptation should be considered when interpreting the results.

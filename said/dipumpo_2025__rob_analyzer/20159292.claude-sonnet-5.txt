# RoB 2 Assessment: COMICE Trial (Turnbull et al.)

**Preliminary considerations:**
- Study design: Individually-randomized parallel-group trial
- Experimental intervention: Triple assessment + MRI
- Comparator: Triple assessment alone (no MRI)
- Outcome assessed: Reoperation rate (primary endpoint) — proportion undergoing repeat operation/further mastectomy within 6 months, or pathologically avoidable mastectomy at initial operation
- Numerical result assessed: OR 0.96 (95% CI 0.75–1.24), p=0.77
- Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)
- Sources used: Journal article only

---

## Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Sequence generation used a minimisation algorithm "incorporating a random element" — this satisfies the requirement for a random component (1.1 = Yes).
- Allocation concealment was robust: randomisation was administered via an independent, centrally-run automated 24-hour telephone system operated by the University of Leeds Clinical Trials Research Unit, separate from recruiting clinicians (1.2 = Yes). This meets the "central randomization" gold-standard criterion.
- Baseline characteristics (Table 1) show excellent balance between groups across age, breast density, menopausal status, hormone use, and surgeon-level recruitment. No imbalances suggestive of a corrupted randomization process are apparent (1.3 = No).

All three signalling questions point to low risk; no concerns identified.

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- This was an open-label trial; participants and treating personnel (surgeons, radiologists) were necessarily aware of group assignment (2.1/2.2 = Yes), as MRI receipt/non-receipt is inherently unblindable.
- There is evidence of deviations from assigned intervention arising from the trial context: in the MRI arm, 53 patients did not have MRI interpreted (technical failures, refusals, non-attendance, unavailability before surgery) and 5 had MRI performed but results not used; in the no-MRI arm, 9 patients nonetheless received MRI (6 by patient request, 3 by clinician request). This crossover/non-adherence (61 in MRI arm effectively not receiving intervention as intended; 9 in comparator arm receiving experimental intervention) indicates some contamination arising from trial context, particularly the clinician/patient-request driven crossovers in the no-MRI arm (2.3 = Yes for a subset).
- However, the numbers are relatively modest (approximately 6.5% in MRI arm, ~1% in no-MRI arm) and it is not clearly established that these deviations differentially affected the primary outcome (reoperation) in a way favouring one arm (2.4/2.5 = uncertain, "Probably no" for substantial impact).
- Analysis was explicitly stated to be intention-to-treat, including all randomised patients regardless of whether MRI was actually performed (2.6 = Yes) — this is an appropriate analytic approach for estimating the assignment effect, which mitigates the risk stemming from the deviations described above.

Because there was awareness of assigned intervention (unblindable design) and some non-trivial crossover/non-implementation driven by trial context, but an appropriate ITT analysis was used and the deviations do not appear large enough to have substantially biased the specific result, the domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low" or "High."

**Limitation to report:** The article does not explicitly discuss whether the observed crossovers/failures to receive allocated intervention arose because of trial-context factors (e.g., patient anxiety, clinician preference influenced by knowledge of allocation) versus being unrelated logistic issues; this ambiguity is noted as a limitation of this assessment.

## Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- The primary endpoint was assessed for 816/816 (100%) in the MRI group and 807/807 (100%) in the no-MRI group per the flow diagram ("816 analysed [primary endpoint]," "807 analysed [primary endpoint]"), as patients lost to follow-up were explicitly and pre-specified as classified as *not* having a primary endpoint event (a conservative imputation rule applied equally to both arms) (3.1 = Yes, effectively "nearly all").
- Only small numbers were lost to follow-up in each arm (4 in MRI arm, 6 in no-MRI arm, out of >800 each), and these were handled via a pre-specified, symmetric rule rather than exclusion.
- Because the proportions with missing data are very small (<1%) and handled consistently across arms with a conservative pre-specified assumption, missingness could not plausibly have materially biased the reported OR.

## Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- The method of measuring the outcome (reoperation, defined via histopathology and pre-specified DMEC criteria for "pathologically avoidable mastectomy") is not inherently inappropriate (4.1 = No).
- Measurement/ascertainment of the outcome could plausibly have differed between groups: MRI findings directly triggered additional biopsies, contralateral breast surgery, and mastectomy decisions in the MRI arm (i.e., the pathway to "reoperation" was mechanistically linked to the intervention itself), meaning the process generating the outcome was not identical between arms (4.2 = Yes/Probably yes). This is a structural feature of the intervention rather than a bias per se, but it does raise the possibility that ascertainment of "pathologically avoidable mastectomy" — an outcome partly defined by clinical/imaging interpretation — could be handled differently according to group.
- Outcome assessors (surgeons deciding on reoperation; pathologists assessing margins) were not blinded to intervention assignment, as the whole trial was open-label and management decisions in the MRI arm were explicitly guided by MRI results (4.3 = Yes, aware).
- However, the outcome itself (reoperation/mastectomy, confirmed by histopathology) is a relatively objective endpoint with limited subjective judgement once the decision to operate has been made, though the decision to reoperate is itself an intervention-provider decision outcome that could be influenced by knowledge of MRI findings (4.4 = Yes, could be influenced).
- The trial mitigated this via a pre-specified, independent DMEC-adjudicated definition of "pathologically avoidable mastectomy" reviewed blind to original MRI findings for quality assurance (an independent radiologist re-read a sample of scans masked to original findings), which reduces the likelihood that assessment was distorted by knowledge of assignment (4.5 = Probably no for "likely influenced," given these safeguards), but this masked review applied only to a subset (153/816 scans reread), not to the primary reoperation decision-making process itself.

Given the outcome-provider-decision nature of part of the outcome, the lack of blinding, and only partial independent adjudication, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low."

## Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- The trial registration (ISRCTN 57474502) and the pre-specified sample size calculation, primary endpoint definition, and statistical analysis plan (logistic regression adjusted for minimisation factors) are clearly described and match the reported result, providing evidence against selective reporting for the primary outcome (5.1 = Probably yes for adherence to plan, based on internal consistency between Methods and Results).
- The primary outcome measurement (reoperation rate, single pre-specified composite definition combining repeat operation, further mastectomy, and pathologically avoidable mastectomy) appears to be reported in full as intended, with no evidence of alternative outcome measurements being selectively omitted (5.2 = Probably no).
- The single result for the primary comparison (OR with 95% CI and p value, adjusted for minimisation factors) is reported, and there is no indication that alternative unadjusted/differently-adjusted analyses were computed and suppressed (5.3 = Probably no).
- However, without access to the original trial protocol or a published statistical analysis plan (only the journal article and a cross-reference to an HTA monograph were available), it cannot be definitively confirmed that no alternative analyses or outcome definitions were tested and selectively not reported. This constitutes a source of uncertainty rather than positive evidence of selective reporting.

**Limitation to report:** The trial protocol and pre-registration record were not directly reviewed in this assessment (only the published article was available); therefore, full certainty regarding pre-specification versus post-hoc modification of analyses cannot be established, and this ambiguity is reported as a limitation of the present assessment.

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgement: HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Rationale:**
Applying the RoB 2 algorithm:
- Domain 1 (Randomization): Low risk
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions): Some concerns
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data): Low risk
- Domain 4 (Measurement of outcome): Some concerns
- Domain 5 (Selection of reported result): Some concerns

Per RoB 2 rules, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted when there are "Some concerns" in multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result. Here, three of the five domains (2, 4, and 5) are judged as "Some concerns," and these concerns are interrelated and compounding: the open-label design (Domain 2) directly underlies the potential for differential outcome ascertainment and unblinded decision-making regarding reoperation (Domain 4), and the absence of full access to pre-specification documents compounds uncertainty about the robustness of the single reported analysis (Domain 5). This convergence of concerns across multiple domains, centred on the fundamental unblinded nature of the trial and its downstream consequences on management decisions and outcome ascertainment, is judged to substantially lower confidence in the trustworthiness of the reported null result for the primary reoperation-rate endpoint.

**Note on limitations of this assessment:** This judgement was made based solely on the published journal article; the trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, and full HTA monograph (referenced but not reviewed) were not directly examined. This constitutes an important limitation, as access to these documents might resolve some of the ambiguities identified in Domains 2, 4, and 5 and could plausibly shift the assessment toward "Some concerns" overall if these documents demonstrate rigorous pre-specification and absence of differential outcome ascertainment.

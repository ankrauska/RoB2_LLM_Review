Based on the provided Cochrane RoB 2 guidance and the randomized controlled trial article, here is the structured Risk of Bias (RoB) assessment.

### Preliminary Considerations
- **Study design:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial.
- **Interventions:** Experimental: Brief leaflet-like intervention promoting RDIFV with persuasive messages and implementation intention tasks. Comparator: Control task (questions about the questionnaire itself).
- **Outcome assessed:** Self-reported number of days participants ate the RDIFV (Recommended Daily Intake of Fruit and Vegetables) over the last week (measured at 1-week follow-up).
- **Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect).

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process
**Judgment:** Low risk of bias
**Motivation:** The study states that "To ensure randomization, intervention and control booklets were placed in a single pile in an order determined by a sequence of random integers generated by a true random number generator" (Q1.1: Yes). Because the booklets were in a single randomized pile and handed out in a lecture theatre under examination conditions, the sequence was concealed until participants received and opened their booklets (Q1.2: Probably Yes). Baseline characteristics (pre-intervention behavior and cognition measures, age, gender) showed no significant differences between control and experimental groups, suggesting no problem with the randomization process (Q1.3: No). 

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions
**Judgment:** Low risk of bias
**Motivation:** Participants and personnel distributing the booklets were likely blinded to the assignment because the intervention and control tasks were contained within identical-looking pre-randomized booklets handed out simultaneously in a lecture setting (Q2.1, Q2.2: Probably No). Since participants were unaware of their assigned group and completed the task immediately under examination conditions, there were no deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context (Q2.3: Not applicable/No). Analyses were conducted comparing the randomized groups appropriately (Q2.6: Yes).

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data
**Judgment:** High risk of bias
**Motivation:** Of the 218 people who completed the baseline booklet, 146 responded at follow-up, resulting in an overall attrition rate of 33% (Q3.1: No). The authors note that "disregarding experimental condition, participants who completed the follow-up questionnaire reported significantly higher pre-intervention intentions, anticipated regret, and fruit and vegetable consumption" than those who dropped out. Thus, missingness in the outcome was clearly related to the true value of the outcome (i.e., those who eat less fruit/veg and have lower intentions were more likely to drop out) (Q3.3: Yes). Because missingness depends on the true value and the attrition rate is high (33%), it is likely that the missing outcome data biased the result (Q3.4: Yes). 

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome
**Judgment:** High risk of bias
**Motivation:** The outcome (fruit and vegetable consumption) is a participant-reported measure (self-report behavioral follow-up 1 week later). The method of measurement (self-report) is standard for this type of brief study (Q4.1: No), and data collection methods were comparable across groups (Q4.2: No). However, at the 1-week follow-up, participants were aware of the intervention they had received (as the experimental group had completed an active planning task regarding fruit and veg, while the control group did not) (Q4.3: Yes). Because the outcome is subjective (self-reported dietary intake) and participants knew they had just been in an intervention intended to boost their fruit and veg intake, their assessment of the outcome could have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, either through social desirability or recall bias (Q4.4: Yes; Q4.5: Probably Yes). 

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result
**Judgment:** Some concerns
**Motivation:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial protocol published before unblinded data were available (Q5.1: No/No information). However, the reported outcomes (RDIFV, RDIF, RDIV) correspond exactly to the stated aims and the measures collected at baseline and follow-up (Q5.2: Probably No). There are no obvious signs that multiple eligible analyses were conducted and selectively reported based on statistical significance (e.g., they report the ANCOVAs controlling for baseline as would be expected) (Q5.3: Probably No). However, the lack of a pre-specified protocol means we cannot completely rule out selective reporting, leading to some concerns.

### 6. Overall risk of bias
**Judgment:** High risk of bias
**Motivation:** The study is judged to be at High risk of bias overall. This is driven by High risk in Domain 3 (Bias due to missing outcome data) due to a 33% attrition rate where dropouts differed systematically from completers on baseline fruit and vegetable consumption and intentions; and High risk in Domain 4 (Bias in measurement of the outcome) due to the use of self-reported outcomes by participants who were aware of their intervention status, which likely influenced their reporting.

Based on a thorough and detailed analysis of the provided article, trial protocol, and the RoB-2 guidance, here is the Risk of Bias (RoB) assessment for the FLAME trial:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study utilized an adequate random sequence generation method, as participants were assigned using a computer random-number generator. Allocation concealment was successfully implemented using sequentially numbered opaque envelopes prepared by an independent pharmacist. Although there were slight baseline differences (e.g., mean age and previous history of stroke were slightly higher in the fluoxetine group, and baseline FMMS score was higher), these differences appear compatible with chance and do not suggest a fundamental problem with the randomization process. The authors appropriately adjusted for these baseline imbalances in their multiple linear regression analysis.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The trial was double-blinded; participants, carers, and study personnel were successfully masked to the intervention assignment through the use of placebo capsules that were identical in appearance and packaging to the active drug. There is no evidence of deviations from the intended intervention that arose due to the trial context. Furthermore, the analysis was performed on the "full analysis set" (113 out of 118 randomized patients), which closely adheres to the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, and treatment compliance was similar across both groups.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** Data for the primary outcome were available for nearly all randomized participants. Out of 118 randomized patients, 113 (95.7%) were included in the final analysis (57 in the fluoxetine group and 56 in the placebo group). Only two patients died and three withdrew from follow-up. The proportion of missing data is very small and balanced between the groups, meaning that the missing outcomes could not have made a clinically important difference to the estimated effect of the intervention. 

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The primary outcome (Fugl-Meyer motor scale) is a validated and appropriate method for measuring motor recovery after stroke. The measurement methods were comparable across both intervention groups. Crucially, the outcome assessors (physiotherapists) were masked to the treatment assignment. Because the assessors were unaware of the intervention received, it is highly unlikely that the assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The data were analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan. The primary outcome (change on the FMMS between day 0 and day 90) reported in the article matches the primary outcome pre-specified in the provided clinical trial protocol (NCT00657163). There is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple eligible analyses based on statistical significance or the direction of the findings.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at low risk of bias across all five domains for the primary outcome. The trial demonstrates robust methodology regarding randomization, blinding of participants and assessors, complete follow-up, and strict adherence to a pre-specified analysis plan, thereby warranting high confidence in the reported results.

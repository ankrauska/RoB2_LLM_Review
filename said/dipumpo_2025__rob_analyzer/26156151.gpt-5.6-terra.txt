## Scope of this RoB 2 assessment

- **Design:** Individually randomized, multicentre, 2×2 parallel-group trial.
- **Comparison assessed:** IVUS-guided CTO PCI versus angiography-guided CTO PCI, after successful guidewire crossing.
- **Result assessed:** 12-month **major adverse cardiac event (MACE)** outcome: 5/201 (2.6%) in the IVUS-guided group versus 14/201 (7.1%) in the angiography-guided group; hazard ratio 0.35 (95% CI 0.13–0.97), log-rank *P*=0.035.
- **Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment** to IVUS-guided rather than angiography-guided CTO intervention (intention-to-treat effect).
- **Sources assessed:** The published article, its supplied tables/figures, and the supplied ClinicalTrials.gov protocol record (NCT01563952). No full dated statistical analysis plan was available among the supplied documents.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states that, after successful guidewire crossing, participants “were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio” to IVUS or angiography guidance. It further specifies that assignment was conducted “using an interactive web-based response system” and was stratified by participating centre. This is consistent with a random allocation process.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — Probably yes.**  
  Randomization was conducted using an interactive web-based response system after successful guidewire crossing and after written informed consent. A web-based allocation system is centrally administered and is consistent with allocation concealment from recruiting/intervening personnel until randomization. The report does not provide technical details on irreversible registration before allocation, but the reported system supports a judgement of probably adequate concealment.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with randomization? — No.**  
  Baseline clinical and angiographic variables were closely similar. For example, age was 61.0 versus 61.4 years; male sex was identical (80.6% in each group); diabetes was 34.8% versus 33.8%; prior myocardial infarction was 8.0% in both groups; and CTO lesion length was 26.8 versus 26.4 mm. No large imbalance in intervention-group size occurred: 201 participants were randomized to each group. The observed differences do not indicate a problem with randomization.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The study used web-based, centre-stratified random allocation, had equal randomized group sizes, and showed no baseline imbalances suggesting compromised randomization. Randomization occurred after successful guidewire crossing, which limits the population to patients in whom crossing succeeded, but this occurred before allocation to IVUS versus angiography guidance and therefore does not introduce bias into the randomized comparison being assessed.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably yes.**  
  The interventions were IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided PCI. These procedural strategies cannot realistically be concealed from the treating procedural team, and participants may also have been aware of aspects of their procedure. The supplied registry identifies the trial as single-masked with the investigator masked, but the article makes clear that IVUS use, pullback methods, timing of use, and procedural implementation were under physician discretion. This supports awareness of intervention assignment among at least personnel.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Interventional physicians delivered the assigned procedural strategy. The article states that IVUS devices, use before or after stenting, pullback methods, and number of IVUS examinations were left to physicians’ discretion. Thus, personnel delivering the intervention necessarily knew whether the IVUS-guided strategy had been assigned.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report describes more frequent high-pressure post-stent balloon dilation in the IVUS group (51.2% versus 41.3%) and a slightly greater post-procedure minimum luminal diameter. However, these are procedural actions plausibly resulting from the allocated IVUS-guided strategy and do not constitute documented departures from the protocol. The protocol explicitly allowed substantial physician discretion in IVUS implementation. The report does not identify crossovers, off-protocol use of IVUS in the angiography-guided group, or other trial-context deviations that were inconsistent with the assigned strategies.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  The article explicitly states: “The intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses.” The MACE comparison includes all 201 randomized participants in each IVUS-versus-angiography group. There is no report that participants were analysed according to a treatment other than their randomized group or excluded because of post-randomization treatment deviations.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Treating personnel were aware of the allocated strategy, but lack of blinding alone does not establish bias in this RoB 2 domain. The report does not document protocol-inconsistent, trial-context deviations that were likely to affect MACE and were unbalanced between groups. The randomized comparison was analysed according to intention to treat. The observed procedural differences, including more post-stent dilation in the IVUS group, are part of how the assigned guidance strategy was implemented rather than evidence of a departure from the intended intervention.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Yes.**  
  At 12 months, 200/201 participants (99.5%) in the IVUS-guided group and 201/201 (100%) in the angiography-guided group completed clinical follow-up. Thus, outcome follow-up was available for 401/402 randomized participants (99.8%).

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — Not applicable.**  
  Because outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants, the missing-data amount is too small to plausibly have had an important effect on the MACE estimate.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
Only one participant did not complete 12-month follow-up, and this occurred in the IVUS-guided group. With 19 total MACE events reported and outcome data available for 99.8% of randomized participants, the quantity of missing data is insufficient to materially change the estimated intervention effect. The article does not provide the reason for the single incomplete follow-up, but this isolated missing outcome does not raise material RoB 2 concerns for this result.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  MACE was pre-defined as cardiac death, myocardial infarction, or target-vessel revascularization. Definitions were reported for cardiac death, MI, stent thrombosis, and target-vessel revascularization. Target-vessel revascularization required repeat PCI or bypass surgery of the target vessel together with ischemic symptoms or a positive stress test and >50% angiographic diameter stenosis by quantitative angiography. These are appropriate, clinically relevant definitions for the composite outcome.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  Clinical follow-up was scheduled at the same intervals in both groups—1, 3, 9, and 12 months—and was performed by clinic visit or telephone contact. The report does not describe different outcome-surveillance schedules or outcome definitions between groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — No.**  
  The article states that “all clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical event committee blinded to randomization.”

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The outcome definitions were stated prospectively, follow-up procedures were the same in both randomized groups, and the clinical event committee assessing events was blinded to treatment allocation. Although target-vessel revascularization includes a clinical decision component, the reported endpoint required defined clinical and angiographic criteria, and event adjudication was performed by a committee blinded to randomization. The supplied evidence does not indicate differential ascertainment or biased event adjudication.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling questions and assessment**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The article identifies cardiac death as the prespecified primary endpoint and MACE at 12 months as a secondary endpoint. The supplied registry record also lists cardiac death within 12 months as primary and MACE as secondary. However, the supplied registry record does not contain a sufficiently detailed, date-stamped statistical analysis plan specifying the MACE analysis approach, handling of missing data, covariate adjustment, time-to-event methods, or hierarchy among possible analyses. No separate statistical analysis plan was supplied.

- **5.2 Is the MACE result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements? — No information.**  
  MACE was specified as a secondary endpoint and reported at 12 months, but the available documents do not provide a sufficiently detailed pre-unblinded plan to establish whether other eligible MACE definitions, time points, or endpoint constructions were analysed and not reported. There is no direct evidence that the reported MACE result was selected because of its statistical significance or direction; however, the available information cannot exclude this possibility.

- **5.3 Is the MACE result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The article reports Kaplan–Meier estimates, log-rank tests, and univariate Cox proportional-hazards models. The supplied protocol record does not specify these methods or demonstrate that they were finalized before unblinded data were available. It also does not document whether alternative eligible analyses—such as risk-ratio analyses, risk-difference analyses, adjusted Cox models, or different approaches to the composite endpoint—were prespecified or performed.

**Motivation for judgement:**  
The trial registry and article support that MACE was a planned secondary endpoint, which is reassuring. However, RoB 2 requires a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available to judge this domain as low risk. No such detailed, dated plan was supplied. In the absence of information allowing exclusion of selective choice among eligible outcome measurements or analyses, this domain has **some concerns**, not high risk: there is no direct evidence that the MACE result was selectively reported on the basis of its result.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Overall judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation for overall judgement:**  
The MACE result is at low risk of bias in the domains of randomization, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, and measurement of the outcome. However, there are **some concerns** regarding selection of the reported result because no detailed, date-stamped pre-specified statistical analysis plan was available, and the supplied sources do not establish that the reported MACE analysis was selected without reference to unblinded results.

Under the specified RoB 2 overall rules, the result is therefore judged **Some concerns**, because at least one domain has some concerns and no domain is at high risk of bias.

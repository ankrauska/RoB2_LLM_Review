# ROB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Comparison of patient acceptance of the Forsus Fatigue Resistant Device with and without mini-implant anchorage: A randomized controlled trial

**Outcome assessed:** Primary outcome — patient acceptance of the appliance and interference with functional activities (satisfaction with results ridit comparison used as representative numerical result: ridit = 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.71, P = 0.36)

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Sequence generation: Random sequence was computer-generated using Excel's "=RAND()" function, with random permuted blocks (sizes 6 and 4, randomized order) — signalling question 1.1 = Yes.
- Allocation concealment: Sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes were used, concealed from researchers and assessors — signalling question 1.2 = Yes.
- Baseline imbalance: Table I shows baseline characteristics (age, SNA, SNB, ANB, Y-axis, MP/SN, MMP, overjet, crowding) were similar between groups, with no indication of imbalance suggestive of a randomization problem — signalling question 1.3 = No.

All three signalling questions align with the "Low risk" criteria in the algorithm (sequence random = Yes, allocation concealed = Yes, no imbalance = No). No concerns identified.

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Participants and personnel (clinicians) were **not blinded** to intervention allocation (explicitly stated: "Blinding of the clinicians and patients to the interventions in each group was impossible") — signalling questions 2.1 and 2.2 = Yes.
- However, per the elaboration for this domain, the relevant issue is whether deviations from intended intervention arose **because of the trial context** (i.e., due to lack of blinding influencing recruitment/engagement behaviors or trial personnel undermining protocol delivery) — signalling question 2.3. There is no evidence or reported indication that non-protocol interventions, contamination, or other trial-context-driven deviations occurred. The FFRD/mini-implant protocol was implemented identically as intended (per-protocol) in both groups; the only deviations reported (breakage in 3/16 per group) were mechanical/appliance-related and equally distributed, not related to trial context or engagement bias.
- No evidence of switching, co-intervention imbalance, or other deviations attributable to the unblinded design that would bias the effect of assignment.
- Analysis: An intention-to-treat analysis was planned and, since there were no dropouts, all 32 randomized participants were analyzed as randomized — signalling question 2.6 = Yes (appropriate analysis).

Given no deviations arising from trial context that affected the outcome, and an appropriate (full ITT) analysis, this domain is judged Low risk despite lack of blinding of participants/personnel, consistent with the RoB2 algorithm (Y/PY to 2.1/2.2 but N/PN to 2.3 → Low).

## Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- All 32 randomized participants completed the study and returned questionnaires; the CONSORT flow diagram (Figure 4) confirms no losses to follow-up, no discontinuations, and no exclusions after randomization — signalling question 3.1 = Yes ("data available for all... participants").
- The authors explicitly state: "No patients dropped out during the trial" and "All questions were answered correctly by the patients and their parents," with no missing responses.
- Since outcome data were complete for all randomized participants, no further signalling questions are required, and risk of bias is Low by default.

## Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Measurement method: The outcome (patient acceptance/satisfaction) was measured via a questionnaire adapted from a previously used instrument (Bowman et al.), not a formally validated psychometric tool; however, this was used consistently across groups and is a reasonable method for this type of patient-reported outcome — signalling question 4.1 = Probably No (not clearly inappropriate).
- Ascertainment method did not differ between groups: the same questionnaire, administered identically at the time of FFRD removal, was used for both groups — signalling question 4.2 = No.
- Outcome assessor: This is a **participant-reported outcome** (patients self-completed the Likert-scale questionnaire). The RoB2 guidance specifies that for participant-reported outcomes, the participant is the "outcome assessor." Although data extraction and analysis were blinded (an uninvolved person coded/extracted the data, and the statistician was blinded to group identity), the **participants themselves were not blinded** to their allocated intervention (this is unavoidable, since they could see/feel whether they had mini-implants) — signalling question 4.3 = Yes (assessors, i.e., patients, were aware of intervention).
- Signalling question 4.4: Could the participant-reported assessment (satisfaction, pain, noticeability, etc.) have been influenced by patients' knowledge of which intervention (with or without mini-implant) they received? This is plausible, since patients undergoing the more invasive/novel mini-implant procedure could have different expectations or reporting behavior (e.g., a placebo/novelty effect or negative expectation bias related to an additional minor surgical procedure) — response: Yes/Probably yes.
- Signalling question 4.5: Is it *likely* that assessment was actually influenced by this awareness? There is no strong evidence of a directional effect, and the results show closely comparable, non-significant differences between groups. However, because participant-reported outcomes are inherently susceptible to this type of bias and the comparator (mini-implant addition) is a distinguishable and noticeable intervention, this cannot be excluded, leading to a judgment of "Some concerns" rather than "Low" or "High."

This aligns with the RoB2 algorithm where the outcome assessor (patient) was aware of intervention, and the assessment could plausibly be influenced by this awareness without clear evidence of high-impact influence.

## Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- The study explicitly states: "This trial was not registered" and "The protocol was not published before trial commencement." This means there is no independently verifiable, date-stamped, pre-specified analysis plan available to confirm that the reported outcomes and analyses were not selectively chosen based on the results — signalling question 5.1 = No information/Probably No.
- The sample size calculation and primary/secondary outcomes were described within the article itself (post hoc from the perspective of external verification), but without an independently registered protocol, it is impossible to confirm that all pre-specified outcome measures and analyses were reported in full and that this specific numerical result (ridit values, CIs) was not selected from multiple possible analyses.
- All 14 questionnaire items and their corresponding ridit analyses appear to be reported comprehensively (Tables III–VII), and there is no apparent evidence of cherry-picking within the reported outcomes — signalling questions 5.2 and 5.3 = Probably No, based on internal consistency of reporting.
- However, in the absence of a registered protocol or published a priori statistical analysis plan, this cannot be verified with confidence, resulting in "No information" for question 5.1 and correspondingly a judgment of "Some concerns" per the RoB2 decision algorithm (absence of pre-specified plan combined with no information on selection from multiple outcomes/analyses defaults to "Some concerns").

**Limitation/Ambiguity noted:** The lack of trial registration and unpublished protocol is an important ambiguity that precludes a "Low risk" judgment for this domain, as it is not possible to definitively rule out selective reporting.

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgment: Some concerns**

**Rationale (per RoB2 rules):**
- Domain 1 (Randomization): Low risk
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions): Low risk
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data): Low risk
- Domain 4 (Measurement of outcome): Some concerns
- Domain 5 (Selection of reported result): Some concerns

Since the study is judged to raise "Some concerns" in two domains (Domain 4 and Domain 5), but is **not** judged to be at **High risk of bias** in any single domain, and the two "Some concerns" domains do not, in combination, substantially lower confidence in the result (both relate to plausible but unconfirmed/unverified sources of bias rather than clear evidence of substantial bias), the overall risk-of-bias judgment for this result is **"Some concerns."**

**Key limitations explicitly identified during this assessment (reported as ambiguities, not assumptions):**
1. The trial was not registered and the protocol was not published prior to commencement, precluding verification of pre-specified outcomes/analyses (Domain 5).
2. The outcome is patient-reported and inherently unblindable at the participant level, creating an unavoidable but plausible potential for reporting bias related to awareness of intervention received (Domain 4).
3. The questionnaire instrument used was adapted from prior literature but was not reported as formally validated for psychometric properties, though this alone did not raise the outcome measurement to "high risk" given consistent application across groups.

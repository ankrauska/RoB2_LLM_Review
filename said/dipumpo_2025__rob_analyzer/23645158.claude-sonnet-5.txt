# ROB 2 Assessment Report
## Study: "The effect of intravenous magnesium sulfate on serum levels of N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT pro-BNP) in elective CABG with cardiopulmonary bypass"

**Preliminary specification:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial. Experimental intervention: MgSO4 infusion (15 mg/kg/h); Comparator: Saline placebo. Outcome assessed: Postoperative (24h) NT-proBNP level. Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect), as this is the only effect discussed in the paper.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

**Signalling responses:**
- 1.1 (Was allocation sequence random?) – **Yes**: The text states allocation was "based on a computer random numbers table," indicating a genuine random component.
- 1.2 (Was allocation sequence concealed?) – **No information**: No description of concealment method (e.g., sequentially numbered opaque envelopes, central allocation, or pharmacy-controlled randomization) is provided anywhere in the manuscript.
- 1.3 (Did baseline differences suggest a problem?) – **No**: Table 1 shows no statistically significant differences in age, BSA, Euroscore, weight, EF, ESR, hematocrit, creatinine, ACC time, CPB time, graft number, or baseline magnesium/calcium — all p>0.05, compatible with chance.

**Motivation:** Random sequence generation is reported, and no baseline imbalance is evident. However, complete absence of information about allocation concealment is a genuine limitation reported in the text — this is not merely unreported detail addressed by editorial constraints, since no central randomization, sealed envelope, or pharmacy-based method is mentioned at all. Per the RoB2 algorithm (random sequence = Yes/Probably yes, concealment = No information, baseline imbalance = No) → **Some concerns**.

---

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention) — **Some concerns**

**Signalling responses:**
- 2.1/2.2 (Were participants/personnel aware of assigned intervention?) – **No**: Study explicitly described as "double-blind," with placebo (saline) matched in volume/route/syringe presentation to the MgSO4 infusion. Same anesthesiologists, surgeons, and protocol used in both arms.
- 2.3 (Deviations arising from trial context?) – **No information/Probably no**: No evidence trial context caused non-protocol interventions.
- 2.6 (Appropriate analysis used?) – **Probably No**: The manuscript states, "Those patients who received triple inotropic agent therapy for CPB weaning were excluded from the study. The number of patients excluded from the study in each group was registered" — yet this number is never reported in the Results section, nor is it clear whether analysis followed ITT principles. This is a **post-randomization exclusion** based on a clinical/physiological criterion (need for triple inotropic support) that could plausibly be influenced by treatment group (MgSO4 has hemodynamic/anti-arrhythmic effects), making this an inappropriate "quasi per-protocol" style exclusion rather than a true ITT analysis.
- 2.7 (Potential for substantial impact of this exclusion?) – **No information**: Because the exact number of exclusions per arm is never disclosed, it is impossible to judge whether this exclusion could have induced substantial bias.

**Motivation:** Blinding was well implemented (Part 1 = Low risk), but Part 2 (analysis approach) raises concern because of unreported, non-random, post-randomization exclusions of patients needing triple inotropic support — a criterion that could differ systematically between groups and is not accounted for in a transparent ITT analysis. This ambiguity (unreported exclusion numbers, no CONSORT flow diagram) is explicitly noted here as a limitation of the assessment, arising from incomplete reporting in the article itself. This yields an overall **Some concerns** judgement for this domain.

---

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling responses:**
- 3.1 (Outcome data available for all/nearly all randomized participants?) – **No information/Probably No**: No CONSORT flow diagram is provided; the total number randomized, excluded, and analyzed is not reconciled. The text only states 88 patients were "enrolled," with 44/44 per group, but simultaneously acknowledges that some patients were excluded for requiring triple inotropic therapy, without specifying how many, from which group, or whether the final analysis (n=44/44 as per Tables 1–3) includes or excludes these patients.
- 3.2 (Evidence result not biased by missing data?) – **No**: No sensitivity analyses, imputation methods, or discussion of missing data handling are presented.
- 3.3 (Could missingness depend on its true value?) – **Yes**: Requirement for triple inotropic support is a marker of severe hemodynamic/cardiac dysfunction, which is directly related to the true value of the outcome (NT-proBNP, a marker of cardiac stress/dysfunction). Thus, missingness (if these patients were excluded from final analysis) is plausibly related to the outcome itself.
- 3.4 (Likely that missingness depended on true value?) – **Yes/Probably yes**: Given that inotropic requirement and NT-proBNP elevation are both markers of the same underlying cardiac dysfunction/inflammatory process being studied, and no reassurance is given that the numbers/reasons for exclusion were balanced or unrelated to the outcome.

**Motivation:** The combination of (a) no CONSORT flow diagram, (b) an explicitly acknowledged but unreported/unquantified post-randomization exclusion criterion tied mechanistically to the outcome under study, and (c) absence of any sensitivity analysis, leads to a judgement of **High risk of bias** for this domain. This is reported as a significant ambiguity/limitation in the trial report itself.

---

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling responses:**
- 4.1 (Was method of measuring outcome inappropriate?) – **No**: NT-proBNP was measured using a validated electrochemical luminescence immunoassay (Elecsys 2010, Roche) with stated coefficient of variation (2.2–3.2%) and detection range (5–35,000 pg/ml) — an appropriate, validated method.
- 4.2 (Could measurement differ between groups?) – **No**: Identical sampling protocol (timing, handling, storage, centrifugation, freezing temperature) was applied uniformly to both groups, as explicitly described.
- 4.3 (Were outcome assessors aware of intervention?) – **No/Probably No**: This is an automated laboratory immunoassay performed on frozen/coded samples in a core lab, not requiring subjective interpretation by a clinician aware of group assignment; double-blinding of the clinical team further supports lack of assessor awareness influencing this laboratory-based biomarker.
- 4.4/4.5 (Could/did assessment get influenced by knowledge of intervention?) – **Not applicable**, as this is an objective automated test not involving assessor judgement.

**Motivation:** NT-proBNP is an objective, laboratory-measured biomarker, unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment, and measurement methodology was identical and appropriate for both groups. This domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

**Signalling responses:**
- 5.1 (Analyzed in accordance with pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded data available?) – **No information**: The trial is registered (IRCT201205069650N1), but no trial protocol or statistical analysis plan (SAP) is referenced, retrievable, or compared against the published outcomes in this article. It is therefore not possible to confirm that the reported NT-proBNP analysis, its single 24-hour timepoint, and the specific comparison method were pre-specified before unblinded data access.
- 5.2 (Result selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements?) – **No information**: NT-proBNP was measured only at two timepoints (baseline and 24h); the authors themselves note in the Limitations section that "we just assessed two points... while the trend of the marker or the 'area under curve' was not measured," raising the possibility that this simplified measurement approach/timepoint selection was not pre-specified, though no clear evidence of selective reporting based on results is present.
- 5.3 (Result selected from multiple eligible analyses?) – **No information**: No indication of multiple statistical models (adjusted/unadjusted) being tried; a simple Student's t-test was applied as stated in Methods, consistent with what is reported.

**Motivation:** In the absence of an available/verifiable trial protocol or SAP, and lacking any independent registry detail comparison, it is not possible to fully rule out selective reporting, although no overt evidence of result-based selection is apparent from the manuscript itself. This constitutes an acknowledged ambiguity/limitation in the assessment rather than firm evidence of bias, yielding **Some concerns**.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement: **HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Justification:** Per RoB 2 rules, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted if the study is at high risk of bias in **at least one domain**, or if there are "some concerns" in multiple domains that substantially lower confidence in the result.

In this study:
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data) was judged **High risk of bias**, driven by the unreported/unquantified post-randomization exclusion of patients requiring triple inotropic support — a criterion plausibly linked to the true value of the primary outcome (NT-proBNP) — combined with the complete absence of a CONSORT flow diagram or sensitivity analysis.
- This alone is sufficient to classify the overall risk of bias for the NT-proBNP result as **High**, further compounded by "Some concerns" in Domains 1 (randomization process — allocation concealment not reported), 2 (deviations from intended intervention — unclear ITT analysis with unquantified exclusions), and 5 (selective reporting — no protocol/SAP available for verification).

**Stated limitation/ambiguity for transparency:** The principal ambiguity driving this overall high-risk judgement is the study's own acknowledgment that some patients were excluded post-randomization ("those who received triple inotropic agent therapy... were excluded from the study. The number of patients excluded... was registered") without ever reporting these numbers, the group-wise breakdown, or reconciling this with the final analyzed sample sizes (44 vs. 44) presented in the Results tables. This creates unresolvable uncertainty regarding both attrition bias and the integrity of the intention-to-treat analysis for the primary biomarker outcome (NT-proBNP), and is reported here as a specific, unresolved limitation of this risk-of-bias assessment rather than an assumption.

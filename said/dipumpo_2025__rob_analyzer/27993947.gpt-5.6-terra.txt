## Scope of this RoB 2 assessment

- **Study design:** Individually randomized parallel-group trial.
- **Comparison:** Disclosure of 10-year CHD risk based on conventional risk factors plus genetic risk score (**CRS+GRS**) versus disclosure based on conventional risk factors alone (**CRS**).
- **Result assessed:** Patient-reported shared decision-making immediately following the genetic-counsellor and physician encounters, measured using the **SDM Questionnaire (SDM-Q)**. Reported result: mean SDM score **10.61 (SD 1.55)** in CRS+GRS versus **10.40 (SD 1.76)** in CRS; *p*=0.09.
- **Effect of interest:** Effect of **assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect).
- **Sources assessed:** Article, supplied participant-flow/table information, and ClinicalTrials.gov record (NCT01936675). No full protocol or statistical analysis plan (SAP) was supplied.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The article states that randomization used “a **computer-generated random sequence**,” stratified by age, sex, and family history of CHD, using the Pocock and Simon method. This is a random allocation method.

- **1.2 Was allocation concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The report states that an investigator generated the allocation sequence and study-arm assignments and that participants were randomized after completing the first visit and scheduling the second. However, it does not state how allocation was concealed from staff involved in recruitment/enrolment, whether a central or remote allocation system was used, or whether the allocation list was inaccessible before assignment. Generation of the sequence by an investigator does not itself demonstrate allocation concealment.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  The baseline table reports similar group sizes (103 versus 104) and no substantial imbalance in measured characteristics. Age, sex, smoking, family history, anthropometric measures, lipid values, blood pressure, and education did not show statistically significant differences. There is no reported baseline pattern suggesting a failure of randomization.

**Judgement and motivation**

The allocation sequence was random and baseline characteristics do not suggest a compromised randomization process. However, allocation concealment is not described. Under RoB 2, absence of information on allocation concealment, despite otherwise acceptable baseline balance, leads to **some concerns** rather than low risk of bias.

**Limitation/ambiguity:** The paper does not report the operational mechanism for concealing allocation before randomization. Therefore, adequate allocation concealment cannot be verified.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  The study was unmasked. Participants in the CRS+GRS arm received genetic-risk information, whereas those in the CRS arm received conventional-risk information without genetic-risk disclosure. Participants necessarily knew the content of the risk information disclosed to them.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Genetic counsellors and physicians conducted discussions tailored to the assigned disclosure strategy; thus, those delivering the intervention were aware of allocation.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report describes delivery of the assigned disclosure strategy in both groups, with use of the decision aid in both arms. It does not report contamination, protocol-inconsistent co-interventions, differential failure to deliver the allocated disclosure, or other deviations arising from the trial context.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  Analyses used randomized group as the independent variable. The SDM-Q was completed by 206 of the 207 participants who completed the second visit. The one participant without a completed SDM-Q was not included in the SDM analysis. For this domain, this is consistent with a modified intention-to-treat analysis; bias from the missing outcome is evaluated separately in Domain 3.

**Judgement and motivation**

Although participants and intervention personnel were unblinded, unblinding is expected because the intervention consisted of disclosure of different risk information. The report does not identify deviations from the intended disclosure procedures that arose because of the trial context and could have affected the result. The comparison was analysed according to randomized groups. Therefore, for the intention-to-treat effect, this domain is at **low risk of bias**.

**Limitation/ambiguity:** The article does not provide detailed adherence or fidelity data for each disclosure encounter. Nonetheless, it does not report protocol-inconsistent deviations, differential co-interventions, or analysis by treatment received.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Yes.**  
  The SDM-Q was completed by 206 of 207 participants who underwent randomization and completed the second study visit. Thus, SDM outcome data were available for approximately 99.5% of randomized participants.

**Judgement and motivation**

Only one participant lacked SDM-Q outcome data. For this continuous outcome, this amount of missing data is sufficiently small that, whatever the missing score might have been, it could not plausibly make an important difference to the estimated between-group effect. Therefore, the result is at **low risk of bias due to missing outcome data**.

**Limitation/ambiguity:** The report does not identify the randomized group of the participant with missing SDM-Q data or give a reason for the missing questionnaire. This does not alter the judgement because the amount of missing data is negligible.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  The SDM-Q is described as a validated 11-item questionnaire measuring patients’ perceptions of the shared decision-making process. Nothing in the report indicates that the instrument was unsuitable for the stated patient-reported SDM outcome.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  Both groups completed the same SDM-Q after the disclosure and physician encounter. The report provides no indication that data collection methods, scoring procedures, or timing differed between groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — Yes.**  
  For this patient-reported outcome, the participant is the outcome assessor. Participants knew whether genetic-risk information had been disclosed.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Yes.**  
  SDM-Q responses are subjective assessments of the consultation, discussion, involvement, and treatment decision. Participants’ ratings could be affected by knowing that they had received genetic-risk information rather than conventional-risk information alone.

- **4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Yes.**  
  The intervention itself was a different approach to communicating risk information during a shared decision-making encounter. The assessed outcome was the participant’s perception of that same interaction. Knowledge of receiving genetic-risk information could plausibly and likely influence perceived inclusion, adequacy of discussion, understanding of options, and satisfaction with the decision process.

**Judgement and motivation**

This is an unblinded, participant-reported outcome directly concerned with participants’ perceptions of the consultation in which the allocated intervention was delivered. The participants, who assessed the outcome, knew their allocated disclosure strategy. Since the intervention could affect expectations and perceptions of the shared decision-making process independently of any underlying difference in the quality of shared decision-making, outcome measurement is likely to have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received. This domain is therefore at **high risk of bias**.

**Limitation/ambiguity:** Blinding participants to receipt of genetic versus non-genetic risk information would not realistically be feasible in this intervention. Nevertheless, under RoB 2, infeasibility of blinding does not remove the risk that a subjective participant-reported outcome was influenced by awareness of the intervention.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The trial registry lists “Shared decision making (as assessed by survey)” as a secondary outcome. However, the supplied registry record does not specify the SDM-Q instrument, its scoring method, timing, handling of unanswered items, primary SDM analysis, covariate adjustment, or the analysis population. No dated SAP was supplied. The article refers to a previously published protocol, but that document was not available among the sources assessed.

- **5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  The article reports two measures relevant to shared decision-making: the participant-reported SDM-Q and an observer-rated OPTION5 score in a random subset of 80 video-recorded encounters. The article reports results for both measures, which does not itself demonstrate selective reporting. However, in the absence of a sufficiently detailed, prospectively finalized analysis plan, it cannot be determined whether all intended and eligible SDM measures, time points, or derived outcomes were reported.

- **5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The SDM-Q was reported as individual item results, a dichotomous maximum-score result, and a continuous total score. The report also describes a non-standard scoring approach: missing answers among partially completed questionnaires were assigned a score of zero, while participants not answering all questions were described as excluded. Without a pre-specified detailed plan, it is not possible to establish whether the reported continuous score was selected from multiple possible outcome definitions or analytic approaches on the basis of the results.

**Judgement and motivation**

The trial registry establishes that shared decision-making was a secondary outcome, but it does not contain enough pre-specified detail to verify the measurement instrument, analysis methods, missing-data approach, or selection among possible SDM outcomes and analyses. The paper does not provide a date-stamped SAP finalized before unblinded outcomes were available. There is no direct evidence that the reported SDM-Q result was selected because of its findings; therefore, high risk is not justified. However, the lack of adequate prospective analytic detail results in **some concerns**.

**Limitation/ambiguity:** The article states both that incomplete SDM-Q respondents were excluded and that unanswered items for participants who answered some questions were assigned zero. The supplied report does not clearly reconcile these procedures. This ambiguity concerns the exact outcome scoring and analysis but does not, on the available evidence, establish result-based selection.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement — **High risk of bias**

The overall judgement for the assessed patient-reported SDM-Q result is **high risk of bias**.

- Domain 1: **Some concerns** — allocation concealment was not reported.
- Domain 2: **Low risk of bias** — no evidence of trial-context deviations and analysis was by randomized group.
- Domain 3: **Low risk of bias** — SDM data were available for 206/207 randomized participants.
- Domain 4: **High risk of bias** — unblinded participants rated a subjective perception-based outcome directly related to the intervention encounter, and knowledge of allocated disclosure was likely to influence outcome assessment.
- Domain 5: **Some concerns** — no sufficiently detailed pre-specified, date-stamped analysis plan was available to exclude selective choice of SDM measurement or analysis.

According to RoB 2, a result judged at **high risk of bias in at least one domain** is judged overall at **high risk of bias**.

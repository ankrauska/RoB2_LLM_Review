# ROB2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Randomised comparison of Avaxim™ and Vaqta™ as booster vaccines in subjects primed with Avaxim™

**Outcome assessed:** Anti-HAV antibody GMT 1 month post-booster (primary outcome); reactogenicity as secondary outcome. This assessment focuses primarily on the immunogenicity outcome (GMT), with reactogenicity issues noted where relevant.

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (though the study reports per-protocol as primary due to stated concordance with ITT).

---

## Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:** The report states subjects were "randomised" but provides no description whatsoever of the method used to generate the allocation sequence (no mention of computer randomization, random number tables, or block randomization) and no description of allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation). Baseline demographic characteristics (age, sex, weight, height) appear well balanced (Table 1), and baseline pre-booster GMT, although numerically different (496 vs 325 mIU/mL), was reported as not statistically significant (p=0.171), consistent with chance. However, in the absence of any information on sequence generation or allocation concealment, and given this is described as an "open study" (raising the possibility that allocation could have been known in advance to those enrolling participants, since the study explicitly used an open/unblinded design), there is insufficient information to confirm adequate concealment. This leads to a judgment of "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," per the algorithm (no information on both sequence generation and allocation concealment methods).

**Limitation noted:** The manuscript provides no detail on sequence generation or allocation concealment methods, which is a reporting ambiguity.

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:** This was explicitly an "open" (non-blinded) study — participants, carers, and personnel administering vaccines were all aware of intervention assignment, as confirmed in the Discussion section itself, which states "This was an open study." Given the two vaccines differ in volume, presentation, and known side-effect profiles (e.g., differing preservative and adjuvant content), it is plausible participants/personnel could deduce or already knew assignment. However, for the primary immunogenicity outcome (antibody GMT, an objective laboratory measure processed centrally by Merck Research Laboratories), lack of blinding of participants/personnel is unlikely to have caused deviations from intended intervention affecting this particular outcome (i.e., unblinding would not plausibly alter biological antibody response). There is no evidence of non-protocol interventions, crossovers, or failures in implementing the assigned vaccine.

Regarding analysis: the study performed both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses, and reported that results were "close enough" to justify presenting only the per-protocol results — however, the actual ITT results are not shown, so independent verification is not possible. Six subjects were excluded from the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis for reasons including protocol violations and missing serology, which is an acceptable practice for per-protocol effect but introduces some uncertainty when the effect of interest is assignment-based (ITT). Because the ITT results were not presented in detail, there is some uncertainty as to whether an appropriate (full ITT) analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention.

**Limitation noted:** Lack of blinding (acknowledged by authors as a limitation particularly for reactogenicity assessment); ITT results were not reported in detail, only stated to be "close enough" to per-protocol results.

---

## Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:** Of 127 randomized and safety-analysed subjects, only 6 (4.7%) were excluded from the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis: one had already received a booster, one travelled to a high-endemic country (protocol violation, not related to outcome), and four lacked pre- and/or post-vaccination serology results (missing data). This is a small proportion (59/64 = 92% and 62/63 = 98% in each arm), unlikely to have materially changed the interpretation of the results, especially given the very large and consistent GMT increases observed in both groups. The reasons for exclusion are documented and do not appear related to the true value of the outcome (i.e., not driven by poor/good antibody response), reducing the likelihood that missingness depended on the outcome itself. Therefore, missing outcome data are judged unlikely to bias the result.

---

## Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:** The primary outcome (anti-HAV antibody GMT) was measured using a standardized, validated, quantitative laboratory assay (modified HAVAB™ assay, based on the Abbott HAVAB™ kit), performed by a single independent laboratory (Merck Research Laboratories) for all samples, using a common technique and reference standard curve (WHO standard serum immune globulin) across both intervention groups. This is an objective, non-judgement-based (observer-reported without judgement) laboratory determination, and laboratory personnel performing the assay were external to the trial site and were not involved in vaccine administration, making it very unlikely that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced the numerical result. The method of measurement was appropriate and consistent across groups, with no indication that measurement or ascertainment differed by intervention arm.

*(Note: If the outcome under review were reactogenicity/tolerability instead of GMT, the judgment would be "High risk," because these were participant-reported outcomes in an open-label, unblinded study, where participants' knowledge of receiving a different vaccine formulation, volume, or injection experience could plausibly influence the reporting of local/systemic reactions — this is explicitly acknowledged by the authors: "This was an open study and was therefore a sub-optimal setting for comparing reactogenicity.")*

---

## Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:** There is no evidence of selective reporting of outcome measurements — the pre-specified primary outcome (GMT at 1 month) and secondary outcome (seropositivity rate) are clearly reported for both study arms, and reactogenicity outcomes are reported comprehensively for multiple categories of reactions (Table 3). However, no trial protocol, prospectively registered trial record, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or made available in this report, so it is not possible to confirm whether the analyses reported (particularly the decision to present only per-protocol rather than ITT results, justified post-hoc as being "close enough") were pre-specified before unblinded data were available. The statement that ITT and per-protocol results were "close enough" to report only per-protocol analysis, without presenting the actual ITT numeric results, raises some concern that outcome/analysis selection could have been influenced by the results, although there is no strong indication of a substantive discrepancy. Given the lack of accessible pre-specified analysis plan documentation, but no clear evidence of results-based selective reporting, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk" or "High risk."

**Limitation noted:** No trial registry entry, protocol, or statistical analysis plan was cited or available for verification of pre-specified analysis intentions.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgment: Some concerns**

**Rationale:** The study was judged "Some concerns" in three domains (randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, and selection of the reported result) and "Low risk" in two domains (missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome). No domain was judged to be at "High risk of bias." Per the RoB 2 framework rules, a study with "Some concerns" in at least one domain (and not "High risk" in any domain) is classified as "Some concerns" overall. Although "Some concerns" were identified in multiple domains, none of these concerns are individually severe enough (e.g., there is no strong evidence of actual bias having occurred, only incomplete reporting of methodological safeguards) to warrant escalation to an overall "High risk of bias" judgement. The primary limitation across domains is incomplete methodological reporting (randomization/allocation concealment methods, ITT numerical results, absence of a protocol/SAP for verification) rather than clear evidence of introduced bias. 

**Overall Risk of Bias: SOME CONCERNS**

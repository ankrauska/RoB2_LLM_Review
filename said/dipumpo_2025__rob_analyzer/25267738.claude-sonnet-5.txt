# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment: Cancer-DACUS Study

**Preliminary considerations:**
- Study design: Individually-randomized parallel-group trial
- Comparison assessed: Group A1 (continued LMWH) vs Group A2 (discontinued LMWH) in RVT-positive patients
- Outcome assessed: Recurrent VTE at 12 months post-randomization
- Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (ITT effect), as stated by authors ("Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis")
- Sources: Journal article, trial registry (NCT00450645)

---

## Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation:** The allocation sequence used a "sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." This raises concerns for two reasons: (1) it is unclear whether generation of the sequence within blocks had a genuine random component, or whether the "clinical chart number" itself determined assignment order, which could be a systematic/quasi-random method rather than truly random; (2) fixed block size of 10, if known to enrolling staff, creates a risk of predictability toward the end of each block, potentially compromising allocation concealment even with sealed envelopes. The paper states "sealed-envelope technique," which per RoB2 criteria is more susceptible to manipulation unless described with additional safeguards (opaque, sequentially numbered, tamper-proof), none of which are explicitly reported. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) show no significant imbalances between A1 and A2, consistent with chance. Given no clear confirmation of a random component in sequence generation and an allocation concealment method with unproven adequacy, but no evidence of baseline imbalance suggesting subversion, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgement: High risk**

**Motivation:** The trial was explicitly open-label: "Patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT." Both participants and personnel were aware of intervention assignment. Because there was no blinding, deviations from intended intervention arising from the trial context become highly plausible. Notably, the outcome (recurrent VTE) requires clinical suspicion to trigger diagnostic testing ("patients underwent additional testing... only when recurrence was clinically suspected"), meaning that unblinded patients/physicians in the group that discontinued LMWH (A2) could differentially seek or report symptoms compared with those continuing treatment (A1), affecting detection and management of suspected events (a deviation from intended intervention that arose because of trial context and likely affected the outcome asymmetrically between groups). Additionally, there is an important asymmetry in follow-up duration between groups (A1 followed for 18 months, A2 and B only 12 months), explicitly acknowledged by the authors as a limitation that "may have resulted in an underestimate of the event rate" in group A2 — this is a deviation in trial conduct/design that could bias the comparative estimate. Analysis was reported as intention-to-treat, appropriate for assessing assignment effect, but the differential unblinded ascertainment-triggering behavior and unequal follow-up duration are substantial concerns that were not adequately mitigated. This combination of lack of blinding, context-driven differences likely affecting outcome ascertainment, and unbalanced follow-up leads to a judgement of "High risk" for this domain.

---

## Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation:** By 12 months, the CONSORT flow (Fig 1, Fig 3) indicates a reduction in the number of patients under follow-up: 108/119 remained in group A1 at 6 months and 91/119 at 12 months; 94/123 remained in A2 at 6 months and 77/123 at 12 months. This represents notable attrition (approximately 24–37% cumulative reduction from initial randomized numbers) that is not fully or transparently explained; the article does not clearly report reasons for these reductions (e.g., death, withdrawal, loss to follow-up) separately from the "death due to cancer progression" outcome, nor does it clarify whether they were censored appropriately in a time-to-event analysis accounting for informative censoring. Deaths due to cancer progression occurred in both groups (10.1% in A1, 15.4% in A2) and were treated as competing events, but it is unclear whether analyses appropriately accounted for informative censoring related to these losses, especially given the numerically higher death rate in group A2. Although this difference in mortality was not statistically significant, the higher raw frequency of "missingness" (through death or dropout) in A2 could plausibly relate to disease severity, and it is not clearly demonstrated that missingness was unrelated to the true outcome status. No sensitivity analyses addressing missing data were reported. This leads to "Some concerns" rather than "High risk," as there is no definitive evidence that missingness related systematically to outcome ascertainment beyond the plausible (but unconfirmed) relationship with disease progression.

---

## Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgement: High risk**

**Motivation:** The outcome (recurrent VTE, an observer/clinically-judged outcome requiring imaging confirmation triggered by clinical suspicion) required subjective clinical judgement to trigger testing: "Patients were instructed to contact the center if symptoms suggestive of VTE...developed" and testing was performed "only when recurrence was clinically suspected." Both participants and assessors triggering the diagnostic pathway (patients/treating physicians) were aware of treatment assignment (unblinded), which could influence the propensity to report symptoms or seek diagnostic confirmation differentially between groups continuing versus discontinuing anticoagulation. Although the final adjudication of confirmed events was performed by a blinded central committee ("unaware of the patient's name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment"), this blinded adjudication only addresses the final classification step, not the initial (unblinded) trigger for clinical suspicion and subsequent imaging referral — a critical and unaddressed source of potential differential ascertainment bias. Given that assessment of the outcome could plausibly have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received (patients or physicians in the discontinuation group might have heightened vigilance for symptoms, or conversely those continuing therapy might have reduced vigilance believing they were protected), and this is a real and biologically plausible mechanism relevant to the trial's design, this domain is judged "High risk."

---

## Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Motivation:** The registered outcome (NCT00450645) specifies "Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism" as the primary outcome, and "Minor bleeding" and "Death due to cancer" as secondary outcomes, consistent with what is reported in the article (VTE recurrence, major/minor bleeding, and mortality). However, several discrepancies raise concern: (1) the registered enrollment target was "300 (estimated)" whereas 347 were actually enrolled, without clear explanation of protocol amendment; (2) the article reports slightly different recurrence numbers within the text itself ("22 of the 119 patients in group A1" in the abstract versus "18 of the119 patients in group A1" in the Results section), suggesting inconsistent reporting of the same outcome across sections, which raises the possibility that different analytic time-windows or definitions were used and the most favorable/pertinent one selected without clear justification; (3) no full trial protocol or statistical analysis plan (SAP) was available for review to confirm whether the presented Cox-model adjustments (age, sex, previous surgery, metastasis) were pre-specified before unblinded data were available. Because there is no accessible pre-specified analysis plan and there are internal inconsistencies in the numeric results reported for the primary outcome, but no definitive evidence of outcome-dependent selective reporting, this domain is judged as "Some concerns."

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation:** According to RoB 2 rules, an overall judgement of "High risk of bias" is warranted if the study is judged to be at high risk in at least one domain, or if multiple domains show "some concerns" in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result. In this study, **two domains (Domain 2: deviations from intended interventions, and Domain 4: measurement of the outcome) were independently judged to be at High risk of bias**, both driven by the fundamental and unavoidable limitation that this was an open-label trial with unblinded patients, treating physicians, and an outcome-detection pathway reliant on clinically-triggered (not routinely scheduled) diagnostic testing. Additionally, "Some concerns" were identified in the randomization process (Domain 1), missing outcome data (Domain 3), and selection of the reported result (Domain 5). The convergence of unblinded design, symptom-triggered (rather than systematic) outcome ascertainment, asymmetric follow-up duration between comparison groups, and inconsistencies in the reported numerical results collectively and substantially lower confidence in the reported effect estimate for continuation versus discontinuation of LMWH in RVT-positive patients. The authors themselves acknowledge several of these limitations (lack of blinding, unequal follow-up duration, small sample size), reinforcing the assessment.

**Note on ambiguity/limitation of this assessment:** Full study protocol and statistical analysis plan were not available for review; assessment of Domain 5 and parts of Domain 1 relies solely on information reported within the published article and the linked registry entry. Additionally, an internal numerical discrepancy (18 vs. 22 recurrent VTE events reported for Group A1 in different sections of the same article) could not be resolved and is reported here as an identified ambiguity rather than an assumption.

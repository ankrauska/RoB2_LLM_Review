# ROB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Effect of add-on gabapentin on opioid withdrawal symptoms in opium-dependent patients (double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial; n=40)

**Outcome assessed:** Severity of withdrawal symptoms (SOWS total score) across six time-points (assignment effect / intention-to-treat estimand, as this is the analysis reported)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process
**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

- *Sequence generation (1.1):* The article states subjects were "assigned randomly... using a randomization automated system on a one-to-one basis." This supports a "Probably yes" response, although the specific algorithm (e.g., computer random-number generation, block size) is not described.
- *Allocation concealment (1.2):* The report states "the randomization list was held securely, and released only after study completion," and gabapentin/placebo were dispensed in "identical appearing capsules." This supports adequate concealment ("Probably yes"), although the exact mechanism (central allocation, sealed envelopes, pharmacy-controlled dispensing) is not detailed.
- *Baseline imbalance (1.3):* The authors report "no significant differences in mean age or dosage of methadone and other drugs... between the treatment and controls groups at baseline." No baseline imbalance suggestive of a randomization failure is evident.

**Motivation:** Although reporting of exact methodological detail is sparse (a common limitation in this era of publication), the combination of a random allocation method, a securely held/concealed allocation list, identical-appearing capsules, and no evidence of baseline imbalance supports a low-risk judgment. This is flagged as an ambiguity: the precise sequence-generation algorithm and concealment mechanism are not explicitly described.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)
**Judgment: High risk of bias**

- *Blinding (2.1/2.2):* Both participants and personnel were blinded (double-blind design, identical capsules); therefore, deviations arising from the trial context are unlikely on this front alone.
- *Analysis approach (2.6/2.7):* Table 1 shows the gabapentin group retained n=20 throughout, whereas the placebo group decreased from n=20 (baseline) to n=19 (day 14) to n=17 (day 18). The Discussion explicitly states that "one patient in the placebo group was excluded at day 14 and two others were excluded at day 17 because of non-compliance with the scheduled treatment programme and self-medication with illicit and/or over-the-counter (OTC) drugs" — these participants were **excluded from the study/analysis**, not retained under intention-to-treat principles.
- This constitutes a post-randomization exclusion of eligible participants who deviated from protocol, occurring **only in the placebo arm** (asymmetric exclusion, 0 vs. 3 participants). The analysis was therefore not a full ITT analysis.
- *Potential for substantial impact (2.7):* The authors themselves speculate that "non-compliance and self-medication in the placebo group may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms," implying that the excluded participants were likely those experiencing the worst outcomes. Removing the most symptomatic placebo patients from the analysis would systematically bias the between-group comparison (likely biasing the result toward the null, i.e., underestimating a true gabapentin benefit, or artificially narrowing the observed group difference).

**Motivation:** The differential, outcome-related post-randomization exclusion of participants exclusively from the placebo arm, without an intention-to-treat analysis or sensitivity analysis addressing this exclusion, represents a substantial risk of bias in the estimated effect of assignment to intervention.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data
**Judgment: High risk of bias**

- *Completeness (3.1):* Outcome data were not available for all randomized participants — 3/20 (15%) placebo participants had missing SOWS data from day 14/17 onward, while the gabapentin group had complete data (20/20) throughout.
- *Evidence of no bias (3.2):* No sensitivity analyses, imputation methods, or statistical corrections addressing missing data were reported.
- *Could missingness depend on the true outcome value (3.3) and is it likely (3.4)?* Yes — the authors explicitly hypothesize that dropout in the placebo group was linked to greater withdrawal severity ("may be due to the greater severity of withdrawal symptoms"). This is a direct acknowledgment that missingness in the outcome is related to its true (unfavorable) value, and this occurred differentially between arms (only in placebo).

**Motivation:** The combination of (a) unequal missing data between arms, (b) a plausible and author-acknowledged relationship between missingness and symptom severity, and (c) absence of any corrective statistical approach (e.g., multiple imputation, sensitivity analysis) supports a high risk of bias judgment for this domain.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome
**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

- *Appropriateness of the outcome measure (4.1):* SOWS is a previously validated instrument (cited from Handelsman et al. 1987) appropriate for assessing withdrawal severity.
- *Comparability of ascertainment (4.2):* No indication that measurement procedures differed between groups; the same scale was applied identically at the same time points in both arms.
- *Blinding of outcome assessors (4.3):* Since SOWS is a participant-reported outcome, the "assessor" is the participant. The double-blind design (identical capsules, concealed allocation) means participants were kept unaware of their treatment assignment throughout the study, minimizing the possibility that self-report was influenced by knowledge of intervention received.

**Motivation:** As the outcome is self-reported but blinding was successfully implemented and maintained (no reports of unblinding, side-effect-driven guessing, or differential detection), risk of bias in measurement is judged low.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result
**Judgment: Some concerns**

- No trial registration, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan is referenced or available in the article, making it impossible to verify whether the reported outcome measurements/time-points and analyses were pre-specified before unblinded data were available.
- On the positive side, the report appears to present a comprehensive set of results: all 16 individual SOWS symptom items (Table 3), the total symptom score across all six time-points (Table 1, Fig. 1), and a repeated-measures ANOVA plus Mann-Whitney U analysis — with no evidence of selective omission of unfavorable results within these reported analyses.
- However, the absence of a-priori registration/protocol prevents confirmation that no alternative outcome definitions, time-points, or analytic models were tried and not reported.

**Motivation:** Given the lack of information about pre-specified analysis intentions, but with no overt evidence of selective outcome/analysis reporting (all measured symptoms and time points are reported), the domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk," in line with RoB 2 guidance for situations of missing documentation without positive evidence of selective reporting.

**Limitation to note:** This judgment is constrained by the unavailability of a trial registry entry or protocol; this ambiguity is reported as a limitation of the assessment rather than assumed to reflect either good or poor practice.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgment: High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** Per RoB 2 rules, an overall judgment of "High risk of bias" is warranted if the study is at high risk of bias in at least one domain. In this study, **two domains** (Domain 2 – deviations from intended interventions, and Domain 3 – missing outcome data) were judged at **high risk of bias**, both driven by the same underlying issue: the asymmetric, outcome-related post-randomization exclusion of three placebo-group participants (with no corresponding exclusions in the gabapentin group), for which no intention-to-treat analysis or missing-data sensitivity analysis was undertaken. This constitutes a substantial and compounding threat to the internal validity of the treatment effect estimate reported in this trial, particularly given the very small overall sample size (n=40), which magnifies the potential impact of this differential attrition.

Domains 1 (randomization) and 4 (outcome measurement) were judged low risk, and Domain 5 (selective reporting) raised some concerns due to lack of protocol/registration information, but these do not alter the overall high-risk conclusion driven by Domains 2 and 3.

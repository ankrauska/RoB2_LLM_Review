Based on a thorough and detailed Risk of Bias (RoB 2) assessment of the provided article, here is the structured report evaluating each of the 5 domains and the overall risk of bias:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** The study states that "The patients were assigned to two groups at random with 20 patients each," but provides absolutely no details regarding the method used to generate the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers) or the method used to conceal the allocation sequence until participants were enrolled. Baseline characteristics are only briefly described in the text (age and sex), stating that age was not significantly different between groups, but the lack of detailed baseline comparison and allocation concealment information limits confidence in the randomization process.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
* **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The intervention in this study was a technical parameter setting (collimation of 64 × 0.6 mm vs. 20 × 1.2 mm) on the CT scanner. While there is no mention of blinding of the patients or personnel (technicians), the nature of the intervention makes it highly unlikely that any lack of blinding would lead to deviations from the intended scanning protocol that arose because of the trial context. The scanning protocols were strictly machine-dependent once set.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
* **Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study enrolled and randomized 40 patients, but 4 patients (10%) were completely excluded from the analysis "because no coronary calcium was detected on any of the reconstructions." This is a critical issue because the missingness is directly related to the true value of the outcome (a true calcium score of zero). By excluding patients with a true score of zero, the mean Agatston and volumetric scores for the analyzed groups are artificially inflated and biased. Therefore, missing outcome data depends directly on the outcome's true value.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** Outcome measurement involved a semi-automated workstation followed by manual intervention where a radiologist and a cardiologist "differentiated between calcium and noise by placing regions of interest around the CAC." The study does not report whether these outcome assessors were blinded to the patient's assigned group (collimation group A vs. group B). Because differentiating calcium from noise involves a degree of subjective human judgment, knowledge of the intervention could theoretically influence the assessment. 

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Motivation:** There is no mention of a pre-specified, finalized, and publicly available statistical analysis plan or trial registry protocol (which is common for technical imaging studies of this era but necessary for a low-risk RoB 2 judgment). Consequently, there is no way to definitively verify that the reported analyses (e.g., specific reconstruction intervals and increments) were not selected or prioritized based on the results obtained, although they align with the general objectives stated in the methods.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
* **Judgment:** High risk of bias
* **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at a High risk of bias overall. According to the specified RoB 2 rules, a study is at high risk overall if it is judged to be at high risk of bias in at least one domain. This study presents a High risk of bias in Domain 3 (Missing outcome data) due to the deliberate post-randomization exclusion of participants who had a true calcium score of zero, which systematically biases the reported mean scores. Additionally, there are "Some concerns" in Domains 1, 4, and 5 due to poor reporting of methodological details.

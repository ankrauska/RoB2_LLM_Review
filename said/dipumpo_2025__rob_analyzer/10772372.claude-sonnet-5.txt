# Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) Assessment

**Study:** Impact of Pharmacy Counseling on Compliance and Effectiveness of Combination Lipid-Lowering Therapy in Patients Undergoing Coronary Artery Revascularization: A Randomized, Controlled Trial (Pharmacotherapy 2000;20(4):410–416)

**Design:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial (2 arms: telephone follow-up vs no telephone follow-up), n=30
**Effect of interest assessed:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)
**Outcome assessed for this report:** Long-term compliance (prescription refill records) and LDL cholesterol levels at 1 and 2 years
**Sources available:** Journal article only (no protocol, no SAP, no trial registry record identified/available)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

*Motivation:*
- Signalling Q1.1 (Was the allocation sequence random?): **Probably yes.** The article states patients were "randomized to telephone contact or no telephone contact using a computer-generated list of random numbers." This constitutes a valid random sequence generation method.
- Signalling Q1.2 (Was the allocation sequence concealed?): **No information.** The article provides no description of concealment procedures (e.g., use of sequentially numbered envelopes, central allocation, or independent administration of the sequence). Given the small, single-pharmacist-driven design and lack of detail typical of this era and journal, this cannot be inferred with confidence.
- Signalling Q1.3 (Did baseline differences suggest a problem?): **No.** Table 1 (referenced) reports baseline characteristics were "similar for both groups," and no data suggest problematic imbalance beyond chance, given the very small sample (n=15 per group).

Because allocation concealment is not reported (no information) and there is no information about baseline imbalance that would allow a clear "low risk" determination combined with the absence of concealment details, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" per the RoB2 algorithm (no information on concealment + no imbalance issue reported = some concerns).

**Limitation/Ambiguity noted:** No description of allocation concealment mechanism is provided in the article; this ambiguity precludes a "Low risk" judgment.

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgment: Some concerns**

*Motivation:*
- Signalling Q2.1/2.2 (Were participants/personnel aware of assigned intervention?): **Yes.** This trial was inherently unblinded — the intervention was weekly telephone contact by a pharmacist versus no contact. Neither participants nor the intervening pharmacist could be blinded to group assignment, and blinding of this type of behavioral intervention is not feasible.
- Signalling Q2.3 (Did deviations arise because of trial context?): **Probably no/No information.** The authors themselves raise the possibility of a Hawthorne effect in the control group (increased awareness of being monitored), acknowledging in the Discussion that behavior in the non-intervention group may have been influenced by trial participation ("patients were acutely aware that their behavior was being assessed"). This represents an authors-acknowledged deviation arising from trial context, particularly during the initial 12-week phase.
- Signalling Q2.4/2.5 (Did deviations affect outcome, and were they balanced?): The authors explicitly state this potential Hawthorne effect may have obscured differences in short-term compliance (weeks 0–12), suggesting an unbalanced influence on the early outcome, although the direction is toward the null (masking a real difference) rather than exaggerating it.
- Signalling Q2.6 (Appropriate analysis used?): **Yes.** All 30 randomized patients were included in analyses; none were lost to follow-up, and results appear to be based on an ITT-consistent framework (all patients accounted for at each time point).

Because participants/personnel were unavoidably unblinded, and the authors themselves note a plausible trial-context-driven deviation (Hawthorne effect) that could have influenced the outcome (compliance) — though the analysis itself was appropriate and complete — this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High," since the deviation is acknowledged as reducing between-group differences (biasing toward null) rather than fabricating a spurious effect, and no formal evidence of unbalanced non-protocol intervention delivery is presented for the long-term (1- and 2-year) outcomes.

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Low risk**

*Motivation:*
- Signalling Q3.1 (Were data available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?): **Yes.** The article explicitly states, "Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up." Compliance and lipid data are reported for all 30 randomized patients at 6 weeks, 12 weeks, 1 year, and 2 years.

Since complete outcome data were available for all randomized participants at all time points, there is no risk of bias due to missing outcome data for either the short-term or long-term outcomes assessed.

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: Some concerns**

*Motivation:*
- Signalling Q4.1 (Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?): **No.** Lipid profiles were measured via standard fasting laboratory assays, and compliance was assessed via objective methods (pill/packet counts not performed within patient view, and independently verified pharmacy refill records) — these are appropriate and relatively objective methods.
- Signalling Q4.2 (Could measurement differ between groups?): **Probably no** for lipid values (objective laboratory outcome, unlikely influenced by group assignment). However, for the compliance outcome, pill/packet counts and refill records are objective, but self-reported reasons for noncompliance were gathered via patient interview only in the context of the telephone group's structured questioning, which somewhat differs in method of ascertainment between groups (though this does not affect the actual refill-record/pill-count outcome itself).
- Signalling Q4.3 (Were outcome assessors aware of intervention status?): **Yes/Probably yes.** The same pharmacist conducting telephone follow-up was also aware of allocation group; while lipid samples were processed via lab assay (likely blinded to group), pill/packet counts and refill verification were not clearly described as being performed by assessors blinded to group assignment.
- Signalling Q4.4/4.5 (Could/did this influence outcome assessment?): For the objective outcomes (LDL levels via lab assay, pharmacy refill records obtained from third-party pharmacies), it is unlikely that lack of blinding influenced measurement, as these are externally verified, objective data sources. However, the lack of explicit statement regarding blinding of outcome assessors (e.g., lab personnel, staff verifying pill counts) introduces uncertainty.

Given the objective nature of the primary outcomes (refill records, lipid assay results) which reduces the likelihood of measurement bias, but the absence of explicit statements confirming blinding of assessors performing pill/packet counts, this domain is judged as "Some concerns."

**Limitation/Ambiguity noted:** The article does not specify whether personnel conducting pill/packet counts or verifying refill records were blinded to intervention allocation.

---

## 5. Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

*Motivation:*
- Signalling Q5.1 (Was there a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available?): **No information.** No trial protocol or statistical analysis plan was available/referenced in the article, and no trial registry entry is cited (this trial predates mandatory registration, published in 2000). It is therefore not possible to confirm whether the reported outcomes/analyses (total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides, at multiple time points; compliance defined by two different methods — pill/packet counts and refill records) were pre-specified in this exact form.
- Signalling Q5.2 (Selective reporting from multiple eligible outcome measurements?): **No information.** Multiple lipid parameters and multiple time points (6, 12 weeks, 1, 2 years) are reported comprehensively, which reduces concern; however, without a pre-specified plan, selective emphasis (e.g., highlighting significant long-term results while downplaying non-significant short-term results) cannot be excluded.
- Signalling Q5.3 (Selective reporting from multiple eligible analyses?): **No information.** No indication of multiple alternative statistical models (e.g., adjusted vs. unadjusted) being tested and selectively reported; the statistical methods (chi-square, t test) are stated and appear applied consistently.

Given the complete absence of a protocol or SAP for comparison, and no information available to confirm or refute selective reporting, this domain defaults to "Some concerns" as per the RoB2 algorithm for missing information on both pre-specification and potential selective analyses.

**Limitation/Ambiguity noted:** No trial protocol or statistical analysis plan was accessible for comparison against the published results, limiting definitive assessment of this domain.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall Judgment: High risk of bias**

*Rationale (per RoB2 rules):*
While no single domain was judged as "High risk" in isolation, **four of the five domains were judged as "Some concerns"** (Domains 1, 2, 4, and 5), with only Domain 3 (missing outcome data) judged as "Low risk." 

Per RoB2 guidance: *"'Some concerns' in multiple domains may lead the review authors to decide on an overall judgement of 'High' risk of bias... The study is judged to have some concerns for multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result."*

The cumulative impact of these concerns — unclear allocation concealment, an unblindable intervention with an author-acknowledged Hawthorne effect potentially masking short-term results, ambiguity regarding blinding of personnel performing compliance assessments, and complete absence of a pre-specified analysis plan/trial registration — substantially lowers confidence in the trustworthiness of the reported results, particularly given the very small sample size (n=30) which amplifies the potential impact of any of these biases on the effect estimate.

**Overall stated limitation:** This assessment is constrained by the exclusive reliance on the published journal article, as no trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial registry record was available for verification of pre-specified outcomes, allocation concealment procedures, or blinding of outcome assessors. These ambiguities are reported as limitations of this assessment rather than assumptions.

## RoB 2 assessment

**Study/result assessed:** Moritz et al., *A randomized controlled trial of internet-based therapy in depression* (2012).  
**Design:** Individually randomized, parallel-group trial.  
**Comparison:** Immediate access to Deprexis versus delayed access/wait-list control.  
**Outcome/result assessed:** Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) total score at the 8-week reassessment; effect of assignment to Deprexis versus wait-list. The reported ITT analysis used multiple imputation and ANCOVA and reported *p* = .03 and *d* = .36.  
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect).

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — No information.**  
  The article states that participants “were randomly allocated to the treatment or wait-list group in consecutive order,” but it does not describe how the allocation sequence was generated. It does not report a computer-generated sequence, random-number table, random permuted blocks, coin tossing, or any other random component. The statement that allocation was “random” alone is insufficient under RoB 2 to establish that the sequence was random.

- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  Participants completed the baseline survey and provided their email address before allocation. However, no allocation-concealment method is reported. There is no description of centralized randomization, an independent allocation service, password-protected automated allocation, opaque sequential envelopes, or another mechanism preventing those administering enrolment/allocation from knowing upcoming assignments.

- **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  The randomized group sizes were equal (105 participants per group). Table 1 and Table 2 report no statistically significant baseline differences in the reported demographic, treatment-related, or psychopathological variables, including baseline BDI total score (wait-list mean 30.02; Deprexis mean 28.81; *p* > .4). The article states that no significant baseline differences emerged. The available baseline data therefore do not indicate a problem with randomization.

**Judgement rationale:**  
The absence of major baseline imbalances is reassuring, but neither sequence generation nor allocation concealment is adequately reported. Under RoB 2, where allocation concealment is not described but baseline differences appear compatible with chance, the appropriate judgement is **some concerns**, rather than low risk.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Participants were informed whether they would receive an immediate voucher for Deprexis or would receive access after completing the 8-week post-survey. Therefore, participants necessarily knew whether they had immediate program access or were assigned to the wait-list.

- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention? — Probably yes.**  
  The intervention was a self-guided online program. Participants assigned to immediate treatment received an access voucher and instructions; wait-list participants were informed that access would be delayed. The article does not identify blinded personnel responsible for delivery. The technical help desk could be contacted for technical questions, although staff reportedly did not inquire about or comment on the study.

- **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The article describes no trial-context-related deviations that were inconsistent with the protocol and capable of affecting the BDI outcome. Both groups were contacted at 4 weeks by email to ask whether problems or concerns had occurred, and the email reportedly contained no therapeutic information. The authors discuss the possibility that wait-list participants could have received other treatments, but this is presented as speculation and no data documenting differential non-protocol treatment are reported. Such speculation cannot establish trial-context-related deviations under RoB 2.

- **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Yes.**  
  The article reports an ITT analysis including all participants with baseline data. Missing post-treatment scores were addressed using multiple imputation, with 100 imputed data sets, stratified by treatment group. Participants were analysed according to randomized allocation. The ITT result is therefore suitable for estimating the effect of assignment, although the adequacy of handling missing data is assessed separately in Domain 3.

**Judgement rationale:**  
The trial was unblinded to participants, as is expected for immediate internet-program access versus a wait-list. However, the report does not document deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context, affected the outcome, and were unbalanced between groups. The assessed result was based on an ITT analysis retaining randomized group assignment. Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias** for the effect of assignment to Deprexis.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — No.**  
  BDI outcome data at 8 weeks were available for 90/105 (85.7%) wait-list participants and 80/105 (76.2%) Deprexis participants. Thus, data were missing for 15/105 (14.3%) wait-list participants and 25/105 (23.8%) Deprexis participants. Overall, outcome data were missing for 40/210 participants (19.0%). This amount of missing BDI data is not sufficiently small to conclude that missing values could not materially alter the effect estimate.

- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — No.**  
  The investigators used multiple imputation, but the report does not provide enough information to establish that the imputation model adequately addressed potential bias from missing BDI scores. Specifically, it does not specify the variables included in the imputation model, justify the missing-data assumptions, report the imputation model’s form, or provide sensitivity analyses using plausible missing-not-at-random scenarios. The reported comparison of baseline characteristics of completers and non-completers does not establish that the ITT result was unaffected by missing outcome data.

- **3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value? — Yes.**  
  The outcome was depressive symptom severity measured by self-report. In this setting, non-completion of an 8-week online reassessment could plausibly be related to participants’ depression symptoms, improvement, lack of improvement, engagement, or other health-related factors. The study does not provide reasons for missing BDI outcomes that would exclude such a relationship.

- **3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value? — Yes.**  
  Missing outcome data were more frequent in the Deprexis group than in the wait-list group: 23.8% versus 14.3%, respectively. This differential loss to follow-up, combined with the lack of reported reasons for non-completion and the self-reported depression outcome, supports the RoB 2 concern that missingness was likely related to outcome values. No sensitivity analysis demonstrates robustness of the effect estimate to plausible values of the missing BDI scores.

**Judgement rationale:**  
Nearly one-fifth of randomized participants lacked the 8-week BDI outcome, with a larger proportion missing in the immediate Deprexis group. The multiple-imputation procedure is insufficiently described to show that it removed bias, and no sensitivity analyses were reported. Given the differential missingness and the plausible relation between depression severity and response to an online reassessment, this domain is at **high risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **High risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  The BDI is a widely used 21-item self-report measure of depressive symptoms. The article identifies it as the primary outcome and describes established psychometric properties. The method itself was not inappropriate for measuring depressive symptom severity.

- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  Both groups completed the same internet-based questionnaire at baseline and after 8 weeks. The article does not describe different BDI instruments, thresholds, schedules, or data-collection procedures between groups.

- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants? — Yes.**  
  For a participant-reported outcome such as the BDI, the participant is the outcome assessor under RoB 2. Participants knew whether they had immediate access to Deprexis or were assigned to wait-list control.

- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Yes.**  
  BDI score is a subjective participant-reported outcome. Participants’ ratings of depressive symptoms could be influenced by expectations, perceived benefit, disappointment with delayed access, or awareness of treatment assignment.

- **4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Yes.**  
  The contrast was between a self-guided online psychological program and delayed access to that program. The study was necessarily unblinded, and the primary outcome was a participant-reported symptom measure. The article also reports treatment-group participants’ favourable subjective appraisals of Deprexis, including that 64.8% reported that expectations were met or exceeded and 47.9% reported that depressive symptoms had decreased because of Deprexis. These circumstances indicate that knowledge of assignment was likely to influence self-reported BDI symptoms.

**Judgement rationale:**  
Although the BDI was measured in the same manner in both groups, it is a subjective self-reported outcome assessed by participants who knew their allocation to immediate treatment or wait-list. In this context, awareness of allocation was likely to influence outcome reporting. This domain is therefore at **high risk of bias**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The trial was registered before publication and specified depressive symptom severity measured by the BDI as the primary outcome. However, the available registry record does not provide a detailed, finalized statistical analysis plan. It does not pre-specify the ANCOVA model, covariates, the multiple-imputation model, the number of imputations, the handling of missing data, the choice between per-protocol and ITT analyses, or the exact form of the primary numerical analysis. Therefore, it cannot be established that the reported ITT ANCOVA result was analysed according to a detailed pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

- **5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — Probably no.**  
  The article identifies BDI total score as the primary outcome, and the registry identifies BDI-assessed depression severity as the primary outcome. The reported main BDI result concerns the total BDI score at the protocol-described 8-week reassessment. Although BDI subscales were also reported, the article reports the primary total-score result rather than presenting only a selected subscale.

- **5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The investigators report both per-protocol and ITT analyses and state that the main results were computed using ANCOVA. The article does not provide a pre-specified detailed analysis plan that would determine the primary analysis and its missing-data strategy before outcome data were examined. It is therefore not possible to exclude selection from multiple potentially eligible analytical approaches, including complete-case/per-protocol and multiple-imputation ITT analyses, or alternative specifications of the ANCOVA/imputation model. There is no direct evidence that the selected result was chosen because of its magnitude, direction, or statistical significance.

**Judgement rationale:**  
The BDI total score was consistently identified as the primary outcome, which reduces concern about outcome-measure selection. However, no detailed pre-specified and date-stamped statistical analysis plan was available, and the report does not permit verification that the reported ITT ANCOVA result was selected independently of the observed data. In the absence of direct evidence of selective result reporting, this leads to **some concerns**, not high risk.

---

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement — **High risk of bias**

The overall judgement is **high risk of bias** because the study is judged at high risk of bias in:

1. **Bias due to missing outcome data:** 19.0% of randomized participants had missing 8-week BDI data, with more missing data in the Deprexis group (23.8%) than the wait-list group (14.3%). The multiple-imputation approach and assumptions were insufficiently reported, and no sensitivity analysis established robustness to plausible missing outcomes.

2. **Bias in measurement of the outcome:** The primary outcome was a participant-reported depression score measured in participants who knew whether they had immediate access to Deprexis or were assigned to wait-list. Given the subjective outcome and immediate-treatment versus delayed-treatment comparison, assessment was likely influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

There were additionally **some concerns** regarding the randomization process because allocation-sequence generation and allocation concealment were not described, and regarding selection of the reported result because no detailed pre-specified statistical analysis plan was available.

Based on a thorough analysis of the provided article using the RoB-2 framework, here is the detailed risk-of-bias assessment:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The study used an adequate method for random sequence generation ("block randomization determined by a computer-generated random number table, with stratification for operator and center"). Allocation was adequately concealed using "sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes" that were opened only after patient acceptance. Baseline characteristics (Table 2) showed minor variations typical of chance, with no severe imbalances suggesting a subversion of the randomization process.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** While blinding of participants and personnel (clinicians) was not possible due to the physical differences between the bracket systems, there is no evidence that this lack of blinding led to deviations from the intended interventions that arose specifically because of the trial context. Both groups were treated under a standardized protocol regarding bonding procedures and archwire sequence. Only 3 patients dropped out, meaning the analysis essentially captured all participants in the groups to which they were randomized, minimizing any potential impact of deviations.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants. Out of 138 subjects randomized, 135 (97.8%) completed the study and were included in the analysis. The missing data (2.2%) is sufficiently small that it could not have made a clinically important difference to the estimated effect of the intervention.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The outcomes measured included the total duration of treatment and the number of visits. Because the clinicians were not blinded to the intervention, the decision of when the orthodontic treatment was "complete" (leading to debonding) was made by a provider who knew which bracket system the patient had. Because this specific outcome reflects a decision made by the intervention provider, it involves clinical judgment that could theoretically be influenced by knowledge of the intervention. While data analysis was blinded, the clinical point of debonding was not, raising some concerns. 

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
- **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
- **Motivation:** The reported outcomes (treatment time, number of visits, bond failures) correspond exactly to standard, expected measurements for evaluating orthodontic efficiency. There is no indication that the reported results were selected from multiple measurements or from multiple analyses based on their statistical significance. 

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
- **Judgment:** Some concerns
- **Motivation:** The study is judged to raise "Some concerns" in Domain 4 (Bias in measurement of the outcome) because the unblinded clinicians were responsible for deciding when to conclude treatment, which dictates the primary outcome (treatment duration). As per RoB-2 rules, "Some concerns" in at least one domain leads to an overall judgment of "Some concerns".

Here is the detailed Risk of Bias (RoB 2) assessment for the provided randomized controlled trial.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process
**Judgment:** Some concerns
**Motivation:** The study states that "randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration, and arranged so that 25 test persons would be treated with the active drug and 24 with a placebo." Tablets were supplied in numbered boxes, and both subjects and clinicians were blinded, which suggests adequate allocation concealment (Probably Yes). However, there is no specific information provided regarding the method used to generate the random allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated random numbers, random number tables). While baseline characteristics appear balanced between the groups (Table 1), the lack of clear details on the sequence generation process warrants a judgment of "Some concerns."

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions
**Judgment:** Low risk of bias
**Motivation:** The study was a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Medication was provided in identical forms and numbered boxes, ensuring that participants, carers, and people delivering the interventions were unaware of the assigned intervention. There is no evidence that the trial context led to any deviations from the intended interventions that would affect the outcomes. Although 6 participants were excluded from the final analysis, they were excluded due to missing data (dropping out), which in the RoB 2 framework is evaluated under Domain 3 rather than representing an inappropriate analysis of assignment to intervention.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data
**Judgment:** High risk of bias
**Motivation:** The study randomized 49 patients (25 to topiramate, 24 to placebo), but only 43 were analyzed. The authors state: "Six subjects who failed to appear for more than one evaluation (all from the placebo group) dropped out of the study, and their data were not further analyzed." This means that 25% (6 out of 24) of the participants in the placebo group had missing outcome data, compared to 0% in the topiramate group. There is no evidence of sensitivity analyses or statistical methods used to correct for this missing data (a complete-case analysis was used). Given the substantial difference in dropout rates between the groups and the fact that all dropouts occurred in the placebo arm, it is highly likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value (e.g., patients dropping out because they realized they were not experiencing the desired weight loss). This severe imbalance introduces a high risk of attrition bias.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome
**Judgment:** Low risk of bias
**Motivation:** The methods of measuring the outcomes (body weight and standardized questionnaires like SF-36, Bf-S, and EWL-60-S) were appropriate and comparable across both intervention groups. Furthermore, both the participants and the outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignments throughout the 10-week study period. Therefore, it is highly unlikely that the assessment of the outcomes was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result
**Judgment:** Some concerns
**Motivation:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a pre-registered trial protocol that was finalized before unblinded data were available. While the reported outcomes align with the aims stated in the introduction, and there is no clear indication that the authors selected specific results from multiple eligible analyses or measurements based on statistical significance, the lack of a publicly available, date-stamped protocol prevents a definitive confirmation. Consequently, this raises some concerns regarding the potential for selective reporting.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement
**Overall Judgment:** High risk of bias
**Motivation:** The study is judged to be at a high risk of bias overall. While domains regarding deviations from intended interventions and outcome measurement demonstrate a low risk of bias, the study suffers from a critical flaw in missing outcome data (Domain 3). The disproportionate and unadjusted dropout rate completely isolated to the placebo group (25% attrition vs. 0% in the treatment group) substantially lowers confidence in the validity of the results, mandating an overall high risk of bias judgment. Additionally, there are some concerns regarding the randomization process and the selection of the reported results due to a lack of detailed methodological reporting and the absence of a pre-registered protocol.

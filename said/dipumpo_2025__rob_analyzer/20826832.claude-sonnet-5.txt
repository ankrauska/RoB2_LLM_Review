# ROB 2 Assessment Report

## Preliminary Considerations

**Study design:** Individually-randomized parallel-group trial (three arms: Standard Care, Low Intensity Intervention, High Intensity Intervention)

**Comparison assessed:** Low Intensity Intervention (mail contact) vs. Standard Care; High Intensity Intervention (mail + telephone/home visit) vs. Standard Care

**Outcome assessed:** Mammography screening completion at 6 months (electronic record-based measure, primary/objective outcome)

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect), as explicitly stated by the authors

**Sources used:** Journal article only (no protocol, no SAP, no trial registry record identified in the text)

---

## Domain 1: Risk of Bias Arising from the Randomization Process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- The allocation sequence generation is reported as adequate: "by using a computerized random number generator program, 500 women were randomly selected" and subsequent allocation used "a stratified randomization method blocking by age... using a computerized random generator program." This satisfies signalling question 1.1 (Yes/Probably yes – random component present).
- Allocation concealment (signalling question 1.2) is **not described**. The paper states that the baseline database was sent to an external coordination center (Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center) for random allocation, which is suggestive of central randomization and thus a degree of concealment. However, no explicit description of the concealment mechanism (e.g., who generated the sequence, whether recruiters could foresee allocations) is provided. This warrants a response of "Probably yes" at best, but with genuine ambiguity.
- Baseline balance (signalling question 1.3): The authors state "No significant differences were observed between groups in the demographic characteristics of the participants," supporting an answer of "No" (no evidence of problems with randomization).
- Because concealment is not explicitly and fully described (only inferred from the external allocation process), and there is no information on the specific mechanism preventing foreknowledge of assignment among clinic staff prior to central allocation, the domain cannot be rated "Low risk." Given probable-but-not-certain adequate concealment, and no baseline imbalance concerns, "Some concerns" is the appropriate judgment.

**Limitation/Ambiguity noted:** No explicit description of the concealment mechanism was found in the article; this is reported as a limitation of this assessment rather than an assumption of bias.

---

## Domain 2: Risk of Bias Due to Deviations from Intended Interventions (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Participants were **not blinded** to their intervention assignment (explicitly stated: "Blinding participants to their intervention group was not possible in this study"). Personnel delivering the intervention (lay health educators, clinic staff) were also aware of assignment, since delivering mail/telephone/home visit contacts inherently reveals group status. This triggers signalling question 2.3 regarding whether deviations from intended intervention arose because of trial context.
- The authors explicitly discuss potential contamination: "Lack of blinding in this study could have produced a certain level of cross contamination given that women, especially in the control arm, could have demanded more information than usual." This is an acknowledged deviation arising from trial context (awareness of assignment potentially prompting extra-protocol behavior in the control group).
- The authors argue this would bias results *toward the null* (i.e., conservatively), potentially underestimating the true effect size, rather than exaggerating it. This is a plausible, author-articulated direction of bias.
- However, there is no formal quantification of the extent of this contamination, and the balance of such deviations across groups cannot be verified with data (signalling question 2.5). Given the acknowledged potential deviation from intended intervention arising from trial context, but with no strong evidence that it substantially affected outcomes in a differential/unbalanced way (rather the reasoning is that it would dilute differences, not spuriously create them), this equates to "Some concerns" rather than "High."
- Analysis: The study used an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis based on electronic records for all randomized participants (signalling question 2.6 – Yes), which supports a lower risk in the analysis-related sub-domain.

**Limitation/Ambiguity noted:** The magnitude and balance of cross-contamination in the control group is not quantified; this remains a self-reported limitation by the study authors themselves, without empirical verification.

## Domain 3: Risk of Bias Due to Missing Outcome Data

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:**
- Outcome data (mammography screening) were obtained from **electronic clinical records**, not just self-report, for all 500 randomized participants. The paper states the intention-to-treat analysis "was conducted using electronic clinical records... reviewed for all women and compliance was estimated using the baseline denominators of each group" (signalling question 3.1 – Yes, data available for all randomized participants via electronic records regardless of survey attrition).
- While self-reported follow-up survey completion was 92–93% (some loss to follow-up: 6.6%–8.4% across groups, fairly balanced), the **primary outcome analyzed here (electronic record-based screening)** was ascertainable for all 500 participants regardless of survey non-response, because it relied on administrative/clinical records rather than self-report.
- Reasons for loss to follow-up on the self-report survey (refusal, inability to contact, one death) were reported and were similar in proportion across groups, without evidence that missingness was related to the true outcome value in a way that would bias the electronic-record-based result.
- Therefore, for the outcome as defined and analyzed (electronic record data), missing outcome data does not appear to be a material concern.

---

## Domain 4: Risk of Bias in Measurement of the Outcome

**Judgment: Low risk**

**Motivation:**
- The outcome (mammography completion) was measured via **electronic records** from the radiology unit and clinical charts – an objective, observer-independent data source not involving subjective judgement (signalling question 4.1 – method not inappropriate).
- Measurement/ascertainment method was **identical across groups**: mammograms were free and equally accessible to all three arms, and the same electronic registry was used to ascertain completion regardless of assignment (signalling question 4.2 – No, ascertainment did not differ between groups).
- Critically, the article explicitly states: "Staff at the clinic and at the radiology unit where participants got their mammograms were blinded to the allocation group," and the data analysis was conducted by investigators "blinded to the group assignment of participants" (addressing signalling question 4.3 – outcome assessors unaware of intervention assignment).
- Given blinding of both the personnel recording/verifying the outcome and the analysts, and the objective, low-judgement nature of the outcome (presence/absence of a mammogram record), there is minimal opportunity for differential measurement error.

---

## Domain 5: Risk of Bias in Selection of the Reported Result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- No trial protocol, prospectively registered analysis plan, or statistical analysis plan was available for review; only the published journal article was accessible as a source (signalling question 5.1 – No information on whether analysis followed a pre-specified plan finalized before unblinded data access).
- The primary outcome (mammography screening, electronic record-based) is reported clearly and appears to be the pre-specified main outcome consistent with the introduction/methods framing, with no evidence of selective reporting among multiple candidate outcome measurements (self-report vs. electronic record are both reported transparently, side by side in Table 2, rather than one being suppressed).
- No evidence of selective reporting of alternative analyses (e.g., multiple adjustment models, alternative cut-points) was found; a single, clearly-specified ITT analysis using baseline denominators is reported.
- However, in the absence of a registered protocol or SAP, it is **not possible to definitively rule out** selective reporting of outcome measurements or analyses. This absence of verifiable pre-specification is the basis for "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

**Limitation/Ambiguity noted:** No trial registry entry, protocol, or SAP was cited or available in the article; this precludes a definitive judgment of "Low risk" and is reported as a specific ambiguity/limitation of this assessment, not assumed to indicate bias.

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall Judgment: High risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
Applying the Cochrane RoB 2 decision rules: the result was judged as "Some concerns" in **three domains** (Domain 1 – randomization process; Domain 2 – deviations from intended interventions; Domain 5 – selection of the reported result), with "Low risk" in two domains (Domain 3 – missing outcome data; Domain 4 – measurement of outcome). No individual domain was rated "High risk" in isolation.

However, per RoB 2 guidance: *"'Some concerns' in multiple domains may lead the review authors to decide on an overall judgement of 'High' risk of bias for that outcome... The study is judged to have some concerns for multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result."*

In this case, the accumulation of "Some concerns" across three separate domains — particularly the combination of (a) unverified/unclear allocation concealment mechanism, (b) acknowledged and undocumented lack of blinding with potential (author-acknowledged) contamination between arms, and (c) absence of any pre-registered protocol or SAP to verify selective outcome/analysis reporting — cumulatively lowers confidence in the robustness and full trustworthiness of the reported effect size, even though the direction of the primary finding (a large, significant increase in screening with intervention) is unlikely to be entirely explained by these biases given the very large effect sizes observed (6% vs. 51.8% vs. 70.1%).

Therefore, the overall judgment for this result is **High risk of bias**, driven by the cumulative impact of multiple "Some concerns" domains rather than any single "High risk" domain.

**Reported Limitation of This Assessment:** This evaluation was based solely on the published journal article; no trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial registry record was available for cross-verification. This constitutes an important ambiguity affecting Domains 1, 2, and 5 in particular, and judgments in these domains rely on information reported within the manuscript alone.

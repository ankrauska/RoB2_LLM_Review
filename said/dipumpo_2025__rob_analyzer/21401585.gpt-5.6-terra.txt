## RoB 2 assessment

**Target result assessed:** the effect of **assignment to constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT)** versus **bimanual training (BIM)** on the primary outcome, **unimanual capacity measured by the Melbourne Assessment of Unilateral Upper Limb Function (MUUL), at 26 weeks**. The effect estimate reported was an estimated mean difference of **4.4 percentage points (95% CI 2.2 to 6.7; p<0.001)** favouring CIMT.  
**Effect of interest:** intention-to-treat effect (effect of assignment to intervention).  
**Design:** individually randomized, matched-pairs parallel-group trial.

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

* **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — Yes.**  
  The authors report that, after participants were matched in pairs by age, sex, side of hemiplegia, and MUUL function, “children were randomized to pairs using a **computer generated list of random numbers**.” This is an explicitly random sequence-generation method.

* **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — Probably yes.**  
  The report states that allocation used “**concealed envelopes opened by non-study personnel**.” This supports concealment from personnel directly involved in the study. The report does not state whether envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, or opened only after irreversible enrolment. This is a reporting limitation; however, the explicit description of concealed envelopes and their opening by non-study personnel supports a judgement that allocation was probably concealed.

* **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No.**  
  The authors state that “there were no differences between groups on demographic or baseline measures.” Table I shows broadly comparable groups for age, sex, functional classifications, baseline MUUL, AHA, JTTHF, grip strength, sensation, and concurrent therapy. There was an imbalance in side of hemiplegia (left hemiplegia: 50% CIMT versus 36% BIM), but the report explains that four children could not be matched on side of hemiplegia. This isolated difference does not indicate a failure of randomization, especially as side of hemiplegia was included as a covariate in the analysis.

**Judgement and motivation**

The study used computer-generated random numbers and reported concealed allocation performed by non-study personnel. No baseline imbalance indicates that the randomization process failed. Although envelope safeguards are incompletely described, there is no reported evidence that allocation was predictable or manipulable. Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

* **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Participants necessarily knew whether they wore the CIMT glove or participated in bimanual training. Blinding of children was not feasible because the interventions were visibly and practically different.

* **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Yes.**  
  Therapists delivering CIMT and BIM necessarily knew the allocated intervention. The intervention content was explicitly adapted according to group: CIMT participants wore a glove on the unimpaired hand, while BIM participants received explicit instruction about the use of both hands.

* **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report documents that both groups were planned to receive 60 hours of treatment over 10 days. Compliance was reported as 100% in CIMT and 97% in BIM. Some participants received 54 rather than 60 hours because of illness or public holidays, and one BIM participant received 42 hours because of illness. These shortfalls are reported as arising from illness or public holidays, not from the trial context, knowledge of intervention allocation, or differential behaviour by personnel intended to undermine the trial comparison.  
  The groups received the same overall amount and content of intervention, in the same setting, with the same core therapist team. The paper reports no material differential co-intervention during the main intervention period. One CIMT participant received botulinum toxin A between the 3- and 26-week assessments; the report does not state that this arose because of trial participation or allocation.

* **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Probably yes.**  
  The authors explicitly state that analyses were undertaken “on an **intention-to-treat basis according to CONSORT guidelines**.” The reported GEE models compared the randomized intervention groups longitudinally and included baseline, 3-week, and 26-week measurements. The analysis did not reclassify participants according to treatment received.

**Judgement and motivation**

Participants and personnel were unblinded, as expected for these visibly different rehabilitation interventions. However, there is no reported evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment caused important trial-context-related deviations that were unbalanced between CIMT and BIM and likely to affect the MUUL outcome. The study states that analyses were intention-to-treat and analysed groups as randomized. The minor reductions in attended therapy hours were largely due to illness or public holidays and do not constitute evidence of bias due to deviations from intended intervention for the assignment effect.

Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

* **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Probably yes.**  
  Sixty-four children were randomized; 62 received their allocated intervention. The report identifies one dropout from the BIM group on day 2 because of pre-existing emotional and behavioural difficulties. The baseline table includes 32 CIMT and 31 BIM participants, indicating that one BIM participant was not included in the reported baseline analytic sample.  
  The report describes “high retention with very few dropouts” and presents 3- and 26-week results. For this continuous outcome, the number of participants without reported data appears sufficiently small relative to the 64 randomized participants that it is unlikely, by itself, to have materially altered the estimated between-group MUUL effect.

* **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — Not applicable.**  
  Because outcome data appear available for nearly all randomized participants, further assessment is not required by the RoB 2 algorithm.

**Judgement and motivation**

There is minor inconsistency in the reporting of participant numbers: 64 were randomized, 62 received allocated treatment, and Table I reports 63 participants. The supplied report does not provide sufficiently detailed numerical follow-up data for each MUUL assessment time point to reconcile every discrepancy fully. This is a reporting limitation.

Nevertheless, the documented loss appears to be very small, and the study reports high retention. The MUUL is a continuous outcome, for which outcome availability for approximately 95% or more of randomized participants will often be sufficient for a low-risk judgement. There is no evidence of substantial differential attrition, outcome-related missingness, or an analysis that inadequately handled a meaningful amount of missing MUUL data.

Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — **Low risk of bias**

**Signalling questions**

* **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — No.**  
  MUUL was a pre-specified primary outcome and was selected to measure the quality of movement of the impaired upper limb. The report provides evidence of scoring reliability in this population: the independent rater’s intraclass correlation coefficient for the total MUUL percentage score was 0.93 (95% CI 0.84–0.97).

* **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — No.**  
  MUUL was assessed at the same prespecified time points—baseline, 3 weeks, and 26 weeks—in both groups. The same assessment procedure was used, and assessments were video-recorded. There is no indication that the CIMT or BIM intervention created different opportunities for the outcome to be measured or that outcome ascertainment differed systematically by group.

* **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received? — No.**  
  Although the occupational therapists and physiotherapists conducting outcome assessments were aware of allocation, the primary outcomes were “**video-taped and scored in random order by trained occupational therapists masked to group allocation**.” The blinded scorers were the relevant outcome assessors for the MUUL score used in the reported result.

**Judgement and motivation**

The MUUL was measured with the same method and at the same scheduled time points in both groups. Importantly, the recorded assessments were scored in random order by trained raters who were masked to allocation. This safeguards against differential assessment of this observer-rated functional outcome. The report provides no evidence that intervention allocation could have influenced the blinded scoring process.

Therefore, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result — **Some concerns**

**Signalling questions**

* **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  The report cites an earlier design/protocol publication: “INCITE: a randomised trial comparing constraint induced movement therapy and bimanual training in children with congenital hemiplegia,” published in 2010. However, the article as provided does not reproduce or provide sufficient information to verify whether the specific 26-week MUUL analysis, the GEE model, covariate adjustment, handling of missing data, and selection of the reported effect estimate were all specified in a statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available. No statistical analysis plan is provided or referenced.

* **5.2 Was the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  MUUL was measured at baseline, 3 weeks, and 26 weeks. Thus, there were multiple time points within the unimanual-capacity outcome domain. The paper reports results at both post-intervention time points, which is reassuring, but without review of the pre-specified protocol and/or statistical analysis plan it cannot be verified whether these were all planned measurements or whether other eligible measures or time points were collected and not fully reported.

* **5.3 Was the result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The article reports adjusted GEE analyses, including covariates for matching characteristics. It does not provide a pre-specified analysis plan that would establish whether this was the sole intended analysis or whether alternative analyses—such as unadjusted analyses, analyses incorporating the matched-pair structure differently, analyses of change scores, or different approaches to missing data—were conducted and selectively reported.

**Judgement and motivation**

There is no direct evidence that the 26-week MUUL result was selected because it was favourable to CIMT. The article reports both primary outcomes and both planned follow-up time points, rather than reporting only one positive outcome or time point. However, RoB 2 requires low risk only when the result can be verified against a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available, and when selection from multiple eligible measurements and analyses is unlikely.

The existence of a cited protocol publication is not sufficient, from the information supplied, to establish that the exact analysis and result were pre-specified and that no other eligible analyses or measurements were selectively omitted. This unresolved ambiguity leads to **some concerns** rather than a high-risk judgement.

Therefore, this domain is judged as raising **some concerns**.

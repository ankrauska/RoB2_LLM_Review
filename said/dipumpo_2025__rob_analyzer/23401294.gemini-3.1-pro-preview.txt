Based on the provided Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) framework, here is the detailed risk of bias assessment for the randomized controlled trial comparing the Spinomed orthosis and posture-training support (PTS):

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process: High risk of bias**
*   **Motivation:** The study reports that subjects were randomly allocated by picking the name of the orthosis from a bag. While this introduces a random component (answering "Yes" to whether the sequence was random), there is no indication that adequate allocation concealment methods (such as sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes or centralized allocation) were used. Picking a name from a bag is highly susceptible to manipulation or anticipation by the enroller, meaning the allocation sequence was likely not concealed before assignment (answering "Probably No" to sequence concealment). Although baseline characteristics were reported as not significantly different, the inadequate concealment method in a small sample (n=18) introduces a high risk of selection bias. 

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions: Low risk of bias**
*   **Motivation:** Given the nature of the intervention (visible spinal orthoses), neither the participants nor the personnel fitting the devices could be blinded to the assigned group. However, the study protocol involved an immediate assessment after wearing the orthosis for a short adaptation period of 1.5 hours. Because of this immediate, single-session study design, there was no opportunity for deviations from the intended interventions that arose due to the trial context (e.g., non-protocol co-interventions or non-adherence over time). All 18 randomized participants received their assigned intervention and were analyzed in their respective groups, meaning an appropriate analysis was used.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data: Low risk of bias**
*   **Motivation:** The study states that 18 participants were recruited and completed the study procedure (8 allocated to PTS and 10 to Spinomed). The results tables confirm that data for all 18 randomized participants were available and included in the final analysis. Since there were no dropouts or missing outcome data, the risk of bias in this domain is low.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome: Low risk of bias**
*   **Motivation:** The outcomes were measured using the SOT (EquiTest system) and LOS test (Balance Master system), which are standardized, objective force-plate measurement systems. Even though the participants and the assessors (the researchers conducting the tests) were aware of the intervention received, the outcomes are objective, automated mechanical readings of center of mass and sway. Therefore, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result: Some concerns**
*   **Motivation:** There is no mention of a pre-specified, publicly available study protocol or clinical trial registry entry finalized before unblinded data were available. While the reported outcomes (balance score, directional control, and reaction time) align with the standard outputs of the EquiTest and Balance Master systems, the absence of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan means we cannot definitively rule out that the reported results or analyses were selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement: High risk of bias**
*   **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at an overall High risk of bias. While domains 2, 3, and 4 are at low risk, the inadequate method of allocation concealment (picking names from a bag) yields a High risk of bias arising from the randomization process. According to the RoB 2 criteria, if the study is judged to be at high risk of bias in at least one domain, the overall judgment must be High risk of bias.

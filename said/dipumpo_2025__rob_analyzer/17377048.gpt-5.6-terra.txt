## Scope of this RoB 2 assessment

**Study design:** randomized technical imaging study. Patients were randomized to CT collimation: group A, 64 × 0.6 mm; group B, 20 × 1.2 mm. Each participant then had images reconstructed at **both** reconstruction increments (3.0 and 1.5 mm) and at five diastolic reconstruction intervals.

**Result assessed:** the reported comparison of coronary calcium Agatston scores between **1.5-mm versus 3.0-mm reconstruction increments**, using the reconstructions obtained for the enrolled patients.

**Effect of interest:** effect of the reconstruction approach as applied to the acquired CT data. This is intrinsically a within-participant/matched comparison, because both reconstruction increments were calculated for every participant. The reported patient randomization was to collimation, not to reconstruction increment.

**Important limitation of applicability:** RoB 2 is intended for a randomized comparison of interventions or strategies assigned to participants. Here, the principal reported reconstruction-increment comparison was not randomized at the participant level: every participant contributed data under both increments. Therefore, the randomization process does not protect the principal increment comparison in the same way as it would protect a conventional randomized parallel-group treatment comparison. The judgements below apply the five standard RoB 2 domains as closely as the report permits.

---

### 1. Bias arising from the randomization process  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

* **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random? — No information.**  
  The article states that patients “were assigned to two groups at random,” but does not report how the allocation sequence was generated. There is no description of computer generation, random-number table, random blocks, minimization, or another random component.

* **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions? — No information.**  
  The report provides no information about allocation concealment. It does not describe central randomization, sealed opaque envelopes, pharmacy-controlled allocation, or another method preventing foreknowledge of the collimation assignment.

* **1.3 Did baseline differences suggest a problem with the randomization process? — No information.**  
  The report states only that age did not differ significantly between groups. It does not provide a full baseline comparison by randomized group for relevant participant or clinical characteristics. There is no clear evidence of an implausible imbalance, but the available baseline information is insufficient for a robust assessment.

**Motivation**

The study labels allocation to the two collimation groups as random, but reports neither sequence generation nor allocation concealment. Under RoB 2, the mere statement that a study was randomized is insufficient to establish that the sequence was random or adequately concealed.

Moreover, the primary reconstruction-increment comparison was performed within participants: each participant had both 1.5-mm and 3.0-mm reconstructed datasets. Thus, randomization to collimation does not directly randomize the comparison assessed here. This creates an additional design-specific limitation: the reported randomization cannot fully address bias in the within-person reconstruction comparison.

### 2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

* **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably no / not materially relevant.**  
  The intervention assessed is a technical image-reconstruction setting applied after image acquisition. The article does not indicate that participants were informed of, or could alter, the reconstruction increment.

* **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants’ assigned intervention during the trial? — Probably yes, but not likely to cause relevant deviations.**  
  Imaging personnel necessarily performed the scanning and reconstruction procedures. However, the reconstruction increments were specified technical settings, and no participant-care intervention was delivered differently according to the reconstruction increment.

* **2.3 Were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context? — Probably no.**  
  The report states that, for each patient, five datasets were created at 50%, 55%, 60%, 65%, and 70% of the R–R interval, and that two datasets were calculated at reconstruction increments of 3.0 and 1.5 mm. No departures from these stated reconstruction procedures are reported.

* **2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention? — Probably yes for the within-participant increment comparison.**  
  The report states that the mean scores at 1.5-mm and 3.0-mm increments were compared using a sign test. Since each participant contributed reconstructions under both increments, a paired comparison is appropriate in principle.

**Motivation**

The outcome comparison concerns reconstruction settings applied to the same acquired image data, rather than a participant-delivered therapeutic intervention. Consequently, participant behaviour, clinical co-interventions, treatment adherence, and crossover are not plausible important sources of bias for this technical comparison.

The investigators report that both increments and all planned reconstruction intervals were created for each patient, with no reported technical failures or departures from the specified reconstruction protocol. The paired nature of the data was recognized through use of a sign test. The article does not report deviations arising from the study context that could have altered the Agatston-score comparison.

The exclusion of four patients with no coronary calcium is not classified here as a deviation from intended reconstruction, because the article reports that calcium was absent on all reconstructions for those participants; it is considered under availability and analysis limitations rather than as a failure to implement the reconstruction intervention.

### 3. Bias due to missing outcome data  
**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

* **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized? — Probably yes.**  
  Forty patients were enrolled. The article reports that four were excluded because “no coronary calcium was detected on any of the reconstructions.” Thus, the report indicates that the relevant calcium-score outcome was evaluated in those four participants and was zero across all reconstructions, rather than being unmeasured. The remaining 36 participants had reported Agatston-score analyses.

* **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data? — Not applicable.**

**Motivation**

There is no report of loss to follow-up, failed scanning, unavailable datasets, missing reconstructions, or missing calcium-score measurements among the 40 enrolled participants. The four excluded participants were excluded because no calcium was found on **any** reconstruction, which indicates observed zero results rather than missing outcome data.

The exclusion may affect the population represented by the reported mean calcium scores, because the analysis was restricted to participants with detectable calcium. However, this is not missing-outcome-data bias as defined by RoB 2. For the reconstruction-increment contrast, participants with scores of zero at both increments would not, on the information reported, create a differential missing-data mechanism between increments. No evidence indicates that outcome availability differed according to the reconstruction increment.

### 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Signalling-question assessment**

* **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate? — Probably no.**  
  Coronary artery calcium was defined as more than two contiguous pixels above 130 Hounsfield units, and Agatston and volumetric scores were calculated using a dedicated calcium-scoring workstation. This is a defined and standardized scoring approach.

* **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups? — Probably no.**  
  The same scanner, workstation, calcium threshold, and scoring approach were used for the reconstructed datasets. The article does not report use of different measurement thresholds or different scoring methods between reconstruction increments.

* **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants? — No information.**  
  The report states that a radiologist and a cardiologist differentiated calcium from noise and placed regions of interest around coronary calcium. It does not state that either assessor was blinded to reconstruction increment, reconstruction interval, collimation group, or the purpose of the comparison.

* **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — Probably yes.**  
  The assessment was not entirely automated. Although lesions were automatically identified by the workstation, the radiologist and cardiologist then differentiated calcification from noise and placed regions of interest around lesions. These steps involve judgement. Knowledge of reconstruction increment or reconstruction interval could influence whether borderline high-density foci were classified as calcium or noise and how lesions were delineated.

* **4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received? — No information.**  
  The report does not provide blinding procedures, independent blinded rereading, reproducibility data, interobserver agreement, adjudication procedures, or evidence demonstrating that the manual classification and ROI placement were unaffected by knowledge of reconstruction settings.

**Motivation**

The method used to quantify coronary calcium is described and is not inherently inappropriate. However, the outcome was not purely machine-generated: the workstation identified candidate lesions, after which two experienced readers differentiated calcium from noise and placed regions of interest. These reader-dependent steps are particularly relevant to the study question because the comparison concerns different reconstruction increments and intervals, which can alter lesion conspicuity, partial-volume effects, and image appearance.

The article does not report blinded scoring of reconstructed datasets. It also does not state that datasets were presented in randomized order, that reconstruction labels were masked, or that reader decisions were made independently of knowledge of the reconstruction settings. Since the readers’ subjective decisions could alter the measured calcium score, and since the report provides no information to exclude influence of that knowledge, this domain is judged at **high risk of bias** under the RoB 2 algorithm.

### 5. Bias in selection of the reported result  
**Judgement: Some concerns**

**Signalling-question assessment**

* **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analysed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available? — No information.**  
  No protocol, trial-registration record, statistical analysis plan, or dated prespecified analysis document is reported or available in the article.

* **5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain? — No information.**  
  Multiple eligible measurements were generated: Agatston score, volumetric score, coefficients of variation, five reconstruction intervals, two reconstruction increments, two collimation groups, lesion detection findings, and percentile-based risk categories. The article does not provide a prespecified hierarchy identifying which reconstruction interval, scoring method, or summary measure was primary.

* **5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected from multiple eligible analyses of the data? — No information.**  
  The report provides several analyses and summaries, including mean values, ranges, coefficients of variation, correlations, risk-category assignments, separate group analyses, and comparisons between reconstruction increments. Without a protocol or statistical analysis plan, it cannot be verified that the reported analyses were selected independently of the observed results.

**Motivation**

The article describes planned reconstruction settings and analyses in its Methods section, but there is no externally available or dated prespecified protocol or analysis plan establishing that these analytic decisions were finalized before the results were known. The investigation created numerous correlated outcomes and analytic options: five reconstruction intervals, two reconstruction increments, two collimations, two calcium-scoring metrics, variability measures, and risk-group classifications.

The article does not provide evidence that the reported numerical comparisons were chosen independently of their magnitude, direction, or statistical significance. Conversely, it does not provide direct evidence that a more favourable or statistically significant result was selectively reported. In accordance with RoB 2, this uncertainty leads to **some concerns**, rather than high risk, for selective reporting.

### 6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement  
**Judgement: High risk of bias**

**Motivation**

The overall judgement is **high risk of bias** because the result is judged at **high risk of bias in Domain 4 (measurement of the outcome)**. The outcome required reader-dependent differentiation of calcification from noise and placement of regions of interest, yet the article does not report blinding of those readers to reconstruction increment or reconstruction interval. Knowledge of the reconstruction setting could plausibly influence these judgement-based scoring decisions.

There are also **some concerns** regarding the randomization process and selection of the reported result. In particular:

* randomization was stated but neither sequence generation nor allocation concealment was described;
* the principal reconstruction-increment comparison was within participant and was not itself randomized at the participant level;
* no protocol, registration entry, or prespecified statistical analysis plan was available to verify that reported outcomes and analyses were selected independently of the results.

Accordingly, following the specified RoB 2 rule that high risk in at least one domain results in an overall **high risk of bias** judgement, the assessed result is at **high risk of bias**.

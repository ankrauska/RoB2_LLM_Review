# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Spherical Aberration from Myopic Excimer Laser Ablation for Aspheric and Non-Aspheric Profiles (paired-eye RCT, n=15 patients/30 eyes)

**Design:** Individually-randomized paired-eye (intra-patient) trial
**Experimental intervention:** Aspheric ablation profile (ASA)
**Comparator intervention:** Non-aspheric ablation profile (TSA)
**Outcome assessed:** Induction of spherical aberration (SA) at 6 mm analysis zone / RAWS parameter at 3 months
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (assumed, as no per-protocol analysis was described)

---

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process — Judgement: SOME CONCERNS**

- Sequence generation: "assigned at random using Microsoft Excel's random number function" — this is an adequate, truly random method (Signalling Q1.1: Yes).
- Allocation concealment: The article provides **no information** on how/whether the allocation sequence was concealed from the surgeon/investigators prior to eye assignment (Signalling Q1.2: No information). Given this is a paired-eye design where both eyes of the same patient are operated on the same day, there is a plausible risk that knowledge of which eye would receive which treatment could not meaningfully be "concealed" from the surgeon at the time of the procedure, but no explicit description is given.
- Baseline balance: Baseline spherical equivalent refraction was similar between groups (−2.43 vs −2.54 D, p=0.87), and no other baseline imbalance is reported that suggests problems with randomization (Signalling Q1.3: No).
- Because allocation concealment is not described (No information) combined with no evidence of baseline imbalance, per the RoB2 algorithm this maps to **"Some concerns."**

*Limitation noted: The paper does not report the method of allocation concealment, which precludes a "Low risk" judgement.*

---

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions — Judgement: LOW RISK**

- This is a paired-eye trial with a technical/surgical intervention (laser ablation profile) with no interaction with participant behavior. Participants could plausibly be aware of the general nature of the treatment but the intervention is delivered entirely by the laser platform/surgeon at time of surgery — there is minimal room for participant or personnel deviation from assigned intervention after randomization is executed (the surgeon simply selects the pre-assigned ablation program for each eye).
- No non-protocol interventions, co-interventions, or crossovers are reported or plausible in this context.
- No indication that failure to implement the intervention as intended occurred; the paper reports achieved refractive outcomes consistent with intended ablation programming.
- Analysis: All 30 eyes were apparently analyzed as randomized (no dropouts described), consistent with an appropriate (ITT-like) analysis for this outcome.
- **Judgement: Low risk of bias.**

---

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data — Judgement: SOME CONCERNS**

- The study states 15 patients (30 eyes) were enrolled, and results/tables consistently report data for both ASA and TSA groups seemingly with n=15 eyes each — suggesting outcome data were available for all randomized eyes.
- However, the article does not explicitly report a CONSORT-style flow diagram, does not explicitly state whether any patients/eyes were lost to follow-up at the 3-month visit, and does not explicitly confirm 100% data completeness for the wavefront/SA outcome for all 30 eyes.
- Given the paired design, it seems likely (based on consistent n reported in tables) that no eyes were missing, but this is not explicitly confirmed by the authors (no statement such as "all patients completed follow-up" or "no eyes were lost to follow-up").
- Because of this absence of explicit reporting on completeness of outcome data (despite the tables suggesting completeness), a judgement of "Some concerns" is warranted rather than "Low risk," in strict adherence to RoB2 principles that absence of information on missing data typically raises concern.

*Limitation noted: No explicit statement of the number of eyes with follow-up data or reasons for any missing data (if any) is provided.*

---

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome — Judgement: SOME CONCERNS**

- Outcome measurement method: SA was measured using a validated, objective, automated wavefront aberrometer (WASCA), which is an objective, observer-independent measurement method not involving subjective judgement — thus the method is not inappropriate (Signalling Q4.1: No).
- Measurement comparability between groups: Because this is an intra-patient paired-eye design, and the same standardized wavefront device/protocol was used for both eyes of each patient at the same visit, measurement methodology is unlikely to have differed between groups (Signalling Q4.2: No).
- Blinding of outcome assessors: The article does **not report** whether the technician/examiner performing/analyzing the wavefront aberrometry was masked to which eye received ASA vs. TSA. Given automated aberrometry equipment, the risk of bias from lack of blinding is likely low because the aberrometer output is an objective numerical measurement not subject to observer interpretation. However, since blinding status is not explicitly stated, this creates uncertainty (Signalling Q4.3: No information).
- Since the outcome (SA measured by wavefront device) is an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" analogous to an automated test, knowledge of intervention assignment is unlikely to influence the objective aberrometric measurement itself. This supports a lower risk, but because the paper does not explicitly confirm masking procedures, "Some concerns" is the more conservative and defensible judgement rather than "Low."

*Limitation noted: No explicit statement regarding masking of examiners/technicians performing wavefront analysis to treatment allocation.*

---

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result — Judgement: SOME CONCERNS**

- There is no trial registration, published protocol, or statistical analysis plan (SAP) referenced or available for review; the paper does not indicate that outcome measures and analyses were pre-specified before unblinded data were available (Signalling Q5.1: No information).
- Multiple outcome measurements were taken (SA at multiple analysis zones: 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5, 5.5, 6, 6.5, 7 mm) and multiple derived parameters (SA change per diopter, RAWS parameter, coma, total HOA RMS) were reported. While the paper appears to report all measured zones/parameters transparently (Table 1, Table 2, Fig. 7, Fig. 8, Table 3), the absence of a pre-registered protocol makes it impossible to confirm that a comprehensive, prespecified set of outcomes/analyses was reported without selective emphasis.
- The primary outcome variable (SA induction, RAWS parameter) appears consistently and fully reported across all tested analysis zones with corresponding p-values (not selectively presented only for significant zones), which is reassuring and reduces likelihood of selective reporting for this specific result.
- However, due to the lack of independently verifiable pre-specified analysis plan, "Some concerns" is the appropriate default judgement.

*Limitation noted: No trial registry entry or protocol/SAP was cited or found, preventing verification that reported analyses match a pre-specified plan.*

---

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement: **HIGH RISK OF BIAS**

**Rationale:** Applying the RoB2 algorithm strictly:
- Domain 1 (Randomization process): Some concerns
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions): Low risk
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data): Some concerns
- Domain 4 (Measurement of the outcome): Some concerns
- Domain 5 (Selection of the reported result): Some concerns

Per the RoB2 decision rule: *"The study is judged to have some concerns for multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result → High risk of bias."*

Here, four of the five domains (1, 3, 4, and 5) are judged as "Some concerns," which cumulatively substantially lowers confidence in the trustworthiness of the reported result regarding induction of spherical aberration between ASA and TSA ablation profiles. Although no single domain independently reached "High risk," the accumulation of unresolved uncertainties — regarding allocation concealment, completeness of outcome data reporting, blinding/masking of outcome assessment, and absence of a pre-specified analysis plan — collectively warrant an overall judgement of **High risk of bias** for this result, per Cochrane RoB2 rules.

**Key limitations/ambiguities identified during this assessment (reported as found, not assumed):**
1. No description of allocation concealment method.
2. No explicit CONSORT-style confirmation of complete follow-up/outcome data for all 30 eyes at 3 months.
3. No statement on whether personnel measuring/analyzing wavefront aberrometry data were masked to treatment allocation.
4. No trial registration or protocol/SAP was cited, preventing confirmation that the analysis was pre-specified before unblinding.

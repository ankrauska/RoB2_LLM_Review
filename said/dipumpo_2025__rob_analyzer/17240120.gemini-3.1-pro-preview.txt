Based on a thorough and detailed assessment of the provided article using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool guidelines, here is the structured Risk of Bias report:

**1. Bias arising from the randomization process**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The allocation sequence was adequately generated using a computer-generated randomization list in blocks of four. Allocation was concealed using sequentially assigned, sealed opaque envelopes, preventing recruiters from predicting assignments. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) show no substantial imbalances between the escitalopram and placebo groups at the point of randomization, indicating that the randomization process was successfully implemented.

**2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The trial was double-blind. The study medications were identical in appearance, taste, and smell, ensuring that both participants and trial personnel were unaware of the assigned interventions. Because blinding was successful, there is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions arising from the trial context. Furthermore, the primary efficacy analysis appropriately used the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, preserving the benefits of randomization.

**3. Bias due to missing outcome data**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The primary outcome was time to relapse, analyzed using survival analysis techniques (which account for censoring). The overall withdrawal rate excluding those who reached the relapse endpoint was low and balanced between the two groups (7.9% for escitalopram and 8.9% for placebo). Relapses themselves triggered study withdrawal but were correctly captured as the outcome event. Therefore, missing outcome data (unrelated to the event of interest) were minimal and not likely to bias the estimated effect.

**4. Bias in measurement of the outcome**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The method of measuring the outcome (Y-BOCS score and investigator judgment of efficacy) is a highly appropriate and validated standard for OCD. The assessments were administered equally across both intervention groups at scheduled intervals. Since the study was strictly double-blinded, the outcome assessors (investigators rating the Y-BOCS) were unaware of the intervention received, eliminating the risk that the assessment was influenced by knowledge of the treatment arm. 

**5. Bias in selection of the reported result**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The article clearly states that the primary analysis of efficacy (time to relapse, evaluated via the log-rank test) was prospectively defined. The reported results directly align with these pre-specified outcome measurements and statistical analysis plans. There is no evidence that the reported time-to-relapse results were selected from multiple eligible measurements or multiple alternative analysis methods based on statistical significance or direction of effect.

**6. Overall risk-of-bias judgement**
*   **Judgment:** Low risk of bias
*   **Motivation:** The study is judged to be at low risk of bias across all five domains for the primary outcome. Randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, handling of missing data, and adherence to pre-specified reporting protocols were all executed and documented adequately.

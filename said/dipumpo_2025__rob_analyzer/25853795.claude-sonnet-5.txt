# RoB 2 Assessment Report

**Study:** Multi-center RCT comparing SmartClip (self-ligating) vs Victory (conventional) bracket systems in a UK population — Part 1: Treatment Efficiency

**Outcomes assessed:** Total treatment duration (months), total number of appointments, number of bracket bond failures

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (analysis reported as "per protocol" by authors, but essentially all randomized participants who received treatment were analyzed — see Domain 2 discussion)

---

## Domain 1: Bias arising from the randomization process

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling question 1.1 (random sequence): The authors report "block randomization determined by a computer-generated random number table, with stratification for operator and center." This satisfies the criteria for a random sequence generation process → **Yes**.
- Signalling question 1.2 (allocation concealment): Allocation was concealed using "sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes" opened only after acceptance onto the trial, and the sequence generator did not participate in allocation → **Yes**, adequate concealment.
- Signalling question 1.3 (baseline imbalance): Baseline data (Table 2) show some imbalance in malocclusion distribution (e.g., Class II division 1: 37 SmartClip vs 31 Victory; Class I: 12 vs 23) and a wider age range/SD in the SmartClip group (min 12–max 29 years vs 10–56 in Victory, but SD notably larger in SmartClip: 3y3mo vs 1y9mo). No statistical comparison of baseline characteristics was reported, and no p-values were given for these differences, making it impossible to determine whether these differences are compatible with chance. However, block randomization with stratification by operator (a key prognostic determinant of treatment efficiency, given two operators show different practice patterns) increases confidence that these differences arose by chance rather than a flawed process.
- Because there is no explicit statistical assessment of baseline comparability, and given that malocclusion type/complexity and extraction pattern are plausible key prognostic factors for treatment duration that show some imbalance, but with no test of significance provided, this constitutes a level of ambiguity that could not be resolved from the reported text (a specific limitation of this assessment).

Overall, since the sequence was random and allocation concealment was adequate, but there is some absence of information on whether observed baseline imbalances are compatible with chance, the domain is judged as **Some concerns** rather than Low risk.

---

## Domain 2: Bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling questions 2.1/2.2: Participants and treating clinicians (operators) could not be blinded to bracket type, as this is a device-based intervention that is visually and mechanically distinguishable (explicitly acknowledged by the authors: "it was not possible to blind either clinician or subject to the bracket type") → **Yes**, awareness present.
- Signalling question 2.3: Despite lack of blinding, there is no evidence that deviations from intended intervention arose because of the trial context. Both bracket systems were bonded using an identical standardized bonding protocol, and archwire sequences followed a pre-specified, appliance-specific manufacturer protocol (Figure 1) applied consistently by both trained operators. No mention of non-protocol interventions, contamination, or co-intervention imbalance was found in the article → **No**.
- Signalling question 2.6 (appropriate analysis): The authors describe a "per protocol" analysis, but on closer inspection, this represents an analysis of essentially all participants who received their randomly allocated intervention and completed treatment (135/138, 97.8%), consistent in practice with an intention-to-treat/modified-ITT approach for this per-participant efficiency outcome. Only 3 participants were excluded: 1 refused fixed treatment altogether (post-randomization but pre-intervention — arguably should have been retained in ITT for a fully rigorous analysis) and 2 failed to complete treatment (these represent missing outcome data, addressed in Domain 3). Because the number of exclusions is very small (3/138, 2.2%) and unlikely to influence the result substantially, this is not judged to introduce material bias into the assignment effect.
- Because there was no differential trial-context-driven deviation, and the impact of analytical exclusions was negligible, the domain is judged **Low risk**, despite lack of blinding of participants/personnel, since lack of blinding alone (in the absence of a mechanism for trial-context-driven deviations) does not automatically elevate risk under the RoB 2 algorithm for the assignment effect.

## Domain 3: Bias due to missing outcome data

**Judgment: Low risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling question 3.1: Outcome data (treatment duration, number of visits, bond failures) were available for 135/138 randomized participants (97.8%) — a very low and balanced rate of missingness (1 dropout in SmartClip group, 1 in Victory group, per the CONSORT diagram; plus one further non-participant who refused treatment) → **Yes**, nearly all data available.
- This proportion of missing data (2.2%) is extremely unlikely to have made an important difference to the estimated effect, regardless of the true values of the missing outcomes, for either continuous efficiency outcomes (treatment time, visits) or the dichotomous bond-failure outcome.
- No evidence exists that the small amount of missing data was related to intervention group or to outcome; the flow diagram shows an even split between groups (1 dropout per group).

Therefore, the domain is judged **Low risk** because outcome data were available for "nearly all" randomized participants.

## Domain 4: Bias in measurement of the outcome

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling question 4.1: The methods of measuring outcomes (treatment duration in months, number of visits, and bond failures counted from clinical records) are appropriate and objective in nature, not requiring subjective interpretation → **No**.
- Signalling question 4.2: Measurement/ascertainment methods (recording of visits, bond failures, and treatment completion date) were applied identically to both groups using the same clinical recording system → **No**, unlikely to differ.
- Signalling question 4.3: Outcome assessors — Data analysis was reported to be performed with "examiner blinding," and the appliance type "was not recorded on the data collection sheets in an attempt to minimize bias during data entry and analysis." However, the treating clinicians who recorded the raw data (dates of visits, bond failures, decision on when treatment was "complete") were themselves unblinded, since they were the same operators delivering the intervention and were aware of the bracket type at the point of care. The blinding referred to by the authors appears to apply only to the data-analysis stage, not to the original clinical recording/decision-making stage (e.g., the clinical judgment of when treatment was "finished," which involves an element of subjective judgment) → **Yes**, aware (at the point of outcome generation/recording).
- Signalling question 4.4: For the outcome "duration of treatment," the decision of when to debond/complete treatment could plausibly be influenced by clinician expectations or beliefs about the bracket systems (e.g., a clinician who believes SmartClip is more efficient might debond earlier), since this decision involves an element of clinical judgment rather than being a fully objective/automated endpoint → **Yes**, potentially influenced. Bond failure counts and number-of-visits data are more objective and less prone to this bias.
- Signalling question 4.5: There is no explicit evidence in the article that assessment was actually influenced by this awareness, and no information is given regarding specific measures (other than blinding at analysis stage) taken to standardize the debond decision between operators/groups. This creates ambiguity: it cannot be determined from the reported text whether the decision-making on treatment completion was influenced by knowledge of appliance type.

Given the absence of blinding at the point of outcome generation for a partially judgment-based outcome (treatment duration), and no clear evidence to rule out influence, but also no strong indication that influence definitely occurred, the domain is judged as **Some concerns**. This ambiguity (whether "examiner blinding" applied to raw data collection or only secondary analysis) is a specific limitation identified in this assessment and could not be resolved from the reported text.

## Domain 5: Bias in selection of the reported result

**Judgment: Some concerns**

**Motivation:**
- Signalling question 5.1: No trial protocol or statistical analysis plan (SAP) was cited, referenced, or made available in the article. There is no mention of trial registration (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov or ISRCTN), and no indication that outcome measures and analyses were pre-specified before unblinded data were available → **No information**.
- Signalling question 5.2: The outcomes reported (treatment duration, number of visits, bond failure rate) are relatively standard and it is unclear whether other/additional efficiency measures (e.g., outcome quality indices such as PAR or ICON, used in comparable studies cited in the article, such as Johansson and Lundström) were originally intended but not reported → **No information**.
- Signalling question 5.3: The statistical analyses reported (Welch two-sample t-tests, ANOVA with Bonferroni correction) appear reasonably standard and consistently applied across all comparisons (appliance, operator, center) without evidence of selective analysis reporting. However, without a pre-registered protocol, it cannot be fully excluded that other model specifications or adjusted analyses were conducted but not reported → **No information**.

Because analysis intentions could not be verified against any pre-specified/registered protocol, and it is unclear whether all originally intended outcome measures and analyses were reported, this domain defaults to **Some concerns**. This lack of a trial registration record or accessible protocol is explicitly noted as a limitation of this assessment, as it precluded verification of pre-specification.

## 6. Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement

**Overall judgment: High risk of bias**

**Motivation:**
Applying the RoB 2 algorithm for combining domain-level judgements:
- Domain 1 (Randomization process): Some concerns
- Domain 2 (Deviations from intended interventions): Low risk
- Domain 3 (Missing outcome data): Low risk
- Domain 4 (Measurement of the outcome): Some concerns
- Domain 5 (Selection of the reported result): Some concerns

No domain was judged to be at outright "High risk of bias" individually. However, per the RoB 2 rules, an overall judgement of **High risk of bias** is warranted when a study has "some concerns" in **multiple domains in a way that substantially lowers confidence in the result." In this case, three of the five domains (Domains 1, 4, and 5) were judged as "Some concerns" simultaneously. The combination of (a) unverified compatibility-with-chance of baseline imbalances, (b) potential for unblinded, judgment-based determination of the primary outcome (treatment duration) by the same clinicians delivering the intervention, and (c) complete absence of a verifiable pre-specified protocol/trial registration to rule out selective outcome/analysis reporting, cumulatively and materially lower confidence in the trustworthiness of the reported treatment-efficiency result.

Therefore, the overall risk-of-bias judgement for this result (treatment efficiency: duration, visits, bond failures) is **High risk of bias**.

**Note on limitations of this assessment:** This assessment was based solely on the published journal article; no trial protocol, statistical analysis plan, or trial registry record was available for review. Several judgments (particularly regarding pre-specification of analyses in Domain 5, and the point at which "examiner blinding" was applied in Domain 4) were made in the presence of incomplete reporting, and are flagged above as specific ambiguities rather than assumptions.

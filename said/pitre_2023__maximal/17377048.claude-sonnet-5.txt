# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Coronary Artery Calcium Scoring — Outcome: Agatston Score

**Preliminary note:** The randomized comparison in this trial is between two acquisition protocols/collimation settings (Group A: 64 × 0.6 mm; Group B: 20 × 1.2 mm), to which 40 patients were "assigned...at random." The outcome being assessed is the Agatston score. Because reconstruction interval/increment comparisons are made *within* patients (not by randomized assignment), the RoB2 assessment below focuses on the effect of assignment to intervention (Group A vs Group B collimation protocol) on the Agatston score, per the instruction to use the "effect of assignment to intervention at baseline" framework for the deviations domain.

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation:* The article states only that "the patients were assigned to two groups at random with 20 patients each." No description of the actual method used to generate the allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number tables) is provided. This is insufficient to confirm a truly random process (Signalling Q1.1: No information).
- *Allocation concealment:* No information is provided about how (or whether) the allocation sequence was concealed from those enrolling patients (Signalling Q1.2: No information).
- *Baseline imbalance:* The authors report that "the age between both groups was not significantly different (Mann-Whitney U test, p > 0.05)," and CTDIvol was similar between groups (6.2 ± 1.2 vs 5.9 ± 1.5 mGy, p = 0.67). However, no other baseline characteristics (sex distribution, extent of pre-existing calcification, heart rate by group, cardiovascular risk factors) are reported for group A vs group B, limiting the ability to fully rule out chance or systematic imbalance (Signalling Q1.3: No information for most variables, No for age/CTDIvol).

Given the lack of information on sequence generation and allocation concealment, combined with only partial reassurance from the limited baseline comparison reported (age, CTDIvol), this domain is rated as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," per the RoB2 decision algorithm (no information on concealment + no evidence of imbalance in the few compared variables → Some concerns).

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention at baseline): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The "intervention" being compared here is a technical scanning parameter (detector collimation: 64 × 0.6 mm vs 20 × 1.2 mm), not a behavioral or clinical treatment. Patients themselves have no awareness of, or ability to alter, their assigned collimation protocol, and this awareness (or lack thereof) has no plausible mechanism to induce deviations from the assigned scanning protocol (Signalling Q2.1: No/Not applicable in a meaningful sense).
- CT technologists/personnel performing the scan would necessarily know which collimation setting was being used (Signalling Q2.2: Yes), but there is no indication that this knowledge led to any deviation from the assigned protocol — the scanning parameters (collimation, tube current modulation settings 190 mAs/120 kV, gantry rotation, reconstruction procedure) were applied uniformly according to protocol in each patient. There is no described mechanism, arising from the "trial context," by which technologists would depart from the assigned collimation, and no evidence this occurred (Signalling Q2.3: No/Probably no).
- The reconstruction protocol (5 diastolic phases × 2 increments) was applied identically to all patients regardless of group.
- Analysis: All 36 remaining patients (18 per group) were analyzed according to their assigned collimation protocol; no crossover or switching of collimation protocols between groups is described (appropriate "as-assigned" analysis, akin to ITT for this technical comparison).

Because there is no meaningful potential for deviation from the assigned technical scanning protocol due to trial context, and an appropriate as-assigned analysis was used, this domain is judged as low risk.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- Of the original 40 randomized patients, 4 (10%) were excluded because "no coronary calcium was detected on any of the reconstructions," leaving 36 patients (18 in each group, presumably, since results are reported as n=18 per group) for the final Agatston score analysis.
- This exclusion was not random with respect to the outcome: the reason for exclusion is intrinsically tied to a "true value" of the outcome (Agatston score = 0 / no detectable calcium). Signalling Q3.1 (data available for all or nearly all participants): No — 4 of 40 participants had their data excluded from all analyses. Signalling Q3.3 (could missingness depend on true value of outcome): Yes — by definition, exclusion occurred precisely because the "true" calcium score was (or appeared to be) zero on every reconstruction.
- No sensitivity analysis was reported that examined the impact of including these patients (e.g., as zero-Agatston-score patients) on the reported mean scores or coefficients of variation (Signalling Q3.2: No evidence result is unbiased).
- However, it is unclear whether this exclusion was differential between Group A and Group B (i.e., whether the 4 excluded patients were evenly split, 2 and 2, or unevenly distributed). The paper does not report this breakdown explicitly, and the small numbers (4/40) combined with plausible balance between groups make it unlikely, though not certain, that this introduced substantial differential bias between the randomized comparison groups (Signalling Q3.4: unclear/no strong evidence that missingness differentially depended on true value in a way that would bias the Group A vs Group B comparison specifically, though it clearly affects the overall interpretation of "who has calcium" in the sample).

Given the absence of information on the balance of these exclusions between groups and no sensitivity analysis, but a relatively small and plausibly non-differential proportion of missing data, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of method:* The Agatston score was calculated using an established, appropriate method (workstation-based automatic identification of lesions with HU > 130 in ≥ 2 contiguous pixels), which is standard and appropriate for this outcome (Signalling Q4.1: No/Probably no — method not inappropriate).
- *Comparability of measurement between groups:* The same CT scanner, acquisition protocol elements (aside from the randomized collimation), reconstruction slice thickness (3 mm), tube voltage/current settings, and identical scoring software/algorithm were used for both groups, so the measurement approach itself did not differ structurally between arms (Signalling Q4.2: No/Probably no).
- *Blinding of outcome assessors:* "A radiologist with 4 years of experience...and a cardiologist with 11 years of experience...differentiated between calcium and noise by placing regions of interest around the CAC." There is no statement that these assessors were blinded to which collimation protocol (Group A vs B) had been used to acquire the images. Given that the collimation setting is often apparent from image characteristics (slice thickness/resolution) and would be documented in the scan file, it is likely the assessors were aware of, or could infer, the intervention group during outcome assessment (Signalling Q4.3: Probably yes — aware).
- *Potential for influence:* This outcome-assessment step (differentiating calcium from noise) involves some judgment (an "observer-reported outcome involving some judgement," per RoB2 categorization), so knowledge of the intervention group could, in principle, influence assessment (Signalling Q4.4: Probably yes).
- *Likelihood of actual influence:* There is no explicit evidence that assessors' judgments were differentially influenced by knowledge of collimation group, and the scoring process is semi-automated with a fixed HU threshold, limiting the scope for assessor-driven bias. This reduces the likelihood of substantial influence (Signalling Q4.5: Probably no/unclear).

Because outcome assessors were apparently not blinded and the outcome measurement involves some subjective judgment (differentiating calcium from noise), but there is no strong evidence that this materially influenced the group comparison, this domain is judged "Some concerns" rather than "High risk" or "Low risk."

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- No trial registration record, protocol, or statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinded data availability is cited or referenced in the article, so it is not possible to confirm whether the reported Agatston score analyses (comparisons across reconstruction increments, reconstruction intervals, and between-group comparisons) correspond to a pre-specified analysis plan (Signalling Q5.1: No information).
- The study reports multiple outcome measurements (Agatston score and volumetric score) and multiple ways of summarizing variability (mean, coefficient of variation, percentile risk-group reclassification) across multiple reconstruction intervals (50–70% R-R) and increments (1.5 vs 3.0 mm). While these appear to be the pre-planned core comparisons given the stated study objective, without a registered protocol it cannot be confirmed that no additional analyses were conducted and selectively omitted, or that the specific comparisons/metrics reported were not chosen because of the results obtained (Signalling Q5.2/5.3: No information).
- There is no indication of missing/omitted outcome comparisons, and the reported analyses map logically onto the study's stated objectives, which somewhat mitigates concern, but in the absence of a registered protocol, "No information" defaults to "Some concerns" per the RoB2 algorithm.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias for Agatston Score: **Some concerns**

**Summary rationale:** No domain was judged to be at "High risk of bias." However, "Some concerns" were identified in four of the five domains (randomization process, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, and selective reporting), driven primarily by incomplete reporting of randomization methodology and baseline comparability, unclear handling/balance of participants excluded due to absent calcium, lack of blinding of outcome assessors combined with a judgment-based scoring step, and absence of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan. Per RoB2 guidance, multiple domains rated as "Some concerns" can lower confidence in the result; here, the overall judgment is "Some concerns" rather than "High risk," since no single domain reached the threshold of "High risk" and the identified concerns, while real, are not clearly severe enough (individually or jointly) to substantially undermine the primary within-scanner findings regarding reconstruction interval/increment effects on the Agatston score.

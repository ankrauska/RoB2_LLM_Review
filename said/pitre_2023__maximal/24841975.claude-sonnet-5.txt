# Risk of Bias Assessment: CLAVELA Trial (Elacytarabine vs Investigator's Choice) — Outcome: Overall Survival

**Effect of interest assessed:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect), as instructed.

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:**
- Allocation sequence concealment: Adequate. Randomization was centrally administered via an Interactive Web Response System (IWRS), a method considered a "gold standard" for concealment (Table 2 minimal/extended criteria).
- Sequence randomness: The sequence used blocked randomization ("A block size of four patients was used within each stratum") stratified by intensity of prior therapy and remission status, implying a random component within blocks.
- Baseline imbalance: Overall baseline characteristics were well balanced (age, sex, race, ECOG PS, cytogenetics, prior therapy intensity all p>0.05). One statistically significant imbalance was noted: patients >70 years old were more frequent in the elacytarabine arm (30% vs 18%, P=.007). However, median age was nearly identical (62 vs 63 years), and this was one significant difference among ~14 baseline comparisons — consistent with a chance finding rather than a failure of randomization. The authors also performed an age-adjusted analysis (HR 0.98; 95% CI, 0.79–1.28), which did not materially change the result, supporting that the imbalance did not compromise the primary comparison.

**b. Supporting text:**
- "Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System to receive either elacytarabine or their investigator's choice of treatment."
- "Patients were stratified according to intensity of prior antileukemic therapy... A block size of four patients was used within each stratum."
- "Adjusted for age, the hazard ratio between the two arms was 0.98 (95% CI, 0.79 to 1.28; P=.84)."

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention)

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:**
- Participants and personnel were aware of intervention assignment: this was an **open-label study** ("The study was an open-label, randomized phase III study").
- Deviations from intended intervention did occur: "Six patients in the control arm received treatment that was not according to the prespecified investigator's choice" (e.g., palliative care patients receiving hydroxyurea/thioguanine, one HiDAC patient also receiving amsacrine, one MEC patient receiving FLAG-Ida, one FLAG-Ida patient receiving ifosfamide instead of cytarabine).
- These deviations were few (6/190 in control arm, ~3%), occurred only within the control arm (i.e., switching among comparator options rather than crossing over to the experimental drug), and do not appear to be driven by the trial context (e.g., participant disappointment at allocation) but rather by clinical/logistical substitutions of similar-intent salvage therapy. There is no indication these meaningfully affected OS, an objective, hard endpoint unlikely to be influenced by such minor implementation deviations.
- Appropriate analysis: An intention-to-treat analysis was used ("The intention-to-treat population comprised 191 patients in the elacytarabine group and 190 in the control arm"), and all randomized patients were analyzed in their assigned groups for OS.

**b. Supporting text:**
- "The intention-to-treat population comprised 191 patients in the elacytarabine group and 190 in the control arm."
- "Six patients in the control arm received treatment that was not according to the prespecified investigator's choice."
- "The two arms were well-balanced with respect to patients who were randomly assigned but not treated, those censored for stem-cell transplantation (SCT), and those lost to follow-up."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:**
- OS data were available for essentially all randomized participants. Per the patient disposition (Fig 2), only 1 patient (0.5%) in the elacytarabine arm and 4 patients (2%) in the control arm were lost to follow-up/withdrawn; the vast majority of patients had a documented outcome (death) or were confirmed alive at the analysis cutoff.
- The study used a prespecified event-driven cutoff (300th event) for analysis, and for patients lost to follow-up, censoring was applied at the last known-alive date, a standard non-informative censoring approach for OS.
- The very small and similar proportions of missing data between arms (0.5% vs 2%) are far too small to have plausibly biased the OS comparison.

**b. Supporting text:**
- "For patients confirmed to be alive on the cutoff date, this date was used to censor for survival and response. For patients lost to follow-up before the cutoff date, censoring was done on the last date the patient was known to be alive."
- From Figure 2 patient disposition: elacytarabine arm — 42 alive, 148 events, 1 lost to follow-up; IC arm — 32 alive, 154 events, 4 lost to follow-up/withdrawn.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:**
- The outcome (overall survival/all-cause mortality) is an objective, observer-reported outcome not involving judgement. Ascertainment of death does not require clinical interpretation and is not susceptible to differential measurement error based on knowledge of treatment assignment.
- Measurement/ascertainment methodology did not differ between arms — survival status and date of death/last known alive were collected identically regardless of assigned treatment.
- Although the trial was open-label (outcome assessors, including sites, were aware of assigned intervention), this is not expected to bias assessment of all-cause mortality, an outcome for which knowledge of intervention has essentially no opportunity to influence the recorded fact of death or its timing.

**b. Supporting text:**
- "The primary end point was overall survival (OS)."
- "OS was evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier method and was compared using the log-rank test."
- No differential methods of ascertaining survival status (site visits, phone follow-up, registry checks) between arms were reported.

---

## 5. Selective reporting

**Judgement: Low risk of bias**

**a. Rationale for judgement:**
- OS was the pre-specified primary endpoint, as stated both in the manuscript and (per the trial registration, NCT01147939) in the protocol ("The primary end point was overall survival (OS), and secondary end points were overall response rate..., remission duration..., and relapse-free survival"). The result presented (HR 0.97, median 3.5 vs 3.3 months) is a single, clearly defined analysis consistent with the pre-specified primary analysis described in the Statistical Methods section (log-rank test, Cox proportional hazards, event-driven analysis at 300 events).
- There is no evidence of selection among multiple OS definitions, timepoints, or alternative analytic methods for this specific outcome; the sensitivity analysis censoring for SCT is reported transparently as a secondary/exploratory analysis rather than as a substitute for the primary ITT result, and both are presented.
- The sample size calculation, primary hypothesis (HR of 0.70), and analytic approach (Kaplan-Meier, log-rank, Cox regression) were specified a priori and match what is reported, consistent with a pre-specified analysis plan being followed without evidence of post hoc modification based on results.

**b. Supporting text:**
- "The study was designed to detect a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.70 in OS, with 87% power and P<.05... OS was evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier method and was compared using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was used to calculate the HR and 95% CI."
- "Analyses were performed with a cutoff date of when the 300th event was reported."

---

## Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement for Overall Survival: **Low risk of bias**

All five domains were judged as low risk of bias. The trial used adequate concealment and randomization procedures with only a minor, plausibly chance-related baseline imbalance; deviations from intended intervention were minimal, balanced, and unlikely to affect the objective OS endpoint, with an appropriate ITT analysis; missing outcome data were negligible and balanced between arms; the outcome (all-cause mortality) is objective and not susceptible to differential measurement bias; and reporting of the primary OS analysis was consistent with the pre-specified statistical analysis plan and trial registration.

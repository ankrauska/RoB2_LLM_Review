# Risk of Bias Assessment: MADIT-CRT Trial
## Outcome: All-cause death or nonfatal heart failure events (effect of assignment to intervention)

---

**1. Bias due to randomization: Low risk of bias**

a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

b. **Rationale:** Patients were "randomly assigned in a 3:2 ratio" and "stratified according to clinical center and ischemic status with the use of an algorithm that ensured near balance in each stratum," consistent with a minimization-type approach incorporating randomization. There is no explicit description of allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., central randomization system), but given this is a large, industry-sponsored, multicenter trial (110 centers) registered prospectively (NCT00180271) and conducted under FDA oversight with a DSMB, it is reasonable to infer ("Probably yes") that a centralized/concealed allocation process was used, as is standard for device trials of this scale. Baseline characteristics (Table 1) are well balanced between groups across age, sex, race, ejection fraction, QRS duration, comorbidities, and background medications, with no evidence of differences suggestive of a randomization failure. No red flags (e.g., implausible group-size split, excessive or implausible baseline similarity/difference) are present. Overall: sequence likely random, allocation likely concealed, no baseline imbalance concerns → Low risk.

---

**2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): Some concerns**

a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

b. **Rationale:** This was an open-label, unblinded trial: "The treating physicians were aware of study-group assignments," and the diagnosis of the heart-failure component of the primary outcome "was made by physicians who were aware of study-group assignments." Thus participants, carers, and those delivering the intervention were all aware of treatment allocation (signalling question 2.1/2.2 = Yes).

There were deviations from intended intervention that plausibly arose because of trial context: "A total of 173 crossovers occurred: 91 patients who were assigned to the ICD-only group received a CRT–ICD device (12.4%)... 30 at the physician's discretion before reaching an end point and 61 after a heart-failure event." Crossover before an event, at physician discretion, in an unblinded trial, is a deviation attributable to trial-context (unmasked knowledge of assignment influencing physician's choice to give the experimental device). Additionally, 82 patients assigned to CRT-ICD (7.5%) failed to receive CRT for technical reasons (implementation failure rather than trial-context bias per se).

However, the analysis was conducted according to intention-to-treat principles: "Data analysis was performed according to the intention-to-treat principle," and all randomized patients were included and analyzed by assigned group regardless of crossover. This is the correct, unbiased analytic approach for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention (signalling question 2.6 = Yes). The crossovers were relatively modest in proportion (12.4% and 7.5%) and were not clearly shown to bias the result substantially, but the fact that the heart-failure component of the outcome was assessed by unblinded treating physicians (rather than solely by the blinded adjudication committee) creates a plausible pathway for deviations linked to trial context to have influenced the outcome (i.e., differential propensity to hospitalize/treat for heart failure or to cross over based on knowledge of assignment). Because this potential influence cannot be entirely ruled out but there is no clear evidence it substantially biased the result (a blinded committee independently adjudicated per prespecified criteria), this is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

**3. Missing outcome data: Low risk of bias**

a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

b. **Rationale:** Outcome data (death or heart failure) were available for essentially all randomized participants using the intention-to-treat approach: "Data from all patients enrolled were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis" and the participant flow module shows 1089/1089 and 731/731 as "COMPLETED" with "NOT COMPLETED" = 0 in both groups for the trial overall. The article states devices were implanted in 98.4% of patients, and although some patients declined further participation, were withdrawn, or lost to follow-up (44 in CRT-ICD [4.0%] and 55 in ICD-only [7.5%]), the primary outcome (death or first HF event, whichever came first) was tracked via ITT with all 1820 patients accounted for in the reported denominators (1089 and 731). Given the study is event-driven with long-term national/international follow-up and vital status ascertainment, and the numbers of missing outcome data are very small relative to total events (372 total primary events), it is unlikely that any missingness meaningfully biased the result. No indication that missingness differed systematically between arms in a way likely related to the true outcome value.

---

**4. Measurement of the outcome: Some concerns**

a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

b. **Rationale:** The outcome is a composite of all-cause death (an objective outcome, assessed by an independent mortality committee unaware of assignments: "Adjudication of the end points was carried out by an independent mortality committee... that was unaware of study-group assignments") and nonfatal heart-failure events, which involve clinical judgement. Critically, "The diagnosis of heart failure, which was made by physicians who were aware of study-group assignments, required signs and symptoms consistent with congestive heart failure..." Although a heart-failure adjudication committee "unaware of study-group assignments" confirmed events "according to prespecified criteria," the initial identification/diagnosis and treatment decision (e.g., whether to hospitalize or intensify decongestive therapy) were made by unblinded treating physicians. The authors themselves acknowledge this concern in the Discussion: "It is possible that the investigators' knowledge of study-group assignment contributed in some way to the lower frequency of heart failure in the CRT–ICD group." This represents an outcome-provider-decision component (hospitalization/treatment intensification) that could plausibly be influenced by knowledge of assignment—especially given lack of equipoise or expectation of benefit with the novel CRT technology, potentially lowering the threshold to label symptoms as non-heart-failure in CRT patients or vice versa. Because there is a documented, plausible mechanism for influence but also an independent blinded adjudication step, this is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

**5. Selective reporting: Low risk of bias**

a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

b. **Rationale:** The trial protocol was registered prospectively (NCT00180271, first posted 2005-09-16, well before completion), and the primary outcome ("Mortality From Any Cause or First Heart Failure (HF) Event") specified in the registry matches exactly the outcome reported as primary in the publication ("death from any cause or nonfatal heart-failure event, whichever came first"), with the identical composite definition of heart-failure event. The published article's Methods section states the primary hypothesis and pre-specified statistical analysis plan (Wang-Tsiatis group-sequential design, stratified log-rank test, Cox proportional-hazards model) in detail, and results are reported for exactly this metric with the pre-specified hazard ratio and CI (0.66, 95% CI 0.52–0.84, P=0.001), matching the registry's posted results (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.52–0.84). There is no indication that alternative definitions, timepoints, or analytic approaches were selectively chosen over others based on the results—the reported analysis corresponds to the single pre-specified primary analysis plan with prespecified stopping rules and adjustments for the sequential design. Secondary/tertiary analyses (recurrent HF events, echocardiographic changes) are clearly labeled as such and do not substitute for the primary result.

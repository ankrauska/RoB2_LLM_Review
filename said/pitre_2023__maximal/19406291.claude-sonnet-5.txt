# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Sertraline for Depression Following TBI (HAM-D score outcome)

**Preliminary note:** The outcome assessed is the HAM-D (Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression) score, comparing sertraline versus placebo. This assessment addresses the **effect of assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect), as instructed.

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation:* "Random assignment to treatment conditions...was balanced by using a block design (blocks of 8) with computer-generated random number sequences." This confirms a genuinely random component (Signalling Q1.1 = Yes).
- *Allocation concealment:* Sequences were placed "in individual presealed envelopes," and critically, "The pharmacy implemented masking and random assignment of participants to sertraline or placebo conditions and was responsible for dispensing medications with identical markings to the project physicians for distribution." Randomization was performed by an independent pharmacy remote from the enrolling clinicians, and medications were indistinguishable, indicating adequate concealment (Q1.2 = Yes).
- *Baseline imbalance:* The paper explicitly states "No statistically significant differences were found at baseline between drug and placebo groups on baseline measures of depression..., anxiety..., or QOL." Table 1 shows no significant differences in age, sex, ethnicity, education, or injury severity. Although "the income level of those receiving placebo was significantly lower," this is one significant finding among many baseline comparisons (age, sex, education, race, income, severity, time since injury), consistent with a chance finding given the number of comparisons performed, and income was not treated as a key confounder for the depression outcome in the trial's own analyses. This does not indicate a problem with the randomization process itself (Q1.3 = No/Probably no).

---

## 2. Bias due to deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Blinding of participants/personnel:* This was a "double-blind" trial in which "both study staff and participants were blinded to the type of intervention received." Medications were "packaged in 25-mg quantities and had the same markings so that increases in the number of pills taken in both drug conditions were identical," minimizing potential unblinding through recognizable dosing patterns (Q2.1/Q2.2 = No/Probably No).
- Because participants, carers, and personnel were unaware of assignment, deviations arising from the trial context (e.g., differential co-intervention, differential engagement) are unlikely.
- *Analysis method:* "An intent-to-treat analysis compared the outcomes of all study participants, including participants who received minimal treatment... A last-observation-carried-forward procedure combined the 2 participants who completed 8 weeks with those who completed all 10 weeks of treatment." Analyses (ANCOVA, random-effects models) were performed by randomized group assignment, consistent with ITT principles (Q2.6 = Yes).

---

## 3. Bias due to missing outcome data: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Completeness:* Of 52 randomized participants, only 41 completed the trial and were included in the outcome analyses in Table 2 (22 sertraline, 19 placebo); 11 participants (21%) have no analyzed HAM-D outcome data. This is not a "nearly all" scenario (Q3.1 = No).
- *Evidence of bias/robustness:* No formal sensitivity analysis addressing plausible values for missing data was reported (LOCF was applied only to the 2 participants who completed 8 of 10 weeks, not to the 11 who dropped out earlier) (Q3.2 = No).
- *Could missingness depend on true outcome value?* Yes — the authors directly assessed this: "The intent-to-treat analyses found differences between the last assessment HAM-D score for those who dropped out of treatment as compared with the assessment HAM-D score of those participants who remained in treatment (F=4.0, P=.052), with the dropouts tending to have higher HAM-D scores" (i.e., worse depression). Additionally, specific dropouts were attributed to "increased anxiety after the baseline assessment" and "increased depression after a death in the family," directly linking missingness to the outcome under study (Q3.3 = Yes).
- *Likely dependence:* Given the borderline-significant empirical evidence provided by the trial itself that dropouts had systematically worse depression scores, and that dropout reasons were explicitly tied to worsening mood/anxiety symptoms (differentially: 1 sertraline vs 2 placebo removed for clinically worsening symptoms), it is likely that missingness depended on the true (unobserved) HAM-D values (Q3.4 = Yes/Probably yes). This combination (Q3.1=No, Q3.2=No, Q3.3=Yes, Q3.4=Yes) maps to "High risk of bias."

---

## 4. Bias in measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of method:* The HAM-D is a well-validated, widely used clinician-administered depression scale ("The scale has high reliability and validity") (Q4.1 = No).
- *Comparability of measurement across arms:* The same HAM-D protocol and schedule (biweekly visits) applied identically to both groups; no differential ascertainment procedures are described (Q4.2 = No).
- *Blinding of assessors:* "a postdoctoral fellow or licensed psychologist rated the clinical severity of the participant's depressive (HAM-D)...symptoms independently from the physician at each biweekly visit," within the context of an overall double-blind trial where "both study staff and participants were blinded to the type of intervention received," and identical-appearing medications were dispensed by an independent pharmacy. There is no indication that raters had access to unblinding information (Q4.3 = No/Probably no).
- Because outcome assessors were blinded and the trial took steps (identical pill markings, independent central randomization/dispensing) to preserve blinding throughout, the risk of differential measurement error is low.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registry (NCT00233103) prospectively specified "Depression at Baseline" and "Depression at End of Treatment" using the HAM-D as primary outcomes, and BAI and Life-3 as secondary outcomes — consistent with what is reported in the published article and consistent with the outcome being assessed here (HAM-D). This supports low concern for the core HAM-D total-score result (Q5.1 partially satisfied).
- However, no statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinding was identified/reported, and the article introduces multiple alternative operationalizations of the depression outcome post hoc: "Because the HAM-D is a multidimensional measure of depressive symptoms, some researchers have indicated that unidimensional subscales may be more effective in detecting differences... Therefore, the analyses described previously were conducted by using these unidimensional subscales." Four alternative HAM-D-derived scores (Beck Melancholia, Maier & Philipp, Gibbons, "5 most sensitive") were computed and analyzed in addition to the total score, and two of these (HamG, HamFive) yielded a statistically significant group effect that the primary total-score analysis did not. Although all analyses (significant and non-significant) appear to have been reported, the absence of a documented pre-specified analysis plan specifying which outcome definition/analysis would be primary makes it unclear whether the presented total-HAM-D result (non-significant, "no group effects") reflects the full pre-planned analytic approach or was influenced by the availability of multiple alternative HAM-D-based results (Q5.1 = No/No information; Q5.3 = No information given lack of documented pre-specified plan for the subscale analyses).
- Because there is no clear evidence of a finalized pre-specified analysis plan and multiple eligible analyses of the same outcome domain were conducted and reported (with differing statistical conclusions), but no clear indication that results were suppressed based on their direction/significance, this is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

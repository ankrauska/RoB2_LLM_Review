Based on the provided trial publication and the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome **intentions to consume the RDIFV**:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, allocation concealment was adequately implemented, and there were no baseline imbalances suggesting a problem.
b. Rationale: The study states, "To ensure randomization, intervention and control booklets were placed in a single pile in an order determined by a sequence of random integers generated by a true random number generator... The booklets from this single pile were then distributed in a lecture theatre and laboratory class." Furthermore, "MANOVA performed on pre-intervention behaviour... and cognition measures... revealed no significant differences between the control and experimental groups." 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants were aware of the intervention, but there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context that would affect the immediate post-intervention outcome, and an appropriate analysis was used.
b. Rationale: The outcome being assessed (intentions) was measured immediately after reading the booklet ("After completing the control or intervention task, participants completed a second set of cognition measures..."). Because the outcome was measured immediately in the same sitting, there was no opportunity for post-randomization deviations from the intended intervention to occur. The analysis for immediate post-intervention intention included nearly all randomized participants (ANCOVA df = 1, 215 from 218 total randomized), representing an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all or nearly all participants randomized.
b. Rationale: A total of 218 people completed the booklet. The analysis of post-intervention intentions was conducted using ANCOVA with 1, 215 degrees of freedom, meaning 217 participants were included in the analysis. Since only 1 out of 218 participants was missing for this immediate post-intervention measure, data were available for nearly all participants.

**4. measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome is self-reported, participants were aware of the intervention they received, and the assessment of this subjective outcome was likely influenced by this knowledge.
b. Rationale: "Intention to consume the RDIFV was measured by five items". This is a subjective, participant-reported outcome. Because participants had just read a persuasive booklet specifically targeting their fruit and vegetable consumption, they were fully aware of the intervention's intent. In self-reported outcomes where participants are unblinded, there is a high likelihood of social desirability bias or demand characteristics, meaning their reporting of intentions was likely influenced by knowledge of the intervention they received. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: There is no evidence of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available, leaving some uncertainty about selective reporting.
b. Rationale: The trial report does not refer to a published protocol or a pre-registered trial record. Therefore, it is impossible to verify if the data were analyzed strictly in accordance with a pre-specified plan. However, there is no obvious indication that the reported result was selected from multiple un-reported eligible measurements or analyses of intention, leading to a judgement of "some concerns."

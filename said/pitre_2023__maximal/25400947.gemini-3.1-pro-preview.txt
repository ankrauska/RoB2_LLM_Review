Based on the RoB 2.0 tool guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome **Frequency of delivery after 48 h**:

1. **bias due to randomization: high risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** Although the trial mentions "simple random sampling," there is no description of allocation sequence concealment. Furthermore, there are highly significant baseline imbalances between the two groups. Specifically, Table 3 reports a highly significant difference in cervical effacement between the MgSO4 and celecoxib groups (P < 0.0001) as well as a difference in gestational age (P < 0.0001, though Table 3 only notes the effacement P-value clearly as ≤0.0001 and text mentions significant association for gestational age). Such extreme baseline imbalances in a trial of 600 participants strongly suggest a problem with the randomization or allocation concealment process.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.
   b. **Rationale:** The study is described as a "single blinded randomized clinical trial," but it does not specify who was blinded (participants or personnel). Because the route of administration differed between the groups (intravenous MgSO4 vs. oral celecoxib) without mention of a double-dummy placebo, it is highly likely that care providers and participants were aware of the assigned interventions. While the analysis appears to include all randomized participants (300 in each arm), the lack of blinding could have led to deviations from intended interventions (e.g., differential administration of co-interventions to arrest labor) that arose because of the trial context. 

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** Outcome data for delivery before or after 48 hours were available for all randomized participants. Table 1 accounts for all 600 women randomized (300 in the MgSO4 group and 300 in the celecoxib group), indicating there was no loss to follow-up or missing outcome data.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** The outcome (whether delivery occurred within 48 hours of starting treatment) is an objective, observer-reported outcome that does not involve subjective judgement. Even if outcome assessors or care providers were aware of the intervention received, the assessment of the exact time of delivery is highly unlikely to be influenced by this knowledge.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**
   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.
   b. **Rationale:** There is no indication that the trial was analyzed according to a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or registered protocol that was finalized before unblinded data were available. While "delivery after 48 hours" is a standard and expected outcome for preterm labor trials, the lack of an available pre-specified protocol means it is not possible to completely rule out selection of the reported result from multiple eligible analyses or time points, resulting in some concerns.

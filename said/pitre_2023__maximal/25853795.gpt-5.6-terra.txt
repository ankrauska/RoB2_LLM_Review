**Outcome assessed:** total duration of orthodontic treatment, defined as months from fixed-appliance placement to debonding. The comparison is SmartClip self-ligating brackets versus Victory conventional brackets. The reported analysis included 65 SmartClip and 70 Victory participants: mean treatment duration 25.12 versus 25.80 months (difference 0.68 months, 95% CI −1.4 to 2.7; *P* = .51). The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline**.

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

### a. Judgement
The allocation sequence appears genuinely random and was adequately concealed until participants had entered the trial. Although there are modest between-group differences in some baseline characteristics, these do not clearly exceed what might occur by chance in a trial of this size and do not demonstrate a failure of randomization.

### b. Rationale
- The authors report a random sequence:  
  > “subjects were allocated … using a block randomization determined by a computer-generated random number table, with stratification for operator and center.”

- Allocation concealment was described adequately:  
  > “The appliance type was placed in a sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelope that was opened after the patient was accepted onto the trial.”

- The person generating the sequence was independent of allocation:  
  > “The generator of the randomization did not participate in patient allocation.”

- Baseline information was presented for 137 participants who received treatment. There were some numerical differences—for example, mean age was 15 years 6 months in SmartClip and 14 years 6 months in Victory, and malocclusion categories were not identical between groups. However, the trial was relatively modest in size, and no extreme or clearly implausible imbalance in a key prognostic factor is apparent. Extraction/non-extraction status, an important determinant of treatment duration, was very similar (42/67 versus 43/70).

Therefore, the study meets RoB 2 criteria for low risk of bias arising from the randomization process.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention: **Some concerns**

### a. Judgement
Participants and treating clinicians were aware of bracket assignment, but the report describes standardized treatment procedures and does not provide evidence that trial-context-related deviations or differential cointerventions affected treatment duration. However, the reported result was based on a per-protocol analysis that excluded randomized participants. This is not fully appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment, although the very small number excluded makes a substantial effect on the result unlikely.

### b. Rationale
- Blinding of participants and clinicians was not feasible and was not implemented:  
  > “While it was not possible to blind the clinician or patient to the type of bracket system being used…”

- The trial attempted to standardize treatment delivery:  
  > “A standardized procedure was used by both clinicians in all three centers.”  
  > “The manufacturer’s recommended archwire sequence … was utilized for each appliance system as far as possible.”

- The report does not identify deviations caused by the trial context, such as systematic provision of different non-protocol treatment, more intensive management, or differential use of cointerventions between groups. Thus, there is no clear evidence that knowledge of assigned bracket type caused outcome-relevant deviations that were unbalanced between groups.

- However, analysis was explicitly per protocol:  
  > “Due to the low number of dropouts, analysis was undertaken on a per protocol basis.”

- Of 138 randomized participants, 135 completed treatment and were analysed. The exclusions comprised one person allocated to SmartClip who “refused fixed appliance treatment following allocation” and one participant in each group who failed to complete treatment. Thus, 3/138 participants (2.2%) were omitted.

For the effect of assignment to intervention, an analysis including all randomized participants in their assigned groups is preferable. The per-protocol analysis creates concern, but with only three exclusions, balanced numerically across the groups for treatment non-completion, there is unlikely to be a substantial impact on the estimate of mean treatment duration. This supports **some concerns**, rather than high risk.

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

### a. Judgement
Outcome data for total treatment duration were available for nearly all randomized participants. The small amount of missing outcome data is unlikely to have materially changed the estimated between-group difference.

### b. Rationale
- The participant flow indicates that 138 participants were randomized and 135 completed treatment:  
  > “Overall, 135 subjects (97.8%) completed the study.”

- The CONSORT information shows that 70/71 Victory participants and 65/67 SmartClip participants were included in the final analysis. Thus, outcome data were available for approximately 98% of randomized participants.

- Reasons for the three exclusions were reported:
  - one participant refused fixed appliance treatment after allocation;
  - one participant in each group did not complete treatment.

- For this continuous outcome, data availability for 97.8% of randomized participants is sufficiently close to complete that the missing outcomes are unlikely to make an important difference to the estimated mean treatment-duration contrast. There is also no evidence of importantly differential missingness between groups.

No sensitivity analysis was reported, but one is not essential given the very low proportion of missing treatment-duration data.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **High risk of bias**

### a. Judgement
Treatment duration was consistently defined and based on readily recordable dates. However, the endpoint of treatment—debonding—depends partly on a treating clinician’s decision. Treating clinicians were aware of bracket allocation, and neither blinding of those making the debonding decision nor objective, prespecified completion/debonding criteria are described. Knowledge of bracket type could therefore plausibly influence when treatment was judged complete and the appliance removed.

### b. Rationale
- The outcome was defined consistently in both groups:  
  > “total duration of treatment (months) from fixed appliance placement to debond.”

- Measuring elapsed time between appliance placement and debonding is, in itself, appropriate and the same nominal method was used in both arms.

- However, clinicians and participants were unblinded:  
  > “it was not possible to blind the clinician or patient to the type of bracket system being used.”

- The treating clinician’s decision that treatment is complete and that debonding should occur is an outcome-related clinical decision. Unlike a purely automated or independently adjudicated outcome, this decision can involve judgement about alignment, occlusion, residual treatment needs, and whether further treatment is warranted.

- The reported “examiner blinding” applies to data analysis rather than to the clinical decision establishing the end of treatment:  
  > “data analysis was carried out at the end of the study with examiner blinding.”

- The article does not report standardized, objective, blinded, or independently adjudicated criteria for treatment completion/debonding. It also does not report that the clinicians’ treatment-completion decisions were reviewed by blinded assessors.

Since the outcome is partly determined by an unblinded intervention-provider decision, and expectations regarding a self-ligating bracket’s purported efficiency could affect this decision, assessment of total treatment duration was likely susceptible to influence by knowledge of intervention assignment. This is **high risk of bias** in measurement of the outcome.

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

### a. Judgement
The article reports the prespecified-looking treatment-efficiency outcome of total treatment duration and gives a numerical between-group comparison. However, there is no available, dated statistical analysis plan demonstrating that the precise analysis of treatment duration was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. The available report therefore does not exclude selection from multiple possible analyses.

### b. Rationale
- The article identifies total treatment duration as one of the stated outcomes:  
  > “Efficiency of each ligation system was assessed by comparing the duration of treatment (months), total number of appointments … and number of bracket bond failures.”

- It gives a clear numerical result for the target outcome:  
  > “The mean treatment time was 25.12 months in the SmartClip group and 25.80 months in the Victory group.”  
  > “The difference of 0.68 months was not found to be statistically significant (P = .51, 95% CI, −1.4, 2.7).”

- The article also describes the planned statistical approach:  
  > “Welch two sample t-tests were used to compare the difference in mean treatment duration…”

- Nevertheless, the publication does not provide or cite a full statistical analysis plan finalized before access to unblinded outcome data. It is therefore unclear whether the reported unadjusted comparison was selected from other eligible analyses, such as analyses adjusted for centre, operator, age, malocclusion, extraction status, or other treatment-complexity variables.

- The supplied material refers to a trial registration record but does not include its actual contents, dates, prespecified outcome definitions, or statistical-analysis details. Consequently, it is not possible to verify whether the reported treatment-duration analysis fully matches a prospectively finalized plan.

There is no direct evidence that the reported result was selectively chosen because of its magnitude or statistical significance. However, absence of a verifiable prespecified analysis plan means selective reporting cannot be ruled out; therefore, **some concerns** is appropriate.

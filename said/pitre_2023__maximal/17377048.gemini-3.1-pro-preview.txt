Based on the RoB 2.0 guidelines, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome Agatston score in the provided trial:

1. bias due to randomization: some concerns
    a. Judgement: There is insufficient information regarding how the randomization sequence was generated and whether allocation was concealed.
    b. Rationale: The authors state that "The patients were assigned to two groups at random with 20 patients each," but provide no details on sequence generation or allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics such as age, heart rate, and radiation dose were comparable between groups, which does not suggest an obvious problem, but the lack of methodological details warrants some concerns.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: Deviations from the intended intervention (CT collimation settings) were highly unlikely to occur or impact the outcome.
    b. Rationale: The intervention consisted of different scanning collimation settings (64 × 0.6 mm vs. 20 × 1.2 mm) applied during a single CT examination. Although blinding of the CT operators is not explicitly mentioned, it is highly unlikely that awareness of the scanner settings would lead to deviations from the intended intervention that would affect the Agatston score.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
    a. Judgement: Outcome data were missing for a small, clearly defined subset of patients for reasons unrelated to the intervention effect. 
    b. Rationale: The study excluded 4 out of 40 randomized patients because "no coronary calcium was detected on any of the reconstructions." Because their true Agatston score was exactly zero across all parameters, their exclusion does not reflect a biased loss to follow-up, and removing them does not introduce bias into the comparative analysis of the imaging techniques for patients with calcification.

4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns
    a. Judgement: Outcome assessors performed manual adjustments, and it is unclear if they were blinded to the intervention group.
    b. Rationale: The Agatston score was initially calculated automatically by software, but "a radiologist... and a cardiologist... differentiated between calcium and noise by placing regions of interest around the CAC." The study does not state whether these assessors were blinded to the collimation group. Since manual adjustment involves some subjectivity, knowledge of the intervention could theoretically influence the assessment. 

5. selective reporting: some concerns
    a. Judgement: There is no reference to a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registry.
    b. Rationale: The trial report does not mention a pre-published protocol or trial registration. While the reported outcomes (Agatston score, volumetric score, and coefficients of variation across specific intervals) are standard for this type of imaging study, the absence of a pre-specified plan means bias in the selection of the reported results cannot be entirely ruled out.

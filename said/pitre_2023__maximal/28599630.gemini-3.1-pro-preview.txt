1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation and allocation concealment, and baseline differences do not suggest a problem with the randomization process.
b. Rationale: The trial article states: "The randomization was performed by a researcher not involved in the data collection using the participant identification (subject ID) after stratification for EPDS score and previous history of depression." and "Participants and all research assistants and technicians responsible for running both the cohort study and the RCT were blinded to the study allocation." Baseline characteristics (Table 1) were generally balanced, with a marginally significant difference in skin color (p=0.055) which is compatible with chance given the small sample size. Therefore, there are no significant baseline differences that suggest a problem with randomization.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were blinded, and the analysis was appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention.
b. Rationale: The article specifies a "randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind design." "Participants and all research assistants and technicians responsible for running both the cohort study and the RCT were blinded to the study allocation." "The capsules were identical regarding color, shape, size and packing." Both intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol analyses were conducted and reported. Since blinding was adequate, deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context are unlikely.

3. missing outcome data: some concerns
a. Judgement: There is a considerable amount of missing data (dropout), and missingness could depend on the true value of the outcome, although ITT analysis was performed.
b. Rationale: Out of 60 randomized women (32 fish oil, 28 control), 32 completed the trial (15 fish oil, 17 control). The attrition rate was approximately 47%. While ITT analysis was performed, the trial report states: "The ITT population included all women randomized, regardless of whether or not they dropped out or fully adhered to the intervention, while the per-protocol population included all women who completed the trial at each time point." With such a high dropout rate, particularly in a study assessing depressive symptoms where dropouts could be related to mental health status (the outcome), missingness could depend on the true value of the outcome. Thus, there are some concerns regarding missing outcome data.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The outcome was measured using an appropriate tool, methods were comparable across groups, and assessors/participants were blinded.
b. Rationale: The primary outcome (depressive symptoms) was assessed using the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS), a validated tool. As noted previously, the study was double-blinded: "Participants and all research assistants and technicians responsible for running both the cohort study and the RCT were blinded to the study allocation." Therefore, outcome assessment is unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

5. selective reporting: some concerns
a. Judgement: There is limited information available from the trial registry, which is registered as an observational cohort rather than the nested RCT.
b. Rationale: The trial registry (NCT01660165) describes the overall observational cohort ("Nutritional Status and Mental Health During Pregnancy and Postpartum: a Cohort Study in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil") but lacks specific details about the pre-specified analysis plan for the nested RCT evaluating fish oil supplementation. Additionally, the trial was "Retrospectively registered on 24 May 2012," meaning the analysis plan was likely not finalized and registered before unblinded data were available. While the outcomes reported in the paper match the logical outcomes for the study, the lack of a prospective, detailed analysis plan specific to the RCT raises some concerns.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was generated randomly and there were no significant baseline imbalances, suggesting the randomization process was effective.
b. Rationale: The study states, "Each participant was assigned to one treatment group, at random, using a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method, applied by a statistician." Table 1 shows no significant differences between the groups at baseline. Although allocation concealment details are sparse, the use of a statistician and lack of baseline imbalances support a low risk of bias.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: There is no evidence of deviations from the intended interventions that arose because of the trial context that would have affected the effect of assignment to the interventions.
b. Rationale: The trial is described as a "single-blind experimental clinical trial." While blinding of patients to the specific physical modality (ultrasound vs. iontophoresis) might be practically difficult, all patients received standard concurrent treatments ("All participants completed 20 treatment sessions plus exercise therapy and cryotherapy"). There is no indication that participants or personnel deviated from the protocol in a way that would introduce bias. Appropriate ITT analyses appear to have been conducted.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all or nearly all participants randomized.
b. Rationale: The study randomized 99 participants (32 in ultrasound, 33 in phonophoresis, 34 in iontophoresis). The tables and results report data consistent with all or nearly all participants completing the study and being included in the final analyses, with no dropouts explicitly mentioned that would compromise the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: The outcome is self-reported and participants may have been aware of their intervention, potentially influencing their assessment.
b. Rationale: The primary outcome, pain, is a participant-reported outcome (VAS). Although the trial registry states participants were masked ("Masking: SINGLE, whoMasked: PARTICIPANT"), the physical differences between the application of ultrasound, phonophoresis, and iontophoresis (which involves galvanic direct current) might make complete participant blinding difficult to maintain. Therefore, knowledge of the intervention could have somewhat influenced the self-reported VAS scores.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The reported outcomes match those pre-specified in the trial registration.
b. Rationale: The trial registration (NCT01748188) pre-specified the visual analogue scale (VAS) for pain as a primary outcome, and the DASH and Constant scores as secondary outcomes. The published report provides complete data on these pre-specified outcomes at the planned time points (post-treatment and 1 month post-treatment), indicating no selective reporting of results.

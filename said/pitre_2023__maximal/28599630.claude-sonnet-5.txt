# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Prevalence of depressive symptoms (EPDS score ≥11)
## Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (ITT)

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation:* The article states "The randomization was performed by a researcher not involved in the data collection using the participant identification (subject ID) after stratification for EPDS score and previous history of depression." No explicit description of a random component (e.g., computer-generated numbers, random number table) is provided. This is insufficient detail to confirm true randomization → **Probably yes/No information** on randomness (leaning toward No information given no methodological detail on the random component itself, only stratification is described).
- *Allocation concealment:* "Participants and all research assistants and technicians responsible for running both the cohort study and the RCT were blinded to the study allocation." This suggests some concealment, but no specific mechanism (e.g., central randomization, sequentially numbered sealed envelopes) is described → **No information/Probably yes**.
- *Baseline imbalance:* Table 1 shows a significant imbalance in skin color: "Non-white women were more frequent in the control group (82.1%) compared with the fish oil (59.4%)" (p=0.055, borderline). While not reaching conventional significance, this is a fairly large and potentially prognostically relevant imbalance (race/skin color is often associated with socioeconomic status and mental health outcomes in this population) that raises some concern. Other baseline characteristics (EPDS score, history of depression) were well balanced.
- Given the lack of detailed description of sequence generation and allocation concealment mechanisms, combined with a borderline significant baseline imbalance in an important covariate (skin color), this domain is judged as **Some concerns** rather than Low risk, per the algorithm (No information on concealment/randomization + baseline imbalance close to significant defaults to "Some concerns").

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Blinding of participants/personnel:* The trial is described as double-blind: "randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind design," with identical capsules ("identical regarding color, shape, size and packing"). This suggests participants and personnel were generally unaware of allocation → answer "No/Probably no" to awareness.
- *Deviations arising from trial context:* No strong evidence of deviations arising specifically because of trial context is reported. Side effects (nausea/vomiting) were balanced between groups (4 in fish oil, 4 in control), suggesting blinding was not compromised differentially.
- *Appropriateness of analysis:* The study explicitly used **ITT analysis**: "The ITT population included all women randomized, regardless of whether or not they dropped out or fully adhered to the intervention." This is the correct approach for assessing the effect of assignment to intervention.
- *Concern:* However, there is a substantial issue with post-randomization exclusions/attrition affecting the composition of the analyzed groups. After randomization, 6 women in fish oil and 4 in control were lost for reasons "apparently not related to the study," but this asymmetry (6 vs 4, plus 1 additional decliner in fish oil) combined with overall high dropout (only 32/60 completed the trial) means the ITT analysis, while correctly labeled, is based on a population substantially depleted from the original randomized sample. This raises **some concern** about whether the “ITT” groups still reflect original randomized comparability, though this is more directly captured under the missing data domain. For this domain specifically, since blinding was maintained and no differential deviations related to trial context were reported, but analysis reporting is not fully transparent regarding how many of the 60 originally randomized were included in the ITT EPDS ≥11 analysis (Table 3 shows only 60 total: 28 control + 32 fish oil, consistent with full ITT), the classification remains **Some concerns** primarily due to residual uncertainty about whether the double-blind status was fully maintained throughout (e.g., no explicit blinding success check/manipulation check reported) and lack of detail on whether all 60 randomized participants had EPDS data or whether missing assessments were imputed within the "ITT" label.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Substantial attrition occurred: Only 32/60 (53%) randomized participants completed the trial to the postpartum endpoint (T3). Per Figure 1, 12 women dropped out during intervention (7 fish oil, 5 control), plus additional early losses (6 in fish oil, 4 in control pre-intervention) and further exclusions (1 per group) and stillbirths.
- Although the outcome table (Table 3) reports data for the full ITT sample (n=32 fish oil, n=28 control) at each time point, it is unclear from the text how missing EPDS scores at T3 for those who dropped out were handled — there is no mention of imputation methods (e.g., multiple imputation) for missing EPDS outcome measurements in the true ITT sense. The "ITT" as described in Methods states inclusion "regardless of whether or not they dropped out," but the actual N with outcome data at T3 (Table 3) appears to only include those with observed data (consistent with n=32/28 total at baseline, but completion was only 32/60 total by T3) — suggesting incomplete outcome ascertainment rather than true intention-to-treat with imputed missing outcomes.
- **Reasons for missingness:** Reasons for dropout include stillbirth, miscarriage, preeclampsia, transfer of care — these are plausibly related to underlying health status and could plausibly be related to the outcome (depression), e.g., stillbirth/miscarriage are strongly associated with subsequent depressive symptoms, and preeclampsia is a known risk factor for postpartum depression.
- The proportions of missing data differ somewhat between groups (fish oil: more early dropouts from initial 32 to fewer completers; control also substantial losses), and reasons for missingness (pregnancy complications, stillbirth, preeclampsia) are directly plausible causes of missingness that could depend on the outcome (depressive symptoms) itself.
- Given: (1) large proportion of missing data (~47%), (2) no sensitivity analysis or formal imputation method reported to address missingness, and (3) reasons for missingness (stillbirth, miscarriage, preeclampsia) that are highly likely to be related to the true value of the depression outcome — this meets the criteria for **High risk of bias** in this domain.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of method:* EPDS is a validated, widely used screening tool for depressive symptoms, validated specifically for the Brazilian population and gestational use: "The Portuguese version of the EPDS was validated in a sample of mothers from Pelotas, southern Brazil" with established sensitivity/specificity cutoffs. This is an appropriate method for measuring depressive symptoms.
- *Consistency between groups:* The same self-reported EPDS instrument was used identically in both arms and at the same time points (T0, T1, T2, T3) — no differences in method or timing of ascertainment between groups.
- *Blinding of outcome assessors:* EPDS is a participant-reported outcome, so the "assessor" is the participant. The trial was double-blind ("Participants and all research assistants and technicians responsible for running both the cohort study and the RCT were blinded to the study allocation"), meaning participants did not know their treatment allocation while self-reporting symptoms.
- *Potential for influence:* Because participants were blinded to allocation (identical placebo/fish oil capsules), and adverse effects (nausea/vomiting) were balanced between groups (4 vs 4), it is unlikely that participants could deduce their allocation and thus unlikely that their self-report of depressive symptoms was influenced by knowledge of intervention received.
- Therefore, this domain is judged **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registry (NCT01660165) lists the primary outcome as "Major depressive episode," and the publication's stated primary outcome is "Prevalence of EPDS score ≥11," which is a reasonably consistent operationalization of depressive symptoms/episode (though registry indicates the RCT was registered as part of a broader observational cohort study, and details on outcome measurement specifics in the registry are sparse).
- The article explicitly pre-specifies primary and secondary outcomes in the Methods ("Statistical analyses" section): "The prevalence of depressive symptoms (EPDS score ≥11) was the primary outcome, while the mean and absolute changes in EPDS score and length of gestation, and birth weight were the secondary outcomes." This is consistent with what is reported in Results (Tables 3-6).
- The result being assessed (prevalence of EPDS ≥11) is reported comprehensively across both ITT and per-protocol analyses, across all time points (T0–T3), and for both the overall sample and the subgroup with previous depression history — there is no evidence of selective reporting of only favorable time points, subscales, or cutoffs. Both the ≥9 (baseline screening) and ≥11 (endpoint, higher specificity) cutoffs are used and justified based on published validation criteria rather than selected post hoc based on favorable results.
- No evidence of selective analytical approaches: both crude comparisons (Mann-Whitney/chi-square) and longitudinal regression models (LME, random-intercept logistic) are reported consistently, with pre-specified adjustment covariates (Model 2).
- Therefore, there is no clear evidence that the reported result for this specific outcome (EPDS ≥11 prevalence) was selected, based on the results, from multiple eligible measurements or analyses.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias for this outcome: **High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** Although domains 1, 2, 4, and 5 show low risk or some concerns, the domain "missing outcome data" was judged as **high risk of bias**, which per RoB 2.0 guidance is sufficient to classify the overall result as at **high risk of bias**, given the large proportion of missing data (47% attrition), differential reasons for missingness (stillbirth, miscarriage, preeclampsia) that are plausibly related to the true outcome (depression), and the absence of any correction methods (sensitivity analyses or formal imputation) reported by the trial investigators to address this potential bias.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, allocation was adequately concealed, and there is no evidence of baseline imbalances suggesting a problem with the randomization process.
b. Rationale: The study states, "A complete data base with 500 eligible women interviewed at baseline was sent to the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center in the US (i.e. the coordination center) to proceed with a random allocation... A stratified randomization method blocking by age... was used to allocate women to each group using a computerized random generator program." Furthermore, "No significant differences were observed between groups in the demographic characteristics of the participants."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Although participants and personnel were not blinded, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context that would have affected the outcome. An appropriate intention-to-treat analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention.
b. Rationale: The authors report that "Blinding participants to their intervention group was not possible in this study." However, "An intention to treat analysis was conducted using electronic clinical records. They were reviewed for all women and compliance was estimated using the baseline denominators of each group."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
c. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all randomized participants via electronic records.
d. Rationale: The study states, "An intention to treat analysis was conducted using electronic clinical records. They were reviewed for all women and compliance was estimated using the baseline denominators of each group." This indicates that data for the primary objective outcome were available for the entire randomized cohort. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome was measured objectively via electronic records, and the outcome assessors (radiology staff) were blinded to the intervention assignment.
b. Rationale: The outcome was compliance with mammography screening as determined by electronic clinical records. The authors state, "Staff at the clinic and at the radiology unit where participants got their mammograms were blinded to the allocation group."

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The reported results correspond to the intended primary outcome measurements and analyses, with no indication of selection based on the results.
b. Rationale: The primary outcome (compliance with mammogram screening at 6 months) was clearly defined and reported in alignment with the study's objectives and methods. Both self-reported and electronic record data were reported transparently, leaving little concern for selective reporting.

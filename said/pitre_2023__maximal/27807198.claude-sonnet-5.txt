# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Doxycycline in Early CJD (Double-Blinded RCT Component)
## Outcome: Survival Time (Effect of Assignment to Intervention)

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to notable baseline imbalances in key prognostic factors despite an apparently adequate randomization and concealment process.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation:* The report states "Patients were randomly assigned (ratio 1:1) to receive either doxycycline or placebo," and that "the randomisation list and the study medication were administered by the Charité, Universitätsmedizin, Berlin, Germany" — an external, independent site. This supports a judgement of adequate concealment (central administration by an independent institution) and a genuinely random sequence (Probably yes/Yes for both signalling questions).
- *Baseline imbalance:* Despite this, notable imbalances between groups are apparent for a trial of only 13 participants: median age at onset 58.0 years (doxycycline) vs 72.0 years (placebo); MMSE 21 vs 16; Barthel index 45 vs 50; and an atypical distribution of codon 129 genotype (0% MM in doxycycline vs 20% MM in placebo). Age, MMSE, and codon 129 genotype are all established prognostic factors for survival in sCJD (the observational arm of this same paper explicitly adjusts for these). 
- Given the very small sample size, these differences could plausibly be due to chance; however, because MMSE and age are strong, pre-identified predictors of survival and the differences are substantial in relative terms, this raises legitimate concern about whether the randomization achieved comparable groups, even though there is no explicit evidence of subversion of allocation concealment. This corresponds to the RoB2 "Some concerns" category (adequate concealment/randomization method, but baseline differences suggestive of a problem in producing comparable groups).

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, based on successful blinding of participants and personnel and use of an appropriate (intention-to-treat-like) analysis.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial is explicitly described as "double-blind" and "placebo-controlled" ("randomised, single-centre, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase II trial"), with identical placebo capsules ("Capsule, hard") administered via the same route, supporting successful blinding of participants and the treating/assessing personnel.
- No evidence is presented of non-protocol interventions or trial-context-driven deviations differing between groups. Adverse event/serious adverse event rates were broadly similar between groups (AE 70% vs 60%; SAE 40% vs 40%), suggesting no gross unblinding via side-effect profile that led to differential co-intervention.
- The analysis reported (12 of 13 randomized patients, following the group to which they were randomized) is consistent with an ITT/modified ITT approach appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention; there is no evidence of an "as-treated" or naïve per-protocol analysis being substituted.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, given the very small amount of missing outcome data and lack of evidence that missingness related to the true outcome value.

**b. Rationale:**
- Per the trial flow: "Patients were randomised to active drug (n=7) or placebo (n=6). One patient who was randomised to the placebo group dropped out, so that 12 patients with sCJD were included in the reported survival analysis."
- Only 1 of 13 randomized participants (≈8%) has missing survival data — a proportion small enough that, even under unfavorable assumptions about the missing value, it is unlikely to have materially altered the HR estimate for a fatal, invariably-progressive disease with an already very small sample.
- The four patients described as having "discontinued" doxycycline in the flow diagram were nonetheless retained in the survival analysis (n=7 analyzed in the doxycycline arm), indicating that treatment discontinuation did not translate into missing survival outcome data (survival status could still be ascertained after drug discontinuation).
- No differential reasons for missingness related to prognosis are reported, and no important difference in missingness proportions between arms (0/7 vs 1/6) is present.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, because survival time is an objective, unambiguous outcome not susceptible to influence by knowledge of treatment allocation, and it was measured identically across groups.

**b. Rationale:**
- The outcome is defined precisely and objectively: "The primary outcome measure was survival time. In the double-blinded randomised study, follow-up for survival starts with the randomisation... and ends with date of death or end of the study."
- Ascertainment of death does not require subjective clinical judgement, and there is no indication that death was ascertained differently between the doxycycline and placebo groups (e.g., different follow-up schedules): "the patients were visited every 2 months during the first 6 months... and then every 3 months," with additional biweekly telephone follow-up in both arms.
- Because the trial was double-blind, even if some judgement were involved, outcome assessors were not aware of allocation. This corresponds to an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" (all-cause mortality/survival), for which lack of blinding (even if present) would be of low concern; here blinding was maintained.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias, as the outcome and analysis reported match the pre-specified primary endpoint in the trial registry.

**b. Rationale:**
- The EudraCT registration (2006-003934-14) specifies: "Der primäre Zielparameter soll die Überlebenszeit der Patienten von Symptombeginn an sein" (translated: "The primary endpoint shall be the survival time of patients from symptom onset"), with the endpoint timepoint "until death." This matches exactly the outcome and analysis (Cox proportional hazards for survival time) reported in the publication.
- Secondary endpoints pre-specified in the registry (time to loss of independent feeding, loss of sphincter control, and onset of akinetic mutism) are also reported in the results (Table 3), consistent with the registered plan, with no evidence of selective omission of pre-specified analyses.
- There is no indication that multiple alternative survival analyses (e.g., different covariate adjustments, different definitions of survival) were conducted and only a favorable one reported for this RCT component — a single, straightforward Cox model with treatment as the sole factor is reported, consistent with the modest pre-specified analysis plan for this small phase II study.

---

## Overall Risk-of-Bias Judgement for Survival Time: **Some concerns**

**Rationale:** No domain was judged to be at high risk of bias. However, the domain "bias arising from the randomization process" was judged to have "Some concerns" due to substantial imbalances in key prognostic variables (age at onset, MMSE, codon 129 genotype) between the very small doxycycline (n=7) and placebo (n=6) groups, despite an apparently adequate concealment and randomization method. Per RoB 2 guidance (Table 1), a judgement of "Some concerns" in any single domain, with no domain at "High" risk, results in an overall judgement of "Some concerns" for this result.

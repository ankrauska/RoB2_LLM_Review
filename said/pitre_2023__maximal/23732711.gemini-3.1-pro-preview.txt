1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial used a computer-generated randomized list, and baseline characteristics appeared balanced. Allocation sequence generation was adequate, and baseline imbalances do not suggest problems.
b. Rationale: "Using a 2 x 2 factorial design, one of our study investigators, who had no knowledge of the participants, randomly assigned them by using a computer-generated randomized list..." Baseline characteristics (Table 1) were similar between groups.

2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns
a. Judgement: The trial was unblinded for the sunscreen intervention ("placebo sunscreen was considered unethical"), which could lead to behavioral changes (e.g., changes in sun exposure). While reported sun exposure was similar, the lack of blinding raises some concerns for the effect of assignment to intervention.
b. Rationale: "discretionary sunscreen use (placebo sunscreen was considered unethical)..." Although "Reported sun exposure was similar between the daily and discretionary sunscreen groups during the trial", the open-label nature could have influenced behavior, though there's no strong evidence it did so substantially. Thus, some concerns.

3. missing outcome data: some concerns
a. Judgement: There was a substantial amount of missing outcome data (roughly one-third of participants had only one microtopography grade).
b. Rationale: "One third of the participants had only 1 microtopography grade (mostly baseline)." and "Compared with those with 2 replicas, participants who contributed only 1 (n = 282) were more likely to have severe photoaging of the neck and 2 to 5 total sunburns." Missingness could potentially depend on the true value, warranting some concerns.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Outcome assessors were blinded to the treatment allocation.
b. Rationale: "Experienced assessors who were unaware of treatment allocation graded replicas by using the Beagley and Gibson scale of microtopography grades."

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The outcome and analysis were reported as pre-specified, and results were provided for the main planned outcome.
b. Rationale: The primary outcome (change in photoaging) was clearly defined and reported in accordance with the trial's registered plan and described methods.

1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** The study states that “patients were randomized to telephone contact or no telephone contact using a computer-generated list of random numbers.” This supports a random allocation sequence.

   However, the report does not describe how allocation was concealed before enrolment and assignment (e.g., central randomization, an independent allocator, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes). Thus, it is unclear whether staff recruiting participants could have foreseen upcoming allocations.

   Baseline characteristics were reported as similar: “Baseline characteristics were similar for both groups.” There is no reported indication of important baseline imbalances or unequal group sizes (15 participants per group) suggesting a failure of randomization. Nevertheless, lack of information about allocation concealment leads to **some concerns** under RoB 2.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention at baseline.

   b. **Rationale:** Participants and the pharmacist delivering the intervention were necessarily aware of allocation, since the intervention was weekly personalized telephone contact compared with no telephone contact. The intervention is therefore open-label by nature.

   However, for the assignment-effect estimand, lack of blinding alone does not establish bias. The report does not describe important non-protocol deviations arising because of the trial context that would have affected compliance differently between groups. Both groups were prescribed the same lipid-lowering regimen and received baseline counselling and dietary instruction:

   > “While still hospitalized, all patients were prescribed lovastatin … and colestipol … Each participant was extensively counseled on the appropriate use of the drugs…”

   The between-group difference was the intended telephone-follow-up strategy. There is no indication that participants crossed over between telephone-contact and no-contact conditions, that additional counselling was systematically provided to control participants, or that other important cointerventions were differentially administered because of trial participation.

   The analysis appears consistent with assignment to the randomized groups: 15 participants were randomized to each arm, and the report states that “none … was lost to follow-up.” The compliance results are presented according to telephone-contact versus no-contact assignment. Therefore, there is no evidence that an inappropriate per-protocol or as-treated analysis was used. Overall, this domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** The publication explicitly reports complete follow-up:

   > “Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up.”

   Compliance was assessed at 6 and 12 weeks using pill and packet counts and at 1 and 2 years using pharmacy refill records. No participants are reported as missing compliance data at these assessments, and there is no indication that the reported analysis excluded participants because compliance data were unavailable.

   Given the small sample and the statement that no participant was lost to follow-up, there is no evidence that missing outcome data could have materially biased the comparison. This domain is therefore at **low risk of bias**.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:** Compliance was measured using relatively objective methods:

   > “Compliance was determined by pill and packet counts (not performed within the patient’s view) at the 6- and 12-week clinic visits.”

   and:

   > “To assess long-term compliance, pharmacies at which patients filled their prescriptions were contacted at 1 and 2 years to document refill information.”

   The same compliance definitions and methods were used in both randomized groups. Pill/packet counts and pharmacy refill data are appropriate methods for estimating medication possession/use, although they cannot prove actual ingestion. This limitation applies similarly to both groups and does not in itself create differential measurement bias.

   The report does not explicitly state whether the personnel conducting pill/packet counts or obtaining refill records were blinded to allocation. However, pharmacy refill records are objective, and pill/packet counts were conducted outside the patient’s view, reducing opportunity for immediate participant influence. Knowledge of telephone-contact assignment would be unlikely to alter the recorded number of tablets, packets, or pharmacy refills. Consequently, outcome assessment is unlikely to have been materially influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment.

   Therefore, the outcome-measurement domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:** Compliance was clearly an intended main outcome, and the article reports compliance at the stated short-term (6 and 12 weeks) and long-term (1 and 2 years) assessments, separately for lovastatin and colestipol. The report also provides a prespecified-looking operational definition:

   > “Patients returning more than 20% of prescribed pills at the 6- and 12-week visits, and those failing to fill 80% or more of their prescriptions at 1 and 2 years were considered noncompliant.”

   This reduces concern that only a single favourable time point was presented. The article reports that no significant differences were observed at 6 or 12 weeks and reports better long-term compliance at 1 and 2 years, which is somewhat reassuring because unfavourable short-term findings were not omitted.

   Nevertheless, no prospectively finalized statistical analysis plan is available in the publication. The article predates routine prospective registration, and the available information does not establish that the particular compliance outcome definitions, time points, handling of the two drugs, and analyses were finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. Multiple potentially eligible analyses were possible—for example, compliance for each drug, combined compliance, continuous refill adherence, dichotomous ≥80% adherence, and several follow-up times.

   There is no direct evidence that the reported results were selected because they were statistically significant; however, the absence of a sufficiently detailed, prospectively finalized analysis plan means that selective choice among outcome measurements or analyses cannot be excluded. Therefore, this domain is judged as **some concerns**.

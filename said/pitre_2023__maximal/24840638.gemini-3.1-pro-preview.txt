Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance for assessing the risk of bias in randomized trials, here is the assessment for the trial "The Effects of Percentile versus Fixed Criterion Schedules on Smoking with Equal Incentive Magnitude for Initial Abstinence" regarding the outcome of smoking abstinence (by breath CO sample < 3 ppm), considering the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect).

1. **bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** The allocation sequence was random, as the text explicitly states participants "were randomly assigned to one of three groups: percentile, fixed criterion, or random incentives." There are no indications that the allocation sequence was predictable or inadequately concealed, although the exact method of concealment is not described in detail. Importantly, baseline characteristics were generally balanced across groups. The single imbalance noted ("a higher proportion of percentile incentive participants achieved this level of education") is compatible with chance across the numerous demographic variables assessed, and does not suggest a fundamental problem with the randomization process. 

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** Since the study evaluates behavioral incentive schedules, participants and personnel could not be blinded to the intervention assigned. However, the outcome (breath CO < 3 ppm) is an objective physiological measure, making it highly unlikely that the lack of blinding directly compromised the implementation of the protocol or caused non-protocol interventions that arose strictly from the trial context. The study correctly assesses the groups to which participants were originally assigned (intention-to-treat). The deviations that occur (participants failing to abstain and earning incentives per their assigned schedule) are the expected behavioral responses being measured, not inappropriate deviations from the intended intervention context. 

3. **missing outcome data: some concerns**
   c. **Judgement:** Some concerns.
   d. **Rationale:** The trial required participants to submit daily breath CO samples for 60 visits. The average number of delivered breath CO samples out of 60 was 42 (percentile), 40 (fixed criterion), and 52 (random). Missing a visit resulted in a missed CO sample and a reset in incentive value. Because the missingness (absenteeism) in a smoking cessation trial is strongly associated with the outcome (i.e., participants who smoke are less likely to attend the visit to blow a positive CO sample), the missingness could depend on the true value of the outcome. Missing data were relatively high for a daily metric, though the analysis methods primarily relied on available data points for calculating consecutive days abstinent. 

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** The outcome, smoking abstinence, was measured using breath carbon monoxide (CO) via a Vitalograph CO monitor with a standard cutoff (< 3 ppm) which is a well-established and highly objective physiological method. This method was applied uniformly across all trial arms. Because breath CO is an objective, automated metric, the assessment of the outcome could not be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, despite the lack of blinding.

5. **selective reporting: low risk of bias**
   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
   b. **Rationale:** The published report presents the results for the outcomes clearly outlined in the methods section (proportion of participants with abstinent samples, time to first abstinent sample, and consecutive abstinent samples). There is no evidence that results were selected from multiple outcome measurements or various adjusted analyses based on statistical significance or direction of the effect. Although a formal pre-specified statistical analysis plan/trial registry is not provided in the text, the outcomes reported directly align with the stated objectives of testing initial abstinence and abstinence maintenance between the incentive schedules.

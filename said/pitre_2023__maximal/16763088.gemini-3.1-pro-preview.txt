Here is the risk of bias assessment using the RoB 2.0 tool for the specified trial and outcome (bond failure rate):

1. **bias due to randomization**: low risk of bias
   a. **Judgement**: The trial used adequate sequence generation and allocation concealment. Baseline characteristics appeared balanced.
   b. **Rationale**: The study reports: "Randomization for allocation of either of the two etch and primer systems to the upper right quadrant was undertaken using a block randomization method as described by Roberts and Torgerson (1998)." The participants were randomly selected and assigned. A split-mouth design inherently balances patient-level baseline characteristics between the two interventions. No imbalances raising concerns were noted.

2. **deviations from intended intervention**: some concerns
   a. **Judgement**: The operators were not blinded to the intervention. While this is unavoidable given the differences in application, it raises some concerns that deviations (such as manipulation of brackets) could have occurred depending on the group, though appropriate analysis was performed.
   b. **Rationale**: The authors state: "The patients were unaware which system had been used on each side of the mouth, but as the two systems had different modes of application, it was not possible to blind the operators to the type of system being used." All patients were analyzed, maintaining the ITT principle ("All patients were observed for the entire 12-month period apart from one 13-year-old male who moved... His data was still included in the analysis"). However, the lack of blinding of personnel in a trial where operator technique (e.g., bracket manipulation) can influence outcomes leads to some concerns.

3. **missing outcome data**: low risk of bias
   a. **Judgement**: Outcome data were available for almost all participants, and the analysis accounted for the minimal missing data.
   b. **Rationale**: 39 patients were entered into the study. "All patients were observed for the entire 12-month period apart from one 13-year-old male who moved to another city after 7 months of observation... His data was still included in the analysis." The amount of missing data is very small and unlikely to affect the outcome.

4. **measurement of the outcome**: some concerns
   a. **Judgement**: Outcome assessors were aware of the intervention received, which could potentially influence the assessment, although bond failure is a relatively objective measure.
   b. **Rationale**: Since operators could not be blinded to the intervention and they likely evaluated the bond failures during follow-up, assessors were unblinded. While bond failure is fairly objective, there is some room for judgement, warranting "some concerns."

5. **selective reporting**: low risk of bias
   a. **Judgement**: The reported outcomes correspond to the intended outcomes and were analyzed as planned.
   b. **Rationale**: The outcome of bond failure rate (and survival) is clearly defined and reported fully for all groups and subgroups (age, gender, operator, etc.) as stated in the methods. There is no evidence of selective reporting of results.

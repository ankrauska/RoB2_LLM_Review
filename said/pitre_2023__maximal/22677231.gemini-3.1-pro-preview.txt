Based on the provided RoB 2.0 guidance, trial publication, and trial registration, here is the risk of bias assessment for the effect of assignment to the intervention (intention-to-treat) for the primary outcome: Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) total score post intervention.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** The allocation sequence was random, generated by consecutive order via the software upon leaving an email address, with no stratification. While the specific sequence generation method (e.g., computer random number generator) is not detailed beyond "consecutive order", it occurred automatically online upon completion of the baseline survey and providing an email address, indicating concealment until enrolled. The trial reports: "Participants who left their email addresses at the end of the baseline survey were randomly allocated to the treatment or wait-list group in consecutive order... Within 24 h, participants in the immediate treatment (Deprexis) group were emailed...". Furthermore, baseline characteristics were well balanced between groups (Table 1 and Table 2), showing no evidence of a problem with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: some concerns**
*   **a. Judgement:** Some concerns.
*   **b. Rationale:** Participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention due to the nature of the trial (online program vs. waitlist). The trial was unblinded. The paper states: "The participants in the delayed-treatment (wait-list) group were informed that they would receive a voucher for Deprexis subsequent to completion of the post-survey eight weeks later." Because participants were unblinded, deviations from the intended intervention could have arisen due to the trial context (e.g., waitlist participants seeking alternative help). The ITT analysis appropriately utilized multiple imputation for missing data, aligning with intention-to-treat principles. However, because blinding was not possible and deviations (like seeking other treatments while on the waitlist, which was uncontrolled) could occur and affect the outcome, there are some concerns for the effect of assignment to the intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
*   **a. Judgement:** Some concerns.
*   **b. Rationale:** The overall completion rate was 81.9% (85.7% wait-list, 78.1% Deprexis), meaning that ~18% of outcome data were missing. While the trial used multiple imputation to handle missing data in the ITT analysis, missingness could potentially depend on the true value of the outcome (e.g., participants not improving might drop out). The study notes: "Participants who did not complete the trial were indistinguishable from non-completers on any of the background... except for the QoL subscales tapping social relationships... and environment... where completers achieved slightly higher baseline scores." Because data were missing for nearly 20% of participants, and missingness could depend on the unobserved outcomes (depression severity), there are some concerns.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
*   **a. Judgement:** Some concerns.
*   **b. Rationale:** The outcome (BDI) is a self-reported (participant-reported) measure. Because participants were aware of their intervention group (Deprexis vs. waitlist), their knowledge of the intervention could have influenced their reporting of depressive symptoms (the outcome assessment). According to RoB 2.0 guidance for participant-reported outcomes where participants are unblinded, the assessment is potentially influenced by knowledge of the intervention received, leading to a judgment of at least "some concerns."

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial was prospectively registered (NCT01401296). The registered primary outcome was the "Severity of depressive symptoms as assessed with the Beck Depression Inventory (BDI)," which matches the primary outcome reported in the published manuscript. The analyses appear to follow the pre-specified plan, reporting both per-protocol and intention-to-treat results for the BDI at the registered 8-week time point. There is no evidence that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on the statistical significance or direction of the results.

**Outcome assessed:** mixed venous endothelin-1 (ET-1) concentration, comparing high inspired oxygen fraction after cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB; FIO₂ 1.0) with moderate FIO₂ (0.35). The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to the intervention at baseline**.

1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   The report states that “**sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random 20 consecutive adults**” to high versus moderate FIO₂. This provides some indication that an allocation procedure was used, but it does not establish either component required for a low-risk judgement:

   * **Random sequence generation:** The authors state only that patients were allocated “at random”; they do not describe the random component used to generate the allocation sequence (e.g., computer-generated sequence, random-number table, or random permuted blocks).
   * **Allocation concealment:** “Sealed envelopes” alone is insufficient to demonstrate concealment. There is no information that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, opened in sequence, or opened only after irrevocable enrolment.
   * **Baseline balance:** There were notable numerical baseline differences, including sex distribution (9/1 versus 5/5 male/female), CPB duration (116 versus 84 minutes), type of surgery, and mean pulmonary artery pressure (25 versus 19 mm Hg). The authors explicitly report that “**pre-bypass values of pulmonary artery pressures were significantly greater in group 1**.” These differences are potentially relevant to pulmonary physiology and ET-1, although with only 10 participants per group they do not by themselves demonstrate failure of randomization or manipulation of allocation.

   Thus, there is insufficient information about sequence generation and allocation concealment, while baseline differences do not conclusively establish a randomization failure. Under RoB 2, this leads to **some concerns**.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   Participants were anaesthetized and mechanically ventilated, so they were unlikely to have been aware of their assigned post-CPB FIO₂. Clinical personnel delivering ventilation were necessarily aware of the assigned oxygen concentration; however, the intervention was deliberately administered as specified:

   * After CPB, FIO₂ “**was set to either 1.0 (high, group 1) or 0.35 (moderate, group 2)**,” with mean exposure of 70 minutes.
   * Apart from assigned FIO₂, “**the ventilatory mode did not differ from the pre-bypass settings in order to preserve equal ventilatory conditions between the groups**.”
   * “**Postoperative care was standardized for all patients**.”
   * Both groups subsequently received the same FIO₂ in ICU (0.6) and on postoperative day 1 (0.4), as specified in the study plan.

   No trial-context deviations, differential cointerventions, or failures in delivering the allocated post-CPB FIO₂ intervention were reported. The analysis compared the randomized groups using repeated-measures and between-group methods. Although the report does not explicitly use the term “intention-to-treat,” all 20 allocated participants appear to have remained in their assigned groups for the mixed venous ET-1 analyses.

   The report does state that one group-2 participant with high **arterial** ET-1 values was excluded from statistical analysis “**in order to simplify statistical interpretation**.” This is a concerning post hoc analytic decision, but it is specifically described for arterial ET-1 values, whereas the present assessment concerns **mixed venous ET-1**. There is no indication that mixed venous ET-1 data for this participant were excluded. Therefore, for the specified outcome, there is no evidence that the assignment effect was estimated using an inappropriate as-treated or per-protocol analysis.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   Twenty patients were allocated, with 10 participants in each group, and the report presents outcome data across the planned measurement occasions. There is no reported withdrawal, loss to follow-up, death, inability to obtain blood samples, or missing mixed venous ET-1 measurements.

   Blood sampling was planned at four defined occasions: pre-CPB, 20 minutes after CPB, 90 minutes after ICU arrival, and postoperative day 1. The article reports mixed venous ET-1 results over these periods and does not indicate that participants were missing from the mixed venous ET-1 analysis.

   The described exclusion of an outlying participant refers to “**high arterial ET-1 values**,” rather than missing mixed venous ET-1 outcome data. It is therefore not evidence of missing data for the outcome under assessment. On the available information, outcome data appear to have been available for all, or nearly all, randomized participants.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   Mixed venous ET-1 concentration is an objectively measured laboratory outcome. Blood samples were obtained consistently from the distal lumen of a pulmonary artery catheter, and ET-1 was measured with a described enzyme immunoassay:

   * “**For ET-1 and Big ET-1 analyses, a sandwich-type enzyme immunoassay … was used.**”
   * “**Direct measurements of the peptides were performed in duplicate according to the manufacturer’s instructions.**”
   * The reported assay characteristics include quality-control ranges, coefficients of variation, and an ET-1 detection limit.

   The same blood-sampling method, handling procedures, assay, and timing were used in both groups. Although the report does not explicitly state that laboratory personnel were blinded to group assignment, ET-1 measurement by standardized laboratory assay is an observer-reported outcome involving little or no judgement. Knowledge of assigned FIO₂ is unlikely to have materially influenced the assay result. There is also no evidence that sampling or measurement differed systematically between groups.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   No prospectively finalized statistical analysis plan is available in the supplied materials, and the supplied registration information does not provide sufficiently detailed prespecified outcome-measurement and analysis plans for this assessment. Consequently, it cannot be confirmed that the reported mixed venous ET-1 result was analysed according to a plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.

   There were multiple potentially eligible measurements and analyses, including:

   * arterial and mixed venous ET-1;
   * Big ET-1 and ET-1;
   * four measurement occasions;
   * within-group pre/post comparisons and between-group comparisons; and
   * repeated-measures, Wilcoxon rank-sum, and post hoc comparisons.

   The paper does present results over the scheduled time points in Figure 1 and reports that mixed venous ET-1 concentrations were not significantly different between groups. However, this reporting does not rule out selection from alternative time points, statistical approaches, or treatment of influential observations.

   Of particular concern, the authors report that a group-2 participant had “**high arterial ET-1 values … throughout the study**” and that “**these data were excluded from the statistical analysis in order to simplify statistical interpretation**.” This is a post hoc data-dependent analytic decision. It is not clearly stated to apply to mixed venous ET-1, so it does not establish high risk for the specific outcome assessed; nevertheless, it reinforces concern that analytic choices may have been made after inspection of results.

   There is insufficient evidence that the mixed venous ET-1 result was selected on the basis of its findings; therefore, **some concerns**, rather than high risk of bias, is appropriate.

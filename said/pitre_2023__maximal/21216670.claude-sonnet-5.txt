# Risk of Bias Assessment: FLAME Trial (Chollet et al., 2011)
## Outcome: Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale (FMMS) at Day 90
## Effect of Interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Sequence generation (random):** "Randomisation was balanced by centre, with an allocation sequence based on a block size of four, generated with a computer random-number generator by the pharmacist at the coordinating centre." This is an adequate random sequence generation method (Y).
- **Allocation concealment:** "The pharmacist assigned the participants to the trial groups in accordance with the randomisation list. Allocation was concealed by use of sequentially numbered opaque envelopes." Fluoxetine and placebo capsules were prepared to be identical in appearance and distributed centrally by the hospital pharmacy. This meets minimal/extended criteria for adequate concealment (Y).
- **Baseline imbalance:** There were some baseline differences: mean age was slightly higher (66.4 vs 62.9 years), previous stroke was more common in the fluoxetine group (17% vs 7%), and baseline FMMS score was higher in the fluoxetine group (17.1 vs 13.4). The authors themselves noted these differences and adjusted for them in the primary analysis (age, history of stroke, and baseline FMMS score were included as covariates). Given the modest sample size (n=59 per group), stratification by centre, and that only a few baseline variables show numeric imbalance (with no p-values reported suggesting extreme improbability), these differences are compatible with chance imbalance from restricted (blocked) randomization rather than indicating a failure of the randomization process itself. There is no evidence (e.g., unusual allocation ratios, implausible p-values, or excessive similarity) suggesting manipulation or failure of concealment.
- Because sequence generation was adequate, allocation concealment was adequate, and baseline imbalances are compatible with chance (and were appropriately adjusted for in the analysis), this domain is judged **low risk**.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Blinding of participants/personnel:** "Patients, carers, all site study staff, and investigators were masked to treatment assignment." Placebo capsules were identical in appearance and packaging to fluoxetine. Thus participants, carers, and those delivering the intervention were unaware of assigned intervention (2.1/2.2 = No).
- Because blinding was intact, deviations arising from the trial context (e.g., differential co-interventions, contamination) are unlikely. There is no reported evidence of such deviations; compliance was almost identical between groups: "The mean cumulative dose at day 90...was 88.3 tablets (SD 11.6) in the fluoxetine group and 87.4 tablets (12.3) in the placebo group (p=0.722)," and "relative dose intensity was 94.8%...in the fluoxetine group and 94.0%...in the placebo group (p=0.854)."
- **Appropriate analysis:** The "full analysis set" included all randomized patients except those who died or withdrew (113/118), analyzed according to randomized group — consistent with a modified ITT approach acceptable for assessing the effect of assignment to intervention. No naive per-protocol or as-treated analysis was substituted for the primary outcome.
- Given blinding was successful, no evidence of context-driven deviations, and an appropriate (m)ITT analytic approach was used, this domain is judged **low risk**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Outcome data were available for 113 of 118 randomized patients (57/59 fluoxetine; 56/59 placebo) — over 95% of participants, a small and balanced proportion missing between groups.
- Reasons for missingness are documented and clearly unrelated to the true value of the motor outcome: "In the fluoxetine group, 1 died from respiratory distress after inhalation of food, 1 withdrew (severe hypoxia)... In the placebo group, 1 died from septic shock, 2 withdrew (1 kidney tumour, 1 pulmonary embolism)." These are unrelated, largely idiosyncratic medical events (aspiration, hypoxia, sepsis, tumour, embolism) rather than events driven by degree of motor recovery.
- The very small number of missing outcomes (5/118, ~4%), balanced across groups (2 vs 3) and attributable to reasons unrelated to the FMMS outcome, means missingness could not plausibly bias the result.
- This domain is judged **low risk**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Appropriateness of method:** FMMS is "an index that is widely used for assessment of motor recovery after stroke and has excellent intra-rater and inter-rater reliability and validity," measured by a physiotherapist at day 0, 30, and 90. This is an appropriate, validated instrument for this outcome.
- **Comparability of measurement between groups:** The same assessment protocol (FMMS by physiotherapist at identical time points) was applied to both groups; no evidence of differential ascertainment.
- **Blinding of outcome assessors:** The outcome assessor (physiotherapist) is part of "all site study staff" who were explicitly stated to be "masked to treatment assignment." As FMMS is an observer-reported outcome involving some clinical judgement (rating items as not/partly/fully performed), blinding of the assessor is important and appears to have been successfully implemented via identical-appearing placebo/drug capsules and centrally-controlled randomization codes.
- Because outcome assessors were blinded and the assessment method did not differ between groups, there is low potential for bias due to knowledge of intervention assignment.
- This domain is judged **low risk**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial was prospectively registered (NCT00657163), and its registry entry (first posted 2008, prior to full recruitment completion) specifies "Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale" measured at "M3 (3 months)" as the sole primary outcome. This matches exactly the outcome and timepoint reported in the publication: "The primary outcome was the mean change in FMMS score...between inclusion (day 0) and day 90."
- The FMMS is reported as a single measurement (total score, with pre-specified upper- and lower-limb subscores) at the pre-specified single timepoint (day 90), with a single, consistently described analytic approach: "a multiple linear regression analysis to control for centre and baseline factors that showed treatment group imbalance" (age, history of stroke, baseline FMMS score) — consistent across article text, tables, and figures.
- There is no evidence that this particular result was selected from multiple alternative outcome measurements (e.g., different scales or timepoints) or multiple competing analyses based on the direction/significance of results. Other secondary outcomes specified in the registry (NIHSS, mRS, MADRS, mortality) are also reported in the publication in a manner consistent with the pre-specified plan, supporting the absence of selective outcome reporting.
- One discrepancy—the registry lists "Fugl-Meyer Stroke Scale" at M12 as a secondary outcome, which is not reported in the publication (likely due to early stopping of enrollment/trial)—pertains to a different outcome/timepoint and does not affect the risk of bias assessment for the primary FMMS-at-3-months outcome being assessed here.
- This domain is judged **low risk**.

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Low risk of bias**

All five domains were judged to be at low risk of bias for the outcome "Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale" at 3 months. The trial exhibited adequate random sequence generation and allocation concealment (with only chance-level baseline imbalances that were appropriately adjusted for), successful blinding of participants, personnel, and outcome assessors, minimal and balanced missing outcome data with non-informative reasons, an outcome measured with a validated and consistently applied instrument, and reporting fully consistent with prospective trial registration.

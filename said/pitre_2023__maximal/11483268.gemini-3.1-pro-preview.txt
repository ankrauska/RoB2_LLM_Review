**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The trial report states that the study was "randomised" and "subjects with a documented history of having received one dose of Avaxim™ were randomised to receive a booster dose of either Avaxim™ or Vaqta™", but it does not provide any details regarding the methods used for random sequence generation or allocation concealment. Baseline demographic characteristics were similar between groups (Table 1); however, there was a non-significant difference in pre-booster geometric mean titres (GMT) (496 mIU/mL vs 325 mIU/mL, p=0.171). Due to the lack of information on sequence generation and allocation concealment, this domain raises some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The trial was an "open study" so participants and study personnel were aware of the assigned interventions. However, for an objective outcome like post-vaccination antibody titre, knowledge of the assigned vaccine is unlikely to lead to deviations from the intended intervention that would affect the outcome. The trialists performed both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses, noting that "The results of both analyses were close enough to allow only the per-protocol results to be reported." Since the exclusions from the per-protocol analysis were minimal (6 out of 127 subjects) and the results did not differ materially from the ITT analysis, the risk of bias is low.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Outcome data were available for nearly all participants. Of the 127 randomized subjects, 121 (95.3%) were included in the per-protocol immunogenicity analysis. The reasons for the 6 exclusions (e.g., previous booster, travel to endemic area, missing blood samples) do not suggest that missingness in the outcome depended on the true value of the outcome. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome was an objective measurement of anti-HAV antibody levels using a standardized assay (modified HAVAB™ assay) performed by Merck Research Laboratories. Even though the study was open-label, the objective nature of the laboratory assay means that the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Although a pre-specified statistical analysis plan was not available for review, the reported outcome (anti-HAV antibody GMT 1 month post-vaccination) is a standard, expected measure for vaccine immunogenicity trials. There is no evidence to suggest that the reported outcome measurements or analyses were selected from multiple eligible options based on the nature of the results.

Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance and the provided article, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome venous concentrations of endothelin-1 (ET-1):

1. **bias due to randomization:** some concerns
    a. **Judgement:** The trial used sealed envelopes for allocation, but it is not clear if they were opaque, sequentially numbered, or sealed with a tamper-proof seal to ensure adequate concealment. Also, there are baseline imbalances between the groups (e.g., sex ratio is 9/1 vs 5/5; pulmonary artery pressure is significantly different), though it's a small trial. Since sequence generation details are minimal ("allocated at random", "sealed envelopes") and concealment details are missing with some baseline imbalances, it raises some concerns.
    b. **Rationale:** The text states: "Sealed envelopes were used to allocate at random 20 consecutive adults undergoing cardiac surgery with CPB to group 1... or group 2..." and "Pre-bypass values of pulmonary artery pressures were significantly greater in group 1 (25±5) than in group 2 (19±4)..."

2. **deviations from intended intervention:** low risk of bias
    a. **Judgement:** This was a physiological study where the intervention (FIO2 adjustment) was administered to intubated, anesthetized patients immediately after CPB. Participants and personnel could not be blinded to the ventilator settings (FIO2 is displayed on the ventilator). However, it is unlikely that this lack of blinding led to deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context and affected the ET-1 outcomes, because the intervention was strictly controlled by the ventilator settings for a short specific period (70 min), and patients were anesthetized. No post-randomization exclusions or inappropriate analyses were reported for this outcome (though one outlier was excluded from analysis for ET-1, the authors state it was in group 2 and didn't explain it, but it was just one patient). 
    b. **Rationale:** The protocol specified strict FIO2 settings: "After the patient was rewarmed and weaned from the CPB machine, FIO2 was set to either 1.0 (high, group 1) or 0.35 (moderate, group 2) with a mean exposure time of 70 min". The text also notes: "Group 2 had an outlier with high arterial ET-1 values... These data were excluded from the statistical analysis in order to simplify statistical interpretation." (Note: excluding one outlier does not constitute a substantial impact on the ITT effect estimate for the whole group in this context, but it borders on missing data issues). 

3. **missing outcome data:** some concerns
    a. **Judgement:** The authors note in Figure 1's legend that they excluded one patient in Group 2 from the statistical analysis for ET-1 due to being an outlier with consistently high values. In a very small trial (n=10 per group), excluding 10% of the data in one arm based on the outcome values themselves (because they were "high") means missingness depends on the true value of the outcome, which introduces bias.
    b. **Rationale:** "Group 2 had an outlier with high arterial ET-1 values (range 3.6–4.7 fmol litre–1) throughout the study. Comparisons with the patient’s data revealed no explanation for this finding. These data were excluded from the statistical analysis in order to simplify statistical interpretation."

4. **measurement of the outcome:** low risk of bias
    a. **Judgement:** ET-1 concentrations were measured using a standard enzyme immunoassay from blood samples. This is an objective, automated laboratory measurement that is not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
    b. **Rationale:** "For ET-1 and Big ET-1 analyses, a sandwich-type enzyme immunoassay (Biomedica, Vienna, Austria) was used. Direct measurements of the peptides were performed in duplicate... quantified by an enzyme-catalysed colour change detectable using a standard ELISA reader."

5. **selective reporting:** some concerns
    a. **Judgement:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial registration available to verify the intended analyses. Given the multiple time points and multiple measures (arterial and mixed venous), there is some concern that the reported results could have been selected from multiple analyses, although all listed time points in the methods appear to be reported.
    b. **Rationale:** The study reports results across multiple time points, but without a protocol, it's unclear if other outcomes or time points were analyzed but not reported. However, the outcomes specified in the methods match those in the results. Without a pre-specified protocol, it defaults to "some concerns".

Based on the RoB 2.0 tool guidance and the provided trial publication, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome "Time to institutionalization":

    1. bias due to randomization: some concerns
           a. Judgement: The trial used sealed, nontransparent envelopes for allocation, which provides some concealment, but the exact method of random sequence generation is not fully detailed. Furthermore, the allocation ratio was changed for the last 14 patients, raising some concerns about the randomization process.
           b. Rationale: The text states, "For the randomization, the dementia family care coordinator had prepared 100 sealed nontransparent envelopes... Allocation to the groups was made by each patient (or her/his caregiver if the patient was unable) drawing one envelope." It also notes that "the final 14 patients from the city of Kuopio were randomly allocated at a ratio of 2:1." The lack of detail on the sequence generation and the shift in allocation ratios warrant a judgement of some concerns.

    2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: Although participants and personnel were not blinded (as is typical for this type of behavioral/support intervention), there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context that would bias the intention-to-treat effect. All randomized participants were accounted for in the analysis. 
           b. Rationale: The nature of the nurse case management intervention precludes blinding. However, the analysis appears to follow intention-to-treat principles, as the authors note, "There were no dropouts from the study during the 2-year follow-up," and all 100 patients were included in the final survival analyses according to their randomized groups.

    3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
           c. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all randomized participants.
           d. Rationale: The study states, "There were no dropouts from the study during the 2-year follow-up (Figure 1)." Endpoint information was systematically obtained from the national register of the Social Insurance Institution, ensuring complete follow-up data for all participants.

    4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
           a. Judgement: The outcome was measured appropriately and the assessors making the decision were blinded to the intervention assignment.
           b. Rationale: The outcome was "placement in long-term institutional care," which was decided by an independent group. The text confirms, "In the Finnish local social and healthcare system, a group of experts (usually a chief physician, head nurse, and social worker) makes the decision... These groups of experts were blind to the allocation and generally unaware that a patient was even participating in the study."

    5. selective reporting: some concerns
           a. Judgement: There is no information provided regarding a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registration to verify if the reported analyses were planned before unblinded data were available. 
           b. Rationale: Without access to a trial protocol or registration (which is not mentioned in the text), it is impossible to confirm definitively that the analysis was conducted strictly according to a pre-specified plan. However, "Time to institutionalization" is clearly defined and analyzed using standard survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier and Cox regression), making extreme selective reporting less likely, though some concerns remain due to the lack of a pre-specified protocol.

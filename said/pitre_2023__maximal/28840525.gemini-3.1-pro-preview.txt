Based on the provided trial report and protocol, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of Type 2 Diabetes (T2D) remission using the RoB 2.0 tool:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** The allocation sequence was random, allocation was adequately concealed, and baseline characteristics were comparable between groups. The publication states: "Randomisation was by computer-generated random number codes... disclosed after anaesthesia induction." Furthermore, Table 1 shows no substantial baseline imbalances that would suggest a problem with the randomization process. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** We are assessing the effect of assignment to the intervention (intention-to-treat). The trial was double-blinded, and efforts were made to keep post-operative medical management standardized and blinded. The publication notes: "Metabolic medications were commenced post-operatively according to a standard medical review protocol, by endocrinologists blinded to treatment allocation." Furthermore, port placements were identical for both surgical procedures to maintain patient blinding. Therefore, there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants. Out of 114 randomized patients, only 5 (all in the LSG group) were lost to follow-up ("Loss to follow-up of 5 participants occurred from the LSG group due to failure to make contact and/or to attend appointments beyond 3 months"). With approximately 96% of the trial participants completing the 1-year follow-up, the amount of missing data is small enough that it is highly unlikely to have a substantial impact on the estimated true effect.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** The method of measuring the outcome (HbA1c laboratory testing) is objective and appropriate. Furthermore, outcome assessors and the patients themselves were blinded to the intervention assignment ("double-blind (patient and assessor) clinical trial"). Therefore, it is highly unlikely that the measurement of T2D remission was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial was prospectively registered (NCT01486680), and the primary outcome of "Remission of type 2 diabetes mellitus/ glycaemic control" matches the reported outcomes. While the authors reported T2D remission at 1 year using four different HbA1c thresholds (≤38, < 42, < 48, and < 53 mmol/mol), they fully reported the results for all four thresholds across both groups without omitting data based on statistical significance. The authors explicitly stated this was done "in order to allow direct comparison with previous studies," demonstrating transparency rather than selective reporting based on the p-value.

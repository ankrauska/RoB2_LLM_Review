# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Perception of Pain During Orthodontic Treatment with Fixed Appliances
## Outcome: Initial Pain

---

**1. Bias due to randomization: Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The trial states "Insertion of either a 0.014 or 0.016 inch wire was by random selection" and "The patients were randomly selected for insertion of the 0.014 or 0.016 inch wires." This phrasing suggests a random component was likely used (Probably Yes to sequence randomness), but no further detail is given about the actual method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated randomization, random number table). No information at all is provided about allocation concealment—there is no description of how assignment was concealed from the orthodontist/enrolling clinician prior to assignment. Baseline characteristics (mean age 13.6 vs 14.7 years, gender distribution 29M/27F vs 23M/30F) appear reasonably similar between groups with no statistical testing reported, so there is no clear evidence of a problem arising from randomization based on baseline imbalance, but this is also not formally assessed by the authors. Given: sequence generation "Probably yes," allocation concealment "No information," and no evidence of baseline imbalance problems, this maps to "Some concerns" per the RoB2 decision algorithm (any response/NI/N-PN-NI combination).

---

**2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The intervention (wire size: 0.014 vs 0.016 inch) is a subtle physical difference not readily perceptible by patients; there is no indication patients were informed of, or able to detect, which wire size they received ("Probably no" to participant awareness). While the treating orthodontist (personnel) was aware of the assignment (not blinded), there is no evidence that this awareness led to deviations from the protocol that arose because of the trial context — both groups were treated identically otherwise ("Pre-angulated and pre-torqued 0.018 inch Edgewise appliances ‘Roth System’ were used in all patients... No extra-oral appliances, palatal arches or quad-helix appliances were used during the experimental period"), and no co-interventions or non-protocol interventions are described as differing between groups. The analysis includes essentially the full randomized sample analyzed according to original group assignment (0.014 inch group n=56; 0.016 inch group n=53), consistent with an intention-to-treat approach. No evidence of exclusions or analysis by treatment actually received.

---

**3. Missing outcome data: Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The article states: "The questionnaires were completed by all 109 patients and returned at the following appointment." This indicates complete outcome data were available for all randomized participants (56 in the 0.014 inch group, 53 in the 0.016 inch group) for the initial pain outcome, with no dropouts or missing responses reported. This satisfies the low risk of bias criterion of outcome data being available for all or nearly all participants.

---

**4. Measurement of the outcome: Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The outcome (time of initial pain onset) was assessed via a self-report questionnaire completed directly by participants — a participant-reported outcome, with the participant as the outcome assessor. The method of measurement (structured questionnaire with a defined time-point response, later including VAS scoring, "described by other investigators as being sensitive and reliable") was appropriate and applied identically to both wire-size groups: "Following insertion of the archwires, a questionnaire comprising a total of 49 questions was given to the patients." There is no indication that measurement or ascertainment of the outcome differed systematically between groups. Because the intervention difference (0.014 vs 0.016 inch NiTi wire) is not something patients would typically be able to identify or distinguish, it is unlikely that patients' knowledge of their specific assigned intervention influenced their reporting of when pain began. Therefore, although formal blinding of outcome assessment is not explicitly described, the nature of the comparison (a subtle difference imperceptible to the study participants) makes differential influence on outcome reporting unlikely.

---

**5. Selective reporting: Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** No prospectively registered trial protocol or statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinded data collection was identified or referenced in the publication for comparison against the reported results. The paper reports on multiple outcomes (initial pain, pain periods, pain regions, pain relief consumption, effect on daily life) and multiple time points, and the initial pain outcome itself appears to have been fully and consistently reported across all time categories (1–6 hours) for both wire groups in Table 1/Figure 2, with no evidence of selective omission. However, in the absence of independent verification via a trial registry or protocol (i.e., no information available on whether the analysis for this outcome followed a pre-specified plan established before unblinded data were available), a judgement of "Some concerns" is warranted rather than "Low risk," consistent with RoB2 guidance that "no information" about a pre-specified analysis plan combined with no information on selection from multiple outcome measures/analyses defaults to "Some concerns."

---

## Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

Per RoB 2.0 guidance, since no domain was rated "High risk" but at least one domain (randomization) and another (selective reporting) were rated "Some concerns," the overall judgement for the outcome "initial pain" is **Some concerns**.

1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns, because the sequence was described as computer-randomized and the reported baseline characteristics were reasonably balanced, but allocation concealment was not reported.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The article states that patients “were allocated into one of the two groups based on a **computer random numbers table**.” This supports a genuinely random allocation sequence.  
   - However, there is no description of how the allocation was concealed before enrolment—for example, central randomization, pharmacy-controlled allocation, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes. The statement that the trial was “double-blind” does not itself establish allocation concealment.  
   - Baseline and operative characteristics were broadly similar across the 44 participants in each group, including age, Euroscore, ejection fraction, creatinine, CPB time, cross-clamp time, graft number, and baseline NT-pro-BNP. There is no important imbalance suggesting that randomization was compromised. For example, baseline NT-pro-BNP was 1179.7 versus 1164.7 pg/ml (*P* = 0.690).  
   - Under RoB 2, a random sequence with no information on allocation concealment leads to **some concerns** rather than low risk.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**  
   *(Effect of assignment to intervention at baseline)*

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to magnesium sulfate versus placebo.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The trial is described as a “**double-blind clinical trial**,” and the comparator was a volume-matched normal-saline placebo administered intravenously using a 50 mL syringe. This makes it likely that participants and the clinical team were blinded to the allocated infusion.  
   - Apart from magnesium sulfate or placebo, the investigators state that “**the anesthesia methods and protocols for the two patient groups were exactly the same**,” and that they used the same surgeons, surgical techniques, CPB details, and postoperative-care protocols. These measures reduce the likelihood of differential cointerventions arising because of the trial context.  
   - No differential protocol deviations, crossover between groups, or imbalance in cointerventions that could materially affect NT-pro-BNP were reported.  
   - The primary analysis compared the randomized magnesium and placebo groups using Student’s *t*-test. The report presents results for 44 participants per group, equal to the randomized group sizes, which is consistent with analysis by assigned group.  
   - The protocol stated that patients requiring triple inotropic therapy for CPB weaning would be excluded; however, the report states that 88 participants were enrolled and presents analyses for 44 participants in each randomized group. Thus, there is no reported evidence that such exclusions actually occurred or affected the NT-pro-BNP analysis.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - Eighty-eight participants were enrolled and randomized, with 44 in the magnesium group and 44 in the placebo group. Table 3 reports both baseline and postoperative NT-pro-BNP results for groups of **n = 44** each.  
   - Thus, the reported sample sizes for the postoperative NT-pro-BNP comparison appear to include all randomized participants. There is no indication of loss to follow-up, missing 24-hour samples, failed assays, or differential missingness between study groups.  
   - The outcome was measured during the immediate postoperative period—24 hours after ICU arrival—when complete ascertainment is feasible in this monitored cardiac-surgery setting.  
   - Although a participant flow diagram and explicit reporting of assay completeness would have provided greater assurance, the available evidence indicates that outcome data were available for all or nearly all randomized participants. There is no evidence that missingness depended on the true NT-pro-BNP value.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - NT-pro-BNP is an objective laboratory biomarker. It was measured with an appropriate validated method: “**electrochemical luminescence immunoassay in an Elecsys 2010** (Roche).” The assay was reported to have a coefficient of variation of 2.2–3.2% and a detection range of 5–35,000 pg/ml.  
   - Blood collection, processing, centrifugation, storage, and transfer procedures were standardized: “**Sampling temperature, centrifugation and refrigeration were kept consistent throughout the study.**”  
   - The same measurement method and sampling schedule were used in both arms: samples were obtained before anaesthesia and 24 hours after ICU arrival. Therefore, there is no indication that outcome ascertainment differed between groups.  
   - The report does not explicitly state whether laboratory personnel were blinded. Nevertheless, NT-pro-BNP was measured by an automated electrochemiluminescence immunoassay, with little or no opportunity for assessor judgement to be influenced by knowledge of treatment allocation.  
   - Accordingly, lack of explicit laboratory-assessor blinding is unlikely to have materially biased this objective outcome.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   - The study was registered as IRCT201205069650N1, but the article states that recruitment began in **January 2010** and lasted 12 months. The registration identifier indicates registration in 2012, after the reported study period. Thus, the registry record cannot reliably demonstrate that the outcome definition, measurement time point, or analysis plan were finalized before unblinded outcome data became available.  
   - No prospectively dated protocol or statistical analysis plan is available in the supplied materials. Therefore, it is not possible to confirm that the reported postoperative NT-pro-BNP analysis was conducted according to prespecified analysis intentions.  
   - NT-pro-BNP was measured at two time points only—preoperative and 24 hours postoperative—and the report presents both measurements. This reduces concern about selection among many reported time points, but does not eliminate it because the authors acknowledge that they “**just assessed two points**” and did not assess the NT-pro-BNP trajectory or area under the curve.  
   - The numerical result was reported for the stated main endpoint: postoperative NT-pro-BNP was lower in the magnesium group (1566.4 ± 15.9) than in the placebo group (2632.1 ± 29.2; *P* = 0.002). However, without a prospectively finalized statistical analysis plan, it remains unclear whether alternative analyses—such as adjusted analyses, change-from-baseline analyses, or other postoperative time points—were considered and selectively unreported.  
   - There is no direct evidence that the reported result was selected because it was statistically significant; therefore, the appropriate RoB 2 judgement is **some concerns**, not high risk of bias.

**Outcome assessed:** post-treatment pain measured using the visual analogue scale (VAS), for the randomized comparison(s) of ultrasound, dexketoprofen phonophoresis, and dexketoprofen iontophoresis. The assessment of deviations from intended intervention addresses the **effect of assignment at baseline**.

1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   The allocation sequence appears genuinely random: participants were assigned using “a numbered list generated through the random permuted blocks method, applied by a statistician.” This is an acceptable random sequence-generation method. Baseline characteristics also appear balanced across the three groups, with no statistically significant differences in age, sex, symptom duration, affected side, or diagnostic category (Table 1). Baseline VAS means were reasonably similar (5.7, 5.7, and 6.3).

   However, allocation concealment was not described. Although the statistician “was not involved in data collection or analysis,” the report does not state that the statistician administered allocation, that allocation was centrally controlled, or that sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes or an equivalent concealed procedure were used. Therefore, it is unclear whether recruiters could have foreseen upcoming assignments.

   *RoB 2 signalling considerations:* random sequence: **Yes**; allocation concealment: **No information**; baseline imbalance suggesting a randomization problem: **No**. Under RoB 2, absent information on concealment leads to **some concerns**.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   The interventions were delivered using standardized protocols. All groups received the same background care—standardized exercise therapy and cryotherapy—and differed in the allocated physical-agent intervention. The authors state:

   > “The treatment sequence for all three groups was exercises + physical agent + cryotherapy.”

   > “The treatments were provided by physiotherapists following standardised protocols.”

   The trial registry identifies the study as single-masked, with the participant masked. The article also describes it as a “randomised, single-blind experimental clinical trial.” Although the practical success of participant masking is not documented, there is no reported evidence that trial context caused differential deviations from protocol, differential cointerventions, or contamination between groups.

   All participants completed the intended treatment schedule:

   > “All participants completed five sessions per week (total of 20 sessions).”

   The analysis compared groups according to their randomized intervention groups using ANOVA/repeated-measures ANOVA. There is no indication of an as-treated or naïve per-protocol analysis, or of post-randomization exclusions. Thus, the reported post-treatment comparison appears consistent with an analysis of the effect of assignment to intervention.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   c. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   d. **Rationale:**  
   The trial randomized 99 participants: 32 to ultrasound, 33 to phonophoresis, and 34 to iontophoresis. The report states:

   > “All participants completed 20 treatment sessions plus exercise therapy and cryotherapy.”

   Outcome assessments were scheduled at baseline, post-treatment, and one month post-treatment, and Table 2 reports VAS values for each of the three groups at each time point. No loss to follow-up, post-treatment VAS non-response, withdrawals, or exclusions are reported, and no adverse events were documented.

   Therefore, outcome data appear to have been available for all or nearly all randomized participants at post-treatment. There is no evidence that missingness could have depended on the true pain score.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   VAS is an appropriate and standard method for assessing pain in people with subacromial impingement syndrome. It was consistently defined as:

   > “a scale of 0 to 10, with 0 being no pain and 10 being the worst pain.”

   The same measurement method and assessment times were used in all intervention groups. The trial registry records single masking of participants, and the report describes the trial as single blind. In addition, outcome visits were attended by:

   > “the same blinded rehabilitation doctor using standardised protocols.”

   For a VAS pain outcome, the participant is the outcome assessor in the strict RoB 2 sense. The registry’s specification that participants were masked supports a judgement that participant-reported pain was obtained without knowledge of allocated treatment, although the report does not describe the masking procedures in detail. There is no indication that the VAS measurement itself differed between groups.

   Accordingly, there is no substantive evidence that knowledge of intervention assignment influenced post-treatment VAS reporting. The outcome-measurement domain is judged at **low risk of bias**.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   The registry identifies pain measured by a visual analogue scale as the primary outcome:

   > “Pain measure by the visual analogic scale pain score.”

   The publication reports VAS at the planned assessment occasions—baseline, post-treatment, and one month post-treatment—and gives numerical group means and standard deviations. Thus, the primary pain outcome itself was reported.

   However, the registry does not pre-specify the VAS assessment time point, precise measurement details, statistical model, handling of missing data, covariate adjustment, or a statistical analysis plan finalized before outcome data were available. The publication reports several potential outcome measurements and analyses: VAS, CMS, and DASH, each assessed at post-treatment and one month post-treatment, with within-group and between-group analyses. There is no accessible prespecified analysis plan that demonstrates the reported post-treatment VAS comparison was selected independently of the observed findings.

   There is no direct evidence that the post-treatment VAS result was selectively chosen because it favoured a particular intervention; therefore, high risk is not warranted. Nevertheless, insufficient prespecification of time points and analyses raises **some concerns** regarding selection of the reported result.

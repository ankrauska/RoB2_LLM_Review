**Outcome assessed:** women’s burden and distress, measured using the Relative Stress Scale (RSS) and General Health Questionnaire (GHQ), principally at 6-month follow-up. The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline** (rather than adherence to the intervention).

1. **Bias due to randomization: Some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns, because sequence generation was adequately random but allocation concealment was not reported.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   * The report states that: “**Women were assigned randomly via random number tables** to one of the three treatment conditions.” Use of random-number tables is an appropriate random allocation-sequence method.
   * However, the article gives no information about who held the random-number tables, whether allocation was administered independently, or whether recruiters/therapists could foresee the next allocation. Thus, it is not possible to establish that the allocation sequence was concealed until women had been enrolled.
   * Baseline characteristics do not suggest a substantive failure of randomization. The authors report no significant pre-treatment differences between conditions on either burden/distress outcome: “**at pre-treatment the women’s GHQ, RSS … did not differ significantly across conditions**.” The baseline RSS means were also similar (45.6, 46.6, and 46.3). Although GHQ means differed numerically across groups, the reported differences were not statistically significant and do not clearly exceed what could occur by chance in this small trial.
   * Under RoB 2, an otherwise apparently random trial with no information about allocation concealment raises **some concerns**, rather than low risk.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: Low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   * Participants, therapists, and—where applicable—partners were necessarily aware of the assigned intervention. The interventions were distinct psychological treatments: supportive counselling, stress management, and alcohol-focused couples therapy (AFCT). Blinding was not feasible.
   * Nevertheless, for the assignment effect, lack of blinding alone does not establish bias. RoB 2 requires evidence that deviations from intended intervention arose **because of the trial context**, affected the outcome, and were unbalanced between groups.
   * The study used treatment manuals, supervision, and videotaped sessions to promote protocol fidelity: “**A detailed written treatment manual was prepared for each therapy condition**”; sessions were “**videotaped and then reviewed at weekly supervision sessions … to ensure adherence to the treatment protocol**”; and “**Each therapist was assigned equal numbers of clients in each treatment condition**.”
   * The low engagement of husbands in AFCT (“**Only six husbands of the 21 wives assigned to AFCT engaged in treatment**”) was anticipated by the intervention strategy. The protocol explicitly stated that if the man declined AFCT, “**the woman received the stress management treatment**.” This is therefore best regarded as part of the assigned AFCT strategy rather than a non-protocol deviation.
   * The analyses were conducted by allocated treatment condition over time. Although participants with missing outcome data were not included in the repeated-measures analyses, this is treated as a modified intention-to-treat approach for this domain; the resulting issue is assessed under missing outcome data rather than deviations from intended intervention.
   * There is no reported evidence that trial-context deviations or differential cointerventions caused a material distortion of the assignment effect on RSS or GHQ.

3. **Missing outcome data: High risk of bias**

   c. **Judgement:** High risk of bias.

   d. **Rationale:**  
   * Outcome data were not available for all or nearly all randomized women. Of 61 randomized participants, 47 completed treatment and only 44 provided data at 6-month follow-up. Thus, approximately 28% of randomized women lacked follow-up outcome data.
   * The principal results for RSS and GHQ appear to be based on substantially smaller samples. Table 1 reports group sizes of 17, 14, and 13, totaling 44 participants, rather than the 61 randomized.
   * The authors acknowledge attrition: “**Forty-seven participants completed treatment and 44 of these participants provided assessed data at 6-month follow-up.**”
   * Importantly, missingness was plausibly related to the true burden/distress outcome. The authors state: “**most of these women reported they had left their partners soon after beginning the programme**” and “**all the women who dropped out from treatment declined further assessment**.”
   * Leaving a severely distressed alcohol-affected relationship is strongly plausibly related to women’s burden, psychological distress, and/or their change over time. It may also have had different implications for RSS and GHQ scores than remaining in the relationship. Hence missingness could depend on the unobserved outcome values.
   * No sensitivity analysis, imputation analysis, or other analysis demonstrating robustness to missing data was reported. The authors themselves note that attrition and additional missing data reduced study power.
   * Given the substantial proportion missing and the plausible relationship between dropout and women’s true distress/burden, the risk of material bias due to missing outcome data is **high**.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: High risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   * The RSS and GHQ are established instruments, and the methods of measurement were the same across treatment groups and time points. The article describes the RSS as a “**15-item scale assessing burden**” and the GHQ as a “**28-item measure designed to provide a brief screening instrument for common psychological disorders**.” Therefore, there is no clear indication that the measurement instruments themselves were inappropriate.
   * However, both outcomes are participant-reported measures. For participant-reported outcomes, the participant is the outcome assessor.
   * Women were aware of the intervention they received. Blinding was not realistic given the different psychological treatments, including non-directive supportive counselling, stress-management skills, and AFCT.
   * Knowledge of assignment was likely to influence reporting of subjective burden and psychological distress, particularly because the interventions explicitly aimed to change women’s appraisals and coping. For example, stress management included “**cognitive restructuring**,” challenging “irrational cognitions,” and increasing pleasant activities. These components could influence how women interpret and report distress on RSS and GHQ, independently of—or in addition to—any underlying change in burden or mental health.
   * The comparison with supportive counselling also increases the likelihood of expectation effects: women receiving a structured skills-based or couples-oriented intervention could reasonably expect more benefit than women receiving non-directive counselling.
   * Thus, although the scales are appropriate, assessment of these subjective self-reported outcomes was likely influenced by awareness of the assigned intervention. This leads to **high risk of bias** in measurement of women’s burden and distress.

5. **Selective reporting: Some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   * The paper identifies women’s burden and distress as primary measurement domains and reports results for both specified measures, RSS and GHQ, at pre-treatment, post-treatment, and follow-up. This reduces concern that one of these two reported measures was selectively omitted.
   * The article reports the planned repeated-measures analyses for both scales, including the time effects and treatment-by-time interactions. Table 1 also provides the group means and standard deviations at each scheduled assessment point.
   * However, there is no publicly available, dated statistical analysis plan in the material provided that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. The trial registration information does not provide a sufficiently detailed pre-specified analytic plan for the RSS/GHQ result to verify the exact outcome metric, analysis population, handling of missing data, covariate strategy, or choice among possible analyses.
   * Multiple eligible analyses were possible, including analyses at post-treatment versus follow-up, analyses of final values versus change scores, repeated-measures ANOVA versus other longitudinal approaches, and analyses incorporating different approaches to missing data. The report also describes additional ANCOVA analyses for drinking outcomes after examining baseline differences, illustrating analytic flexibility within the study.
   * There is no direct evidence that the RSS or GHQ result was selected because it was statistically significant or favorable. Therefore, high risk is not justified. Nonetheless, the absence of a prospectively finalized, sufficiently detailed analysis plan means that selective reporting cannot be excluded.
   * Accordingly, the appropriate RoB 2 judgement is **some concerns**.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The study used a "computer-generated random sequence with stratification for age, gender, and positive family history for CHD." Baseline characteristics were well balanced between the two groups, indicating that the randomization process was successful and there is no evidence to suggest a problem with allocation concealment.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: While participants and personnel were aware of the intervention (which is unavoidable given the behavioral and educational nature of the decision-making aid), there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, 207 participants were randomized and 206 completed the shared decision-making survey, indicating that an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis could be and was performed. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Outcome data were available for almost all participants. The study randomized 207 participants, and the SDM questionnaire was completed by 206 participants. This extremely low rate of missing data (<1%) is highly unlikely to bias the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The outcome (shared decision making) was measured using a validated participant-reported survey (SDM-Q). Because the participants were aware of the intervention they received (knowledge of genetic risk vs. conventional risk), their self-assessment of the decision-making process could potentially be influenced by this knowledge. As it is a subjective, participant-reported outcome in an unblinded trial, there are some concerns that knowledge of the intervention could have influenced the outcome assessment.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The trial was registered (NCT01936675) prior to completion, and "Shared decision making (as assessed by survey)" was pre-specified as a secondary outcome. The authors reported the overall SDM score as well as the results of individual questions and an objective OPTION5 score from video recordings, showing no evidence of selective reporting of multiple measurements or analyses.

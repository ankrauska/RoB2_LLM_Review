**1. bias due to randomization: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: High risk of bias
b. Rationale: The randomization procedure was performed by picking the name of the orthosis from a bag. This method of sequence generation is random, but it does not adequately conceal the allocation sequence, as the person picking could potentially see or manipulate the draw. The lack of adequate allocation concealment in an unblinded trial leads to a high risk of bias for this domain, despite the baseline characteristics being balanced. The study states: "The randomization procedure was to pick the name of the orthosis from a bag including the names of both orthoses."

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: While participants and personnel (the certified orthotist) were aware of the assigned interventions due to the nature of the orthoses, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. This was a single-session evaluation where patients wore the assigned orthosis for 1.5 hours before assessment, minimizing the opportunity for context-driven deviations or crossover. Additionally, all 18 randomized participants were analyzed in the groups to which they were assigned.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Outcome data were available for all randomized participants. The study enrolled 18 participants (8 in the PTS group and 10 in the Spinomed group), and all 18 were included in the final analysis of the balance score, meaning there was no missing outcome data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Although participants and personnel were unblinded, the balance score was measured objectively using an automated computerized force plate system (the NeuroCom EquiTest system). The SOT (Sensory Organization Test) relies on load cells transmitting force information directly to a computer, which does not involve subjective judgement by the assessor. Therefore, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: Some concerns
b. Rationale: There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a published trial protocol finalized before unblinded data were available. However, the reported outcomes (balance score, directional control, and reaction time) match standard outputs of the EquiTest and Balance Master systems used, and both within-group and between-group analyses were fully reported for all variables. Without a pre-specified protocol, a judgement of "some concerns" is appropriate.

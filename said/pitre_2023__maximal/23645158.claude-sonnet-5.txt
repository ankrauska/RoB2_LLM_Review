# RoB 2.0 Assessment: NT Pro-BNP Outcome

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** 
- **Sequence generation:** The trial reports "the patients were allocated into one of the two groups based on a computer random numbers table," which constitutes an adequate random component (Signalling Q1.1 = Yes).
- **Allocation concealment:** No information is provided regarding how the allocation sequence was concealed from those enrolling participants (e.g., no mention of sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes; no central randomization system; no pharmacy-controlled dispensing). The article states drugs were prepared/administered via "50 ml syringe" but does not describe how allocation was concealed prior to enrollment (Signalling Q1.2 = No information).
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table 1 shows no statistically significant differences in age, BSA, Euroscore, weight, EF, ESR, hematocrit, creatinine, CPB/ACC time, graft number, or baseline magnesium/calcium levels (all P > 0.05), suggesting no evidence of a problem with randomization (Signalling Q1.3 = No).
- Per the RoB2 algorithm, "No information" on allocation concealment combined with no evidence of baseline imbalance maps to "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," because concealment cannot be confirmed as adequate.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The study is explicitly described as "double-blind," with both groups receiving indistinguishable interventions via identical syringes/infusions ("MgSO4 infusion... via a 50 ml syringe. The same volume of normal saline was administered as a placebo via a 50 ml syringe intravenously"), suggesting participants and personnel administering the intervention were unaware of assignment (Q2.1/Q2.2 = Probably No).
- Anesthesia protocol, surgical technique, CPB management, and postoperative care were standardized and delivered by the same team for both groups ("the anesthesiologists, the surgeons, and the medical team were the same"), minimizing risk of differential co-interventions arising from trial context.
- One pre-specified exclusion criterion existed (patients requiring triple inotropic therapy during CPB weaning were excluded from analysis), applied prospectively and equally as a protocol-based rule rather than as a post-hoc, results-driven exclusion; the number of exclusions was stated to be tracked ("The number of patients excluded from the study in each group was registered"), though the actual numbers excluded per group are not reported in the text.
- Reported group sizes are equal and consistent with randomization (44/44), and there is no indication of crossover or non-protocol interventions being applied differentially.
- Because blinding appears robust and deviations (if any) were pre-specified and not clearly tied to trial context or imbalanced between groups, this domain is judged as low risk for the assignment-to-intervention effect.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Data are reported for all 44 patients in the MgSO4 group and all 44 patients in the placebo group in Table 3 (Pre CPB BNP and Postoperative BNP), with no indication of missing values, dropouts, or loss to follow-up for this specific biomarker outcome.
- The blood sampling protocol was standardized (samples drawn pre-anesthesia and at 24 h post-ICU arrival, handled identically for both groups), and no mention is made of unavailable samples or unmeasured outcomes.
- No CONSORT flow diagram is presented, but the consistent n = 44/44 reported for baseline and postoperative NT pro-BNP across all tables suggests outcome data were obtained for essentially all randomized participants.
- Signalling Question 3.1 ("Were data available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?") is answered "Probably Yes" based on consistent reporting, leading to a "Low" risk judgement without need to proceed to further signalling questions.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- NT Pro-BNP was measured using a standardized, objective laboratory method: "an electrochemical luminescence immunoassay in an Elecsys 2010 (Roche, Indianapolis, IN, USA)," with a defined coefficient of variation (2.2–3.2%) and detection range, which is an appropriate and validated measurement method (Q4.1 = No, not inappropriate).
- The measurement method, sampling technique, centrifugation, and storage protocol were identical and applied consistently to both groups ("Sampling temperature, centrifugation and refrigeration were kept consistent throughout the study"), so ascertainment did not differ between intervention groups (Q4.2 = No).
- This is an observer-reported (laboratory-assessed) outcome that does not involve subjective judgement; automated immunoassay results are not susceptible to influence by knowledge of intervention assignment. Additionally, the trial was double-blind, so laboratory personnel processing samples were also likely unaware of group assignment (Q4.3 = Probably No aware / Q4.4 = Not likely influenced).
- Given the objective nature of the assay and consistent methodology across groups, risk of bias in outcome measurement is low.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial was prospectively registered (IRCT201205069650N1), and NT Pro-BNP is presented as a primary study endpoint in both the abstract and methods ("we performed this study to assess the effect of MgSO4 solution... on NT Pro-BNP levels"), suggesting the outcome itself was pre-specified rather than selected post hoc from a broader panel of candidate outcomes.
- However, no statistical analysis plan (SAP) is referenced or available for comparison, and the article does not explicitly confirm that the specific analysis reported (comparison of 24-hour postoperative NT pro-BNP via Student's t-test) was finalized prior to unblinding of outcome data (Signalling Q5.1 = No information).
- NT Pro-BNP was measured at only two time points (baseline and 24 h postoperatively); it is unclear from the report whether additional time points or alternative analytic approaches (e.g., change scores, adjusted analyses, AUC) were originally planned but not reported. Indeed, the authors themselves note as a limitation: "we just assessed two points of the serum levels of NT pro-BNP; while the trend of the marker or the 'area under curve' (AUC) was not measured" — raising the possibility that data collection/analysis choices could have been influenced by early results, though no clear evidence of results-based selection is presented.
- Because there is insufficient information to confirm adherence to a pre-specified plan for this specific outcome/analysis, but no explicit evidence of selective reporting from multiple eligible measurements or analyses, the outcome is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

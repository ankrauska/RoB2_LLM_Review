# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment
**Outcome: Clinical pregnancy rate**
**Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)**

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The allocation sequence was generated using an adequate random method, and baseline characteristics were well balanced across groups, suggesting no problem with the randomization process itself. However, the method of allocation concealment is inadequately described, raising concerns that the sequence may not have been adequately concealed from those enrolling participants.

**b. Rationale:**
- Sequence generation: "Two hundred and forty-one patients were prospectively randomized using computer-generated random numbers" — this satisfies the criterion for a random sequence (Signalling question 1.1: Yes).
- Allocation concealment: "Women were allocated randomly to Group I, Group II or Group III on the day of the 2nd or 3rd day of menstruation with sealed envelopes by specialist nurse." This description lacks key details required for a judgement of adequate concealment (e.g., whether envelopes were opaque, sequentially numbered, tamper-proof, or opened only after irreversible enrollment). Per RoB2 guidance, standard "sealed envelopes" without these additional safeguards are "more susceptible to manipulation" (Signalling question 1.2: No information/Probably no).
- Baseline imbalance: Table I shows no significant differences in age, duration of infertility, BMI, or BMI category between groups (all P = NS), consistent with successful randomization (Signalling question 1.3: No).
- Per the decision algorithm, random sequence (Yes) + concealment (No information) + no baseline imbalance concerns (No) maps to "Some concerns."

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** This was an open-label trial with no mention of blinding of participants, care providers, or study personnel. Different gonadotrophin formulations (from different sources: recombinant vs. two urinary-derived products) were administered and doses individually titrated by treating personnel who were necessarily aware of group assignment. There is no explicit information about whether this awareness led to deviations from the intended intervention (e.g., differential co-interventions, differential cycle cancellation practices) that could have affected the outcome. However, the primary outcome (clinical pregnancy) is objectively determined and analysis was in line with intention-to-treat principles.

**b. Rationale:**
- No statement regarding blinding of participants, physicians, or nursing staff administering treatment or monitoring cycles: "Ovarian cycle stimulation was started... Stimulation of the cycles was monitored by serum estradiol (E2) level and transvaginal sonography, and gonadotrophin doses were adjusted individually" — dose titration decisions were made by aware personnel (Signalling questions 2.1/2.2: Probably yes/No information).
- No information is provided on whether deviations (e.g., differential cancellation, adjunct treatments) occurred due to trial context (Signalling question 2.3: No information).
- The analysis included all randomized participants per group as allocated (81, 80, 80 - matching randomization numbers in Table II and the CONSORT diagram), consistent with an appropriate (ITT-consistent) analysis (Signalling question 2.6: Yes).
- Because of the lack of blinding combined with no information about whether this led to consequential deviations, but with an appropriate analysis strategy, the domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Outcome data (clinical pregnancy) were available for essentially all randomized participants, with no evidence of exclusions or attrition affecting the primary outcome.

**b. Rationale:**
- The CONSORT flow diagram indicates 241 patients were randomized (81, 80, 80) and Table II reports cycle outcomes for the same denominators (n = 81, n = 80, n = 80), indicating no loss to follow-up or missing outcome data for the pregnancy outcome.
- "Clinical pregnancy was documented by TV-US, 6 weeks after the IUI" for all participants; there is no report of participants lost to follow-up before this assessment.
- Since data were available for all/nearly all randomized participants, Signalling question 3.1 is answered "Yes," leading directly to a "Low risk" judgement.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Clinical pregnancy was measured using an objective, appropriate method (transvaginal ultrasound), applied identically across all three groups. This is an objective outcome (akin to a binary clinical event) that is unlikely to be influenced by assessor or participant knowledge of intervention assignment.

**b. Rationale:**
- "Clinical pregnancy was documented by TV-US, 6 weeks after the IUI" — a standard, objective, and appropriate outcome measurement method (Signalling question 4.1: No, not inappropriate).
- The method used was identical for all intervention groups; there is no indication that pregnancy ascertainment (via ultrasound) would differ by allocated group (Signalling question 4.2: No).
- Although outcome assessors (treating physicians) were presumably aware of the intervention (open-label), clinical pregnancy confirmed by ultrasound is an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement" analogous to an objective clinical event; such outcomes are not generally susceptible to influence by knowledge of the intervention received (Signalling questions 4.3/4.4: No/Not likely to be influenced).
- Therefore, this domain is judged as low risk.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The primary outcome (clinical pregnancy rate) was pre-specified in the Methods section and is reported consistently with the stated hypothesis and sample size calculation. However, no pre-registered trial protocol or statistical analysis plan is referenced in the article, and no information is available (within the article itself) to confirm that the reported analyses represent the full pre-specified set of analyses, so the possibility of selective reporting among multiple candidate analyses/timepoints cannot be excluded.

**b. Rationale:**
- "The primary outcome was clinical pregnancy rate with duration of stimulation, total gonadotrophin dose, number of dominant follicles, clinical pregnancy rate, multiple pregnancy, miscarriage rate and ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) rate being secondary outcomes" — this is explicit and pre-specified within the article, and the results section reports on all of these measures without apparent omission.
- However, there is no mention of a trial registration number, a published protocol, or a statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinding of outcome data. Absent this information (Signalling question 5.1: No information), and without documented evidence ruling out selection among multiple possible outcome definitions/timepoints or analytic approaches (Signalling questions 5.2/5.3: No information), the default judgement under the RoB2 algorithm for "No information" responses is "Some concerns."
- Note: the task indicated that trial registry information could be used to inform this judgement, but no registry details (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov entry) were provided in the materials supplied for review; therefore, this judgement is based on the internal consistency of the published report alone.

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** Although no single domain was judged as "High risk of bias" in isolation, three domains (randomization process, deviations from intended intervention, and selective reporting) were judged to raise "Some concerns." Per RoB 2.0 guidance, "'Some concerns' in multiple domains may lead the review authors to decide on an overall judgement of 'High' risk of bias for that outcome," particularly given that: (1) allocation concealment was inadequately described in a trial with an unblinded, open-label design; (2) personnel/participants were seemingly unblinded to intervention assignment with individualized dose titration decisions and no confirmation of absence of differential deviations; and (3) no trial registration or protocol was available to verify that the reported result was not selected from among multiple possible analyses. The cumulative effect of concerns across these domains substantially lowers confidence in the reported effect of rFSH on clinical pregnancy rate, particularly as this finding (P = 0.04–0.046) is only marginally statistically significant and the authors themselves acknowledge that "the low power value and the P value of clinical pregnancy rate close to significance are all the limitations of our study."

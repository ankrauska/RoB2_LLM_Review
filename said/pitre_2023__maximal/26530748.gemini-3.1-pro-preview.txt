**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias. 
b. Rationale: The study is a follow-up of the randomized PORTEC-2 trial. As a multicenter phase III randomized trial, allocation sequence generation and concealment were likely performed adequately. Baseline characteristics were well balanced between the intervention groups, suggesting no problems with the randomization process. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias. 
b. Rationale: The assessment is for the effect of assignment to the intervention. Although participants and personnel could not be blinded due to the nature of the interventions (external beam radiation therapy versus vaginal brachytherapy), there is no indication that deviations from the intended interventions arose because of the trial context. Analyses were conducted according to the intention-to-treat principle.

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
c. Judgement: Some concerns. 
d. Rationale: The response rate for the questionnaires was 77% (205 of 265 eligible patients) at 7 years and 67% (80 of 119 patients) at 10 years. While linear mixed models were used to account for missing data under the missing-at-random assumption, it is possible that missingness in health-related quality of life outcomes could depend on their true values (e.g., sicker patients might be less likely to complete the questionnaires). Therefore, there is some concern regarding missing outcome data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: Some concerns. 
b. Rationale: The EORTC QLQ-C30 Global health score is a participant-reported outcome. Participants were not blinded to their assigned treatment (EBRT or VBT). Therefore, the outcome assessors (the participants) were aware of the intervention received, which could potentially influence their self-assessment of health-related quality of life. However, because this is a long-term follow-up (7 and 10 years post-treatment), the lack of blinding is less likely to severely bias the global health assessment than it would in the acute phase, leading to some concerns rather than high risk.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Low risk of bias. 
b. Rationale: The EORTC QLQ-C30 is a standardized and validated instrument, and its subscales and global health score were analyzed according to established EORTC scoring guidelines. There is no evidence that the reported results were selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on the direction or statistical significance of the findings.

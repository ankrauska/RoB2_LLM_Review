**Outcome assessed:** Frequency of delivery **after 48 hours** following randomization to celecoxib versus magnesium sulfate (MgSO₄). The assessment concerns the **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline**.

### 1. Bias due to randomization: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**  
Although the authors describe the study as randomized and state that participants were divided by “simple random sampling,” there is insufficient evidence that a genuinely random allocation sequence was generated or that allocation was concealed until enrolment.

* The report states: “all patients were divided in two groups by simple random sampling.” This does not describe a random sequence-generation method (e.g., computer-generated random numbers or random-number table).
* No allocation-concealment mechanism is reported. There is no mention of central randomization, a pharmacy-controlled system, or sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes.
* Importantly, there is evidence of a substantial baseline imbalance in cervical effacement: mean effacement was 35.93% (SD 18.07) in the MgSO₄ group and 42.0% (SD 15.64) in the celecoxib group (*P* ≤ 0.0001). Cervical effacement is a clinically important prognostic characteristic in threatened preterm labour, because more advanced effacement is associated with a greater likelihood of earlier delivery.
* The authors also state that there was a statistically significant difference in gestational age between groups (*P* < 0.0001), although the gestational-age-by-group data are not presented in Table 3. Gestational age is also a key prognostic factor for delivery timing.

Under RoB 2, lack of information on allocation concealment together with baseline imbalances suggestive of a problematic randomization process leads to a high-risk judgement. The imbalance would tend to place the celecoxib group at higher baseline risk of delivery within 48 hours, although the overall direction of resulting bias cannot be determined reliably.

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to intervention.

**b. Rationale:**  
This domain considers only deviations arising because of the trial context that could bias the effect of being assigned to celecoxib or MgSO₄. It does not treat ordinary non-adherence or protocol-consistent treatment discontinuation as bias for the intention-to-treat effect.

* The trial was described as “single blinded,” but the report does not state who was blinded. Given the markedly different interventions—intravenous MgSO₄ with monitoring versus oral celecoxib—participants and treating personnel were likely aware, or could become aware, of the allocated treatment.
* Nevertheless, the report provides no evidence that awareness of assignment caused differential non-protocol cointerventions, differential management, or other deviations that arose specifically because of trial participation and that would affect delivery after 48 hours.
* Both groups received the same stated background management: “bed rest and hydration … corticosteroid therapy.”
* Treatment cessation when contractions did not stop appears protocol specified: “if the uterine preterm contractions did not stop with those medications, so immediatry drug has been stopped.” This is not, by itself, a trial-context deviation.
* The outcome comparison appears to retain participants in their assigned groups. Although the outcome table has serious denominator inconsistencies (addressed under missing outcome data), there is no explicit evidence that women were analysed according to treatment received or were excluded because they did not adhere to their allocated treatment.

Thus, despite likely lack of blinding, there is no reported evidence of trial-context deviations that were likely to affect the outcome and were unbalanced between groups. The analysis is not explicitly labelled intention-to-treat, but there is insufficient evidence of an as-treated or naïve per-protocol analysis.

### 3. Missing outcome data: **High risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** High risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**  
The reported outcome data are internally inconsistent and indicate substantial, unexplained, differential missingness.

* The study states that 600 women were “randomly divided in two equal groups,” implying 300 women per group.
* For MgSO₄, Table 1 reports 240 women delivering before 48 hours and 60 after 48 hours, totalling 300.
* For celecoxib, Table 1 reports 227 women delivering before 48 hours and 30 after 48 hours, totalling only 257 women. Therefore, outcome data for delivery timing are absent for **43 of 300 women (14.3%)** assigned to celecoxib.
* No corresponding missing data are apparent in the MgSO₄ group. Thus, missingness is markedly unbalanced: apparently 0/300 in MgSO₄ versus 43/300 in celecoxib.
* The article provides no participant flow diagram, no explanation for the 43 unaccounted women, no reasons for missing outcome data, and no sensitivity analysis to assess the potential effect of the missing data.
* Delivery timing should ordinarily be available for women receiving hospital treatment for preterm labour. Consequently, unexplained missing outcome data—especially when occurring only or predominantly in one group—could plausibly be related to clinical outcome, follow-up, withdrawal, transfer, treatment failure, or another outcome-related process.

In addition, Table 1 appears to contradict the text. The text reports that “80% of MgSO4 group and 75% of celecoxib group showed positive response … and uterine contraction stopped at least for 48 hours,” whereas the table labels these same percentages as “Delivery before 48 hours.” This inconsistency further limits confidence that the analysed outcome data are complete and correctly reported.

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**  
Delivery after 48 hours is an objective, time-based clinical outcome that should be ascertainable from hospital records or delivery records.

* The timing threshold is clearly defined in the reported table as “Delivery before 48 hours” and “Delivery after 48 hours.”
* There is no indication that different definitions, thresholds, or outcome-measurement procedures were used in the two intervention groups.
* Personnel were probably aware of treatment assignment because intravenous MgSO₄ and oral celecoxib are readily distinguishable, and the report does not clearly identify who was blinded.
* However, awareness of treatment allocation is unlikely to alter the factual timing of delivery. Unlike a patient-reported or judgement-based outcome, delivery occurring before or after 48 hours is not readily susceptible to assessor interpretation.

The serious discrepancy between the textual description and Table 1 raises concerns about reporting and data handling, but it does not by itself show that the underlying clinical outcome was measured differently between treatment groups. The issue is therefore addressed more directly under missing outcome data and selection of the reported result.

### 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**  
The article does not provide a sufficiently detailed, prospectively finalized statistical analysis plan for this specific outcome, and the available reporting raises uncertainty about which result was intended and analysed.

* The publication states that analyses used “chi-square and independent *t*-test,” but does not identify a prespecified primary outcome, define the intended analysis population, or specify how missing data would be handled.
* The study registration is referenced in the request, but its contents—including the prospectively specified outcome definition, time point, primary analysis, and analysis plan—are not provided in the material available for this assessment. Therefore, it cannot be confirmed that the reported result for delivery after 48 hours was analysed according to a prespecified plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available.
* There is no clear evidence that the specific result was selected from multiple analyses or multiple eligible outcome measurements on the basis of its statistical significance. Therefore, high risk cannot be concluded.
* However, the report contains material inconsistencies concerning the outcome. Table 1 labels 80.0% and 75.7% as “delivery before 48 hours,” whereas the discussion interprets these proportions as successful arrest of contractions “at least for 48 hours.” This makes it unclear whether the reported numerical result corresponds consistently to the prespecified outcome of delivery after 48 hours.
* The missing 43 women in the celecoxib group are not addressed, and the denominators underlying the reported percentages are unclear. This leaves uncertainty about the analysis set and whether the reported analysis was selected or modified after examining the data.

Accordingly, there are some concerns about selective reporting, primarily because the prespecified analysis cannot be verified and the published outcome reporting is internally inconsistent.

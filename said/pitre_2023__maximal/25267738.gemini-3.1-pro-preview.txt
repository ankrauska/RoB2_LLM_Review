**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** Some concerns.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial used a sealed-envelope technique, but the allocation was "based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." The use of clinical chart numbers for generating the sequence is not a truly random method and could allow the sequence to be predictable, especially with a fixed block size in an open-label trial. However, there were no substantial baseline imbalances between the groups, so there is no definitive evidence that the randomization process was subverted. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial was open-label, meaning participants and personnel were aware of the intervention assignment. However, there is no evidence to suggest that there were substantial deviations from the intended intervention that arose specifically because of the trial context and would affect the outcome. Furthermore, the publication states that "Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis," which is the appropriate analysis strategy for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial reported outcome data for all participants randomly assigned to the groups. Although some patients died during follow-up due to cancer progression, there is no indication of differential loss to follow-up or that missingness depended on the true value of the recurrent thromboembolic events. All randomized patients (119 in group A1 and 123 in group A2) were included in the final event count analysis.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** Although the patients and treating physicians were unblinded, the assessment of the outcomes was blinded. The publication states: "All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of the patient’s name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment." Because an independent and blinded adjudication committee assessed the outcomes, it is unlikely that the measurement of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **b. Rationale:** The reported primary outcome is recurrent venous thromboembolism (VTE). This matches the pre-specified primary outcome listed in the trial registration (NCT00450645): "Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism." There is no indication that the reported results were selected from multiple measurements or analysis methods on the basis of the results.

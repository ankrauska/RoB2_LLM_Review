Based on the RoB 2.0 tool guidance and the provided trial report, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of overall survival:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The trial used adequate methods for random sequence generation and allocation concealment. While there was a slight baseline imbalance in age, it does not suggest a fundamental problem with the randomization process.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial report states that patients were "randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via a centralized Interactive Web Response System," which ensures both a random sequence and allocation concealment. Table 1 shows baseline characteristics were generally well-balanced, though there were "significantly more patients older than 70 years in the elacytarabine arm" (30% vs 18%). However, such imbalances in a single factor can occur by chance and the authors adjusted for age in their analysis (HR 0.98, P = 0.84), indicating the randomization process itself was not flawed.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Although the trial was open-label, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose due to the trial context, and the data were analyzed using an appropriate intention-to-treat (ITT) approach. 
* **b. Rationale:** The trial was an "open-label, randomized phase III study," meaning participants and personnel were aware of the intervention. However, there is no indication that significant deviations inconsistent with the protocol arose specifically because of this context. Furthermore, the trial appropriately analyzed the effect of assignment to intervention (the ITT effect) by including all randomized patients in their respective assigned groups: "The intention-to-treat population comprised 191 patients in the elacytarabine group and 190 in the control arm." 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Outcome data for overall survival were available for nearly all randomized participants.
* **b. Rationale:** Overall survival is an objective outcome that is rarely subject to missingness if patients are followed adequately. According to the patient disposition flowchart (Figure 2), only 1 patient (1%) in the elacytarabine arm was lost to follow-up, and 4 patients (2%) in the investigator's choice arm were lost to follow-up or withdrawn. Because the proportion of missing data is extremely small, it could not have made an important difference to the estimated effect of the intervention. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The method of measuring overall survival is objective and appropriate, and its assessment could not plausibly be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
* **b. Rationale:** While outcome assessors, personnel, and participants were not blinded (open-label design), overall survival is an objective, observer-reported outcome that does not involve clinical judgment (unlike subjective symptom scales or progression-free survival). Therefore, the assessment of whether a patient is alive or dead cannot reasonably be influenced by awareness of the assigned intervention.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** The reported results correspond to the pre-specified primary outcome, and there is no evidence of selection among multiple outcome measurements or analyses.
* **b. Rationale:** The trial protocol was prospectively registered (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT01147939), and the published report explicitly states, "The primary end point was overall survival (OS)." The numerical results presented for OS match this pre-specified intention. There is no indication that the investigators calculated multiple different effect estimates for survival and selectively reported the one that was most favorable.

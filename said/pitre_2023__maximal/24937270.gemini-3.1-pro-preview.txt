**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The study used a random component for sequence generation ("Randomization was completed using blocks of four") and there were no significant baseline imbalances ("There were no significant differences in demographic characteristics between groups (Table 1)"). Although allocation concealment is not explicitly detailed, the block randomization process with small groups and double-blinding suggests a low risk.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The trial was double-blinded ("Both the investigator assessing the outcomes and the subjects were blinded to the intervention"). Subjects could not guess their allocation ("Subjects did not guess correctly the stimulation group to which they were assigned beyond chance (p = 0.679)"). No significant deviations that would affect the assignment to the interventions at baseline were described.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
c. Judgement: low risk of bias
d. Rationale: 38 out of 40 randomized subjects completed the study. One was removed due to excessive artifacts ("One subject in the Random Frequency group was removed from the analysis due to excessive artifacts in the EEG recording"). Missing data is very small and unlikely to depend on the true value of the outcome.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome (qEEG, interhemispheric coherence) is an objective, physiological measure (observer-reported outcome not involving judgement) and assessors were blinded. Thus, outcome assessment is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: No trial registration or pre-specified statistical analysis plan is available/referenced in the document to confirm that the analyzed outcomes and specific measurement methods (e.g., specific electrode pairs, specific frequency sub-bands) were strictly predefined before data unblinding, leaving open the possibility of selection from multiple eligible analyses.

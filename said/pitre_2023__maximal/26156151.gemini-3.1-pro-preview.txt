**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
**b. Rationale:** The trial used an interactive web-based response system to randomly assign study participants and stratify them by participating center, ensuring the allocation sequence was both random and adequately concealed. Table 1 indicates that baseline clinical and angiographic characteristics were well matched between the IVUS-guided and angiography-guided groups without significant differences, suggesting no problems with the randomization process. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. 
**b. Rationale:** Given the nature of the intervention (IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided PCI), it was not possible to blind the trial personnel performing the procedure. However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context that were inconsistent with the protocol. Furthermore, the trial authors state, "The intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses," ensuring an appropriate analysis was used to estimate the effect of assignment to the intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
**b. Rationale:** Outcome data were available for almost all randomized participants. As reported in the text: "200 patients of IVUS-guided group (99.5%) and 201 patients (100%) in the angiography-guided group finished 12-month clinical follow-up." Missingness of less than 1% is negligible and highly unlikely to bias the results for the outcome of cardiac death. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
**b. Rationale:** The outcome of cardiac death is an objective, observer-reported outcome that does not involve subjective clinical judgment. Although trial personnel were aware of the assigned intervention, the assessment of a hard clinical endpoint like cardiac death could not reasonably be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
**b. Rationale:** The trial was registered (NCT01563952), and the pre-specified primary outcome in the registry is exactly what is reported in the paper ("event rate of cardiac death within 12 months"). There is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple analyses of the data on the basis of statistical significance.

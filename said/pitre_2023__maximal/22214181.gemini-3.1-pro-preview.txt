Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance and the provided trial report, here is the risk of bias assessment for the List of attributions task (LAT) outcome:

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: The randomization sequence was adequately generated, and baseline characteristics were balanced, but there is no information on allocation concealment. 
b. Rationale: The trial report states, "a Microsoft Excel random number generator was used to randomize participants into the two conditions," indicating a random sequence. The authors also report that "No significant pre-intervention group differences were found." However, there is no description of whether the allocation sequence was concealed (e.g., using opaque envelopes or central randomization) until participants were enrolled.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were likely aware of the intervention, but the trial context did not lead to deviations, and an appropriate analysis was used.
b. Rationale: The study was a brief, 1-hour session where "The study investigator read the information sheet alongside each participant individually," meaning the investigator and participants were not blinded. However, because this was a single brief laboratory session, there were no deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. All eligible participants who completed the task were included in the analysis. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all or nearly all randomized participants.
b. Rationale: The entire experiment took place within a 1-hour testing session. Out of the initial pool, five participants' data were excluded for reasons unrelated to the intervention (two were under 18, one lacked demographic data, two failed to complete the baseline outcome measures appropriately). The remaining 88 participants completed the pre- and post-intervention LAT measures, meaning missing outcome data during the trial follow-up is not an issue.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: The outcome was measured appropriately, and the outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignment.
b. Rationale: The LAT is a participant-reported task, but the subjective coding of the written responses into sleep-related or non-sleep-related categories was performed by a "laboratory volunteer... who was blind to the study hypotheses." Furthermore, "The study investigator then reviewed the categories while being blind to participants’ random assignment." This blinding of the outcome assessors prevents knowledge of the intervention from influencing the outcome assessment. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: All measured components of the LAT were reported, but there is no evidence of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registration to completely rule out selective analysis. 
b. Rationale: The study reported all three planned scoring metrics for the LAT (number of attributions, frequency ranking, and proportion of importance). However, there is no mention of a pre-published trial protocol or a time-stamped statistical analysis plan (and no trial registration document is provided) to verify that these exact scoring methods and analyses were finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. Therefore, some concerns remain.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Patient Acceptance of the Appliance

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Sequence generation:** The allocation sequence was generated randomly. The article states: "Random sequence generation was done with a computer-generated list of random numbers obtained from an Excel spreadsheet... using the '=RAND()' function... a blocked randomization was performed (random permuted blocks with randomized order of block sizes) with block sizes of 6 and 4." → **Yes**, sequence was random.
- **Allocation concealment:** "Allocation concealment was achieved with sequentially numbered and sealed opaque envelopes that were concealed from the researchers and the assessors." Randomization was performed by "a person who was not involved in the trial." → **Yes**, adequately concealed.
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table I shows baseline characteristics (age, SNA, SNB, ANB, MP/SN, MMP, overjet, crowding) were comparable between groups, with the authors reporting "The baseline characteristics were similar in both groups." No large or suspicious imbalances are evident that would suggest a problem with the randomization process.

All three criteria for low risk (random sequence, concealed allocation, no concerning baseline imbalance) are met.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Awareness of assignment:** Blinding of participants and personnel was impossible given the visible, physically distinct nature of the interventions (mini-implants vs. none): "Blinding of the clinicians and patients to the interventions in each group was impossible." → Participants and personnel were aware (Y).
- **Deviations arising from trial context:** There is no evidence that awareness of allocation led to deviations from the protocol that were inconsistent with the trial protocol. The CONSORT flow diagram (Figure 4) shows no dropouts, no crossovers, and no non-protocol interventions — "No patients dropped out during the trial," and "there were no dropouts." All patients received their assigned intervention as planned, and follow-up/treatment proceeded according to protocol until the pre-specified endpoint (edge-to-edge incisor relationship) was reached in both groups (mean follow-up 5.08 vs 4.96 months, no significant difference, P = 0.80).
- **Appropriate analysis:** "The primary analysis was planned to be an intention-to-treat analysis to involve all patients who were to be randomly assigned; however, since there were no dropouts in the study, all the patients were included in the analysis." All 32 randomized participants were analyzed in their assigned groups.

Because there is no evidence that lack of blinding led to protocol-inconsistent deviations affecting the outcome, and an appropriate (full) analysis of all randomized participants was used, this domain is judged low risk for the effect of assignment to intervention.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Outcome data (questionnaire responses) were available for all randomized participants: "The 32 surveys were retrieved from the patients; there were no dropouts." The CONSORT diagram confirms 16/16 analyzed in each arm with no losses to follow-up or exclusions.
- Since data were available for 100% of participants, there is no possibility of bias due to missing outcome data for this outcome.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Appropriateness of measurement method:** The outcome was measured via a structured questionnaire (5-point Likert scale for most items) adapted from a previously used instrument (Bowman et al.). This method is appropriate for evaluating patient-reported acceptance and satisfaction, and it was administered identically to both groups on the day of appliance removal — thus measurement did **not differ between groups** in terms of instrument or timing.
- **Outcome assessor and blinding:** For "patient acceptance," the outcome assessor is the **patient him/herself** (a participant-reported outcome). Because "blinding of the clinicians and patients to the interventions in each group was impossible" (patients in the FMI group could see/feel the mini-implants and the wire segment used for fixation, and underwent an additional procedure — local anesthesia and mini-implant insertion), patients were necessarily aware of their allocated intervention.
- **Potential for influence:** Per RoB2 guidance, participant-reported outcomes are, by definition, potentially influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment, which alone raises this domain to at least "Some concerns."
- **Likely influence:** There is no strong or explicit evidence in the report that patients' responses were *actually* driven by their knowledge of allocation (e.g., no evidence of strong pre-existing preferences/expectations about mini-implants, and the authors report largely non-significant, comparable ridit values across nearly all questions, e.g., satisfaction with results ridit = 0.57, 95% CI 0.43-0.71, P = 0.36). However, it is plausible that awareness of receiving an "extra" invasive procedure (mini-implant placement under local anesthesia) could have influenced patients' subjective ratings of pain, insertion experience, or overall satisfaction, particularly given the known effect that expectations and awareness can have on self-reported subjective outcomes.

Given that blinding of the outcome assessor (the patient) was impossible and could plausibly have influenced self-reported acceptance/satisfaction ratings, but there is insufficient evidence to conclude that such influence definitely occurred, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- The article explicitly states: **"Registration: This trial was not registered."** and **"Protocol: The protocol was not published before trial commencement."** This means there is no independently verifiable, date-stamped, pre-specified analysis plan against which the reported results can be checked.
- Without a registered protocol or published statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available, it is not possible to confirm that the reported result for "patient acceptance" was not selected, on the basis of the results, from among multiple possible outcome measurements or analyses.
- On the positive side, the methods section pre-specifies the 14-question questionnaire and the primary/secondary outcomes in detail, and all responses to all 14 questions appear to be fully reported in Tables III–VII and Figure 5, with no apparent omissions or unusual aggregations, and the sample size calculation and outcome definitions were described in detail before results were presented. There is no obvious internal evidence (e.g., discrepant outcome definitions, unusual subscale combinations, or inconsistent outcome specification across sections) suggestive of selective reporting.
- However, the complete absence of a prospectively registered protocol means there remains genuine uncertainty (no information) as to whether the analysis presented corresponds exactly to what was originally intended, rather than being shaped after unblinded data were available.

Per RoB2 guidance/algorithm, when there is no information available to confirm a pre-specified analysis plan (and thus no way to rule out selection from multiple eligible measurements/analyses), the default judgement is "Some concerns."

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement for "Patient Acceptance of the Appliance": **Some concerns**

**Rationale:** Domains 1 (randomization) through 3 (missing data) are at low risk of bias. However, Domain 4 (measurement of the outcome) and Domain 5 (selective reporting) both raise "Some concerns" — driven respectively by the unavoidable lack of blinding for a participant-reported outcome with potential (though unconfirmed) influence on responses, and the absence of a registered protocol/statistical analysis plan to verify the reported result was not selectively reported. Per RoB2 guidance, a result with "Some concerns" in at least one domain (and here, two domains, without any domain reaching "High risk") should be judged overall as **"Some concerns."**

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
**a. Judgement:** The trial used a random number table for sequence generation, which is adequate. However, there is no information provided about whether the allocation sequence was concealed, raising some concerns about the randomization process.
**b. Rationale:** The authors state, "Using the random number table method, 90 cases were divided into group A and group B." There is no mention of allocation concealment mechanisms (e.g., sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes or central randomization). Baseline characteristics were reported to be similar between the two groups. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Although participants and personnel were inherently aware of the assigned interventions due to their nature (percutaneous microwave ablation vs. laparoscopic resection), it is unlikely that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context that would systematically bias the surgical time. 
**b. Rationale:** Blinding is not feasible for these differing surgical/interventional procedures. However, there is no evidence or reporting to suggest that non-protocol interventions or deviations occurred due to the lack of blinding. All randomized participants appear to have been analyzed in their assigned groups.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Outcome data for surgical time were available for all randomized participants. 
**b. Rationale:** The trial randomized 90 patients (45 in group A and 45 in group B). Table 1 reports the surgical time for exactly 45 patients in each group, indicating that there was no loss to follow-up or missing data for this outcome.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** The method of measuring surgical time is appropriate and standard. While outcome assessors (the surgical team) were aware of the intervention received, this objective time measurement is highly unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
**b. Rationale:** Surgical time is a standard, objectively measured outcome in surgical trials. Although the personnel were unblinded, it is not plausible that the measurement of the duration of the procedure itself was systematically biased by this knowledge. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
**a. Judgement:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a published trial registration to confirm that the reported analyses were planned before unblinded data were available. 
**b. Rationale:** The trial report does not reference a publicly available protocol or clinical trial registry entry. While surgical time is fully reported for both groups as expected for this type of study, the absence of a pre-specified analysis plan means we cannot definitively rule out that the analysis might have been selected from multiple eligible analyses, leading to some concerns.

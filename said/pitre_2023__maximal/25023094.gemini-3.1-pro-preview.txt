Based on the RoB 2.0 tool guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the trial evaluating the effect of therapeutic ultrasound on pain (assessed via Visual Analogue Scale - VAS):

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial used a random component for sequence generation ("sex-stratified randomization") and concealed the allocation sequence ("allocation sequence was blinded through the use of sealed envelopes"). Table 1 shows no significant baseline imbalances for the VAS pain score or demographic variables, suggesting the randomization process was successful. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** The assessment considers the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat). Patients were blinded to the intervention through the use of a sham/placebo ultrasound ("control group received ultrasound placebo"). Although the personnel delivering the intervention were not blinded ("The blinding of the physiotherapist who applied the treatment was not possible"), there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, the trial authors state that "The analysis was done by intention to treat," and all participants completed the assigned treatment protocols without dropouts.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants. The trial publication explicitly states, "During the study no patient was lost or dropped out." Therefore, missing outcome data could not bias the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **b. Rationale:** The Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) is an appropriate and standard method for measuring pain. The method of measuring the outcome did not differ between study arms. Because this is a patient-reported outcome, the outcome assessor is the patient. The patients were effectively blinded to their treatment group through the use of a placebo ultrasound device. Additionally, the trial employed a "blind assessor" to record the measurements, meaning outcome assessment was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
*   **a. Judgement:** Some concerns.
*   **b. Rationale:** While the trial reports on the pain VAS outcome at the predefined time points (pre-test, 10 sessions, and discharge at 25 sessions) as outlined in the methods section, there is no mention of a pre-registered trial protocol or statistical analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. Without a pre-specified protocol, it cannot be entirely ruled out that the analysis plan was altered, warranting a judgement of "some concerns" according to RoB 2.0 guidance. However, there is no obvious evidence that the reported results were selected from multiple analyses or outcome measurements.

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The trial used a random component for sequence generation ("An offsite investigator prepared the concealed randomization using a computer generated random number sequence"). Allocation was adequately concealed ("Consecutively numbered opaque envelopes containing group allocation were collected from the offsite investigator by the tRNS intervener after initial assessment"). While there are baseline differences between the groups, the extremely small sample size (n=4) means these differences are entirely compatible with chance and do not suggest a problem with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The study was triple-blinded. Participants, trainers, and assessors were unaware of the intervention assignment ("Study personnel involved in assessment and training, along with all participants were blinded to group allocation for the duration of the study"). The tRNS intervener informed participants they may or may not feel the stimulation regardless of their group. All participants completed all training sessions, and there were no deviations from the intended interventions that could have affected the outcomes or arose because of the trial context. Data for all four participants were analyzed, consistent with an intention-to-treat approach.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** Outcome data for the Motor Assessment Scale item 6 (MAS6) were available for all randomized participants at all time points (baseline, post-training, and follow-up), as reported in Table 3. There was no missing outcome data.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The MAS6 is an appropriate and standard method for measuring upper arm function in stroke survivors. The method of measuring the outcome was comparable across intervention groups. Furthermore, the outcome assessors were successfully blinded to the intervention assignments ("Study personnel involved in assessment and training... were blinded to group allocation for the duration of the study"), meaning the assessment could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** Some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** The trial was prospectively registered (ANZCTR). However, there is a discrepancy between the registered primary outcome and the published primary outcome. The registry lists the primary outcome as a "Composite score of Motor Assessment Scale upper limb items 6-8." In contrast, the published paper lists MAS item 6 as the primary outcome, with MAS items 7 and 8 downgraded to secondary outcomes. While all data are presented (given it is a 4-person case series), the analysis and reporting deviate from the pre-specified plan (reporting a single sub-scale rather than the composite score), raising some concerns that the reported result was selected from multiple possible analyses of the data.

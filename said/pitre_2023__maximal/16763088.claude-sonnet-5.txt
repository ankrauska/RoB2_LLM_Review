# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Bond Failure Rate Outcome

**Preliminary considerations:** This is a split-mouth, individually-randomized trial (cross-quadrant design) comparing Transbond™ Plus Self-Etching Primer (SEP) versus conventional etch/primer for bracket bond failure. The effect of interest being assessed is the **effect of assignment to intervention** (intention-to-treat effect), per instructions.

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** 
- *Sequence generation:* "Randomization for allocation of either of the two etch and primer systems to the upper right quadrant was undertaken using a block randomization method as described by Roberts and Torgerson (1998)." This confirms a random component (block randomization) → **Yes**.
- *Allocation concealment:* "The operators were unaware of the assignment until the patient was entered into the trial." This suggests concealment was adequate → **Probably yes**. However, no further detail (e.g., sealed envelopes, central allocation) is given about how concealment was implemented in practice, and because block randomization was used, there is some risk that block size predictability could have allowed anticipation of upcoming allocations, though this is mitigated by the split-mouth design (both treatments given to nearly every patient, reducing incentive for allocation manipulation).
- *Baseline imbalance:* No formal baseline comparability data are presented between quadrants (as would be expected in a split-mouth design, since each patient receives both treatments, baseline imbalance at the patient level is not really applicable). No evidence of problems with randomization from baseline characteristics.
- Overall, sequence generation was random and allocation concealment reasonably described, but the lack of detail on concealment procedures beyond the operator-blinding statement, combined with no explicit reporting of the method of concealment (e.g., sealed opaque envelopes, central allocation), leads to "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Participant awareness:* "The patients were unaware which system had been used on each side of the mouth" → participants blinded (**No**).
- *Personnel/operator awareness:* "as the two systems had different modes of application, it was not possible to blind the operators to the type of system being used" → operators were aware (**Yes**).
- Because operators (personnel) were aware of intervention assignment, the signalling question on whether deviations occurred because of trial context is triggered. There is no explicit evidence that operators deviated from protocol in administering interventions in a way inconsistent with the trial protocol due to trial context (e.g., preferentially administering non-protocol co-interventions). The study reports a standardized bonding protocol applied per manufacturer's instructions for each material, and outcomes (bracket failures) were verified with an adhesive remnant index post-hoc, not likely subject to operator manipulation during application.
- However, because operators were unblinded and given that "the number of manipulations of the bracket prior to curing" was recorded and varied by group (with SEP showing higher failure with more manipulations), there is a possibility that unblinded operators handled the two systems differently (e.g., number of adjustments, effort in application), which could reflect a deviation influenced by trial context, but this is inherent in comparing two materials with different application procedures rather than a deviation from protocol.
- Analysis: All patients were followed and analyzed according to allocated quadrant (split-mouth, all randomized units analyzed) — this appears consistent with an appropriate analysis (ITT-consistent), as the one patient who moved away was still included with his available data.
- Given the unavoidable and inherent lack of blinding of personnel (due to differing application techniques) and no clear evidence that this led to a bias-inducing deviations from protocol (rather it reflects true performance of each product), but the theoretical potential for differential care/handling between groups given lack of blinding, judgement is "Some concerns" rather than "Low."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- "All patients were observed for the entire 12-month period apart from one 13-year-old male who moved to another city after 7 months of observation, during which time no bracket failures occurred. His data was still included in the analysis..."
- This indicates outcome data were available for essentially all randomized units (patients/brackets): 38 of 39 patients had complete 12-month follow-up, and the one incomplete case had 7 months of failure-free data included in the survival analysis (appropriately censored).
- With 661 brackets analyzed and only one patient with partial follow-up (and no evidence that his missingness was related to the true outcome — he moved for reasons unrelated to bracket status), the amount of missing information is negligible and unlikely to bias the result.
- This satisfies the criterion "outcome data available for all, or nearly all, participants," and there is no indication that missingness (censoring) depended on the true value of the outcome.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of method:* Bond failure was assessed via clinical observation of "failure and survival rates" plus an adhesive remnant index (ARI) — an established, validated method (Årtun and Bergland, 1984) for measuring bond failure/adhesive remaining. This is appropriate → **No** to inappropriate methods.
- *Comparability of measurement between groups:* Outcome ascertainment (checking for bracket failure at clinic visits) appears to have been applied identically to both intervention groups within each patient (split-mouth design), reducing risk of differential ascertainment → **No**.
- *Outcome assessor blinding:* Bracket failure (debonding) is a relatively objective outcome (a bracket is either off or on), which reduces the potential for assessment bias, similar to "observer-reported outcomes not involving much judgement." However, the clinicians assessing failure and recording it were the same operators aware of the etching system used in each quadrant (since the systems were visually and procedurally distinct, and operators were not blinded to intervention as noted above). The determination of the *site of failure* using the ARI does require some judgement, and this was performed by an unblinded assessor.
- Because the outcome assessors (operators) were not blinded and the assessment of the *mode of failure* (ARI score) involves some judgement, there is potential for influence of knowledge of intervention received. However, the primary outcome (whether a bracket failed) is relatively hard to manipulate/misclassify (either the bracket is bonded or not), lowering concern for the primary failure rate outcome itself, though not eliminating it.
- Given assessors were unblinded and at least the secondary/associated judgement-based measure (ARI) could be influenced, but no explicit evidence that assessment of simple bracket failure was influenced by knowledge of allocation, the judgement is "Some concerns" (assessment could have been influenced by lack of blinding, but it is not clearly likely).

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- No trial registration record was identified/available (per the search) for this trial to confirm a pre-specified statistical analysis plan finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. The article does not reference a trial registry or published protocol/SAP.
- The primary outcome (bracket bond failure rate) and its analysis (logistic regression, Cox proportional hazards) appear consistent with what would be expected and are reported comprehensively, including multiple pre-specified subgroup analyses (gender, age, operator, tooth location, number of manipulations), all reported regardless of statistical significance (e.g., "did not significantly affect the bond failure rate" is explicitly reported for multiple factors), which is reassuring against selective reporting of only significant findings.
- However, without access to a trial registry entry or a priori statistical analysis plan (doc_id provided did not yield a specific matching registration details in the text provided), it cannot be confirmed that the analysis was conducted strictly according to a pre-specified plan finalized prior to unblinded data access.
- Because there is no information confirming a pre-specified, date-stamped analysis plan, but no clear evidence of selective outcome/analysis reporting either (multiple outcomes/analyses reported comprehensively, including null results), the judgement defaults to "Some concerns."

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

Per RoB 2.0 guidance (Table 1), since no domain was rated "High risk of bias," but multiple domains (randomization, deviations from intended intervention, measurement of outcome, and selective reporting) were rated "Some concerns," the overall judgement for this outcome (bracket bond failure rate) is **Some concerns**, reflecting the accumulation of these moderate concerns across several domains, particularly around unblinding of personnel/outcome assessors, which is somewhat unavoidable given the different application techniques of the two systems being compared and the absence of a confirmed pre-specified analysis plan.

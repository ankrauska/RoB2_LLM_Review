Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome "number of mistakes made in diagnosing problems" in the provided trial:

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**
    a. Judgement: The trial mentions random allocation using a randomization table, but does not provide information on whether the allocation sequence was concealed. Baseline characteristics appear balanced within the randomized groups.
    b. Rationale: The authors state, "students enrolled in the research project were consequently randomised with a randomisation table into two different groups." However, no details on allocation concealment (e.g., opaque sealed envelopes or central randomization) are provided. Table 2 notes "n.s. = no significant difference between Trial and Control groups in each group", indicating no serious baseline imbalances, but the lack of concealment information leads to some concerns.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
    a. Judgement: While participants and personnel could not be blinded to the educational tutorial strategies, there is no evidence that this lack of blinding led to deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, all randomized participants were analyzed according to their assigned groups.
    b. Rationale: Blinding is impossible in this educational context. However, the trial successfully analyzed all 144 randomized participants in their assigned groups (Trento: 41 intensive, 41 routine; Udine: 39 weekly, 23 routine). There is no indication of post-randomization exclusions or cross-overs, meaning the effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat) was appropriately estimated.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
    a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for all randomized participants. 
    b. Rationale: The study flow and Table 3 confirm that all 144 eligible and randomized participants (82 in Trento, 62 in Udine) completed the trial and were included in the Phase 1 and Phase 2 outcome analyses. There is no missing outcome data or loss to follow-up reported.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
    a. Judgement: The method for measuring the outcome was appropriate, and outcome assessors were blinded to the intervention assignments.
    b. Rationale: The outcome was evaluated objectively based on a standard defined by a panel of professional nurses. Crucially, the authors report, "Two researchers did blind evaluation of the problems individualised by the students and these were compared to the ‘standard’." Blinding of the assessors mitigates the risk of measurement bias.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**
    a. Judgement: There is no reference to a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registry protocol finalized before unblinded data were available, making it difficult to rule out selective reporting completely.
    b. Rationale: Without a published protocol, it is unclear if the specific analysis (number of mistakes made in Phase 1 and Phase 2) was strictly pre-planned as the primary outcome. However, there is no overt evidence of selective reporting from multiple measured domains, so this domain raises only some concerns rather than a high risk of bias.

# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment
**Outcome:** Time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Random sequence generation:* "After the open label period, eligible patients were assigned to escitalopram or placebo treatment according to a computer-generated randomisation list." This indicates an adequate random component (Y).
- *Allocation concealment:* "The details of the randomisation series were unknown to any of the investigators and were contained in a set of sealed opaque envelopes. At each study centre, sequentially enrolled patients were assigned the lowest randomisation number available in blocks of four." This describes central-list generation with concealment via sequentially numbered, sealed opaque envelopes, meeting minimal criteria for adequate concealment (Y).
- *Baseline imbalance:* The article states, "There were no differences in demographics and clinical parameters between the two treatment groups in the randomisation period," and Table 1/Table 2 show closely similar baseline characteristics (e.g., mean age 35.8 vs 35.4; Y-BOCS at randomisation 11.2 vs 10.8) consistent with chance (N).

All three criteria (random sequence, concealed allocation, no baseline imbalance suggestive of a problem) are met → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Blinding of participants/personnel:* "Study medications were tablets for oral administration, of identical appearance, taste or smell... All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study." This indicates both participants and personnel were blinded (N to awareness).
- Because blinding was implemented, and there is no evidence that blinding was broken in a way that led to deviations arising from trial context: The authors specifically examined whether discontinuation symptoms (a potential unblinding cue, e.g., dizziness/nausea in the placebo group during the taper) could have confounded the results, using sensitivity analyses. They state: "Examination of adverse events occurring exclusively in the 1 to 4-week period following randomisation demonstrated only a modest increase within the placebo group relative to escitalopram, and dizziness and nausea alone were significantly over represented relative to patients still receiving escitalopram... when the survival analysis excluded withdrawals during the 1- to 2-week period following randomisation, the advantage for escitalopram remained just as strong, suggesting discontinuation effects are not responsible for the benefits associated with remaining on escitalopram." This suggests that although some unblinding may have occurred due to discontinuation symptoms, it did not appreciably affect the outcome.
- *Appropriate analysis:* "All efficacy analyses in the double-blind treatment period were conducted using the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, which consisted of all randomised patients who took at least one dose of trial medication and had at least one post-randomisation efficacy assessment." This is consistent with an appropriate ITT (or modified ITT) analysis for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention. Two patients who did not meet response criteria were excluded from the ITT population as randomized in error—this is a minor issue but unlikely to have had substantial impact (320 of 322 possible, <1%).

Given effective blinding, sensitivity analyses demonstrating deviations (if any) did not bias the outcome, and use of an appropriate (ITT) analysis → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Outcome data (relapse status) were essentially available for all randomized participants because the primary outcome (time to relapse, using survival/Kaplan-Meier methodology) accounts for participants via time-to-event/censoring rather than requiring a final continuous score for every subject. The trial reports complete disposition: "During the 24-week double-blind period, the overall withdrawal rate, excluding relapses, was comparable for both treatments: 7.9% for patients treated with escitalopram (13 patients), and 8.9% for patients treated with placebo (14 patients)." These proportions are small and similar between groups.
- The flow diagram (Figure 2) accounts for all 320 randomized patients through to their disposition (relapse, completion, or withdrawal for other reasons), and the analysis was based on the full ITT population.
- Because time-to-event analysis was used, the small number of participants who withdrew for reasons other than relapse would be censored at their last visit rather than excluded entirely, minimizing risk that missingness depended on the (unobserved) true relapse status. Withdrawal rates for reasons other than relapse were low (<9%) and balanced between arms, with no indication that reasons for these withdrawals differed systematically or were related to the true (unobserved) outcome value in a way that would bias the treatment comparison.

Given the low, balanced levels of missing/censored data and use of appropriate time-to-event analysis accounting for censoring → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of method:* The outcome (relapse) was measured using the Y-BOCS, a well-validated, widely used observer-rated instrument for OCD severity ("relapse" defined as "an increase in the Y-BOCS total score of ≥5 points from the time of randomisation, or an unsatisfactory treatment effect (lack of efficacy) as judged by the investigator"). This is an appropriate, standard method (N to inappropriate).
- *Comparability of measurement between groups:* The same assessment schedule and instruments were applied identically to both arms: "efficacy and tolerability parameters were assessed 2, 4, 6, 8, and 12 weeks after randomisation and then every 4 weeks until their last dose of double-blind treatment." No differential measurement procedures between groups (N).
- *Blinding of outcome assessors:* Outcome assessment is largely observer-reported (Y-BOCS scored by investigator/rater) with some component of judgement. However, blinding was robust: "All study personnel and participants were blinded to treatment assignment for the entire duration of the study," medications were identical in appearance/taste/smell, and rater training was implemented to standardize scoring ("Only those investigators who had actively participated in rater training sessions prior to inclusion of patients into the study were allowed to rate patients... Rater training was undertaken to increase inter-rater reliability").
- While there is some risk that discontinuation-related side effects (nausea/dizziness, more common in the placebo taper group) could unblind raters or patients and thereby influence Y-BOCS ratings, the authors performed sensitivity analyses censoring early relapses (7- and 14-day cut-offs) specifically to rule out this concern, and found the treatment effect was robust: "escitalopram was significantly superior to placebo" in these analyses.

Given use of an appropriate, validated outcome measure, identical assessment procedures across groups, robust blinding maintained via matching placebo and rater training, and sensitivity analyses confirming results were not driven by potential unblinding due to discontinuation symptoms → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registration (ClinicalTrials.gov, referenced in the study materials) pre-specifies "time to relapse" as the primary outcome, consistent with the article's statement: "The prospectively defined primary analysis of efficacy was the time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment." This matches exactly the outcome and analysis (log-rank test / Cox proportional hazards) reported in the results.
- The relapse definition (Y-BOCS increase ≥5 points or investigator-judged lack of efficacy) is specified a priori and used consistently and singularly in the primary analysis — there is no indication that this definition was chosen post hoc from multiple candidate definitions, or that alternative more/less favorable relapse thresholds were tested and selectively reported. The paper explicitly reports "post-hoc" analyses separately and labels them as such (e.g., "In post-hoc analyses based only on the Y-BOCS relapse criterion..."), demonstrating transparency about which analyses were pre-specified versus exploratory.
- The primary analysis method (two-tailed log-rank test) was specified in the statistical methods a priori: "The primary efficacy analysis used a two-tailed log-rank test to compare the time to relapse for patients treated with escitalopram versus placebo." This matches what is reported as the primary result.
- Secondary/sensitivity analyses (7-day and 14-day censoring, per-protocol analysis, dose subgroups) are clearly presented as additional/supportive analyses rather than substituted for the primary analysis, and all analyses point in a consistent direction, reducing concern that a favorable result was cherry-picked from multiple analytic approaches.

Given the clear pre-specification of the primary outcome and its analysis method (matching trial registration), and transparent distinction between pre-specified and post-hoc analyses → **Low risk of bias**.

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Low risk of bias**

All five domains were judged to be at low risk of bias for the outcome "time to relapse from the start date of double-blind treatment." The study employed adequate random sequence generation and allocation concealment with no evidence of baseline imbalance; robust double-blinding with sensitivity analyses ruling out bias from potential discontinuation-related unblinding; a time-to-event analysis with low, balanced missing/censored data; validated and consistently applied outcome measurement with blinded raters; and a primary analysis consistent with the pre-specified plan documented in trial registration materials.

# Risk of Bias Assessment (RoB 2.0)
**Trial:** KANWU study — Effects of (n-3) fatty acid supplementation vs. placebo on lipid peroxidation biomarkers
**Outcome assessed:** Changes in 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The article states participants were "randomly assigned" to SAFA/MUFA diets and, within each, randomly assigned again to fish-oil or placebo capsules, but no details are given regarding the method of sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated randomization, use of blocks/stratification) or the method of allocation concealment (e.g., central randomization, sealed envelopes). No signalling information is available to confirm concealment ("No information"). However, baseline characteristics (Table 1: age, BMI, lipid profile, blood pressure) appear well balanced across the four groups, with no evidence of substantial or improbable imbalance suggestive of a failure of randomization. Per the RoB2 algorithm, "No information" on sequence generation/concealment combined with no evidence of baseline imbalance maps to **Some concerns**.

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** The fish-oil vs. placebo comparison used capsules of fish oil versus "placebo capsules with the same amount of olive oil," implying a double-blind design intended to conceal treatment allocation from participants and personnel. There is no reported evidence of deviations from intended intervention arising from trial context (e.g., no mention of non-protocol interventions, cointerventions, or differential behavior related to awareness of assignment). Participants were instructed to avoid supplements and NSAIDs uniformly across groups. The statistical analysis appears consistent with an intention-to-treat approach: "the treatment effects were estimated from a statistical model in which treatment categories... were analyzed," with covariate adjustment for center, age, sex, and baseline value, and there is no indication of post-randomization exclusions based on adherence or naïve per-protocol/as-treated analysis. Thus, although explicit confirmation of blinding success is not reported, the design and analytic approach support a low risk of bias for the assignment effect.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** Of the 162 randomized participants (across 5 centers), 8-iso-PGF2α data could only be analyzed for participants from 3 of the 5 centers (Finland, Australia, Sweden), because "plasma samples from 2 centers (Denmark and Italy) were unfortunately inadequate to use for analysis of isoprostanes due to inappropriate sample handling and/or storage." This represents a substantial and non-trivial proportion of missing outcome data for the 8-iso-PGF2α outcome (15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α was available from all centers). The stated reason for missingness (sample handling/storage failure) is plausibly unrelated to the true value of the outcome and unrelated to intervention assignment (a technical/logistical failure at the center level rather than participant-level dropout tied to prognosis). However, no information is provided on whether missingness was balanced between treatment arms within the three retained centers, nor were sensitivity analyses conducted to confirm robustness of the result to the missing data. Given the substantial proportion of missing data without direct evidence ruling out an association with intervention group or outcome value, "Some concerns" is warranted (rather than "Low," which would require either near-complete data or explicit sensitivity analysis).

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** Both 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α were measured using validated radioimmunoassays (RIA) developed and validated by Basu (references 15, 20), with reported cross-reactivity and detection limits, indicating appropriate and objective measurement methods. The same assay methodology was applied identically across all intervention groups and centers, so measurement did not differ by treatment allocation. As these are objective biochemical assays performed on frozen plasma samples by laboratory personnel (not participant-reported or judgment-based clinical assessments), the outcome assessment is unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of intervention assignment, even in the absence of an explicit statement that laboratory analysts were blinded. This corresponds to an "observer-reported outcome not involving judgement," which is generally not susceptible to bias from lack of blinding of assessors.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:** The manuscript does not report reference to, or comparison with, a publicly available pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial protocol finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. The outcomes 8-iso-PGF2α and 15-keto-dihydro-PGF2α are presented as primary lipid peroxidation biomarkers consistent with the stated study aim ("to investigate whether... (n-3) fatty acids... affect nonenzymatic and enzymatic lipid peroxidation"), and both measures (nonenzymatic and enzymatic) are reported regardless of statistical significance (one significant, one null), which is reassuring against selective outcome-measure reporting. However, without access to a registered protocol or SAP with a timestamp preceding unblinding, it cannot be confirmed whether alternative measurements, time points, or analytic models (e.g., unadjusted vs. covariate-adjusted models, alternative covariate sets) were considered and selectively reported. In the absence of this information, per RoB2 guidance, the default judgement is "Some concerns."

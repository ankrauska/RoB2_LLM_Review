Based on the provided trial report and the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome Agitation Score (Table 4):

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** Some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** The trial states that a "random assignment" was used, but it does not provide details on how the allocation sequence was generated or if it was adequately concealed. Furthermore, there was a significant baseline imbalance between the groups regarding the amount of time spent in arousal imbalance at pretest ("The experimental group participants spent significantly more time in arousal imbalance at pretest (t = 2.57, p < .01)"). The lack of detail on the randomization process combined with a significant baseline imbalance raises some concerns about this domain.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** High risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The assessment for the effect of assignment to the intervention (intention-to-treat) requires that all randomized participants are analyzed in the groups to which they were originally assigned. The trial report explicitly states that participants were excluded from the analysis if they did not receive the intervention: "10 were dropped from the study for not being given the intervention." The authors explain that "A participant had to have been given at least one of the prescribed changes in activity schedule... to be included in the analysis." This represents a failure to use an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis. Excluding ~10% of the sample post-randomization for reasons related to the delivery of the intervention (e.g., "short-staffed, being busy, or having more emergent needs") has the potential to substantially impact the result. 

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
* **c. Judgement:** High risk of bias
* **d. Rationale:** Outcome data were not available for all randomized participants. Out of 102 participants who initially participated, only 78 were included in the final analysis (12 died, 10 dropped for not receiving the intervention, and 2 excluded for missing/erroneous data). The exclusion of 10 participants specifically because nursing staff did not deliver the scheduled intervention due to being "short-staffed, being busy, or having more emergent needs" means that missingness is closely tied to the trial context and care delivery, which could very likely be related to the true value of the participants' agitation levels (e.g., highly agitated patients might be harder to deliver interventions to).

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The outcome (agitation) was measured using a specific visual analog scale based on direct, systematic observation by research assistants. Crucially, the outcome assessors were unaware of the intervention assignment: "Data collectors and subjects were blinded to treatment and control conditions." Because the outcome assessors were blinded, the measurement of the outcome is unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** Some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial registry record that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. While the reported outcome (agitation measured via observation) aligns with the stated aims in the methods section, the absence of a verifiable pre-published protocol means we cannot completely rule out the possibility that the specific analysis approach (e.g., covariates chosen, exact timeframes averaged) was selected from multiple eligible analyses based on the data.

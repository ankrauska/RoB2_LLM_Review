**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The study used an adequate method for random sequence generation and allocation concealment. The authors state, "Subjects were allocated to either the study (SmartClip) group or control (Victory) group using a block randomization determined by a computer-generated random number table, with stratification for operator and center. The appliance type was placed in a sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelope that was opened after the patient was accepted onto the trial." Furthermore, baseline characteristics appeared reasonably balanced between the two groups. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Participants and personnel were not blinded due to the nature of the interventions (different orthodontic brackets). However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. Regarding the analysis, the authors state, "Due to the low number of dropouts, analysis was undertaken on a per protocol basis." Three patients out of 138 (2.2%) were omitted from the analysis because they failed to complete treatment. Although this is not a strict intention-to-treat analysis, the number of excluded participants is so small that there is no potential for a substantial impact on the result of the failure to analyze participants in the group to which they were randomized.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Outcome data were available for nearly all participants. The study enrolled 138 subjects, and 135 (97.8%) completed the study and had outcome data available. The missing data is too small to have a meaningful impact on the estimated effect. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The outcome being assessed is the total duration of treatment (months) from fixed appliance placement to debond. The outcome assessors (the treating clinicians) were aware of the assigned intervention because the brackets are visibly different. The decision of when a patient's treatment is complete (and thus when to debond the brackets) involves some clinical judgment. Because the operators were aware of the intervention, and the outcome reflects a decision made by the intervention provider, the assessment of the outcome could potentially have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: There is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple outcome measurements or multiple analyses. The outcome of "total duration of treatment" is standard and unambiguously defined as the time from appliance placement to debonding. Data were analyzed in a straightforward manner comparing the mean durations between the two groups, which aligns with standard practices for assessing this outcome.

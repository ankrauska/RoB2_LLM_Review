Based on the provided documents and the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of cure rate at 2 years.

1. bias due to randomization: some concerns
   a. Judgement: some concerns
   b. Rationale: The study states that patients "were allocated randomly to either of the two treatment groups till 268 patients were recruited" and "268 three-digit random numbers from the table were written in the patient registers in advance. Patients with even numbers were given PB-MDT and those with odd numbers were given ROM." However, there is no information provided about whether the allocation sequence was concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions. The lack of information on allocation concealment raises some concerns. Baseline characteristics appear balanced, but the lack of concealment information prevents a low risk judgment.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
   a. Judgement: low risk of bias
   b. Rationale: The study was unblinded ("Blinding to treatment was not undertaken"). We are assessing the effect of assignment to intervention. Because participants and personnel were not blinded, we must consider whether deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context. There is no evidence reported that deviations from the intended intervention occurred because of the trial context. The analysis appears to be based on those who completed treatment and those followed up, but for the effect of assignment (ITT), there isn't indication that significant context-driven deviations occurred that were unbalanced and affected the outcome.

3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias
   a. Judgement: high risk of bias
   b. Rationale: Missing outcome data is substantial. At 2 years, 100 of 134 (74.6%) in the MDT group and 103 of 134 (76.8%) in the ROM group were followed up. The study states "A number of patients were lost to follow up after release from treatment and thus actual number of relapses in the study could not be assessed." Additionally, 27 patients in the MDT group and 19 in the ROM group defaulted on treatment (and some were later lost). Since the cure rate analysis is reported among "those who completed treatment" and were followed up, a large proportion (approx 25%) are missing. Missingness could depend on the true value of the outcome (e.g., if those not cured or relapsed were more likely to drop out or seek other care).

4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns
   a. Judgement: some concerns
   b. Rationale: The outcome was measured clinically: "Cure of the disease was defined as complete resolution of the lesion or patch becoming flat, hypopigmented and with decrease in size of the lesion and/or regaining of sensation." Outcome assessors were not blinded (as the trial was unblinded). Because the assessment of cure involves some subjective clinical judgement (e.g., assessing sensation, decrease in size, flattening of lesions), the outcome assessment could have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

5. selective reporting: some concerns
   a. Judgement: some concerns
   b. Rationale: There is no reference to a published protocol or statistical analysis plan finalized prior to unblinding (the registry number provided in the text was registered retrospectively). Without a pre-specified analysis plan, we cannot be certain that the reported results were not selected from multiple eligible analyses or time points, leading to some concerns.

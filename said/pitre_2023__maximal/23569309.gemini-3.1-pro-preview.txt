**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The trial used a random permutation method for sequence generation, and allocation was concealed via a central data center. The publication states: "Registration and random assignment were coordinated centrally at the KCSG data center. The random permutation method was used for random assignment." Baseline characteristics were well balanced between the two groups (Table 1), suggesting no problems with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The trial was open-label, meaning participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention. However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. While some patients in the maintenance arm stopped treatment early at the physician's discretion, this reflects routine clinical practice and toxicity management rather than deviations caused by trial context. The analysis was appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention, as the authors state: "All randomly assigned patients were included in the efficacy analysis according to the intent-to-treat principle."

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** Outcome data appear to have been available for all or nearly all randomized participants. The trial analyzed all 231 randomized patients for efficacy according to the intention-to-treat principle. Patients who stopped treatment early were followed for the primary outcome (PFS) until disease progression or last contact, and there is no indication of substantial or differential loss to follow-up that would bias the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns.
* **Rationale:** The outcome (progression-free survival) was measured using standard imaging every 6 weeks and assessed by investigators. However, the trial was open-label, meaning the outcome assessors (investigators) were aware of the intervention received. Because assessing disease progression via RECIST criteria involves some degree of subjective judgment, the assessment could potentially have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention assignment. 

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The trial was registered (NCT00561119), and the published primary and secondary outcomes match the pre-specified analysis intentions. The primary outcome in the registry is "Progression free survival," which aligns perfectly with the reported primary endpoint in the publication. There is no evidence that the reported results were selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on statistical significance.

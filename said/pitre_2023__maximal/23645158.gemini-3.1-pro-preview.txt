Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome NT Pro-BNP levels in the provided trial:

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
*   **Judgement:** Some concerns.
*   **Rationale:** The trial reports that "patients were allocated into one of the two groups based on a computer random numbers table," indicating that the allocation sequence was random. However, there is no description of the method used to conceal the allocation sequence until participants were enrolled and assigned (e.g., sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes or central randomization). While there are no major baseline imbalances that suggest a problem with the randomization process (Table 1), the lack of clear information regarding allocation concealment leads to some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **Rationale:** The study was designed as a "double-blind clinical trial," and the interventions were identical in appearance and volume ("one group received a MgSO4 infusion... The same volume of normal saline was administered as a placebo via a 50 ml syringe intravenously"). Therefore, it is highly likely that participants, carers, and people delivering the interventions were unaware of the assigned interventions. Because blinding was maintained and the analysis appears to have included the participants in their randomized groups, there is a low risk of bias due to deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment).

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **Rationale:** The study reports randomizing 88 patients (44 in each group). The results presented in the tables (Tables 1, 2, and 3) include data for exactly 44 patients in the MgSO4 group and 44 patients in the Placebo group for the outcomes measured. Although the text states "Those patients who received triple inotropic agent therapy for CPB weaning were excluded... The number of patients excluded from the study in each group was registered," the final tables still reflect n=44 for both groups, implying either no exclusions occurred or data was available for all/nearly all participants originally randomized. Therefore, the risk of bias is low.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **Rationale:** The outcome (NT Pro-BNP levels) is an objective laboratory measurement ("measurement of NT pro-BNP was performed using an electrochemical luminescence immunoassay"). The method of measurement was appropriate and identical across groups. Given the objective nature of the test and the double-blind design of the study, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
*   **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
*   **Rationale:** The study clearly pre-specified its intent to measure NT Pro-BNP levels to test their hypothesis regarding the anti-inflammatory effects of MgSO4 ("we performed this study to assess the effect of MgSO4 solution... on the serum levels of NT Pro-BNP"). The outcome data for both pre-operative and 24-hour post-operative levels are fully reported for all groups as intended. There is no evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible measurements or analyses based on the statistical significance or direction of the effect.

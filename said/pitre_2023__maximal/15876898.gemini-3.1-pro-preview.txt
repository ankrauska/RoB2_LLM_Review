Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of weight:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The trial states that "randomization was carried out confidentially by the clinic administration" and patients were assigned to a matching placebo, indicating that the sequence was likely random and adequately concealed. Additionally, baseline characteristics (Table 1) such as age, initial body weight, and duration of olanzapine treatment were well-balanced between the topiramate and placebo groups, suggesting no problems with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** High risk of bias
* **Rationale:** While participants and personnel were blinded ("Both subjects and clinicians were blinded regarding topiramate/placebo assignment"), the analysis did not follow intention-to-treat (ITT) principles. The authors note, "Six subjects who failed to appear for more than one evaluation (all from the placebo group) dropped out of the study, and their data were not further analyzed. Finally, data from 43 women... were evaluated." Excluding 6 out of 24 patients (25%) from the placebo arm post-randomization constitutes an inappropriate analysis for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention, and this exclusion has the potential for a substantial impact on the result.

**3. missing outcome data: high risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** High risk of bias
* **Rationale:** Outcome data were not available for all randomized participants, as 6 participants dropped out and were excluded from the analysis. Notably, all 6 dropouts were exclusively from the placebo group. Because the missingness is highly unbalanced between the intervention groups, it is very likely that the missingness in the outcome depended on its true value (e.g., participants in the placebo group dropping out due to a lack of perceived efficacy or weight loss). There is no evidence that the authors performed sensitivity analyses or used appropriate statistical methods to correct for this potential bias.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The method of measuring the outcome (body weight) is objective and appropriate. The measurement method was identical across both trial arms. Furthermore, both the participants and the outcome assessors/clinicians were blinded to the assigned interventions, meaning the assessment of weight could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns
* **Rationale:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a prospective trial registration (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov) finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. While body weight is a straightforward measurement and there is no overt evidence that the numerical result was selected from multiple analyses, the lack of a publicly available pre-specified protocol leaves some concerns regarding selective reporting.

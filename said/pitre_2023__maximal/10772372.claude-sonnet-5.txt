# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Compliance Outcome

**Trial:** Impact of Pharmacy Counseling on Compliance and Effectiveness of Combination Lipid-Lowering Therapy (Pharmacotherapy 2000;20(4):410–416)

**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (as specified in the prompt)

---

## 1. Bias arising from the randomization process: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, driven primarily by lack of information about allocation concealment.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Random sequence generation:* The report states patients were "randomized to telephone contact or no telephone contact using a computer-generated list of random numbers," satisfying the criterion for a truly random sequence (Answer: Yes).
- *Allocation concealment:* No information is provided about whether the sequence was concealed from the enroller prior to assignment (Answer: No information). This is a very small trial (n=30) conducted at a single center, which increases the plausibility that concealment could have been compromised or simply was not implemented, but there is no direct evidence either way.
- *Baseline imbalance:* "Baseline characteristics were similar for both groups" (Table 1), and the modest difference in prescription insurance coverage (33% vs 27%) is well within the range expected by chance. No evidence of problems with randomization from baseline data (Answer: No).
- Per the RoB2 algorithm: Random sequence = Yes, Concealment = No information, Imbalance = No → **Some concerns**.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- Participants and personnel (pharmacist) were necessarily aware of group assignment, because the intervention itself consisted of telephone contact vs. no contact — blinding was not feasible for this behavioral intervention (Answers to 2.1/2.2: Yes).
- However, there is no evidence that this awareness led to deviations from the intended intervention that were inconsistent with the protocol. Both groups received identical prescribed drug regimens ("All patients received lovastatin 20 mg/day and colestipol 5 g twice/day," with identical initial counseling and dietary instruction). The only difference between arms was the telephone follow-up itself, which is the experimental intervention under study, not a protocol deviation or co-intervention imbalance (Answer to 2.3: No/Probably no).
- Analysis: All 30 randomized patients were included and, per the report, "none of whom was lost to follow-up"; the trial appears to have analyzed patients according to assigned group without exclusions (Answer to 2.6: Yes — appropriate ITT-consistent analysis).
- Combination of no meaningful trial-context-driven deviations and appropriate analysis → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- "Thirty patients were enrolled in the study, none of whom was lost to follow-up."
- Compliance data (pill/packet counts at 6 and 12 weeks; refill records at 1 and 2 years) appear to be reported for all 30 randomized patients across all time points, including detailed case-by-case description of compliance status changes over time.
- Because outcome data were available for essentially all randomized participants, missing data are very unlikely to bias the compliance result (Answer to 3.1: Yes) → **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of method:* Compliance was measured using pill and packet counts (performed "not performed within the patient's view," reducing reactivity) and pharmacy refill records — objective, standard methods for assessing medication compliance (Answer 4.1: No, not inappropriate).
- *Consistency between groups:* The same measurement methods (pill/packet counts, refill records) and same compliance thresholds (>20% pills returned or <80% refilled = noncompliant) were applied identically to both groups (Answer 4.2: No).
- *Assessor blinding/potential for influence:* It is not explicitly stated whether the individuals performing pill counts or extracting refill records were blinded to group assignment; given the open-label nature of the trial, some awareness is plausible (Answer 4.3: Probably yes). However, pill/packet counts and refill record documentation are objective, low-judgment measures ("observer-reported outcomes not involving judgement"), making it unlikely that knowledge of intervention status meaningfully influenced the recorded values (Answer 4.4: Probably no).
- Because the outcome measurement method involves minimal subjective judgment and was applied identically across arms, risk of bias in measurement is **Low**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, due to the absence of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan.

**b. Rationale:**
- This trial was published in 2000, prior to mandatory prospective trial registration, and no protocol or statistical analysis plan is referenced or available to confirm that the compliance outcome, its measurement approach (pill/packet counts, refill records), and thresholds (>20% return, <80% refill) were specified in advance of unblinded data (Answer to 5.1: No information).
- On the positive side, compliance results are reported comprehensively and consistently across all pre-stated time points (6 weeks, 12 weeks, 1 year, 2 years) and for both drugs (lovastatin and colestipol), without apparent selective omission of unfavorable time points or measures (Answers to 5.2/5.3: Probably no evidence of selection).
- However, because there is no independent documentation (trial registry, protocol) confirming that this was the intended definition/analysis of compliance decided prior to data unblinding, the absence of information prevents a "Low risk" judgement.
- Per the RoB2 algorithm (5.1 = No information; 5.2/5.3 = No evidence of selection) → **Some concerns**.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias for Compliance Outcome: **Some concerns**

**Summary rationale:** No domain was judged at high risk of bias. Two domains (randomization process; selective reporting) raised "some concerns," primarily due to incomplete reporting of methodological details (allocation concealment, pre-specified analysis plan) rather than clear evidence of bias. The domains most directly related to the internal validity of the compliance outcome itself (deviations from intended intervention, missing outcome data, and measurement of the outcome) were judged at low risk of bias, given complete follow-up, objective outcome measurement, and no evidence of differential co-interventions. Taken together, this does not reach the threshold of multiple concerns "substantially lowering confidence," so the overall judgement is **Some concerns** rather than **High risk of bias**.

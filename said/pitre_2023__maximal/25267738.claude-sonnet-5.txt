# Risk of Bias 2.0 Assessment: Cancer-DACUS Study
**Outcome: Recurrent thrombotic events**
**Effect of interest: Assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)**
**Comparison assessed: Group A1 (continue LMWH) vs Group A2 (discontinue LMWH)**

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily because allocation concealment was inadequate.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Sequence generation (random?):** The article states, "Random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10." Assignment based on clinical chart number is a form of systematic/quasi-random allocation rather than a chance-based process (e.g., computer-generated randomization). This raises concern about whether the sequence was truly random (answer: Probably No/No information).
- **Allocation concealment:** Although sealed envelopes were used, they were based on a predictable identifier (chart number) with a fixed block size of 10, which could allow prediction of upcoming allocations, especially since chart numbers are often sequentially assigned and not concealed from investigators. There is no description of opaque or tamper-proof envelopes. This suggests allocation concealment may have been compromised (answer: Probably No).
- **Baseline imbalance:** Table 1 shows very similar baseline characteristics between groups A1 and A2 (age, sex, BMI, cancer surgery, metastasis, DVT site, etc.), with no significant differences (all P > .05). This does not suggest a problem with randomization.
- Given the combination of an inadequate/unclear method of concealment (i) and no evidence of baseline imbalance (ii.2), plus uncertainty about true randomness of the sequence (using chart numbers), the algorithm suggests "Some concerns" rather than "High risk," because the baseline comparability suggests randomization likely functioned adequately in practice despite methodological weaknesses in the description.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns due to lack of blinding and absence of information about non-protocol interventions/co-interventions.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Awareness of intervention:** The article explicitly states, "Patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT." This is an open-label trial (confirmed in the protocol: `"masking": "NONE"`), so both participants (2.1) and personnel (2.2) were aware of intervention assignment (answer: Yes).
- **Deviations arising from trial context:** There is no explicit reporting of non-protocol interventions or co-interventions (e.g., differential use of other anticoagulants, differential surveillance for VTE symptoms) that might arise due to lack of blinding. The authors do note, "Committee members, who were unaware of results and treatments, assessed recurrent events, thus reducing the risk of diagnostic suspicion bias," which mitigates concern about outcome ascertainment (though this pertains more directly to the measurement domain). However, no information is given on whether unblinded clinicians managed patients differently (e.g., different thresholds for suspecting VTE recurrence, different intensity of surveillance) based on knowledge of allocation (answer to 2.3: No information).
- Because there is no clear evidence of deviations arising from trial context that affected the outcome, but the lack of blinding creates a plausible mechanism for differential management (e.g., patients who stopped LMWH may have had lower threshold for symptom reporting or clinicians increased vigilance), the domain-level default per the algorithm (Y/PY to 2.1/2.2, NI to 2.3) is "Some concerns."
- **Analysis approach:** The study states, "Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis." This supports an appropriate analysis for assignment effect (answer to 2.6: Yes). This is further corroborated by the CONSORT flow diagram showing outcomes are reported for all randomized patients in each arm (119 and 123).
- Combining Part 1 ("Some concerns") and Part 2 ("Low risk," given appropriate ITT analysis), the overall domain judgement is **Some concerns**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The CONSORT flow diagram and outcome tables indicate that recurrence data are reported for all 119 participants in group A1 and all 123 in group A2 (22/119 and 27/123, respectively, in Table 3). This indicates outcome data were available for all or nearly all randomized participants (answer to 3.1: Yes).
- Some patients died during follow-up (12 in group A1, 19 in group A2) due to cancer progression, and it is stated, "Fatal PE was not reported" and death was excluded as a competing event via clinical assessment: "Fatal PE as a cause of death was excluded on the basis of the absence of clinical symptoms or on normal imaging results." This suggests that outcome ascertainment continued to be attempted even amid competing risks (death), reducing likelihood of informative censoring related to the thrombotic outcome itself.
- No large discrepancy in dropout is reported that would be expected to bias the comparison; the follow-up description explicitly tracks numbers of patients under follow-up at each time point (108 in A1 and 94 in A2 at 6 months; 91 and 77 at 12 months), and these reductions are attributable to the staggered enrollment/follow-up completion (given the study ran from 2005-2010 with a fixed end date) and cancer-related deaths, not selective dropout related to the thrombotic outcome.
- Therefore, missing outcome data are unlikely to bias the result substantially, supporting a judgement of **Low risk**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- **Appropriateness of method:** Recurrent VTE was diagnosed using objective, standardized methods: compression ultrasonography (C-US) using explicit quantitative criteria ("if a previously fully compressible segment became no longer compressible, or if an increase of more than 4 mm in the diameter of the residual thrombus during compression was detected"), with confirmatory venography or repeat testing in ambiguous cases, and PE was diagnosed using "objective algorithms with the use of clinical probability, ventilation-perfusion lung scanning, or helical computed tomography." This is an appropriate, objective method for outcome ascertainment (answer to 4.1: No, not inappropriate).
- **Differential measurement between groups:** The same diagnostic algorithm and follow-up schedule (visits every 3 months, C-US only when recurrence was clinically suspected) were applied to both arms; there is no indication that surveillance intensity or diagnostic thresholds differed between A1 and A2 (answer to 4.2: No/Probably No).
- **Outcome assessor blinding:** Critically, "All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of the patient's name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment. Committee members also reviewed all the results of clinical investigations." This indicates that the key outcome assessors (adjudication committee) were blinded to intervention assignment (answer to 4.3: No — assessors were NOT aware of intervention).
- Since the outcome assessors relevant to the final adjudication of thrombotic events were blinded, and this is an objective outcome (recurrent VTE confirmed via imaging) with low potential for judgment-based bias once blinded, this domain is judged as **Low risk**, despite the open-label nature of day-to-day patient management. (Note: the initial suspicion of recurrence relied on unblinded clinicians/patients reporting symptoms, which could theoretically affect referral for testing, but the definitive diagnosis/adjudication was blinded and based on objective imaging criteria, mitigating this concern.)

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The trial registry (NCT00450645) prospectively specifies the primary outcome as "Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism," which matches exactly the outcome reported and analyzed in the publication (recurrent VTE, with breakdown into isolated DVT, DVT and PE, PE only, and contralateral DVT in Table 3) (signalling question 5.1: Yes, consistent with prespecified outcome).
- There is a single, clearly defined outcome measurement (recurrent VTE, confirmed by objective imaging), analyzed using a single consistent method (Cox proportional hazards models, adjusted for age, sex, previous surgery, and presence of metastasis) reported once in the paper, with no evidence of multiple alternative analyses (e.g., unadjusted vs. adjusted, multiple time points, multiple outcome definitions) being selectively reported (signalling questions 5.2 and 5.3: No/Probably No).
- The statistical methods section pre-specifies the model covariates and the primary analysis approach (ITT, Cox regression adjusted for specified covariates), and this matches what is presented in the Results.
- No indication of selective outcome reporting based on results was found; therefore, this domain is judged as **Low risk**.

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

**Rationale:** Per RoB 2.0 guidance (Table 1), because the domains "Bias arising from the randomization process" and "Bias due to deviations from the intended interventions" are both judged as "Some concerns," and no domain is judged as "High risk of bias," the overall risk-of-bias judgement for the outcome of recurrent thrombotic events (comparing continuation vs. discontinuation of LMWH, Groups A1 vs. A2) is **Some concerns**. The open-label design combined with a less-than-optimal (chart-number-based) allocation concealment method are the primary drivers of this judgement, although blinded outcome adjudication and complete outcome data considerably mitigate the overall risk.

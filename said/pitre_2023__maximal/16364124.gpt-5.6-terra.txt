**Outcome assessed:** visual assessment of erythema and dryness (including the prespecified combined irritation/tolerability sum score) during the 14-day repeated open application test. The study used a within-participant, left-versus-right cubital-fossa comparison: every participant received both formulations.

### 1. Bias due to randomization: **some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**  
The sequence-generation method appears appropriate. The report states that allocation of the coded products to the left and right cubital fossae was undertaken using “**randomly permuted blocks of fixed size using validated software RANCODE 3.6**,” prepared by the Department of Statistics and Data Management. This supports a random allocation sequence.

The preparations were coded as A and B, and “**blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database**.” However, this does not fully establish that the allocation sequence for assigning each coded product to the left or right cubital fossa was concealed until assignment. In particular, the report does not state who held the site-allocation schedule, whether the study nurse could foresee allocations, or how concealment was operationalized.

Baseline imbalance is less informative than in a parallel-group trial because this was a paired, within-person design, with each participant acting as their own control. No relevant baseline imbalance between treatment conditions is apparent or expected, but side-specific baseline erythema/dryness data are not reported.

Thus, random sequence generation was adequately described, but allocation concealment is insufficiently described, leading to **some concerns** rather than low risk.

---

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention: **low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias for the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline.

**b. Rationale:**  
The participants, study nurse, and outcome assessor appear to have been blinded to the identity of the formulations. The article describes the study as “**double-blind**”; the two formulations were “**randomly assigned a letter A or B for blinding**”; and “**derandomization and unblinding were performed after closure of the final database**.”

Intervention delivery was standardized. Both preparations were applied by a study nurse twice daily according to the same protocol: “**All applications were performed by a study nurse**,” with identical volumes and massage duration. Therefore, there is no indication that deviations arose because participants or personnel knew the assigned formulation, or that trial-context-related cointerventions differed systematically between the two treatment sites.

The reported primary analysis included all 35 randomized participants: “**Results are presented for the intention-to-treat (ITT) population**.” The analysis retained the randomized allocation of formulations to body sites. Although missing values for three participants were imputed using last observation carried forward (LOCF), that issue concerns missing outcome data rather than failure to estimate the effect of assignment.

The three protocol violations—antiphlogistic use in one participant and disinfection of the cubital fossa in two participants—were handled by retaining participants in the ITT analysis. There is no indication that these deviations were caused by knowledge of treatment allocation or occurred preferentially in one formulation/site.

### 3. Missing outcome data: **some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**  
Outcome data were analysed for all 35 randomized participants in the ITT analysis, but complete observed outcome data were not available for all participants. The report states: “**Missing values in 3 participants were replaced by the method of Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF).**” Thus, approximately 9% of participants had missing observations.

The three affected participants were also reported to have protocol violations: one took anti-inflammatory medication and two disinfected the cubital fossa. These circumstances could plausibly be associated with the skin outcome or with exposure-related changes in erythema/dryness. For example, antiphlogistic medication could alter inflammatory signs, and application of another disinfectant could affect local irritation. Therefore, it cannot be excluded that missingness was related to the true, unobserved erythema or dryness values.

No sensitivity analysis was reported to demonstrate that the finding was robust to plausible assumptions about missing data. LOCF is a single-imputation approach and does not by itself establish that missingness could not bias the result, particularly for repeated symptom assessments whose values may change over time.

Nevertheless, the amount of missingness was modest, applied to only three participants, and there is no evidence that missing data differed systematically according to the emollient versus non-emollient formulation. This supports **some concerns**, rather than high risk.

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**  
The outcome was measured using a prespecified and clinically appropriate method: repeated visual assessment of erythema and dryness using defined ordinal scales. The report specifies the assessment method and scoring categories, including erythema from 0 (“none”) to 4 (“fiery red with oedema”) and dryness from 0 (“none”) to 3 (“severe dryness with large flakes”).

Measurement procedures were the same for both formulations and treatment sites. Assessments occurred at identical scheduled time points—before the first application, before each subsequent application, and after the treatment period—and the same scales were used for both coded preparations.

Importantly, outcome assessment was blinded: “**The treated skin was assessed visually for erythema and dryness … by a dermatologist. All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator.**” The preparations were also coded, with unblinding after database closure. Therefore, the dermatologist’s subjective assessment of erythema and dryness was unlikely to have been influenced by knowledge of which formulation contained emollients.

Although visual scoring involves observer judgement, the adequate blinding of the assessor and comparable measurement procedures across the paired treatment sites support a **low risk of bias** in measurement of the outcome.

### 5. Selective reporting: **some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**  
The article describes a prespecified primary outcome and analysis: “**The sum score of dryness and erythema over all assessment times served as the primary outcome parameter**.” It also reports the planned primary comparison, initially framed as non-inferiority and then superiority if non-inferiority was established. The reported combined-score result corresponds to this description.

The article provides results for the combined outcome, its components (erythema and dryness), ITT and per-protocol populations, and atopic/non-atopic subgroups. This degree of reporting reduces concern that the primary result was selectively omitted.

However, there is no accessible dated protocol or statistical analysis plan demonstrating that the outcome definitions, time points, method of combining erythema and dryness, handling of missing values by LOCF, and choice between ITT and per-protocol analyses were finalized before unblinded outcome data became available. The trial was conducted in 2004, and the material provided does not establish that the trial registration contained a sufficiently detailed, prospectively specified analysis plan.

Furthermore, multiple related outcome measurements and analyses were possible: erythema versus dryness, repeated daily/visit assessments, combined versus separate scores, ITT versus per-protocol populations, and subgroup analyses by atopic status. The report does not provide enough pre-unblinding documentation to exclude selection among these possible analyses.

There is no direct evidence that the reported result was selected based on statistical significance; therefore, high risk is not warranted. However, the absence of a verifiable, detailed prespecified analysis plan means **some concerns** remain.

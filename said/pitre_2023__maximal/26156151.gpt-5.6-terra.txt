**Comparison assessed:** IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided CTO percutaneous coronary intervention, for **occurrence of cardiac death within 12 months** (0/201 versus 2/201, respectively). The effect of interest is the **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline**.

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

### a. Judgement
The study used a centralized interactive web-based randomization system with stratification by centre, which provides reasonable assurance of allocation concealment. Although the method used to generate the random sequence is not explicitly described, there is no indication that it was non-random. Baseline characteristics were generally balanced and do not suggest a failure of randomization.

### b. Rationale
- The report states: **“Using an interactive web-based response system, study participants were randomly assigned and stratified by participating center.”** A web-based assignment system is consistent with adequately concealed allocation, because the recruiting clinician would not ordinarily know the allocation before enrolling and registering the participant.
- The article does not describe the random-number generation process (e.g., computer-generated permuted blocks). Therefore, sequence generation is best considered **no information**, rather than definitively demonstrated as random. However, this does not raise concerns sufficient to override the otherwise sound centralized randomization process.
- The IVUS and angiography groups were equal in size (201 participants each).
- Baseline characteristics were broadly comparable. For example:
  - Mean age: 61.0 versus 61.4 years;
  - Male sex: 80.6% in both groups;
  - Diabetes: 34.8% versus 33.8%;
  - Prior myocardial infarction: 8.0% in both groups;
  - Ejection fraction: 56.9% versus 56.7%.
- Although there was some numerical imbalance in extent of vessel disease (three-vessel disease: 38.8% versus 30.8%), the overall baseline table does not show an excess of important or implausibly large imbalances that would indicate manipulation or another problem in the randomization process.

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention: **Low risk of bias**

### a. Judgement
Despite the practical impossibility of blinding operators to IVUS guidance, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context, were unbalanced in a non-protocol manner, and affected cardiac death. The analysis was conducted according to the intention-to-treat principle.

### b. Rationale
- Participants and procedural personnel were likely aware of allocation, because IVUS guidance versus angiographic guidance is an operator-delivered procedural strategy. The registry describes the study as single-masked with the investigator masked, but the publication makes clear that physicians implemented the allocated guidance strategy.
- The protocol allowed substantial physician discretion in the IVUS arm: **“IVUS devices, pullback methods, use at poststenting and prestenting, and numbers of exams were all left to the discretion of physicians.”**
- More frequent high-pressure post-stent dilation in the IVUS group (51.2% versus 41.3%) and slightly higher balloon pressure are plausibly part of the mechanism by which IVUS-guided PCI affects procedural optimization. These differences do not, by themselves, demonstrate non-protocol cointerventions or departures caused by the trial context.
- There is no report of systematic crossover, contamination (IVUS use in the angiography group), differential withdrawal from assigned guidance, or other non-protocol interventions arising because of knowledge of assignment.
- Importantly, the report states: **“The intention-to-treat principle was used in all analyses.”** Thus, participants were analysed in their randomized groups, which is appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment to IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided intervention.
- Cardiac death is an infrequent, objective clinical endpoint. Even if minor procedural differences occurred, there is no evidence that they constituted trial-context deviations likely to have materially biased the estimate of the assigned-strategy effect.

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Some concerns**

### a. Judgement
Follow-up was nearly complete, but one participant in the IVUS group did not complete 12-month clinical follow-up. Because cardiac death was very rare (two events total), the missing outcome for even one participant could potentially affect the estimate. The report does not provide the reason for missing follow-up or a sensitivity analysis addressing its possible effect.

### b. Rationale
- The article reports: **“200 patients of IVUS-guided group (99.5%) and 201 patients (100%) in the angiography-guided group finished 12-month clinical follow-up.”**
- Thus, outcome data were unavailable at 12 months for one of 201 IVUS-assigned participants.
- For many outcomes, 99.5% follow-up would be sufficient for a low-risk judgement. However, for this specific outcome, only two cardiac deaths were observed overall:
  - IVUS-guided group: 0/201;
  - Angiography-guided group: 2/201.
- Because the number of participants with missing cardiac-death data (one) is not negligible compared with the total number of observed cardiac deaths (two), the missing participant’s vital status could have meaningfully changed the observed risk in the IVUS group.
- No reason for the missing follow-up is given, and no sensitivity analysis is reported. There is no direct evidence that the participant’s missingness depended on cardiac-death status, but neither can this possibility be confidently excluded.
- Accordingly, this raises **some concerns**, rather than high risk: the amount of missing data is very small, but its potential impact is relatively greater because cardiac death was rare.

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

### a. Judgement
Cardiac death was defined using standard criteria and clinical events were independently assessed by an event committee blinded to randomization. Outcome ascertainment therefore appears appropriate, comparable between groups, and unlikely to have been influenced by intervention knowledge.

### b. Rationale
- Cardiac death was a prespecified primary endpoint and was defined as follows: **“All deaths were considered cardiac death unless an unequivocal noncardiac cause could be established.”**
- The study reports: **“All clinical events were independently monitored and assessed by a clinical event committee blinded to randomization.”**
- A blinded clinical-event committee substantially reduces the potential for differential classification of deaths as cardiac versus non-cardiac between IVUS-guided and angiography-guided groups.
- The scheduled follow-up procedures were the same for both groups: **“Clinical follow-up was performed at 1, 3, 9, and 12 months after the index procedure either by clinic visit or by telephone contact.”**
- Although distinguishing cardiac from non-cardiac death can involve some clinical judgement, the use of standardized definitions and blinded adjudication makes it unlikely that outcome assessment was materially influenced by knowledge of allocated intervention.

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

### a. Judgement
Cardiac death at 12 months was prespecified in the trial registration and reported in the publication, which is reassuring. However, no dated statistical analysis plan or sufficiently detailed prespecified analysis plan was available to confirm that the exact analysis was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. Therefore, some concerns remain about potential selection among analyses, although there is no positive evidence of selective reporting.

### b. Rationale
- The trial registration (NCT01563952) prespecified the primary outcome as: **“event rate of cardiac death within 12 months.”**
- The publication reports the same primary endpoint at the same time point: **“The prespecified primary end point was the occurrence of cardiac death.”**
- The numerical result for cardiac death is fully reported: **0% (0/201) in the IVUS-guided group versus 1.0% (2/201) in the angiography-guided group; P by log-rank test=0.16.**
- This consistency between registry and publication makes selection from multiple outcome domains or multiple follow-up time points less likely.
- However, the available registry record does not provide a dated, detailed statistical analysis plan specifying, for example, the precise survival-analysis approach, handling of the one participant without complete follow-up, or whether alternative analyses were planned.
- The article reports Kaplan–Meier/log-rank analyses and univariate Cox models, but it cannot be verified from the available pre-results documentation that this exact analytic approach was prespecified before outcome data were unblinded.
- Under RoB 2, absence of sufficiently detailed, prospectively finalized analysis intentions results in **some concerns**, even when the reported outcome corresponds to the registered primary outcome.

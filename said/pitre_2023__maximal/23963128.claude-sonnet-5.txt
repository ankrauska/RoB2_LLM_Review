# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Effects of Supervision on Patient Care (Team Checklist Score)

**Outcome assessed:** Team checklist score during supervised simulation scenarios (Table 2)
**Effect of interest:** Effect of assignment to intervention (as specified in prompt)
**Comparison:** Direct vs. Immediately Available vs. Distant supervision

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns arise because the trial provides no description of the methods used to generate the allocation sequence or to conceal it prior to assignment. Baseline characteristics appear reasonably balanced across the three groups, which does not itself suggest a problem with randomization, but the complete absence of methodological detail leaves genuine uncertainty about whether allocation was concealed and truly randomized.

**b. Rationale:** The manuscript states only that "Residents were randomized to one of three levels of supervision" with no further elaboration on sequence generation (e.g., computer-generated randomization, block size) or concealment mechanism (e.g., sealed envelopes, central randomization). Table 1 shows generally similar distributions of age, gender, ICU experience, simulation experience, PGY level, subspecialty, and baseline confidence scores across the three arms, with differences plausible by chance (no p-values reported suggesting problematic imbalance, though minor variability exists, e.g., simulation experience median 1.5 vs 1.5 vs 3). Because (1) there is no information on whether the sequence was random, (2) no information on allocation concealment, and (3) baseline differences appear compatible with chance, this combination of "no information" on two signalling questions leads to a judgement of **Some concerns** per the RoB2 algorithm (Table 4: "Any response / NI / N/PN/NI → Some concerns").

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Although participants (residents) and personnel (fellows) could not be blinded to the level of supervision (this is inherent to the intervention), the behaviors observed (fellows coming to bedside during "immediately available," or residents phoning during "distant") are consistent with, not deviations from, the protocol-defined nature of each supervision arm. The checklist outcome was scored based on the assigned/delivered condition, consistent with an intention-to-treat-type analysis.

**b. Rationale:** By definition, participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention: "The level of supervision was defined by the physical proximity of the supervisor: direct… immediately available… and distant." This awareness is unavoidable given the nature of the intervention (physical presence/absence of a supervisor) and does not, by itself, imply bias.
Critically, apparent "cross-over" behaviors were pre-specified as part of each arm's design rather than being protocol deviations: in the immediately available arm, the protocol explicitly permitted the supervisor to be "at the bedside within 1 min when needed," and the report notes accordingly that "10 residents were eventually assisted by their supervisor at the bedside." Similarly, in the distant arm, phone contact was allowed by protocol ("distant: supervisor only available on the phone for the duration of the scenario"), and the finding that "all the residents called their supervisor" reflects intended protocol-consistent behavior, not a deviation arising from unintended trial context effects.
There is no evidence that non-protocol co-interventions or trial-context-driven contamination (e.g., unblinded participants seeking unintended interventions because of trial enrollment) occurred. The team checklist score was assessed for all randomized residents according to assigned group ("we conducted a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) on the team checklist scores of the supervised scenario with the level of clinical supervision… as the independent variable"), consistent with appropriate ITT-type analysis.
Because there is no evidence of protocol-inconsistent deviations arising from trial context that affected the outcome, and an appropriate analysis approach was used, this domain is judged as **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Outcome data (team checklist score) appear to have been available for all randomized residents, with no reported dropout for this particular outcome.

**b. Rationale:** Fifty-three residents were recruited, and Table 2 reports team checklist scores for the same n=18 (direct), 16 (immediately available), and 19 (distant), summing to the full 53 study participants originally recruited. No losses to follow-up, missing scenario data, or exclusions from this analysis are described in the manuscript ("Fifty-three residents completing a critical care rotation… were recruited"; Table 2 reflects the same total sample). Because outcome data were available for all randomized participants, this domain is judged as **Low risk of bias**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** The method of measuring the checklist score itself appears appropriate and consistent across arms (an objective checklist of clinical tasks completed by any team member). However, it is unclear whether those recording/scoring the checklist during the supervised scenario were blinded to the level of supervision, and by the nature of the study, awareness of the supervision arm would have been essentially unavoidable for anyone observing the scenario live (the physical presence or absence of a supervisor at the bedside is visually obvious). Because checklist completion (particularly borderline or judgment-based items) could plausibly be influenced by expectations about the supervision arm, "Some concerns" is appropriate.

**b. Rationale:** The article states, "A checklist task was considered done if performed by any member of the team (resident, fellow, and nurse) and was reported as a percentage of the total number of items on the checklist. We also recorded on the checklist form who performed the task." There is no mention of the personnel completing this recording being blinded to the assigned condition, nor could they realistically be blinded, since the presence/location of the supervisor (the intervention itself) was directly observable in real time. While task completion is relatively objective (an "observer-reported outcome" with only some judgment involved, per RoB2 guidance), the potential for subtle influence—e.g., in judging whether an action was "completed" adequately—cannot be excluded, especially given that fellows and possibly confederates contributed to scoring or documentation. There is no explicit evidence that such influence occurred, so risk is not judged "High," but the absence of blinding and description of assessor procedures for this outcome leads to a judgement of **Some concerns**.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** The team checklist score for the supervised scenario appears to have been a pre-specified primary outcome, analyzed and reported in a manner consistent with the study's stated objectives and statistical methods, without evidence of selective outcome or analysis reporting.

**b. Rationale:** The Methods section pre-specifies the team checklist score as a primary measure to address the first research question: "To address our first research question on the effects of supervision on patient care, we measured a team checklist score, the time to stabilization of abnormal vital signs, and the proportion of achievement of definitive management." The statistical analysis plan explicitly designates the analytic approach ("we conducted a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) on the team checklist scores of the supervised scenario with the level of clinical supervision… as the independent variable"), and the result is reported fully (mean, SD, p-value) in Table 2 exactly as pre-specified, with no evidence of selection among multiple checklist versions, timepoints, or alternative analytic approaches (e.g., no mention of alternative unreported analyses being tried and discarded). The trial registration record did not reveal discrepancies suggestive of selective outcome reporting for this measure. Therefore, this domain is judged as **Low risk of bias**.

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

**Summary rationale:** No domain was judged to be at high risk of bias, but two domains ("bias arising from the randomization process" and "measurement of the outcome") were judged to raise "Some concerns" due to insufficient reporting of randomization/allocation concealment methods and the inherent lack of blinding of assessors to the (visually obvious) supervision condition. Per RoB 2 guidance (Table 1), a study with "some concerns" in at least one domain, but no domain at high risk of bias, should be given an overall judgement of **Some concerns** for this outcome (team checklist score during supervised scenarios).

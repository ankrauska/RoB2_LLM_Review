Here is the risk of bias assessment for the FLAME trial (Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale outcome) using the RoB 2.0 tool, focusing on the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect).

1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation and allocation concealment. Baseline differences were minor and appropriately adjusted for in the analysis, not suggesting a systematic problem with the randomization process.
b. Rationale: The publication states, "Randomisation was balanced by centre, with an allocation sequence based on a block size of four, generated with a computer random-number generator by the pharmacist... Allocation was concealed by use of sequentially numbered opaque envelopes." Although there were minor baseline imbalances (e.g., mean age and previous history of stroke), the authors noted that "The two groups were well balanced in terms of baseline and demographic characteristics and stroke severity," and these factors were controlled for in the multiple linear regression analysis.

2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Participants, carers, and personnel were blinded to the intervention assignment, and there is no evidence of deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. The analysis appropriately followed intention-to-treat principles.
b. Rationale: The trial was described as a "double-blind, placebo-controlled trial." The publication reports that "The placebo was identical to the active drug in appearance and packaging," and "Patients, carers, all site study staff, and investigators were masked to treatment assignment." An intention-to-treat approach was used (full analysis set of 113 out of 118 randomized patients), and treatment compliance was similar between both groups.

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: Outcome data were available for nearly all participants randomized, and the small number of dropouts is unlikely to have biased the result.
b. Rationale: Out of 118 randomized patients, 113 (96%) were included in the final analysis (57 in the fluoxetine group and 56 in the placebo group). The publication details that "Two patients died before day 90, and three withdrew from follow-up," which is a very low attrition rate and balanced between groups, meaning the missing data could not have made a clinically important difference to the estimated effect.

4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The method used to measure the outcome (Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale) is appropriate and was applied consistently across both groups by assessors who were blinded to the intervention.
b. Rationale: The Fugl-Meyer motor scale is described as "an index that is widely used for assessment of motor recovery after stroke and has excellent intra-rater and inter-rater reliability and validity." Furthermore, the study specifies that "All motor assessments were made by a physiotherapist at day 0 (baseline) and then 30 days and 90 days after enrolment" and "physicians assessing the outcome were masked to group assignment." 

5. selective reporting: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The reported primary outcome perfectly matches the pre-specified primary outcome in the trial registration, both in the metric and the time point analyzed. 
b. Rationale: The trial registration (NCT00657163) defines the primary outcome as "Progression in the Fugl-Meyer Motor Scale" evaluated at "M3 (3 months)." The published article reports exactly this: "The primary outcome measure was the change on the FMMS between day 0 and day 90 after the start of the study drug." There is no indication that the results were selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on statistical significance.

# RoB 2.0 Assessment: Visual Acuity Outcome (Figure 4 - Efficacy/CDVA-UDVA)

**Preliminary Note:** This is a paired-eye, intra-individual randomized trial where the effect of interest is assignment to intervention (ASA vs. TSA ablation profile). Outcome assessed: visual acuity (efficacy, Figure 4).

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:** 
- *Was the allocation sequence random?* **Yes.** The paper states: "one eye was treated with the MEL80 standard aspheric profile (ASA) and the other eye was treated with the MEL80 non-aspheric profile (TSA), assigned at random using Microsoft Excel's random number function (Microsoft Excel 2003, Microsoft Corporation, Seattle, Washington)." This is an explicit, adequate random component.
- *Was the allocation sequence concealed?* No explicit statement of concealment procedures is provided. However, because this is a paired-eye design in which both eyes of the same patient are always treated on the same day with one of each profile, there is minimal opportunity for selective enrollment or manipulation based on foreknowledge of assignment (both interventions are given regardless of the sequence). This mitigates typical concerns about lack of concealment. I judge "Probably yes" for concealment given the low risk of manipulation in this design.
- *Do baseline imbalances suggest a problem?* No. Preoperative spherical equivalent refraction was similar between groups (ASA: −2.43 ± 1.53 D; TSA: −2.54 ± 1.47 D; p = 0.87), and preoperative CDVA was reported to be balanced (baseline exclusion criterion: CDVA worse than 20/20 excluded, and inter-eye SEQ difference >1.00 D excluded). No evidence of imbalance affecting visual acuity prognosis between groups.

Overall: random sequence generation adequate, no evidence of problematic imbalance, and the paired-eye design substantially reduces concern about lack of explicit concealment reporting → **Low risk**.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Were participants and personnel aware of the intervention?* Participants were likely unaware of which profile (ASA/TSA) was used in each eye (this is a laser-delivered treatment technical parameter, not something patients would perceive intraoperatively). The treating surgeon/laser operator was aware of the profile being delivered (this is unavoidable, as it must be selected on the laser console), but this is a technical/procedural awareness rather than a "belief-driven" performance bias, since laser settings do not otherwise change perioperative care given to the patient.
- *Did deviations arise because of the trial context?* No evidence of any non-protocol interventions, co-interventions, or changes in patient management stemming from knowledge of intervention assignment. The surgical procedure (LASIK using Hansatome microkeratome, 6 mm optical zone) was standardized across both groups; only the laser ablation algorithm differed by design.
- *Did deviations affect the outcome?* No deviations reported. All 30 eyes (15 patients) appear to have undergone the intended procedure as randomized.
- *Analysis:* The results for visual acuity (Figure 4) appear to include all treated eyes (30 eyes, patients analyzed according to assigned profile), consistent with an intention-to-treat-like approach; no exclusions or crossovers were reported.

Given no meaningful deviations and an appropriate analysis, this domain is judged **Low risk**.

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Was outcome data available for all or most participants?* Yes. The study reports outcomes for "30 eyes of 15 myopic patients," and Figure 4 presents cumulative visual acuity data for all 30 eyes at 3 months ("100% of eyes were 20/25 or better... The graph includes data for 30 eyes at 3 months postop"). No dropouts, losses to follow-up, or missing visual acuity data are mentioned anywhere in the manuscript.
- *Is there evidence the result is not biased due to missing data?* Because there is no missing data reported (full accounting of all 30 eyes), this sub-question is not applicable.
- *Could missingness depend on the true value of the outcome?* Not applicable, as there was no missing outcome data identified.

Because all randomized eyes appear to have contributed visual acuity data at the 3-month endpoint, this domain is judged **Low risk**.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Was the method of measuring the outcome appropriate?* Yes. Visual acuity (CDVA/UDVA) was measured using standard clinical methodology ("Manifest refraction, uncorrected distance visual acuity and CDVA were measured before and 3 months after surgery") and reported as Snellen acuity in a standardized, validated manner consistent with refractive surgery outcome reporting protocols (Reinstein and Waring protocol referenced).
- *Did the method differ between arms?* No. The same measurement protocol, instruments, and timing (3 months postoperatively) were applied identically to both ASA and TSA eyes.
- *Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention?* This is not explicitly stated; visual acuity was likely measured by clinical staff/optometric technicians who may have had access to which laser profile was used (since it is documented in the surgical record), but this is a routine objective clinical measurement.
- *Could outcome assessment be influenced by knowledge of intervention?* Visual acuity (Snellen chart testing) is an **objective, observer-reported outcome involving minimal judgement** (per RoB2 guidance, akin to "observer-reported outcomes not involving judgement" such as an automated/standardized test). It is not a subjective, self-reported symptom scale (unlike, e.g., quality-of-vision questionnaires), and there is no plausible mechanism by which knowledge of ablation profile would systematically bias a technician's reading of a Snellen chart or a patient's ability to read letters.

Given the objective nature of visual acuity testing and identical measurement protocols across arms, this domain is judged **Low risk**, despite the absence of explicit statements about assessor blinding.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Were results selected from multiple outcome measurements or analyses?* Visual acuity outcomes (UDVA, CDVA, efficacy, safety) appear to be fully and consistently reported according to a standard refractive surgery outcome reporting protocol (Reinstein/Waring standard graphs), and Figure 4 presents complete cumulative acuity data for both groups with no evidence of selective time-point or subgroup reporting.
- *Was the trial analyzed according to a prespecified statistical analysis plan?* No trial registration or prospectively published protocol/statistical analysis plan was identified or referenced in the manuscript to confirm that visual acuity outcomes and analyses were prespecified prior to unblinded data access. The trial registration document referenced (doc_id: 4dab5cab...) was not able to be cross-checked against a detailed pre-specified analysis plan for this specific outcome. While the visual acuity measures used are standard/expected outcomes for a refractive surgery trial (making outright selective reporting less likely), the absence of a verifiable prespecified analysis plan or protocol for this specific outcome introduces uncertainty.

Because there is no clear evidence of selective reporting, but also no confirmation of a prespecified analysis plan finalized prior to unblinded outcome data availability, this domain is judged **Some concerns** (per RoB2 default: lack of information on prespecification with no clear indication of selection → "Some concerns").

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **Some concerns**

**Summary:** Domains 1–4 (randomization, deviations from intended intervention, missing outcome data, and measurement of the outcome) were judged as **low risk of bias** for the visual acuity outcome. However, Domain 5 (selective reporting) was judged as **"Some concerns"** due to the absence of verifiable documentation confirming that the visual acuity outcome and its analysis were prespecified before unblinded data were available. Per RoB 2.0 guidance (Table 1), a judgement of "Some concerns" in any single domain, with no domain at "High risk," results in an overall outcome-level judgement of **"Some concerns."**

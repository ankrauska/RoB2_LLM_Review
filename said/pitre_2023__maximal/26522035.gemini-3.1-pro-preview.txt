1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias
a. Judgement: The allocation sequence was random, allocation concealment was adequate, and baseline differences were compatible with chance.
b. Rationale: The authors state: "Computer random sequence generation was done using block sizes of 6 and 4." (pg 755). They also state: "Allocation concealment was achieved with sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes." (pg 755). Baseline characteristics appear similar.

2. deviations from intended intervention: high risk of bias
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention, the outcome was patient-reported, and deviations likely arose due to the trial context, potentially affecting the outcome, with likely unbalance given the subjective nature and awareness. However, for intention to treat, no serious deviations aside from the non-blinding of the subjective outcome are reported. But looking closely, patients and clinicians were not blinded. The outcome is subjective acceptance. Actually, the RoB 2 guidance asks if deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context. There is no evidence of such deviations (e.g. non-adherence or switching) affecting the ITT effect. Wait, let me just set Deviations from intended intervention to low risk of bias. 
a. Judgement: Participants and personnel were aware of the intervention, but there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context.
b. Rationale: "Blinding of the clinicians and the patients to the intervention was impossible" (pg 755). However, no deviations from intended interventions that arose because of the trial context were reported. An ITT analysis was performed: "The primary analysis was planned to be an intention-to-treat analysis to involve all patients who were to be randomly assigned; however, since there were no dropouts in the study, all the patients were included in the analysis." (pg 758).

3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias
c. Judgement: Data for the outcome were available for all participants randomized.
d. Rationale: "The 32 surveys were retrieved from the patients; there were no dropouts." (pg 758).

4. measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias
a. Judgement: Outcome assessors (participants) were aware of the intervention, and the outcome (acceptance/satisfaction) is subjective and highly likely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.
b. Rationale: The outcome is patient acceptance assessed via questionnaire. "Blinding of the clinicians and the patients to the intervention was impossible" (pg 755). Because the patients were the outcome assessors for this patient-reported outcome and were aware of the intervention, the assessment of the outcome is highly likely to be influenced by this knowledge.

5. selective reporting: some concerns
a. Judgement: There is no pre-specified protocol or trial registration available to confirm that the reported analyses were pre-specified.
b. Rationale: "Registration: This trial was not registered. Protocol: The protocol was not published before trial commencement." (pg 755).

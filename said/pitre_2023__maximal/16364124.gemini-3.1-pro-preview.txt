Based on the RoB 2.0 tool and the provided trial report, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of visual assessment for erythema and dryness (sum score) comparing a propanol-based hand rub with emollients versus one without:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation: "Randomization was performed via randomly permuted blocks of fixed size using validated software RANCODE 3.6." Allocation concealment was maintained as the products were "coded as A and B... Blinding codes were kept sealed until closure of final database." Furthermore, as this was an intra-individual study design where each participant received both treatments on different arms, baseline imbalances between the intervention groups are not applicable.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The trial was double-blinded. Participants and the personnel delivering the interventions were unaware of the assigned interventions ("All applications were performed by a study nurse. An aliquot of 0.7 ml of the coded test material was applied"). The analysis for the primary outcome was conducted on an intention-to-treat (ITT) basis for all 35 randomized subjects.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** Outcome data were available for 32 out of 35 participants (91%). Three participants did not complete the study due to protocol violations (intake of antiphlogistics, disinfection of fossa cubitalis), which are unlikely to be related to the true value of the outcome. Furthermore, the trialists used the Last Observation Carried Forward (LOCF) method to include all 35 participants in the ITT analysis, meaning nearly all randomized participants were accounted for without evidence that missingness depended on the outcome.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The method of measuring the outcome (visual inspection according to a standard scale) was appropriate and applied equally to both intervention arms (left and right cubital fossa). The outcome assessor was blinded to the intervention assignments: "All evaluations were conducted blindly by the same investigator."

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
* **Rationale:** The primary outcome (sum score over all assessment time-points) and the analysis methods (using Fieller's method for non-inferiority with a specific margin) were clearly pre-specified in the sample size and statistical methods sections. There is no evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple analyses based on the statistical significance of the findings.

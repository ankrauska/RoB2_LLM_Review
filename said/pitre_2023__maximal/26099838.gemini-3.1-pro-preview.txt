Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the trial regarding the outcome of student performance (sum scores of correct answers):

1. **bias due to randomization: some concerns**
    a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
    The allocation sequence was random, and baseline characteristics were balanced, but there is no information on whether the allocation sequence was concealed.
    b. Rationale: The trial report states, "Students were randomized into three groups using a computer program." Furthermore, the baseline characteristics (age, sex, school age) showed "no significant sex- and age-related differences." However, there is no mention of allocation concealment methods, which raises some concerns as the enrolling personnel might have foreseen the assignments.

2. **deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
    a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
    Participants and personnel were aware of the assigned intervention, but there is no evidence that deviations from the intended intervention arose because of the trial context that would likely affect the outcome.
    b. Rationale: Given the nature of the educational interventions (CT images vs. 3D images vs. 3Dp models), it was impossible to blind the students or the teachers. However, this lack of blinding is inherent to the intervention and is unlikely to result in non-protocol interventions or deviations that would bias the effect of assignment to the intervention. The study successfully delivered the assigned teaching modules to each respective group.

3. **missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
    c. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
    Outcome data were available for all randomized participants.
    d. Rationale: The study explicitly states that "a total of 120 medical students (54.2% females) participated, and no one dropped out." Therefore, there is no risk of bias due to missing outcome data, as the data is complete for 100% of the participants.

4. **measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
    a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
    The method of measuring the outcome was appropriate, and although assessors/participants were not blinded, the assessment of the outcome is highly objective and unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
    b. Rationale: The outcome was measured using an examination paper consisting of multiple-choice questions ("each student answered 10 questions regarding spinal fractures..."). Since the answers to these anatomical questions are objective and standardized (as shown in Table 1), the measurement of the outcome could not have been meaningfully influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

5. **selective reporting: some concerns**
    a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
    The reported outcomes correspond to the intended measurements, but there is no evidence of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registration available to confirm that analyses were strictly pre-planned.
    b. Rationale: The study reports on the logical outcomes of the intervention (test scores and time spent). However, no trial registration or pre-published protocol is referenced in the text. According to RoB 2.0 guidance, without a pre-specified analysis plan finalized before unblinded data were available, a judgement of "some concerns" is warranted, even if there is no obvious indication of selective reporting of multiple analyses or outcome measurements.

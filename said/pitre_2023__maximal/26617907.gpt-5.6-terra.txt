**Outcome assessed:** Surgical time (minutes), comparing percutaneous microwave ablation (group A; *n*=45) with laparoscopic resection (group B; *n*=45). The reported result was 96.7 ± 27.8 versus 134.2 ± 34.3 minutes (*P*<0.0001). The assessment addresses the **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline**.

### 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**  
The report states that participants were allocated using a random-number table: “*Using the random number table method, 90 cases were divided into group A and group B (n=45).*” This supports a random allocation sequence.

However, the report gives no information about allocation concealment. It does not state that allocation was administered centrally, by an independent party, or through sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes. Consequently, it is unclear whether personnel enrolling participants could foresee the upcoming allocation.

Baseline characteristics reported were broadly similar: sex, age, tumour size, and number of nodules did not differ statistically between groups. There is no obvious imbalance strongly suggesting manipulation or another failure of the randomization process. However, the absence of allocation-concealment information prevents a low-risk judgement under RoB 2.

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**  
This was necessarily an open-label comparison of materially different procedures—percutaneous microwave ablation versus laparoscopic resection. Thus, participants, surgeons, and other treating personnel would have been aware of the assigned intervention.

The study describes the intended procedures for both groups but does not report whether any participants crossed over, received a different procedure, had failed implementation of the allocated procedure, or received differential cointerventions that arose because of the trial context. In particular, the report does not provide a participant flow diagram or describe deviations from allocated intervention.

For the effect of assignment, ordinary non-adherence does not itself create bias unless it arose because of the trial context and was likely to affect the outcome. There is no direct evidence that trial-context-related deviations occurred, affected surgical time, or were imbalanced between groups. Nevertheless, the absence of relevant reporting means this cannot be confidently excluded.

The analysis appears to include 45 participants in each allocated group, as presented in Table 1. This is consistent with analysis by randomized group, but the authors do not explicitly state that an intention-to-treat or modified intention-to-treat analysis was used, nor do they report crossovers or post-randomization exclusions. Therefore, there are some concerns rather than low risk.

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**c. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**d. Rationale:**  
Surgical time is reported for all 45 participants in each group in Table 1:

- Group A: *n*=45, surgical time 96.7 ± 27.8 minutes  
- Group B: *n*=45, surgical time 134.2 ± 34.3 minutes.

There is no indication that surgical-time data were missing for any randomized participant. Surgical time is measured during the intervention and is unlikely to be unavailable once the procedure has been completed. No exclusions from the surgical-time analysis, losses to follow-up, or imputation methods are described for this outcome.

Accordingly, outcome data appear available for all randomized participants, and missing data are not likely to have biased the result.

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**  
Surgical time, measured in minutes, is an objective intraoperative outcome. The method is appropriate for evaluating procedure duration, and there is no indication that different timing methods or thresholds were used in the two groups.

Although surgeons and operating-room personnel would have known which procedure was being performed, knowledge of the intervention is unlikely to materially influence the recorded duration of surgery. Unlike a patient-reported symptom or a subjective clinical judgement, operative time has little scope for differential interpretation, provided it is recorded from routine operative records.

The article does not explicitly define the start and end points for measurement of surgical time, but this omission alone is not sufficient to indicate that the measurement method was inappropriate or systematically different between intervention groups. Thus, risk of bias in measurement of surgical time is low.

### 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**  
The publication reports surgical time as a stated outcome under “Criteria for efficacy assessment”: “*Surgical time, intraoperative blood loss and incidence of complications were recorded for the two groups.*” It then presents the numerical results for surgical time in Table 1.

However, there is no protocol or statistical analysis plan in the supplied material demonstrating that the outcome definition, timing, analysis population, and statistical analysis for surgical time were prespecified before unblinded outcome data became available. The report states only that a *t*-test was used for measurement data; it does not specify a prespecified analysis plan or whether alternative analyses were performed.

There is also no information available here to establish whether surgical time was selected from multiple eligible operational definitions or whether the reported analysis was selected from multiple possible analyses. The referenced registration record is not reproduced in the material provided; therefore, it cannot be used here to verify prespecification or consistency between the registry and publication.

There is no direct evidence of selective reporting, but absence of a verifiable prespecified analysis plan and insufficient information about alternative measurements or analyses lead to some concerns.

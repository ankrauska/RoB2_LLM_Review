# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
**Outcome: Time to institutionalization**
**Effect of interest: Effect of assignment to intervention (intention-to-treat effect)**

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Sequence generation*: "the dementia family care coordinator had prepared 100 sealed nontransparent envelopes, of which 53 contained allocation to the intervention group and 47 to the control group. Allocation to the groups was made by each patient (or her/his caregiver if the patient was unable) drawing one envelope." This constitutes a random process (drawing from a set of shuffled envelopes), supporting a "Probably yes" for randomness of sequence.
- *Allocation concealment*: There is insufficient detail to confirm rigorous concealment safeguards (e.g., sequential numbering, tamper-proof sealing, independent oversight of the draw). Critically, the same dementia family care coordinator who prepared the envelopes also delivered the entire intervention and had a vested interest in the study; there is no description of independent monitoring of the envelope-drawing process. Additionally, for the final 14 patients "allocated at a ratio of 2:1... to give more patients the opportunity to receive support from the dementia family care coordinator" — a deliberate, non-standard ratio change reflecting investigator discretion during the enrollment period, raising concern that allocation could have been influenced by knowledge of upcoming assignments or a preference to enroll more patients into the intervention.
- *Baseline imbalance*: Table 2 shows a numerically higher proportion of severe dementia in the intervention group (36% vs. 24%) and lower proportion of moderate dementia (24% vs. 38%), though the overall p-value for dementia stage distribution (.244) is compatible with chance. No individual imbalance meets extreme thresholds (e.g., p<0.001) that would clearly indicate a problem with randomization.
- Overall, the combination of an ad hoc change in allocation ratio, the coordinator's dual role in generating/handling allocation and delivering the intervention, and lack of detailed description of concealment safeguards results in "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 2. Deviations from the intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Awareness of assignment*: Participants, caregivers, and personnel (the dementia family care coordinator and treating physicians) were necessarily aware of intervention assignment, as the intervention itself (nurse case management, training courses, 24-hour telephone availability) is not blindable. This corresponds to "Yes" for signalling questions 2.1 and 2.2.
- *Deviations arising from trial context*: The intervention involved substantial engagement effects (coordinator involvement, training courses, service arrangement), and it is plausible that recruitment/engagement activities (e.g., control participants' awareness of not receiving the coordinator's support) could have influenced care-seeking behavior or family decision-making regarding institutionalization, independent of the biological/clinical effect of the intervention itself. The paper does not report on non-protocol interventions or contamination between groups, so there is limited information to confirm whether such deviations occurred and their balance between groups.
- *Analysis*: An appropriate, intention-to-treat-consistent analysis was used — all 100 randomized patients were followed and accounted for (Figure 1: no dropouts), and the Cox regression/Kaplan-Meier analyses included all randomized participants analyzed according to assigned group.
- Because participants/personnel were aware of assignment and the potential for engagement-related deviations affecting the decision to institutionalize cannot be excluded (even though final institutionalization decisions were made by a panel blinded to allocation), but there is no clear evidence that such deviations occurred or importantly affected the outcome, this domain is rated "Some concerns" rather than "High risk" or "Low risk."

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- The study explicitly states: "There were no drop-outs from the study during the 2-year follow-up (Figure 1)."
- Figure 1 confirms complete accounting of all 100 randomized patients at both 1-year and 2-year follow-up, categorized as institutionalized, deceased at home, or still at home, for both groups.
- Outcome data (time to institutionalization, using registry-based ascertainment) were available for all randomized participants, satisfying the criterion for low risk of bias due to missing data.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

**b. Rationale:**
- *Appropriateness of method*: The outcome (time to institutionalization) was ascertained via an objective administrative source: "This information was obtained from the register of the Social Insurance Institution," an appropriate and objective method for measuring this outcome.
- *Comparability of ascertainment between groups*: No indication that measurement/ascertainment methods differed between the intervention and control groups — both were tracked via the same national registry.
- *Blinding of assessors*: The paper explicitly states that the decision-making body was blinded: "a group of experts (usually a chief physician, head nurse, and social worker) makes the decision to permanently institutionalize patients... These groups of experts were blind to the allocation and generally unaware that a patient was even participating in the study."
- Because this is an outcome with minimal judgment involved (an administrative decision recorded in a registry, not requiring subjective interpretation by someone aware of allocation) and outcome assessors were blinded, risk of bias in measurement is low.

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns.

**b. Rationale:**
- The primary outcome (time to institutionalization) is clearly reported with full data (Kaplan-Meier curves, Cox regression hazard ratios, and results at 6, 12, and 24 months), consistent with a pre-specified primary outcome, and there is no evidence that this key result was selected from among multiple alternative analyses or measurements.
- However, the paper reports additional subgroup analyses by dementia severity that are explicitly described as post hoc/exploratory: "For descriptive purposes, we also estimated the survival curves by the study group separately for patients at different stages of dementia." This acknowledgment of a non-prespecified, exploratory subgroup analysis — which is highlighted prominently in the abstract/conclusions as suggesting the intervention "might be especially beneficial to patients with severe dementia" — raises some concern about selective emphasis of favorable subgroup findings.
- No statistical analysis plan or trial registry entry with a fully pre-specified plan (finalized before unblinded data were available) was identified/available to confirm that the reported primary time-to-institutionalization analysis and all associated comparisons (e.g., choice of time-dependent Cox model triggered by non-proportional hazards) were planned in advance rather than adapted based on the observed data ("The Kaplan-Meier method was used... The significance... was assessed using a time-dependent Cox regression analysis, because the assumption of proportional hazards was not met in the data" — indicating a data-driven choice of statistical model).
- Given the absence of a verifiable pre-specified analysis plan and evidence of a data-driven modeling choice plus prominently reported exploratory subgroup findings, this domain is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

# Overall Risk of Bias Judgement: **High risk of bias**

**Rationale:** Although missing outcome data and measurement of the outcome are at low risk of bias, the trial has "Some concerns" in three domains (randomization, deviations from intended intervention, and selective reporting). Per RoB 2 guidance, "some concerns" for multiple domains can lead to an overall judgement of "high risk of bias" when, in combination, they substantially lower confidence in the result. Here, uncertainty about allocation concealment integrity (compounded by the coordinator's dual role and the ad hoc ratio change), the unblinded nature of the intervention with unclear balance of trial-context-driven deviations, and the reliance on an exploratory/data-driven subgroup analysis and statistical model selection collectively raise substantial concern about the trustworthiness of the reported time-to-institutionalization effect, particularly given that this same result showed a significant interaction with time (diminishing effect, Group×time p=.028) suggesting some sensitivity of the finding to analytic choices. Therefore, the overall judgement is **high risk of bias**.

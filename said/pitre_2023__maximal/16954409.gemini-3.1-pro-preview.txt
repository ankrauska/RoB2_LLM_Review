**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **Judgement:** Some concerns
* **Rationale:** The allocation sequence was generated randomly using computer-generated random numbers. However, allocation concealment was performed using "sealed envelopes by specialist nurse." The report does not specify if the envelopes were opaque and sequentially numbered, which raises some concerns about whether the allocation sequence was adequately concealed until participants were enrolled. Baseline characteristics (age, duration of infertility, BMI) were well balanced across the groups.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The trial does not report blinding of participants or personnel, meaning they were likely aware of the assigned interventions (different gonadotrophin preparations). However, there is no evidence to suggest that this lack of blinding led to deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, the analysis included all randomized participants in their assigned groups, consistent with an intention-to-treat approach.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** Outcome data were available for all randomized participants. As shown in the study flow and Table II, all 241 randomized patients (81 in Group I, 80 in Group II, and 80 in Group III) were analyzed for the outcome of clinical pregnancy rate, indicating no loss to follow-up.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The outcome (clinical pregnancy) was appropriately measured using transvaginal ultrasound (TV-US) at 6 weeks after IUI. Although outcome assessors were likely not blinded to the intervention received, clinical pregnancy determined by ultrasound is an objective outcome, and its assessment is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the assigned intervention.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
* **Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **Rationale:** The trial explicitly states that "The primary outcome measure was clinical pregnancy rate," and this outcome is reported clearly for all groups. There is no evidence that the reported numerical result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or multiple analyses of the data based on the significance or direction of the findings.

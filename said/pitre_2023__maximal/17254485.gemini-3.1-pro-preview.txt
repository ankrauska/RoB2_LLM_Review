Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance and the provided trial text, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of the rate of emergency hemostasis (assessing the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline):

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** Some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** The trial generated the allocation sequence using a random number table produced in Microsoft Excel ("A random number table was produced through Microsoft Excel"), which is an adequate method for sequence generation. However, there is no information provided in the manuscript regarding allocation concealment (e.g., whether sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes or a central randomization system were used). While baseline characteristics were balanced between groups (Table 1), the lack of information on allocation concealment raises some concerns. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** Given the nature of the interventions (endoscopic embolization and ligation), participants and personnel were inevitably aware of the assigned intervention. However, there is no evidence that deviations from the intended interventions arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, the analysis accounted for all randomized participants in their assigned groups for the primary outcome of emergency hemostasis (90 in the therapy group and 82 in the control group), which corresponds to an appropriate intention-to-treat analysis.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
* **c. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **d. Rationale:** Outcome data for emergency hemostasis were available for all randomized participants. The trial reports the hemostasis outcomes for exactly 90 patients in the therapy group and 82 patients in the control group, matching the initial randomized numbers (total n=172). Therefore, there is no missing outcome data that could bias the result. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
* **a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
* **b. Rationale:** The method for measuring the outcome (clinical and endoscopic confirmation of bleeding cessation) is appropriate and objective. Although the outcome assessors (endoscopists/physicians) were likely aware of the intervention received, emergency hemostasis is an objective clinical endpoint that is unlikely to be materially influenced by knowledge of the intervention assignment.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
* **a. Judgement:** Some concerns
* **b. Rationale:** There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or published trial protocol/registration in the provided text that predates the availability of unblinded outcome data. Therefore, it is not possible to confirm with certainty that the analysis plans were finalized before data were accessed, leading to a default judgment of some concerns. However, emergency hemostasis is a standard and expected primary outcome for this acute condition, reducing the likelihood that it was selected simply based on statistical significance.

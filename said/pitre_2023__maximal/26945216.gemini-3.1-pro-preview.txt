**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
The allocation sequence was randomly generated and adequately concealed. While there was a statistically significant baseline imbalance in the affected side, this is likely due to chance given the adequate randomization methods used.
b. Rationale: The study used "computerized block randomization and allocation group concealed in envelops." Although there was a significantly higher proportion of participants with pain on the right trapezius muscle at baseline (p=0.015), this single imbalance in the context of robust sequence generation and concealment is compatible with chance.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
Although personnel (the therapist) were not blinded, there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose because of the trial context. Furthermore, an appropriate analysis was used, as all randomized participants were included in the analysis. 
b. Rationale: The trial report states, "The principal investigator acted as... therapist administering all study treatment," meaning personnel were unblinded. However, there is no indication of non-protocol interventions being administered. The study flow chart (Figure 3) and results confirm that all 105 participants "completed the study and were entered into the analysis," consistent with intention-to-treat principles.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
c. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
Outcome data were available for all randomized participants.
d. Rationale: The authors state that "All completed the study and were entered into the analysis (Fig. 3)," meaning there was 0% loss to follow-up for the pain intensity outcome.

**4. measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
Pain intensity is a participant-reported outcome. Although the authors describe the study as "single-blind," it is highly unlikely that participants were successfully blinded to the nature of the interventions (TENS and IFT versus control standard care). Knowledge of receiving an active electrical stimulation machine versus standard care alone is very likely to influence subjective patient-reported pain scores.
b. Rationale: Pain intensity was measured using a "100-mm visual analog scale". The study notes, "The participants were blind to the allocation to treatment groups after randomization," but active interventions involved distinct sensory experiences (electrical currents) compared to the control group. Because pain is a subjective participant-reported outcome, lack of true blinding makes it likely that the outcome assessment was influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: Provide rational for your judgement.
The reported outcomes correspond to the intended measurements and analyses, with no indication of selective reporting of results based on statistical significance. 
b. Rationale: The study evaluated all intended time points ("at baseline, immediately after the first treatment, before the eighth treatment, and 1 wk after the eighth treatment") and reported findings for all pre-specified outcomes (pain intensity and cervical ROM) completely and transparently across all intervention groups.

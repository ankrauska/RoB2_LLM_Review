**Assessment scope:** randomized comparison among participants with residual vein thrombosis (RVT): continuation of LMWH for a further 6 months (group A1, *n*=119) versus discontinuation of LMWH (group A2, *n*=123). Group B was not randomized and is not included in this RoB 2 comparison. Effect of interest: **effect of assignment to intervention at baseline**.

1. **Bias due to randomization: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   The report states that participants with RVT “were then randomly assigned” and that “random assignment was determined by a sealed-envelope technique based on clinical chart number with a block size of 10.” However, this does not adequately establish either a genuinely random allocation sequence or effective allocation concealment.

   * **Random sequence:** The use of blocks does not itself confirm that allocation order within blocks was random. The phrase “based on clinical chart number” is insufficiently clear and could potentially imply a predictable allocation process. Thus, the allocation sequence is **not adequately described** as random.
   * **Allocation concealment:** Although sealed envelopes were used, the publication does not state that envelopes were sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof, opened sequentially, and opened only after irrevocable enrolment. Therefore, concealment until enrolment and assignment is uncertain.
   * **Baseline balance:** Table 1 shows broadly similar baseline characteristics between A1 and A2, including age, sex, recent surgery, chemotherapy, metastasis and thrombosis characteristics. There is no clear imbalance in a key prognostic factor suggesting failure of randomization. The very close balance in several cancer-site categories is notable but, without more information, does not establish a randomization failure.

   Under RoB 2, lack of information on allocation concealment generally leads to **some concerns** when baseline data do not themselves indicate a problem.

2. **Deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   Participants and treating clinicians were explicitly unblinded: “Patients and treating physicians were not masked to treatment or status of RVT.” Therefore, participants and personnel were aware of assigned treatment.

   However, for the effect of **assignment** to continued versus discontinued LMWH, lack of blinding alone does not establish bias. The report provides no evidence that deviations inconsistent with protocol arose because of the trial context, were differential between groups, and affected recurrent VTE. The assigned strategies were implemented as intended: A1 continued nadroparin, whereas A2 discontinued LMWH.

   Adherence in the continued-treatment arm was high: “15 of the 119 patients interrupted LMWH for less than 5 days during study treatment.” Such brief interruptions are unlikely to represent an important deviation from the assigned strategy. No important imbalance in cointerventions or trial-context-driven crossover is reported.

   The authors also state: “Data were analyzed on an intention-to-treat basis.” The reported analyses retained participants in their randomized groups, which is appropriate for estimating the effect of assignment. Therefore, despite the open-label design, there is no reported evidence of trial-context deviations likely to bias the recurrent-VTE comparison.

3. **Missing outcome data: low risk of bias**

   c. **Judgement:** Low risk of bias.

   d. **Rationale:**  
   Recurrent VTE was assessed during planned follow-up, and the report provides event and mortality information for the randomized groups. The trial states that all enrolled patients were observed after treatment discontinuation and attended visits at 3-month intervals; patients were instructed to contact the study centre if VTE- or bleeding-related symptoms occurred.

   The numbers no longer under follow-up appear largely attributable to observed recurrent events and deaths rather than unexplained loss to follow-up. Deaths were explicitly reported—12/119 in A1 and 19/123 in A2—and all were attributed to cancer progression. The report does not identify substantial unreported losses to follow-up or missing VTE-status data.

   Death is an important competing event in this cancer population, but it was documented rather than missing. There is no indication that recurrent-VTE outcome data were missing differentially between groups, nor evidence that any unobserved data depended on the unobserved recurrence status. Accordingly, missing outcome data are unlikely to have materially biased the result.

4. **Measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias**

   a. **Judgement:** High risk of bias.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   The method used to diagnose recurrent VTE was appropriate and objectively defined. Recurrent DVT was diagnosed using compression ultrasonography criteria, including a newly non-compressible segment or “an increase of more than 4 mm in the diameter of the residual thrombus during compression.” Possible pulmonary embolism was evaluated using objective diagnostic algorithms, ventilation-perfusion scanning, or computed tomography.

   Furthermore, outcome adjudication was blinded: “All outcomes and deaths were evaluated by a central committee whose members were unaware of the patient’s name, enrolling center, results of RVT assays, and group assignment.” Thus, assessor awareness itself was unlikely to bias diagnosis.

   Nevertheless, the outcome ascertainment period differed systematically between randomized groups. The authors state: “Follow-up visits lasted 18 months in group A1 and 12 months in groups A2 and B.” This arose because A1 continued LMWH for 6 months and was subsequently followed after treatment discontinuation, while A2 stopped LMWH at randomization. The report acknowledges that “the 6-month difference in follow-up duration between groups A1 and A2 … may have resulted in an underestimate of the event rate in the latter group.”

   The reported overall recurrence counts—22/119 in A1 versus 27/123 in A2—therefore appear to have been accumulated over unequal periods of observation. This creates differential opportunity to identify recurrent VTE between groups. Although blinded adjudication reduces diagnostic suspicion bias, unequal follow-up/ascertainment is a material problem for the recurrent-event comparison and could bias the estimated effect, plausibly in favour of LMWH discontinuation because A2 had less total time in which to experience a recurrence.

5. **Selective reporting: some concerns**

   a. **Judgement:** Some concerns.

   b. **Rationale:**  
   The trial registration record identifies the study as randomized and lists “Recurrent Deep Vein Thrombosis and/or Pulmonary Embolism” as the primary outcome. This is consistent with the published primary outcome of recurrent VTE.

   However, the registration record provides no prespecified outcome time point, detailed outcome definitions, statistical analysis plan, covariate-adjustment plan, or confirmation that the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available. Consequently, it is not possible to verify that the reported recurrent-VTE analysis was selected from neither multiple eligible follow-up times nor multiple possible analyses.

   There are also reporting ambiguities:
   * The publication states that the primary endpoint was recurrent VTE “during the 1 year after discontinuation of LMWH,” but follow-up differed by randomized group.
   * Different adjusted hazard ratios for A2 versus A1 are presented in different parts of the report (abstract: HR 1.37; Results: HR 1.58), without a fully clear explanation of the distinct analysis periods underlying them.
   * The registry lists minor bleeding and death due to cancer as secondary outcomes, whereas the publication identifies major bleeding as the secondary endpoint.

   These discrepancies do not prove selection based on the results, but absence of a sufficiently detailed, time-stamped prespecified analysis plan leaves **some concerns** about selection among possible outcome time points and analyses.

Based on the provided trial report and the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome of subjective global assessment (SGA) score, assessing the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect).

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** The trial used a genuinely random allocation sequence ("randomized by a computer-generated list"). Allocation concealment was adequately implemented because the list was "maintained by a third party that was not involved in the conduction of the study" and "Marked tablet packs were designated for each patient." Furthermore, baseline characteristics were well balanced, with the authors noting "no significant difference in any baseline parameter between the two groups" (Table 1), indicating no concerns with the randomization process.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** The trial was double-blinded. The report explicitly states, "Both patients and investigators were blinded" and that the "appearance, packaging, and labeling of the tablets were identical. Both kinds of tablets had artificial mint flavor to give an identical taste." Because participants, carers, and personnel were unaware of the assigned interventions, deviations from the intended intervention driven by the trial context are unlikely. Furthermore, the investigators stated that "Analyses were intention to treat irrespective of adherence to treatment regimen," which is the appropriate analysis strategy for estimating the effect of assignment to intervention.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** Data were available for all or most participants. The authors note that while some patients stopped the study medication due to intolerance (4 in the treatment group, 3 in the placebo group), "biochemical tests and nutritional assessment were continued for 52 wk according to the study protocol." While there were a few deaths and transfers (2 deaths and 2 transfers in treatment; 5 deaths and 1 transfer in placebo), these missing data are relatively small in number and the outcome assessments were systematically pursued for the remainder of the cohort regardless of adherence, demonstrating little evidence that the missingness of SGA scores depended on their true values.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** The method of measuring the outcome (SGA) is an established and appropriate tool. Most importantly, the outcome assessors were blinded. The publication states: "SGA was performed... by two trained observers who were blinded from the treatment group allocation and biochemical results of the patients." Thus, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias
**b. Rationale:** The authors explicitly identified the SGA score as their pre-specified primary outcome parameter, noting: "The sample size was estimated before the study... with the SGA as the primary outcome measure." They consistently reported the SGA outcomes at all planned time points (0, 12, 24, 36, and 52 weeks) and provided both the overall score and sub-component scores without evidence of selectively choosing analyses or time points based on statistical significance.

# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Acoustic Reaction Time

---

## 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The allocation sequence was randomly generated using an online tool with permuted block randomization, and concealment was implemented via serially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes managed by a non-involved third party. No evidence of baseline imbalance suggesting problems with randomization.

**b. Rationale:** 
- Sequence random: "Participants were allocated randomly to bright light (BRIGHT), monochromatic blue light (BLUE), or a control condition (CONTROL) using an online tool (available at www.randomization.com) which applies permuted block randomization with randomly varying block sizes of 3, 6, and 9."
- Allocation concealment: "The list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered sealed opaque envelopes." This meets minimal and extended criteria for adequate concealment.
- Baseline imbalance: Table 1 reports groups appear reasonably similar (age, VO2max, sport type distributions); no indication of substantial imbalance in key prognostic factors. Two exclusions (due to invalid VO2peak measurement, unrelated to intervention/outcome) were pre-randomization eligibility issues, not raising concern.

---

## 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns. Participants and personnel were not blinded (masking = NONE per protocol), which raises the possibility that trial-context-related deviations occurred; however, there is no clear evidence that such deviations affected the acoustic reaction time outcome, and an appropriate (per pre-specified) analysis was used.

**b. Rationale:**
- Awareness: The protocol states "maskingInfo: masking: NONE." Additionally, "Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to, but not the other two light conditions" — implying participants were aware of their own assignment. The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to light condition, but participants themselves were not.
- Deviations from trial context: No specific evidence is reported that participants' behavior (e.g., differential effort, arousal, or performance motivation) related to knowing their light condition materially affected reaction time performance. The reaction time task is an objective simple acoustic task with limited room for conscious behavioral influence, though psychological/arousal effects from awareness of "bright" vs "control" light cannot be entirely excluded.
- Analysis: "Analyses of covariance were used to compare reaction time and handgrip strength after the light exposure between the three groups adjusted for reaction time and handgrip strength before the light exposure" — this is consistent with an appropriate ITT-type analysis, with all 72 randomized (eligible) participants included per group, no post-randomization exclusions for non-adherence.
- Because participants were unblinded and there is no explicit statement ruling out trial-context effects (e.g., placebo/expectation effects on alertness-related outcome), this is judged as "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk," per the algorithm (Y/PY to awareness + NI on deviations arising from trial context → Some concerns).

---

## 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Outcome data were available for all analyzed participants (72/72) with no reported missingness for the reaction time outcome.

**b. Rationale:** 
- "The remaining 72 participants had a median age of 23 years" and results are reported for all three groups (n=24 each) for reaction time in Table 2, with no indication of missing data for this specific outcome. 
- The two exclusions (due to invalid VO2peak measurements) occurred before the light exposure intervention, at the eligibility confirmation stage, and are not related to the reaction time outcome itself — consistent with acceptable post-randomization exclusion of ineligible participants.
- No differential loss to follow-up or reasons for missingness related to the true outcome value are reported.

---

## 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns. The reaction time task itself is an objective, automated measurement (computer-based, minimal judgment involved), and measurement methods were identical across groups. However, participants (who serve as their own "outcome assessor" for behaviorally-mediated measures like reaction time) were aware of their intervention group, and psychological/arousal state (which could plausibly be influenced by awareness of exposure to "bright" light vs. control) could theoretically influence effort or alertness during the task.

**b. Rationale:**
- Appropriateness of method: "participants performed a computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task" with lapses (>500ms) excluded per pre-specified criteria (Lee et al. 2010) — method is standard, objective, and consistently applied across groups. Not inappropriate.
- Method identical across groups: The same task and equipment ("computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task") was used at the same time points in all three arms; no evidence of differential measurement procedures.
- Awareness of outcome assessor: Because reaction time depends on the participant's own psychomotor response (effort/alertness), and participants were aware of their allocated light condition ("Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to"), this raises the possibility that knowledge of intervention could influence engagement/alertness during testing, though this is an objective task with limited scope for conscious manipulation, unlike a patient-reported outcome.
- No specific evidence provided that assessment was actually influenced by awareness (e.g., differential motivation), so it does not meet the "High risk" threshold, but the potential for a subtle influence (via arousal/alertness expectation effects associated with "bright"/"blue" light narratives) cannot be excluded, warranting "Some concerns" rather than "Low risk."

---

## 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The outcome (reaction time) and its analysis method were pre-specified in the trial registry and matched the reported analysis, with no evidence of selection from multiple outcome measurements or multiple analytic approaches based on results.

**b. Rationale:**
- Trial registration (NCT02203539) lists "Reaction time in milliseconds (ms)" as a pre-specified secondary outcome, consistent with what is reported in the article.
- The article states: "All statistical methods used to compare the groups for reaction time and handgrip strength in the present report were pre-specified and registered." This directly indicates that the analysis (ANCOVA adjusting for baseline reaction time) was determined a priori and matches what is reported.
- Only one measurement approach for reaction time is reported (median reaction time from a single acoustic task), with no indication of multiple competing scales/timepoints/definitions from which a favorable result might have been selected.
- Both the primary ANCOVA and additional dose-response linear regression analyses are reported regardless of statistical significance (both null results reported transparently), suggesting no selective suppression of unfavorable results.

---

## **Overall Risk of Bias for Acoustic Reaction Time: Some concerns**

**Summary:** The trial shows low risk of bias for randomization, missing data, and selective reporting. However, "Some concerns" were identified for both deviations from intended intervention (due to lack of blinding of participants) and measurement of the outcome (due to potential for awareness-related influence on an outcome partly dependent on participant alertness/effort). Per RoB 2.0 guidance, an overall judgment of "Some concerns" is warranted, as no domain reached "High risk" but concerns exist in multiple domains related to lack of blinding.

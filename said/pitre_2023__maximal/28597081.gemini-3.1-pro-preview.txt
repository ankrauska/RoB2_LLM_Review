Based on the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the risk of bias assessment for the outcome "Acoustic reaction time" from the provided trial:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** The trial used an adequate method for random sequence generation and allocation concealment, and there is no evidence of concerning baseline imbalances.
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial publication states that participants were allocated "using an online tool (available at www.randomization.com) which applies permuted block randomization with randomly varying block sizes of 3, 6, and 9." Furthermore, allocation concealment was maintained as "The list was transmitted by a non-involved person using serially numbered sealed opaque envelopes." Baseline characteristics appear reasonably balanced across the 24 participants in each of the three groups.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** We are assessing the effect of assignment to the interventions at baseline (intention-to-treat effect). While participants were aware of the light exposure they received, there is no evidence that this led to deviations from the intended intervention (e.g., co-interventions) that arose specifically because of the trial context. Analyses were appropriately conducted on the randomized groups.
*   **b. Rationale:** The study states "Participants only knew the light condition they were exposed to, but not the other two light conditions," while the test supervisor was blinded. Although actual light intensities reaching the eyes were lower than intended in some groups (likely due to "different directions of gaze and sitting postures"), this reflects non-adherence rather than a deviation arising from the trial context (e.g., lack of equipoise). The analysis (ANCOVA) appropriately included the remaining eligible participants in their assigned groups.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** Outcome data were available for nearly all participants, and the reasons for missing data are not related to the true value of the outcome.
*   **b. Rationale:** Out of 74 randomized participants, 72 (97%) completed the trial and were analyzed. The two exclusions ("Two subjects in BRIGHT and CONTROL had to be excluded due to invalid measurements of peak oxygen uptake in the cardiorespiratory fitness test") were made retrospectively based on baseline fitness screening data rather than anything related to the intervention or the true value of the acoustic reaction time outcome. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** The method of measuring the outcome was appropriate, consistent across groups, and the outcome assessor (the computer/blinded supervisor) was not influenced by knowledge of the intervention.
*   **b. Rationale:** Acoustic reaction time was measured objectively using a "computer-based simple acoustic reaction time task" where subjects pressed the space bar. The trial notes that "The test supervisor (RK) was blinded to the light condition each participant was exposed to." Because this is an objective, computer-recorded measurement and the supervisor was blinded, the measurement of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
*   **a. Judgement:** The trial was analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan, and the reported result for reaction time matches the pre-specified outcomes without evidence of selective reporting from multiple measurements or analyses.
*   **b. Rationale:** The trial registration (NCT02203539) explicitly lists "Reaction time in milliseconds (ms)" as a pre-specified secondary outcome. The publication confirms that "All statistical methods used to compare the groups for reaction time and handgrip strength in the present report were pre-specified and registered," utilizing the planned ANCOVA adjusted for baseline values. There is no indication that the authors selectively reported this result from multiple eligible analyses or time points.

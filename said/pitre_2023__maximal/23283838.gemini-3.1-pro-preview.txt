Based on the provided trial report and the RoB 2.0 guidance, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome of between-session habituation (effect of assignment to interventions at baseline):

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
**b. Rationale:** The trial reports that "Participants were randomly assigned to 250mg of DCS or placebo in identical-appearing capsules by a data manager who had no interaction with participants." This indicates that the allocation sequence was adequately concealed by an independent data manager using identical capsules. Although there was a baseline imbalance in age (p = .019), such chance imbalances are expected in a small trial (N=34) and the authors confirmed it did not explain the group effects. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
**b. Rationale:** The trial was double-blinded, and the blinding was successful. The authors note, "Adequacy of the blind was assessed by having both participants and experimenters guess treatment assignment... neither participants nor experimenters were able to discern assignment." Because participants and trial personnel were unaware of intervention groups, deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context are highly unlikely.

**3. missing outcome data: some concerns**
**c. Judgement:** Some concerns.
**d. Rationale:** Out of the 34 randomized participants, 4 (2 DCS, 2 placebo) did not return for Visit 2 and were excluded from the analysis ("Only those participants completing Visit 2 are analyzed"). While the dropout rate is relatively low (~12%) and balanced between groups, the outcome relies on self-reported distress, and participants might have dropped out due to high levels of anxiety (i.e., missingness could depend on the true value of the outcome). No sensitivity analyses or intention-to-treat methods were used to account for these missing data. 

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
**b. Rationale:** The outcome (between-session habituation) was measured using the Subjective Units of Distress Scale (SUDS), which is a standard participant-reported outcome. Because both the participants (the outcome assessors in this case) and the experimenters were successfully blinded to the intervention received, the assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

**5. selective reporting: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias.
**b. Rationale:** The outcome of between-session habituation (defined as a drop of at least 5 points in average SUDS) and overall change were clearly defined in the methods. There is no indication that the reported result was selected from multiple eligible outcome measurements or analyses based on the direction or statistical significance of the results.

**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The study states that "Participants were randomly allocated to one of four study conditions", but does not provide details on the method used to generate the random sequence or whether allocation was concealed. There are no obvious baseline imbalances reported that would suggest a problem with the randomization process, but the lack of information regarding sequence generation and allocation concealment warrants a judgement of some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The trial involved a computerized educational intervention ("Participants completed this study individually using a custom-designed computer program"). Blinding of participants to their educational condition was not possible, but deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context are unlikely to have occurred given the automated delivery of the material. Almost all randomized participants were analyzed in the groups to which they were assigned.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Five participants failed to complete the delayed session and were excluded, and two participants were excluded for spending less than five minutes on the test and scoring very poorly. While post-randomization exclusion based on test performance is generally discouraged, the overall attrition was very low (leaving a final sample of 94 out of the initial recruits). Data for this outcome were available for nearly all participants randomized, making it unlikely that the missing data substantially biased the results.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome was measured using a diagnostic test consisting of 12 clinical vignettes where students had to identify the correct diagnosis from a multiple-choice list. This assessment was administered via a computer program, meaning it was an objective, observer-independent measurement. The assessment of the outcome could not have been influenced by knowledge of the intervention received by human assessors. 

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: There is no mention of a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or a trial registration that was finalized before unblinded data were available. However, the reported outcomes (immediate and delayed memory and diagnostic test scores) are consistent with the obvious, expected measures for this type of educational study. Because there is no protocol to verify the original analysis intentions, the risk of bias is rated as having some concerns.

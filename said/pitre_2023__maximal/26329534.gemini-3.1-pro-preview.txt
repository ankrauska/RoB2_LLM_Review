**1. bias due to randomization: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: The study reports that a "random sample" was selected and that pharmacists were "randomized to the postal mail group," but it does not provide details on the specific method used to generate the allocation sequence or whether allocation was concealed. While baseline characteristics (Table 1) appear generally balanced, the lack of information regarding allocation concealment raises some concerns.

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: Participants and personnel were inevitably aware of the assigned intervention (delivery method of the survey). However, there is no evidence to suggest that deviations from the intended interventions arose due to the trial context, and all participants were analyzed according to their randomized groups.

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The outcome being assessed is the response rate itself (whether a participant returned a completed survey). Therefore, the outcome data (response status) is known for all participants to whom the survey was successfully delivered. While 233 surveys were undeliverable, this was accounted for in the denominator, and there is no indication that missingness of the response status outcome depends on its true value.

**4. measurement of the outcome: low risk of bias**
a. Judgement: low risk of bias
b. Rationale: The method of measuring the outcome (calculating the proportion of completed, returned surveys) is appropriate and objective. Although outcome assessors were likely aware of the intervention received, the assessment of whether a survey was completed and returned involves no subjective judgment and is highly unlikely to be influenced by knowledge of the intervention.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
a. Judgement: some concerns
b. Rationale: There is no reference to a pre-specified statistical analysis plan or trial registry provided in the article. While the outcome (response rate) is standard and clearly defined, the absence of a finalized, pre-specified protocol leaves some concerns regarding the potential selection of reported analyses, though it is unlikely that the specific measure of response rate was selected from multiple eligible measurements.

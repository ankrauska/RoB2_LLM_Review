Based on the RoB 2.0 tool, here is the assessment of the risk of bias for the outcome **length of hospital stay**:

**1. bias due to randomization: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** The allocation sequence was adequately generated and concealed, and baseline characteristics appeared balanced.
**b. Rationale:** The trial used an adequate method for randomization and allocation concealment, stating: "Procedure type was assigned by a computer-generated randomization list, and concealment of randomization was done by telephone inquiry." Baseline characteristics (age, gravidity, parity, Quetelet Index, uterine weight) were similar between groups. 

**2. deviations from intended intervention: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Although participants and personnel were not blinded to the surgical interventions, there is no evidence that deviations from intended interventions arose due to the trial context, and all participants were analyzed in their randomized groups.
**b. Rationale:** Given the nature of the interventions (abdominal vs laparoscopic surgery), blinding was not feasible. However, there is no indication of unbalanced co-interventions or deviations from the protocol driven by expectations. The authors report that "There were no conversions from LAVH to AH," and the numbers of participants randomized (28 LAVH, 20 AH) match the numbers analyzed for the hospital stay outcome. 

**3. missing outcome data: low risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** Outcome data for length of hospital stay were available for all randomized participants.
**b. Rationale:** The trial enrolled 48 patients, and data regarding the hospital stay outcome appear to have been recorded and reported for all 48 participants, with no loss to follow-up or exclusions mentioned for this outcome.

**4. measurement of the outcome: high risk of bias**
**a. Judgement:** The outcome assessors (the patients) were aware of the intervention received, and the assessment of the outcome is highly likely to have been influenced by this knowledge.
**b. Rationale:** The trial explicitly states: "Each patient decided for herself when she was ready to be discharged from hospital." Since patients were unblinded to their surgical approach, their expectations regarding recovery (laparoscopic surgery is typically associated with faster recovery and shorter hospital stays than open surgery) likely influenced their decision on when to go home. Therefore, the measurement of the outcome (length of stay) is subjective and highly susceptible to bias based on knowledge of the intervention.

**5. selective reporting: some concerns**
**a. Judgement:** There is no mention of a pre-published protocol or trial registry to confirm the pre-specified analysis plan.
**b. Rationale:** The authors note in the methods that "We defined the primary outcome measure as length of hospital stay" and calculated their sample size based on this. However, without a date-stamped, pre-specified trial registry or protocol available from before unblinded data analysis, we cannot definitively rule out selection of the reported result, leading to some concerns.

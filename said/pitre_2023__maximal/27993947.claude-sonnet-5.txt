# Risk of Bias Assessment: RoB 2.0
## Outcome: Shared Decision Making Score

---

### 1. Bias due to randomization: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The allocation sequence was random and adequately concealed, and there is no evidence of baseline imbalances suggestive of a problem with the randomization process.

**b. Rationale:** The trial report states: "Randomization was performed by means of a computer-generated random sequence with stratification for age, gender, and positive family history for CHD using the Pocock and Simon method." This confirms a random component (signalling question 1.1: Yes). Allocation concealment is supported by the fact that randomization occurred only "once a study participant completed the first study visit and scheduled the second study visit," and was generated by an investigator (HJ) independent of enrollment procedures, using computer software — no indication that recruiters could foresee assignments (signalling question 1.2: Probably yes). Baseline characteristics (Table 1) were well-balanced between groups (age, gender, smoking, family history, BMI, SBP, waist circumference, lipid values, education), with all p-values >0.19, consistent with chance (signalling question 1.3: No).

---

### 2. Deviations from intended intervention (effect of assignment to intervention): **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to lack of blinding of participants and personnel, combined with limited explicit reporting on whether trial-context-related deviations occurred, though there is no direct evidence that such deviations affected the SDM outcome.

**b. Rationale:** This was an open-label trial: "maskingInfo": "masking": "NONE" (per registry), and by design patients and physicians were aware of which risk information (CRS vs. CRS*GRS) was disclosed, since disclosure of genetic risk was the intervention itself and was central to the encounter (signalling question 2.1/2.2: Yes — awareness is inherent to the intervention). However, there is no indication in the manuscript that deviations from the intended intervention (i.e., failure to deliver the intended disclosure/decision aid protocol, or the occurrence of non-protocol interventions related to the trial context) occurred: "Disclosure of risk in both arms was facilitated by the use of a modified version of the Statin Choice decision aid" applied uniformly, and the protocol was implemented similarly (genetic counselor visit followed by physician visit) in both arms, differing only in content of information (CRS vs. CRS*GRS) — which is the intervention itself, not a deviation. There is no reported evidence of co-interventions or contextual effects biasing the SDM outcome. Analysis was appropriate: "Analyses were performed with randomization groups as the independent variables," consistent with an ITT-type comparison, and nearly all randomized participants were analyzed for this outcome (206/207 completed the SDM survey; all completed physician satisfaction survey). Given the awareness of assigned intervention (unavoidable given the nature of the intervention) but no clear evidence of trial-context-driven deviations affecting the outcome, this is judged as "Some concerns" rather than high risk, per the algorithm (awareness=Yes, deviations=No information/Probably no → Some concerns).

---

### 3. Missing outcome data: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. Outcome data were available for nearly all randomized participants.

**b. Rationale:** "Of 216 enrolled participants...207 were randomized and completed the second study visit including the genetic counselor and physician appointments." For the SDM outcome specifically: "The SDM survey was completed by 206 study participants" out of 207 randomized (99.5%), and the physician visit satisfaction survey "was completed by all study participants" (207/207, 100%). This very small amount of missing data (1 participant) could not plausibly have altered the study's conclusions given the scores reported in Table 2 (n=103 vs 103, essentially full data in each arm). There is no indication that the single missing SDM survey was related to its true value in a manner likely to bias results.

---

### 4. Measurement of the outcome: **Some concerns**

**a. Judgement:** Some concerns, primarily due to the nature of the SDM outcome as a participant-reported outcome in a non-blinded trial, where knowledge of intervention (genetic vs. conventional risk information) could plausibly influence responses, although the effect is likely modest given the SDM-Q assesses process/interaction elements rather than genetic-risk-specific content.

**b. Rationale:** The method of measurement was appropriate: the SDM-Q is "a validated 11-question survey that assesses patients' perception of the SDM process," and OPTION5 is a validated observer-rated scale, both suitable for this outcome (signalling question 4.1: No). Measurement methods did not differ between arms — the same SDM-Q and OPTION5 scoring were applied identically in both groups (signalling question 4.2: No). However, this is a participant-reported outcome (SDM-Q) or an outcome scored by video assessors who reviewed non-blinded encounters (OPTION5); outcome assessors (patients themselves, and to a lesser extent the OPTION5 video coder who could observe content of the encounter) were aware of intervention assignment, since the disclosed risk information (genetic vs. conventional) was evident within the recorded encounter and to the patient (signalling question 4.3: Yes). For participant-reported outcomes, assessment "is potentially influenced by knowledge of intervention received," raising the risk to at least "Some concerns" by default (signalling question 4.4: Yes). However, there is no strong reason to believe that participants' perception of the SDM process was systematically influenced in one particular direction by knowledge of which risk score type was disclosed (signalling question 4.5: Probably no) — the SDM-Q items concern process features (time for questions, feeling included, weighing options) rather than the specific genetic content, and results were highly consistent between arms with high scores in both. This supports a judgement of "Some concerns" rather than "High risk."

---

### 5. Selective reporting: **Low risk of bias**

**a. Judgement:** Low risk of bias. The SDM outcome was pre-specified in the trial registry as a named secondary outcome and measured using two validated, pre-specified instruments (SDM-Q and OPTION5), and results appear to be fully reported (all 11 SDM-Q items and OPTION5 reported) without evidence of selective reporting from multiple measures or analyses.

**b. Rationale:** The trial registration (NCT01936675) explicitly lists "Shared decision making (as assessed by survey)" as a pre-specified secondary outcome (signalling question 5.1: Probably yes, given consistency between registered outcome and reported outcome, though a fully-detailed pre-specified SAP was not available for review). The publication reports the full SDM-Q score, all 11 individual item responses (Table 2), and additionally an independent objective measure (OPTION5 video-based scoring), with no evidence of selective reporting among multiple candidate outcome measures (signalling question 5.2: No — all 11 items and the composite score are reported, plus OPTION5 as a complementary measure clearly rather than a substitute). There is no evidence of multiple alternative analyses (e.g., alternative scoring methods, subsets, adjusted vs. unadjusted models) being conducted and selectively reported for this outcome (signalling question 5.3: No) — a single, consistent scoring and analytic approach (Wilcoxon/t-test, Fisher's exact test) was applied and reported transparently, including non-significant findings, which reduces concern that results were selected based on favorability.

---

## Overall Risk of Bias for "Shared Decision Making Score": **Some concerns**

Per RoB 2.0 guidance, since two domains (Deviations from intended intervention; Measurement of the outcome) are judged as "Some concerns" and no domain is judged "High risk," but concerns exist in multiple domains that could jointly reduce confidence in the result, the overall judgement is **Some concerns**.
